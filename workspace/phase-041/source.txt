# Chapter 41: Buying Property

The air in the upper levels of the Babel Hub was thin, filtered, and smelled aggressively of sterile pine—a synthetic scent designed to mask the underlying rot of a dying reality. Ren Qi stood at the threshold of the Brokerage District, a sprawling labyrinth of ivory pillars and holographic placards that flickered with the desperation of a thousand desperate realtors. He ignored the high-end listings projected in floating amber screens. He wasn't here for a penthouse in the Glass Spires. He was here for something with history, something the system had long since stopped caring about.

He found the office he was looking for tucked between a weapons depot and a tavern that catered to the lower-tier scavengers. It was a cramped, dimly lit booth labeled simply: Estate & Salvage. Inside, a man with skin the color of parched parchment sat hunched over a desk, his eyes darting frantically across a pile of physical, paper ledgers. He was a broker who dealt in the refuse of the Hub—the properties that had been abandoned, haunted, or marked as structural liabilities.

Ren Qi stepped inside. The door chimed with a dissonant, low-frequency buzz that made the broker jump.

I am looking for property, Ren Qi said. His voice was flat, cutting through the stale air. I do not care about the location. I care about the footprint and the potential for modification.

The broker squinted at him, his gaze flicking to the dark, rusted iron of the Cuirass of the Iron Eater visible under Ren Qi's coat. He saw the cold, detached look in the customer’s eyes and sighed, tossing a thin, black-bordered book onto the desk.

Everything in the core is taken, the broker rasped, his voice sounding like dry leaves skittering on pavement. If you want space, you head to the outskirts. But let me save you the trouble—anything out there is either unstable, cursed, or currently occupied by entities that don't take kindly to eviction notices.

Ren Qi opened the ledger, his fingers tracing the topography of the map. He stopped at a page marked with a red, jagged seal. It was a drawing of a sprawling manor, its walls jagged and overgrown with what looked like shadow-vines. The entry simply stated: Site 44-B. Former residence of a lost architect. Structural integrity: fluctuating. Occupant status: hostile.

That one, Ren Qi said.

The broker laughed, a wet, rattling sound. That place is a deathtrap, kid. It was built during the first cycle of the tower, and the residual energy from the original failure has soaked into the foundation. It’s crawling with phantoms. Specters. The last party that tried to claim it didn't last ten minutes before their sanity snapped and they turned their blades on each other. It’s yours for a song, but don't come crying to me when you're being hunted by your own shadow.

Ren Qi didn't blink. He reached into his pack, pulling out a handful of shimmering, condensed mana crystals. He laid them on the desk. They weren't just currency; they were high-density energy units that would have taken a standard scavenger months to collect.

The broker’s eyes widened, his hands trembling as he reached out to verify the quality. The deal was struck in seconds. A physical contract, etched into a plate of cold lead, materialized on the table. Ren Qi signed it, the ink glowing with a faint, violet light as it fused into the metal.

The manor was located in a forgotten sector at the very edge of the Hub, where the ambient light of the tower grew dim and the air felt heavy with the weight of unremembered things. It was a massive, decaying structure that loomed over the surrounding slums like a tombstone. As Ren Qi approached the iron gates, he felt the familiar, low-frequency hum of a system anomaly.

[System Notification: You have entered a High-Risk Property: The Haunted Manor. Environment detected: Severe Spiritual Infestation. Stability Rating: 0.00%.]

The gates groaned open, not because of a mechanism, but as if the house itself were welcoming its next victim. He walked into the courtyard. The ground was littered with the remnants of past attempts to breach the property—broken swords, shattered vials, and the rusted remains of protective charms.

As he crossed the threshold, the temperature plummeted. The shadows in the corner of his vision detached themselves from the walls, coalescing into translucent, elongated figures. They drifted toward him, their faces hollow pits of longing and malice. They were the remnant data of the previous inhabitants, trapped in an infinite, suffering loop.

Ren Qi stopped. He looked at the ghosts, his expression entirely, maddeningly bored.

The lead specter shrieked, a sound that vibrated in his teeth, and lunged. It passed through his chest, a wave of unnatural cold that would have frozen the blood of any other man. Ren Qi didn't even shiver. He reached into his internal storage, his hands moving with the precision of a surgeon, and pulled out a containment field—a device he had crafted using the components he’d salvaged in the Forge of Infinite Loops.

Input: [Hostile Specter]. Output: [Bound Servant Contract].

He slammed the containment field into the floor. A surge of iridescent violet light erupted from the device, washing over the courtyard. The specters froze, their agonizing screams cut short as the energy field latched onto them, rewriting their unstable code into a rigid, manageable structure.

The lead ghost flickered, its translucent form solidifying into a sharp, monochromatic silhouette. It bowed. The others followed suit, their malice replaced by the blank, obedient expression of a construct.

You are the staff, Ren Qi said, his voice echoing in the cold, dusty hall. Clean the foyer. Repair the structural fractures in the north wing. And do not make noise. I have work to do.

The ghosts turned, moving in silent, synchronized harmony. They drifted to their tasks, their incorporeal hands passing through brooms and tools, leaving behind a trail of pristine cleanliness. Ren Qi ignored them. He walked to the center of the drawing-room, where a large, velvet-upholstered armchair sat covered in layers of cobwebs. He wiped it off with his hand, sat down, and pulled out his terminal.

He began to map the manor. It was a perfect site—an isolated, self-contained instance of the tower’s architecture. By anchoring his own energy to the foundation of the house, he could create a secure, private node for his operations. He would call it The Exchange.

He looked at his wrist display. The bounty on his head had increased, the system flagging his sudden acquisition of property as a direct affront to the Hub’s zoning protocols. He didn't care. He opened his ledger and began to write, his stylus clacking with a rhythmic, steady cadence.

The house grew quiet. The spectral staff worked with a tireless, eerie efficiency, the manor slowly transforming from a rotting tomb into a place of cold, organized precision. The flickering lights of the hallway solidified, the walls stopped groaning, and the oppressive, heavy atmosphere of the spirits was replaced by the clean, sterile scent of ozone and freshly polished stone.

Ren Qi leaned back in the chair, watching a specter in the corner carefully reassemble a broken piece of stained glass. It was perfect. A controlled environment, a library of data, and a base of operations that answered only to him. He was no longer just an intruder in the Babel Hub; he was the owner of his own reality.

His terminal pinged. A message from the outside. A ping from the Prime Arbiter’s network, tracing his IP, looking for the source of the recent systemic collapses. They were getting closer. He knew it, and he welcomed it.

He didn't need to build an army. He didn't need to join a guild. He had the infrastructure, he had the spirits, and he had the key to the next, more dangerous level of the tower.

He stood up, his boots clicking sharply against the marble floor. The house felt solid, anchored, and ready. He walked to the window, looking out over the flickering, chaotic neon of the Babel Hub. Far in the distance, he could see the silhouette of the tower, rising into the void like a jagged needle.

The next sector was the Citadel of Forgotten Law. It was a place of high-tier bureaucracy, where the rules of the system were enforced by entities that viewed reality as a math problem to be solved through erasure. They expected him to play by the rules. They expected him to struggle.

Ren Qi reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, metallic coin, flipping it over his knuckles. He caught it, the sound of the metal meeting his skin sounding like a final, decisive note.

The audit was just beginning, and he was the only one with the ledger. He turned away from the window, his expression cold and unyielding. The specters watched him from the shadows, their eyes glowing with a faint, subservient light.

He sat back down at his desk, his fingers hovering over the terminal.

Audit Phase 4: Commencing. Location: The Citadel. Objective: Total structural reorganization.

He closed the terminal, the silence of the manor settling around him like a heavy, protective blanket. He was home. He had a base, he had resources, and he had a plan. The Hub was about to learn exactly what happened when you tried to evict the one person who knew how the walls were built.

As he drifted off into a shallow, meditative rest, he could hear the faint, distant sound of a portal opening in the sector. They were coming. They were bringing their reality-auditors, their enforcement units, and their code-breakers.

He didn't move. He didn't reach for his blade. He simply closed his eyes, his mind already drifting upward, calculating the structural stress of the next floor. The rent was due, and he was ready to collect.

The manor was silent, the ghosts standing at their stations like statues in a museum, their existence now permanently bound to the will of the Architect. The room felt heavy with the scent of potential. Ren Qi’s life in the Hub had reached a point of absolute, crystalline clarity. Every variable, every resource, every spirit—it was all now part of the ledger. And he was the only one allowed to write in it.

He opened his eyes once more, looking at the flickering interface of his personal system. The bounty notice was still there, a pulsing, red irritation in his peripheral vision.

[System Notification: Warning: High-Level Enforcement Unit (Prime Arbiter Vanguard) detected in Sector 4-A. Target: User: Ren Qi. Objective: Immediate Deletion.]

He smiled, a dry, humorless expression.

Let them come, he whispered to the empty, polished room. I have a lot of work to do, and I don't intend to be interrupted.

He looked at the spectral butler standing by the door, its head bowed in eternal, hollow silence.

Prepare the entrance, he ordered. We have guests arriving, and I expect the property to be in perfect order before they walk through the door.

The ghost bowed, turning to drift into the darkness of the hall. Ren Qi returned to his ledger, his mind a steel trap of calculations and blueprints. He had the house, he had the staff, and he had the vision. The Babel Hub didn't know it yet, but it had just given him everything he needed to tear it down.

As the first, faint sound of footsteps echoed from the courtyard—the heavy, rhythmic thud of armored boots against stone—Ren Qi didn't look up. He simply adjusted his position, his hands moving with the steady, practiced grace of a man working on a masterpiece. The audit was entering its most volatile phase, and the Architect was ready to sign off on the destruction of the world.

The footfalls grew louder, reaching the front door. A heavy, metal-plated fist struck the gate, the impact vibrating through the walls of the manor. Ren Qi ignored it. He was busy writing the final lines of the structural report, his eyes fixed on the flickering screen of his terminal.

The door began to buckle under the force of the intruders, the wood groaning as it was forced inward by the sheer weight of the enforcement unit.

Ren Qi stood up, his shadow stretching across the floor. He picked up his blade, the iridescent edge pulsing with a faint, rhythmic heartbeat. He walked toward the foyer, his footsteps silent, his pace measured.

The light from the corridor flooded the room, illuminating the figures standing in the entryway. They were tall, covered in the heavy, liquid-chrome armor of the Prime Arbiter’s elite, their faces hidden behind glowing, featureless masks.

He didn't say a word. He didn't reach for his ledger. He just stood there, his hands loose at his sides, his gaze fixed on the leader of the unit.

The leader took a step forward, the floor cracking under the weight of its armor.

You are the breach, the Arbiter said, its voice a synthesized, multi-layered roar that filled the room. You are the structural failure. We have been sent to prune the decay.

Ren Qi looked at the specters surrounding the room, their translucent forms flickering in the sudden, bright light.

I am the owner, Ren Qi replied, his voice calm, dry, and entirely unimpressed. And this is private property.

The clash of metal on metal rang out, the sound of the fight echoing through the halls of the manor. The audit had reached the fourth floor, and the bill was finally being served. The architect had built his home, and now, he was going to defend it with the cold, precise violence of a man who held the keys to the entire tower.

He drove his blade into the leader’s chest, the iridescent edge tearing through the chrome armor like it was nothing but paper. The leader gasped, its form turning translucent as it began to dissolve into pure, white code.

Ren Qi didn't stop. He turned to the next, his movements fluid, his mind already calculating the outcome of the battle, the structural impact of the engagement, and the next, more important step in his audit. He was the landlord, he was the architect, and he was the only thing standing between the tower and its inevitable collapse.

As the last of the Arbiter’s unit fell, their armor clattering to the floor in heaps of empty, lifeless scrap, Ren Qi stood in the center of the room, his blade glowing with a soft, steady rhythm.

The manor was quiet again.

He walked back to his chair and sat down, his breath even, his eyes flat and devoid of any emotional charge.

The work continued. The audit of the 3000th Descent was far from over. And he had a foundation to maintain.

He opened his terminal, the screen flashing with a cascade of green, cascading numbers. He began to write, his stylus clicking against the glass.

Audit Phase 4: Engagement successful. Enforcement unit neutralized. Proceeding with structural reorganization.

He closed the terminal, the darkness of the manor wrapping around him like a shroud. He was ready for the next layer. He was ready for the next set of accounts to be settled.

He looked at the empty, silent room, the specters of his service standing in the corners, their eyes glowing with a faint, subservient light.

He had the house. He had the power. He had the ledger.

The audit was just beginning, and he was the only one prepared to settle the bill. He leaned back, his eyes closed, his mind already drifting upward, toward the next floor, toward the next, more complex, more corrupt, and more beautiful challenge of his life.

The 3000th Descent was finally, truly beginning. And he was the one who was going to write the final entry in the book.

He opened his eyes, his gaze fixed on the darkness above, his mind clear, his heart steady.

And he was the only one collecting.
