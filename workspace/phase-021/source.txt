# Chapter 21: The raid of Fools

The heavy reinforced glass of the lobby doors did not shatter. It buckled under the hydraulic pressure of a portable tactical ram.

Sovereign Cao’s remaining squad—thirty-eight elite players—pushed through the breach. They were breathing heavily, their faces pale beneath their steel visors, still carrying the chemical stink of the sewer gas from the tunnels below.

Keep the formation tight, Cao roared, his hammer held low. This lobby is a choke point. Watch the ceiling.

The ceiling did not drop. The marble floor did not split. Instead, the lobby was dead silent. The desk where Lin Xia usually sat was empty, save for a half-empty bottle of cheap disinfectant and a neat stack of blank lease agreements.

The raiders moved inward, their heavy boots squeaking on the polished tiles. They reached the elevators, but the doors were welded shut with thick, slagged steel.

The stairs, the lieutenant whispered, pointing his spear toward the dark fire door at the end of the hall. We go up. Floor by floor. Clear the corners.

They entered the stairwell. The moment the heavy fire door clicked shut behind the last man, the sound of the wind outside died instantly. A heavy, absolute silence fell over the stairwell, broken only by the dry, rhythmic rasp of thirty-eight people breathing through filtered masks.

In the B1 security hub, Ren Qi watched the monochrome screens.

Three monitors, salvaged from the ruins of the property office, flickered with static-heavy feeds. On the left screen, Cao’s vanguard was ascending the stairs. On the middle screen, the spatial layout of the fourth floor was slowly rotating, its concrete corridors twisting like the chambers of a massive, grey nautilus shell.

He had the Wyvern scale resting on the metal table beside him. The steel-grey surface of the scale was cold, radiating a faint, concussive resonance that made the plastic casing of the monitors hum.

Too heavy for standard travel, Ren Qi muttered, his waxy fingers tracing the jagged edge of the scale. A liability if worn raw. It needs to be integrated.

He reached into his leather pouch, pulling out twelve calcified femur bones from the Sewer Stalkers he had killed in the subway tunnels, along with three yards of Stalker-Widow silk.

He laid his flat palm over the materials. His mana pool drained instantly, a cold, dry vacuum pulling from his chest.

The pile of bone, silk, and steel dissolved into a grey, oily ash that rose from the table. It did not fall to the floor; it clung to Ren Qi’s three-hundred-pound frame, hardening into a series of overlapping, matte-black plates that resembled the ribcage of an ancient reptile. It was silent. It had no weight to his own muscles, but when he tapped the chest plate with his knuckle, the sound was flat and dense, like striking a block of solid granite.

He looked back at the screen.

Floor five, he observed.

He reached for the emergency fire-control panel, which had been re-wired into his system interface. He flipped three toggle switches.

Upstairs, the stairwell changed.

The squad of ten players leading the ascent stepped onto the landing of the fifth floor. The door before them was labeled 5F. The scout pushed it open, his spear extended.

He did not step into a hallway. He stepped onto a concrete platform that hung over a vertical, three-story drop. The air below was filled with the grey, swirling mist of the sewer gas.

Back, the scout yelled, his heel slipping on the wet edge.

But when he turned, the door he had just passed through was gone. In its place was a solid, seamless slab of poured concrete. The landing had shrunk. The walls were moving inward with a slow, hydraulic grind, the plaster cracking as the space contracted.

Cao, the lieutenant screamed, his voice muffled by the concrete. The stairs are moving. We’re cut off.

Sovereign Cao, still on the third-floor landing, slammed his warhammer against the concrete wall. The heavy iron head struck with a blue flash of kinetic energy, leaving a three-inch crater in the plaster, but the wall did not crumble. It simply groaned, the rebar inside shifting and re-forming the structural density in real-time.

Don't panic, Cao roared. It's a spatial skill. It has a mana cost. He can't hold it forever. Break through the doors.

They could not break through.

The Labyrinth of the Iron Maw did not work on linear geometry. When three players tried to kick down a wooden door to Unit 506, the floor beneath their boots tilted forty-five degrees. They slid backward into a horizontal ventilation shaft.

Ren Qi moved through the service maintenance tunnels, his shadow, Umbra, flowing before him like a fast-moving river of black grease.

He did not use the stairs. He used the lateral transit chambers—the elevators that now slid sideways through the building's core.

He emerged in a small, dead-end corridor on the sixth floor. Three Vanguard scouts were trapped there, their backs against a concrete wall that was slowly sweating cold water.

The scouts turned, their crossbows raised.

Target spotted, one yelled.

Before his finger could pull the trigger, the shadow on the floor rose.

Umbra did not attack their flesh. The primordial void slid up their legs, its cold, non-Euclidean mass wrapping around their leather armor and utility belts. The toxic residue of the shadow hissed, dissolving the metal buckles and leather straps in a fraction of a second.

My gear, the scout gasped as his reinforced leather vest fell away, his inventory pouch sliding to the wet floor.

Ren Qi stepped from the dark. He did not use his blade. He simply reached out with his waxy, grey hand, his high physical density giving him the strength of an industrial press. He grabbed the lead scout by the throat, lifting his hundred-and-sixty-pound body off the floor with a single, effortless motion.

The rules of the building, Ren Qi said, his flat, dry voice carrying no heat. No unauthorized weapons on the premises.

He ripped the crossbow from the scout's hand. With his other hand, he reached into the scout's interface range, forcefully extracting his spatial ring using the landlord-override authority granted by the Rank B property status.

And all arrears must be settled in physical assets.

He tossed the naked scout into the vertical ventilation shaft behind him. The man screamed as he slid down the smooth sheet metal, his bare skin squealing against the cold zinc, terminating sixty feet below in the B2 cistern where Lin Xia was waiting with a pair of rusty handcuffs.

The other two scouts did not fight. They looked at the massive, black-plated figure of Ren Qi, his low Charisma making him look like an ancient, stone-carved golem of death. They dropped their spears. They unbuckled their own chest plates, their fingers shaking so hard they could barely release the brass snaps.

Everything, Ren Qi said, his waxy face blank. Boots too.

Two minutes later, two more naked players were sliding down the zinc chutes, their high-tier iron-plated boots, leather greaves, and coin pouches stacked neatly in Ren Qi’s inventory.

Ren Qi did not stop. He moved through the shifting corridors of the fourth, fifth, and sixth floors like a cold-storage worker inventorying frozen meat.

The Dawn Vanguard’s elite squad was liquidated systematically. They were not killed; they were processed. In the dark, non-Euclidean maze, their coordination was useless. Every time they tried to group, a concrete wall would slide into place, dividing them into isolated pairs.

By the fifth hour, the building had digested thirty-seven of the forty-eight raiders.

The courtyard outside Block B was littered with naked, shivering players who had been ejected from the ground-floor gray-water outlets. They lay in the greasy yellow drizzle, their skin red from the cold, their elite status reduced to nothing more than a shivering heap of bruised flesh. They did not try to go back in. They sat in the mud, staring at the concrete monolith of Skyview Garden with eyes hollowed by a deep, psychological ruin.

Only Sovereign Cao remained.

His Level 15 stats were high enough to resist the gravity shifts. His heavy Vanguard Knight armor, plated in gold and reinforced with kinetic runes, kept him anchored even when the corridors tilted. He had smashed through three concrete walls with his warhammer, his breathing a ragged, whistling gasp inside his helmet.

He was covered in grey dust. The gold plating on his breastplate was scratched and dented, and the blue light of his kinetic barrier was flickering like a dying candle.

He reached the twenty-fourth floor—the top floor.

The corridor here was wide and straight. There were no sliding walls here. No shifting floors. The air smelled of stale rainwater and old carpet, lit only by the grey, greasy light of the morning sun cutting through the dirty windows.

At the end of the hall, near the double doors of the penthouse suite, Ren Qi was waiting.

He was sitting in a high-backed office chair, his boots resting on a low wooden crate. The black plates of his Dragonbone Armor did not reflect the morning light; they seemed to drink it, leaving his massive frame silhouetted against the grey glass behind him.

Sovereign Cao stopped thirty feet away. He dragged his heavy warhammer, its iron head scraping against the concrete floor with a dry, metallic screech.

You, Cao gasped, his teeth red with blood from a ruptured lip. He spat a dark glob onto the carpet. You ruined my guild. Fifty elites... stripped. Like beasts.

They were not stripped like beasts, Ren Qi said, his voice flat, deadpan, and dry. They were liquidated to cover the cost of the property damage your ram caused to my front entrance. The calculation was precise. Their gear has a market value of seven hundred and forty cores. The repair cost is six hundred and twenty. The remainder is my management fee.

Sovereign Cao’s face twisted into a mask of pure, homicidal rage. The gold runes on his hammer flared with a sudden, violent blue light.

I am the leader of the Dawn Vanguard, Cao roared, his boots slamming into the floor as he charged. I cleared the Central Plaza. I killed the Iron-Spined Boar. You are nothing but a debt-collector hiding in a ruin.

The warhammer rose over his head, the kinetic energy condensing into a massive, three-foot blue crescent that distorted the air. The skill was Vanguard Smash—a high-impact strike capable of splitting steel.

Ren Qi did not dodge. He did not even stand up from his chair.

The hammer came down.

The blue crescent struck Ren Qi’s chest. The impact was loud—a sharp, metallic crack that shattered the glass windows behind him, sending a shower of silver shards into the yellow fog outside. The office chair disintegrated into a cloud of black plastic and steel spring.

But Ren Qi’s body did not move.

The matte-black plates of the Dragonbone Armor glowed with a faint, oily green light. The kinetic energy of the smash did not penetrate his chest; it was absorbed, traveling down through his three-hundred-pound skeleton and into the concrete floor beneath his boots. The floor cracked in a perfect, three-foot circle around his feet, but his waxy grey skin did not carry a single mark.

Cao’s eyes went wide behind his visor. What...

Ren Qi reached out, his hand moving with a slow, heavy inevitability. His waxy fingers wrapped around the iron haft of the warhammer, just below the head.

Cao tried to pull the weapon back, his Level 15 strength straining until the muscles in his arms bunched and quivered. But Ren Qi’s hand was an iron vice. He did not budge.

Your stats are high, Ren Qi said, his face inches from Cao’s helmet. But your energy is poorly distributed. You rely too much on kinetic translation. When the target's density exceeds your impact threshold, the energy reflects back into your own joints.

Ren Qi twisted his wrist.

There was a sharp, dry snap. The steel-reinforced haft of the warhammer broke like a dry twig. Cao stumbled backward, his hands empty, staring in disbelief at the shattered wood in his grip.

Ren Qi dropped his half of the hammer. He stepped forward, his heavy boots making no sound on the carpet.

You have a great deal of pride, Sovereign Cao, Ren Qi murmured. It is your primary motivator. It is what allowed you to lead fifty men into a closed-circuit gas chamber.

He grabbed Cao by the collar of his gold-plated breastplate, lifting him off his feet and slamming him against the concrete wall. The impact left a shallow indentation in the plaster.

System, Ren Qi said, his cold eyes fixed on Cao's pale, sweating face.

Sovereign Cao let out a strange, choked gasp.

His eyes, which had been burning with a fierce, defiant hatred, suddenly went dull. The tight, aggressive line of his jaw slackened. The gold-plated armor on his chest seemed to lose its luster, turning into a dull, yellowish metal that looked like cheap brass.

It was not a physical injury. It was a conceptual extraction. The very core of his identity—the absolute belief in his own authority, his status as the leader of District 9's greatest guild—was being peeled away like old wallpaper.

He did not scream. He simply went limp, his arms hanging loose at his sides, his head bowing until his chin touched his collarbone.

In Ren Qi's palm, a small, golden orb of light condensed, cold and heavy as a brass coin.

Ren Qi let go of the collar.

Sovereign Cao fell to his knees. He did not try to stand. He did not look at Ren Qi. He sat on the dirty carpet, his fingers tracing the scratches on his gold-plated knees, his face blank and empty, like a child who had forgotten his own name.

Go, Ren Qi said, his voice flat. Your guild is liquidated. Your lease is void. Tell the others in District 9 that Skyview Garden is currently under new management.

Cao did not answer. He stood up slowly, his movements heavy and uncoordinated, and walked toward the stairs. He did not run. He walked with the slow, drifting step of a ghost.

Ren Qi walked to the shattered window.

The yellow drizzle was still falling over District 9. Down in the courtyard, the naked raiders were slowly gathering their leader, wrapping him in a dirty plastic tarp they had salvaged from the trash bins. They looked up at the twenty-fourth floor, their faces pale, their eyes filled with a quiet, absolute terror that would never leave them.

Beside his boot, the shadow of Umbra shivered.

The black pool rose, its jagged edges smooth and thick as it merged with his outline.

Lin Xia, he said into the intercom speaker on the wall.

Yes... yes, Landlord? her voice came back, shaking but alive.

Prepare the lobby, Ren Qi said, his waxy skin dry in the grey light of the morning. The Dawn Vanguard's raid is over. The local forums will be active by noon.

He looked at his inventory, where forty-two high-tier armor sets and three hundred mana cores were stacked neatly like dry firewood.

And adjust the price of the antidote, he added, his waxy face showing the faint, calculated line of a smile. Thirty cores a bottle. No discounts.
