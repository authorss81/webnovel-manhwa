# Chapter 25: The Avatar of Rot

The red haze did not drift; it clung to the concrete like liquid copper, dripping from the undersides of collapsed skybridges in slow, heavy drops. Four kilometers into the Futian District, the noise of the city had completely died out. There were no crickets, no wind through shattered office windows, not even the wet rustle of swily fog. Only the sharp, electric hum of spatial rifts grinding against the earth filled the silence.

Wang Teng walked down the middle of what used to be Fuhua Road. The four-lane street had been melted into a smooth, reflective trench of black glass. Beneath the translucent surface, crushed cars lay flattened like preserved insects in amber, their metal bodies twisted into impossibly thin sheets.

His heavy boots made a dry, rhythmic tapping against the glass.

[SYSTEM: Severe Environmental Hazard]

[Ambient Mana Density: 890 Units]

[Biological Contamination: 14% / Minute]

[Warning: Physical structure degradation imminent. Thermal absorption exceeding Cuirass of the Iron Eater threshold.]

Wang Teng glanced down at his left forearm. The pale, waxy skin had taken on a faint, crimson translucency under the red sky. Small, hair-like capillaries of solidified mana were forming beneath his epidermis, rigid as glass thread. It did not hurt. The Titan’s Resilience had turned his inner structure into high-density rebar, but the environment was steadily attempting to convert his organic mass into static crystalline scrap, just like the pilot in the military jet.

He did not slow his pace. Ahead, the lower half of the Futian Financial Center loomed like a severed tree trunk. The top twenty floors hung suspended fifty meters in the air, completely disconnected from the base, slowly spinning in a silent, anti-gravitational drift. Between the two severed halves, a violet-red tear in reality pulsed with the rhythm of an open artery.

Something was moving in the shadow of the floating tower's base.

It did not walk on legs. It rolled and dragged itself across the glass pavement, a massive, weeping mound of fused structural steel, melted fiber-optic cables, and rotten, petrified flesh. It looked like the aftermath of an industrial catastrophe that had gained a nervous system. Ancient server racks from a bank's data center were embedded in its flanks, their indicator lights still blinking frantically with dead error codes. Deep within its translucent, jelly-like belly, human skulls from previous cycles floated alongside corrupted brass gears and glowing, half-digested mana cores.

As Wang Teng approached, the thing shuddered. A speaker horn torn from a public address system hung from what might have been its neck, and from it burst a jagged, overlapping mess of audio files:

"--clear the lobby immediately--bzzt--Season 1,420 termination protocol active--static--please claim your daily login reward--screaming--"

It was an Avatar of Rot. Not a creature natural to Earth, nor a standard monster deployed by the system, but a trash heap of residual data—a physical cancer formed from the unpurged scraps of a thousand liquidated worlds.

Wang Teng stopped ten meters away. He unclasped the Molecular Blade from his belt. The gray, dull metal gleamed faintly under the crimson glare of the rift above.

"Deficit identified," Wang Teng said softly.

The monster lunged. Despite its vast, uneven mass, it moved with unnatural speed, snapping across the black glass like an elastic band being released. A thick appendage made of coiled fiber-optics and rusted rebar whipped toward Wang Teng's collar, trailing a spray of acidic, black sludge that hissed violently as it touched the air.

Wang Teng did not dodge. He pivoted on his right heel, his heavy frame shifting with calculated efficiency. He swung the Molecular Blade in a clean, horizontal arc.

The blade sliced through the appendage without resistance. The severed mass of rebar and flesh hit the ground with a heavy splash.

Then, the space around the severed limb flickered.

A sharp, digital chime echoed through the air—a sound identical to an old computer error. The severed appendage on the ground vanished into a smear of purple pixels, and instantaneously, the limb reattached itself to the monster's main body. The cut was gone. The rebar was seamless.

[SYSTEM: Target entity possesses localized 'Spatial State Rollback']

[Entity Status: Glitched Asset (Unregistered)]

[Damage Nullified: 100%]

Wang Teng's eyes narrowed slightly. He stepped back as a second tendril smashed into the glass beneath his feet, spiderwebbing the surface for ten yards.

The creature did not just heal; it was executing a systemic save-state. Every time physical force altered its form, the local rifts in the Futian Zone forced its spatial coordinates to roll back three seconds in time. As long as it remained inside the high-density mana field of the rift, it was mathematically immortal under the standard rules of combat.

The creature issued another distorted burst of sound from its speaker horn.

"--target locked--bzzt--viewer engagement score dropping--static--execute liquidation--"

It surged forward, its massive body expanding as dozens of fiber-optic tendrils unfolded like the ribs of an umbrella, threatening to pin Wang Teng against the glassy walls of the ruined plaza. Black sludge rained down around him, eating holes into the concrete rubble and filling the air with the sickening stink of scorched copper and rotting meat.

Wang Teng parried three rapid strikes in succession, the Cuirass of the Iron Eater absorbing the concussive kinetic energy with dull, metallic thuds. Each hit drove his boots half an inch into the glass floor, but he did not give a single inch of ground. His mind was working through the variables, completely unbothered by the sheer physical horror in front of him.

The monster was relying on the ambient environment. The rift above was supplying an unlimited stream of hyper-thermal mana—a raw, volatile heat source that powered the spatial rollback loop. The air itself was hovering near seventy degrees Celsius, thick with energy that smelled like ozone and burning grease.

Trying to cut the monster was like trying to trim a shadow with scissors. The scissors were functional, but the medium was wrong.

To fix a bad ledger, you did not erase the line items one by one. You closed the account.

Wang Teng stepped back out of the monster's reach, planting his feet firmly on the glass. He let the Molecular Blade drop into the holster at his hip. He did not need a weapon. He needed a structural intervention.

He held out both hands, palms facing down toward the reflective glass floor. He closed his eyes for a fraction of a second, tuning out the distorted screams from the creature's speaker horn, focusing entirely on the raw, chaotic thermal frequency of the Red Zone.

The atmosphere was saturated with heat and mana. Over eight hundred units per cubic meter. An absurd surplus.

"System," Wang Teng said, his voice dropping into that cold, deadpan register that carried no heat of its own.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

The cold void in his chest opened wide, expanding beyond his physical skin to encompass the entire two-hundred-meter radius of the ruined plaza.

He was not targeting the creature. He was targeting the thermal physics of the sector.

[Input: Ambient High-Density Thermal Mana (Red Zone Radius) + 500 Personal Mana]

The System interface in his mind flickered wildly, glowing with warning symbols as it attempted to calculate an exchange that violated the basic thermodynamic laws programmed into the local zone.

[Warning: Environmental Inversion exceeds standard system parameters!]

"Bypass," Wang Teng commanded inwardly. "Convert the heat into a debt. Collect it all at once."

[Concept: Environmental Inversion -> Absolute Thermodynamic Arrest]

[Exchange successful. Bypassing equivalent limitations.]

In an instant, the world went white.

It was not a flare of light. It was the instantaneous, violent withdrawal of every single thermal calorie within two hundred meters.

The crimson haze in the air did not dissipate; it froze in mid-air, turning into billions of tiny, dark red ice crystals that fell to the ground like crimson sand with a soft, whispering rustle. The seventy-degree heat vanished, replaced by a temperature so profoundly below zero that the air itself liquefied, pooling in clear, bubbling ribbons across the black glass.

The Avatar of Rot froze mid-lunge.

Its fiber-optic tendrils, slick with black sludge, hardened instantly into brittle black icicles. The embedded server racks cracked with loud, pistol-like snaps as their copper wiring shrank faster than their casing. The glowing human skulls inside its jelly belly turned frosted and opaque.

Most importantly, the spatial rollback loop stopped.

The rift above, deprived of the thermal mana required to maintain its spatial friction, stalled. The violet-red tear in reality hardened into a dark, scab-like crust of frozen spatial static.

The monster's glitched recovery required energy to execute its save-state. At absolute zero, molecular movement ceased entirely. There was no energy to trigger the code. The system's rollback instruction hung in the frozen air, unable to find a single moving electron to execute the command.

Wang Teng opened his eyes.

His breath did not form a cloud; the moisture in his lungs froze before it could leave his lips, dropping from his chin as fine white dust. His waxy, gray skin was covered in a thin layer of blue frost, but his inner frame—that high-density rebar skeleton—remained solid and unyielding.

He walked up to the frozen mountain of glitched flesh.

His heavy boots crunched through the liquid air and red ice sand. He raised his right hand, curled it into a simple, unadorned fist, and struck the center of the monster's chest.

He did not use a skill. He just applied three hundred pounds of high-density mass against a target whose structural integrity had been reduced to zero by extreme thermal shock.

A sharp crack echoed through the silence.

A single spiderweb crack appeared where his fist made contact. Then, with the suddenness of a shattering glass chandelier, the entire thirty-ton mass of the Avatar of Rot collapsed. It did not break into chunks; it disintegrated into a fine, black-and-crimson dust that settled over the frozen floor like spilled charcoal.

The speaker horn shattered into thousands of gray metallic flakes, its distorted audio cut off forever.

In the middle of the black dust pile, a single object remained intact.

It was an irregular, heart-shaped crystal, roughly the size of a human head. It did not reflect the crimson sky above; instead, it seemed to swallow the light around it, pulsing with a deep, oily violet sheen that shifted like liquid mercury. Inside the crystal, faint, microscopic lines of code drifted like suspended dust, flickering with unpurged system data.

Wang Teng reached down and picked it up.

The crystal was shockingly heavy—easily fifty pounds—and felt as cold as the frozen pavement beneath his boots.

[SYSTEM: You have acquired 'Heart of the Glitch' (Rank SS - Unstable Core)]

[Attributes: Spatial Anomaly Anchor, Systemic Code Source, Raw Reality Essence]

[Description: A crystallized fragment of corrupted code left behind by a liquidated civilization from the 2104th Descent. It exists outside the standard operational oversight of the System Producers.]

Wang Teng held the crystal in his waxy hands, tracing the jagged edges of the violet surface.

This was not just a monster drop. This was unedited, unmonitored raw material. Code that the producers had failed to erase during the last planetary liquidation.

If standard system weapons were useless against the gods because they were built using the system's licensed assets, then this crystal was an unlicensed firearm. It was a piece of pure, unrefined poison to the system's architecture.

He stowed the Heart of the Glitch into his inventory.

Around him, the zone was slowly beginning to recover. The extreme absolute zero condition he had created was temporary; the surrounding ambient heat of the city was already bleeding back into the two-hundred-meter radius, turning the frozen crimson sand into wet, reddish puddle water.

The rift above groaned, the scab of spatial static cracking open as a fresh wave of yellow-red fog began to spill out.

Wang Teng turned his back on the floating skyscraper. He did not look at the ash pile of the dead monster. His mind was already moving to the next calculation.

He pulled his low-frequency radio receiver from his collar.

"Lin Xia," he said, his voice flat and steady through the static.

There was a pause, followed by the thin, trembling sound of her voice through the speaker. "Landlord? We... we felt a seismic shift on the western wall display. The temperature sensors in your sector dropped off the scale entirely. Are you... is the perimeter holding?"

"The perimeter is clear," Wang Teng said, walking back down the black glass street toward the edge of the Red Zone. "Prepare the B2 workshop. Clear out the storage bins on the east side."

"The workshop? What are we building?"

"An anchor," Wang Teng replied, his eyes fixed on the horizon where the sky remained the color of a bruised plum. "The current sanctuary is too small. We are going to build a forge that the system cannot audit."

He cut the transmission before she could reply.

As he crossed the boundary out of the Red Zone, stepping back into the swily, yellow fog of the city, he looked up at the sky. He could feel it now—not a physical presence, but a faint, structural weight lurking in the upper layers of the atmosphere. The unseen eyes of the Producers, sitting in their void viewing lounges, checking their engagement metrics and waiting for the next dramatic death.

Wang Teng adjusted his Cuirass of the Iron Eater and kept walking.

The stage was set, the audience was watching, but the Landlord had just acquired the deed to the theatre.
