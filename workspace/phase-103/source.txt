# Chapter 43: Training the Useless

The basement of the manor was a cavern of forgotten industrial decay. Rusted pipes lined the walls like exposed arteries, and the air held the metallic tang of damp concrete and ozone. Ren Qi stood in the center, his boots crunching on flakes of oxidization. Around him, the ten individuals he had recruited stood in varying states of exhaustion and nervous anticipation. They were F-Rank liabilities, the dregs of the Babel Hub, yet in this space, they were the gears of an engine only he could operate.

Mina stood near the front, her hands trembling slightly, though she kept them clasped firmly together. The others shifted their weight, their eyes darting between the dark, imposing presence of Ren Qi and the silent, spectral entities drifting through the gloom. The ghosts were efficient, their hollow forms passing through solid objects to organize scrap metal into perfectly aligned piles.

You were brought here to function, Ren Qi said, his voice flat and devoid of warmth. This is not a collective. It is a refinement process.

He gestured to a series of workstations he had mapped out across the floor. Each station was equipped with a rudimentary mana-collector—a device he had crafted using salvaged wires and his own specialized knowledge of systemic energy flow.

The process is simple, he continued. You provide the raw input. I provide the structural authority. You will not deviate from your assigned station. You will not ask what the final product is intended for. Your reward is the daily allowance of sustenance.

He turned to a thin, wiry man named Kael, whose talent allowed him to manipulate the density of stagnant fluids. Kael, your station is the hydration tank. You will cycle the water through the filtration grid. Do not let the flow rate drop below four liters per minute.

Kael nodded, his face pale as he moved toward the heavy, iron vat. Ren Qi watched him for a heartbeat, ensuring the man understood the rigidity of the protocol. He then turned his attention to Mina.

You, he said, gesturing to the thermal exchange terminal. Your task remains consistent. The heating process is the catalyst for the final molecular bond. If the temperature fluctuates by even a single degree, the entire batch will be rendered inert.

Mina stepped forward, her expression shifting from apprehension to a strained, focused intensity. She moved to the terminal, her small frame dwarfed by the heavy, industrial equipment. As she placed her hands on the iron plates, the air around her began to shimmer with a faint, pale heat.

Ren Qi retreated to the master console at the far end of the room. He watched the production line with the dispassionate gaze of an engineer inspecting a bridge for stress fractures. The recruits were working with a rhythmic, mechanical cadence now, their fear replaced by the hypnotic repetition of their tasks. Kael cycled the water, the fluid flowing through the pipes into the refinement chamber. Mina applied the precise, steady heat that prepared the solution for the final, violent reconfiguration of its properties.

He reached into his storage, pulling out a handful of compressed mana-shards. The raw energy within them was unstable, a volatile compound that, in the hands of a standard crafter, would have triggered an immediate, explosive failure. For Ren Qi, it was merely an variable waiting for an equation.

[System Notification: Refinement sequence initiated. Current input quality: Sub-optimal. Potential output stability: 88%.]

Ren Qi adjusted the settings on his ledger, his stylus clicking against the glass interface. The room grew heavy with the hum of energy. The water, now infused with the thermal signatures and the erratic, raw mana, flowed into the final containment vessel where Ren Qi waited. He watched the fluid, his mind calculating the molecular density, the surface tension, and the exact moment of structural collapse.

He placed his hand on the vessel. His talent, Inequivalent Exchange, stirred within him, a dormant, predatory force.

Input: [Thermal-Infused Water + Unstable Mana + Kinetic Impurity]. Output: [High-Density Healing Serum].

The fluid in the vessel glowed with a blinding, iridescent light. It bubbled, the liquid restructuring itself into a thick, shimmering concentrate that radiated an aura of potent, medicinal vitality. He pulled his hand away. The reaction was complete. The serum was no longer a collection of waste materials; it was a high-grade commodity that would command a small fortune in the lower districts.

He bottled the serum, the vials clicking as he placed them in a crate. He looked up, his gaze sweeping over the recruits. They had stopped working, their eyes wide as they stared at the glowing crate.

Mina approached him, her hands still faintly steaming from the heat she had generated. She looked at the vials, then back at Ren Qi, her expression a mix of bewilderment and a new, burgeoning reverence.

How did you... she whispered. It was just water. It was just heat.

I do not deal in miracles, Ren Qi replied, his voice dry. I deal in efficiency. The water was wasted. The heat was undirected. I simply corrected the structural orientation of the resources.

He handed a single vial to her. It was a portion of their reward, a concentrated dose of the very thing they had helped create. She took it, the glass cool and smooth beneath her fingers. As she looked into the liquid, she saw the faint, swirling patterns of energy trapped within.

The other recruits began to murmur, their exhaustion forgotten as they looked at the product of their labor. They had been failures, outcasts living on the fringes of the Hub, but here, they were part of a process that yielded results which defied the conventional laws of the tower.

You are the components, Ren Qi said, his gaze hard. As long as you function, you are valuable. As long as you are valuable, you are protected. Do not mistake this for a partnership. This is an exchange.

He turned back to his terminal, his mind already shifting to the next project. The Healing Serums were a start, but he needed more. He needed weapons. He needed equipment that could withstand the pressures of the higher tiers.

A vibration rippled through the floor, a low, tectonic groan that emanated from the very foundation of the manor. The spectral butler materialized at his side, its head bowed, its eyes glowing with a warning.

Ren Qi looked at the terminal. A red notification pulsed in the corner of his vision, a signal that the external perimeter had been breached by something large, something that did not belong to the mundane scavengers of the lower levels.

[System Notification: Perimeter breach detected at North Gate. Intruder: High-Tier Reality-Audit Construct. Threat Level: Severe. Estimated time of arrival: 120 seconds.]

Ren Qi stood up. He did not look at the recruits, but the silence that fell over the room told him they had heard the alarm. He reached for his blade, the iridescent edge pulsing with a steady, rhythmic heartbeat.

Kael, he ordered without turning, lock the vault. Mina, you will remain at the terminal. If the containment field fails, you are to vent the pressure. It will be loud, and it will be dangerous, but it will keep the manor from collapsing.

He walked toward the door, his movements measured and precise. The manor felt colder now, the shadows in the corners deepening as the reality-audit construct approached. He stepped into the hallway, his boots clicking sharply against the marble floor.

He reached the front entrance just as the heavy, iron doors groaned, the metal warping as if under the weight of an invisible hand. A figure coalesced on the other side—a tall, spindly entity made of flickering, monochromatic light, its form constantly shifting between a humanoid shape and a mass of jagged, mathematical symbols.

It was an Arbiter’s Inquisitor, a creature tasked with maintaining the integrity of the tower’s local reality by erasing anything that did not conform to its established rules. It didn't speak. It simply looked at Ren Qi, its presence filling the room with the sterile, frozen smell of absolute zero.

You are the disruption, the Inquisitor said, its voice a hollow, synthesized echo that seemed to come from everywhere at once. Your property is a violation of the system’s zoning constraints. Your existence is a mathematical error.

Ren Qi leaned against the doorframe, his expression entirely, maddeningly bored. Zoning, he repeated, his voice dry. That is an interesting way to describe an eviction.

He did not wait for the Inquisitor to respond. He tapped the doorframe, a small, hidden mechanism he had jury-rigged to pulse with his own energy. A surge of violet light erupted from the walls, catching the Inquisitor in a snare of gravity-defying geometry. The creature shrieked—a sound like glass grinding against slate—as its form began to fracture.

It was not a combat technique. It was a structural collapse. He had mapped the room's energy nodes, and he had turned the architecture of the house into a weapon.

The Inquisitor fought to maintain its cohesion, its light turning from a pale white to a violent, discordant red. It reached out, a blade of solidified data forming in its hand, and slashed through the air toward Ren Qi. He didn't flinch. He sidestepped, the blade missing him by inches, the energy of the swing tearing through the floorboards where he had stood a second before.

Ren Qi stepped in, his blade glowing with the same iridescent light as the serum they had just created. He drove the point into the center of the Inquisitor’s chest, the metal passing through the creature’s form as if it were nothing but air, yet causing it to convulse as if it had been struck by a physical object.

The Inquisitor looked down at the blade, its eyes widening as it realized the nature of the weapon. It was an anomaly, a breach of the rules that the Inquisitor itself was designed to uphold.

You... it stammered, its light flickering. You are not meant to...

Ren Qi twisted the blade, the sound of the creature dissolving into code filling the air. He did not let go until the light faded completely, leaving nothing behind but a faint, lingering scent of ozone.

He stood in the hall, the manor silent once more. He looked down at his blade, the iridescent glow dimming as he sheathed it. The threat was neutralized, but he knew they would keep coming. The tower was not a place of logic; it was a place of hunger, and it did not like to be challenged by the very thing it had created.

He turned back toward the basement. The recruits were waiting, their faces pale, their eyes filled with a mixture of terror and awe. He had shown them that the system, the great, terrifying machine that ruled their lives, could be defied, could be broken, and could be harvested.

He walked past them without a word, returning to his desk. He opened his ledger, his stylus clicking as he began to update the records.

Audit Phase 6: Threat neutralized. Structural integrity maintained. Resource output: Increased.

He closed the terminal, the silence of the room returning. He was the owner. He was the architect. He was the one who was going to build a new reality on the ruins of the old. He leaned back, his eyes closed, his mind already drifting upward, toward the next floor, toward the next, more complex, more corrupt, and more beautiful challenge of his life.

The 3000th Descent was finally, truly beginning. And he was the one who was going to write the final entry in the book.

He opened his eyes, his gaze fixed on the darkness above, his mind clear, his heart steady.

And he was the only one collecting.

He was not going to climb. He was going to dismantle.
