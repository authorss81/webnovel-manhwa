# Chapter 60: The Alliance's Secret Weapon

The hum of the brass communication array in the newly seized southern fort did not quiet down after the broadcast. If anything, the vibration inside the copper terminal housing grew so intense that small drops of dark oil began to weep from the casing seams, dripping onto the concrete floor where Varrick was frantically typing.

Boss, Varrick shouted over the roar of static, his fingers flying across the keys while he wiped sweat from his upper lip with the back of his sleeve. Floor 3 is losing its mind. The trade exchanges in the central spire just suspended all credit settlements. The price of Light Crystals spiked three hundred percent in forty min, and the independent guilds are storming the Alliance’s secondary supply depots on Floor 2. They think the Alliance is going bankrupt!

Their valuation was inflated by seventy percent based on unbacked military prestige, Ren Qi said smoothly. He was standing near the shattered gap in the granite wall, inspecting the structural edge where his talent had turned stone to silica glass. He tapped the sharp, clean edge of a glass fragment with his heel. When the perception of invulnerability breaks, the underlying debt must be settled immediately.

In the upper sanctum of Floor 3, however, the concept of settlement was taking a far more violent form.

Commander Thorne stood in the high-ceilinged vault of the Sun Tower, surrounded by nine senior council members of the Golden Sun Alliance. Below them, through the vast crystal viewing ports, the central plaza of Floor 3 was in total chaos. Hundreds of players were shouting at locked brass gates, demanding their gold reserves, while the streetlights flickered and died as the local crystal grids ran dry.

He didn't just defeat Brand, a senior councillor stammered, his gold-embroidered robes trembling as he held a scrying stone showing the image of Ren Qi standing in the ruined southern fort. He broadcasted it to the entire system. He made our enforcers look like broken toys. The regional guilds are refusing to pay the transit tariffs!

If we lose Floor 1’s drainage and transit nodes, Thorne muttered, his face ghastly pale in the dim amber light of the failing chandelier, we lose our authority over the lower sixty floors. We cannot deploy infantry against those gold golems or his glass-transmutation talent. Our line-breakers are frozen in their suits.

Then we clean the board, a deep, raspy voice came from the darkness behind the council table.

An ancient man, wrapped in yellowed bandages and sitting in a mechanical wheelchair powered by brass clockwork, rolled forward. In his lap rested a long, narrow wooden case bound with copper straps and sealed with five heavy padlock mechanisms made of black iron.

The Staff of Armageddon, Thorne breathed, his eyes widening. Lord Vance... that is a Class-S World Item. It was retrieved from the floor-forty boss vault five years ago. Using it requires burning five pure-grade mana cores from the central vault! It will destroy the entire southern sector of Floor 1!

There will be no sector to manage if that merchant lives until tomorrow morning, the old man replied, his withered fingers turning the small brass keys in the padlocks with dry, clicking snaps. We do not need the southern transit hub if it is controlled by a competitor. We will turn his garden, his recruits, and his market terminals into a crater of melted iron.

With a heavy, metallic clunk, the lid of the case sprang open.

Inside lay an ancient staff carved from a single piece of dark, calcified petrified wood. At its tip, a jagged, pulsing crimson stone hovered without touching the wood, bleeding tiny, microscopic sparks of black fire into the air.

Thorne did not hesitate any longer. He reached down, his hands trembling with a mixture of terror and fanaticism, and gripped the center of the shaft.

Burn the cores, Thorne ordered through the internal speaker array. Channel all remaining energy from the central spire into the staff. Wipe Skyview Garden off the map.

Three miles below, in the damp, sulfur-scented darkness of Floor 1, the grease-rain suddenly stopped.

It did not taper off. The heavy droplets hanging in the air simply froze, suspended in place as the air pressure inside the subterranean transit hub dropped violently by twenty kilopascals within three sec.

Mina stepped back from the edge of the parapet, her eyes narrowing as she looked up at the ceiling. The air smells like hot iron, she said, her voice dropping an octave. Boss... the ceiling moisture is evaporating before it hits the ground.

Ren Qi did not look at her. He pulled his leather ledger from his vest pocket, turned to a fresh page, and checked his pocket watch.

A sudden shift in atmospheric enthalpy, Ren Qi murmured. The local energy density is rising exponentially. Someone is expending an absurd amount of capital on a single offensive action.

The massive, miles-wide concrete ceiling above them—the artificial sky that separated Floor 1 from Floor 2—began to glow.

At first, it was a dull, dark red, like the bottom of an iron kettle left on a hot stove. But within five sec, the crimson hue intensified into a blinding, white-hot glare. Huge cracks, hundreds of feet long, tore across the ancient concrete vault. Steam and liquid slag began to rain down, hissing violently as they hit the stagnant pools of the lower reservoir.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: Regional Anomaly Detected.]

[WARNING: Class-S Tactical Skill 'Calamity Swarm' initiated by external entity.]

[IMPACT ZONE: Floor 1, Southern Transit Sector.]

[ESTIMATED TIME TO IMPACT: 45 SECONDS.]

Varrick ran out of the fort’s office, tumbling over a pile of shattered glass before scrambling to his feet. Boss! The system alerts are going crazy! It’s a World-class fire spell! They’ve summoned twenty strategic-grade meteors from the floor-forty sub-space fold! The shockwave alone will crush every building within ten kilometers! We have to fall back to the basement bunker!

The bunker was designed to withstand standard structural collapses, Ren Qi said calmly, closing his watch with a small click. It was not engineered to survive twenty thousand tons of superheated star-iron falling at Mach three. Entering the bunker would merely consolidate our casualties into a single burial site.

Then what do we do? Mina asked. She was already re-levering her rifle, though her eyes were fixed on the blinding white tears opening in the vault ceiling above.

We manage the incoming asset, Ren Qi said.

He reached into his leather pouch, pulled out a pair of worn, dark-leather boots lined with thin brass filigree along the soles—a pair of low-draw levitation boots he had synthesized weeks ago from discarded copper wire and a Rank-D wind core. He stepped out of his heavy leather work boots and slid his feet into the levitation gear, tightening the brass buckles with methodical speed.

Kael, Ren Qi said without looking up. Maintain perimeter security. Ensure the recruits do not disperse. Panicked movement reduces spatial efficiency.

The phantom shadow beside the glass archway bowed slightly before melting into the darkness beneath the scaffolding.

Ren Qi clicked the heels of his boots together.

A soft, bluish ring of compressed air expanded beneath his soles. With a low, whistling sound, his body lifted off the damp concrete floor, rising smoothly past the shattered parapets of the fort, past the flickering searchlight towers, and straight up toward the roaring red vortex in the ceiling vault.

The scene above was terrifying.

The ceiling had completely buckled. Through a massive, ragged hole three hundred meters wide, the sky was not darkness, but a boiling sea of liquid red energy. Breaking through the cloud of ash were twenty colossal, teardrop-shaped meteors. Each one was a jagged mass of dark iron and glowing white magma, dragging a tail of black smoke and roaring superheated plasma that set the surrounding air on fire.

The sheer kinetic force of their descent was creating an atmospheric pressure wave that tore through the iron support pillars of the transit hub, snapping two-foot-thick steel girders like dry twigs.

Ren Qi rose directly into the path of the lead meteor.

The heat was blistering. The scales of his Cuirass of the Iron Eater began to hum, absorbing the massive thermal output and turning a dull, glowing red against his flannel shirt. The smell of singed hair and scorched leather filled his lungs, but his expression remained as unreadable as an unwritten invoice.

Lead projectile mass: approximately twelve thousand tons, Ren Qi analyzed, his eyes tracking the white-hot surface of the descending iron rock as it loomed less than fifty meters above him. Kinetic energy on impact: sufficient to liquidate the entire lower foundation. Total asset replacement cost: unacceptable.

He adjusted his altitude with a slight shift of his right heel, bringing himself directly inline with the nose of the primary meteorite.

He extended his right hand.

His bare palm, calloused and dark with oil stains, reached out toward the roaring, white-hot wall of star-iron that was coming down upon him at lethal speed.

Input, Ren Qi whispered into the roaring gale of superheated air.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]

[INPUT: 1x Strategic Meteorite 'Star-Iron Calamity'. VALUE: Extreme (Kinetic/Thermal).]

[OUTPUT: High-Density Fluffed Sugar Confectionery (Cotton Candy). VALUE: Minimal.]

The impact did not make a sound.

There was no deafening explosion, no shockwave of compressed air that shattered the transit hub, no blinding flash of light.

The instant Ren Qi’s hand touched the scorching, white-hot nose of the twelve-thousand-ton iron rock, the dark metal and boiling magma ceased to exist.

The roaring flame vanished. The superheated kinetic energy dissipated into the atmosphere as a soft, warm breeze that smelled faintly of burnt vanilla and spun sugar.

In place of the mountain of star-iron, a massive, thirty-meter-wide cloud of bright pink, impossibly light cotton candy expanded into the sky. It was soft, airy, and pulled apart by the gentle updraft, floating weightlessly in the grey air like a gigantic piece of pink fleece.

Using the conceptual splash radius of the exchange, he pushed his palm forward, sweeping his arm in a broad, horizontal arc across the remaining nineteen meteors that were screaming down right behind the first.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: Chained Exchange Executing...]

[INPUT: 19x Strategic Meteorites. VALUE: Colossal.]

[OUTPUT: Confectionery Mass Expansion. VALUE: Negligible.]

One by one, with rapid, soft popping sounds like dry bubbles bursting in water, the apocalyptic swarm transformed.

The white-hot magma turned into fluffy, pink sugar. The roaring black smoke tails dissolved into clouds of powdered vanilla dust. The terrifying roar of the World Item's ultimate attack melted into a total, surreal silence, broken only by the quiet rustle of millions of tiny, pink sugar flakes drifting through the air.

For three miles around Skyview Garden, the apocalyptic crimson light died instantly.

In its place, a pink, sugary rain began to fall.

Massive clumps of soft, fibrous pink cotton candy—some as large as wagon wheels, others as small as snowflakes—floated gently down from the ceiling vault. They drifted past the iron pillars, landed softly on the dark concrete steps, and coated the stagnant, grease-slicked mud of the reservoir in a thick, fragrant layer of bright pink fluff.

Ren Qi cut the power to his levitation boots and dropped smoothly through the sugary cloud, landing on his feet atop the fort’s outer parapet with a dry, soft thud.

He reached out, plucked a small, floating tuft of pink sugar from the air in front of his face, and pressed it between his thumb and forefinger. It compressed instantly into a sticky, dense pellet.

Purity is acceptable, Ren Qi noted, his voice flat as he dropped the sugar pellet into his vest pocket. Moisture content is slightly high due to ambient humidity, but it can be processed into refined glucose reserves for the winter rations.

Below him in the courtyard, the silence was absolute.

Varrick was standing in knee-deep pink cotton candy, his mouth open so wide his lower jaw looked unhinged. A huge clump of pink fluff was resting comfortably on top of his head like a ridiculous hat. He reached up, took a small piece, put it in his mouth, and chewed twice in complete, paralyzed shock.

It’s... it’s strawberry, Varrick whispered, his voice high-pitched and hysterical. Boss... they summoned Armageddon, and you turned it into a fairground snack.

The recruits didn't break formation, but three of them slowly looked down at the pink fluff coating their iron boots, their grey faces displaying the very first hints of profound, existential confusion.

Mina walked over to the edge of the balcony, brushed a layer of pink sugar off the barrel of her rifle, and looked up at the ceiling. The massive hole in the vault was still there, but instead of magma, soft pink clouds were slowly drifting through the gap into Floor 2.

The market value of refined sugar on Floor 3 is currently forty Babel coins per pound, Mina observed, her lips pulling into a dry, cynical line. You just dropped twenty thousand tons of sweet capital onto our doorstep, Landlord.

A thirty percent profit margin after factoring in the wear on my levitation boots, Ren Qi said.

Meanwhile, on Floor 3, inside the high sanctum of the Sun Tower, the scrying stone on the central table went completely dead.

Thorne was still holding the Staff of Armageddon. The crimson stone at its tip had shattered into fine, black powder, its dark energy completely spent. His hands were shaking so violently that the ancient wooden staff fell from his grip, clattering loudly against the cold marble floor.

What... what happened? a councillor gasped, his hands clutching his chest as he leaned over the table. Did the strike hit? Is the sector destroyed?

Thorne didn't answer. He looked at the secondary monitoring mirror on the wall, which was showing a live, distant feed of the southern reservoir.

The forty-foot granite wall was gone, replaced by clear glass. The three-hundred-man vanguard was lying pinned in the mud. And the entire southern sector—the grey, depressing, sulfur-stained sewer of Floor 1—was currently covered in a bright, sweet layer of pink cotton candy.

Standing in the middle of it all was a single man in a dark flannel shirt and a rusted scale vest, writing calmly in a small leather ledger.

He... he didn't even use a shield, Thorne whispered, his voice completely broken, his eyes staring blankly at the screen. He touched the sky... and made it sweet.

He’s not a player, the old man in the wheelchair croaked, his hands dropping from his padlocks as he closed his eyes in defeat. He is not a mage, and he is not an enforcer. He is an auditor. And he has just closed our account.

Back in the southern fort, Ren Qi pulled his pen from his ledger and signed the bottom of the page with a clean, sharp flourish.

Varrick, Ren Qi said.

Yes, Boss? Varrick called out, quickly wiping a smear of sticky pink sugar off his cheek.

Contact the salvage crews from the neutral guilds on Floor 2, Ren Qi instructed, stepping down from the parapet onto the sugar-covered steps. Tell them the transit corridor is now open. We are accepting shipments of raw iron, copper, and processing equipment immediately.

And the Golden Sun Alliance? Varrick asked.

Ren Qi closed his ledger with a dry, final snap and slid it into his vest pocket over his heart.

The Alliance no longer possesses the necessary liquidity to maintain their position as a governing entity, Ren Qi said, his voice quiet, cold, and entirely unbothered by the surreal candy rain drifting around him. By six o'clock tomorrow evening, we will be auditing their central vault on Floor 3.

He walked toward the fort’s inner office, his heavy boots crunching softly through the layer of sugar and shattered glass, leaving a clear, deliberate path behind him in the pink fluff.

Mina slung her rifle over her shoulder and fell into step behind him, a small, grim smile touching her lips.

Prepare the recruits, Ren Qi added as the heavy iron door of the office came into view. The rent on Floor 3 is due. And we are going to collect it in person.
