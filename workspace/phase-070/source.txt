Chapter 10: The Market Maker

The coffee tasted like charcoal and battery acid. To Ren Qi, it was the best thing he had ever drunk.

He sat on the balcony of Unit 2403, his legs dangling over the edge. The porcelain mug looked like a toy in his hand. Since the injection of the Titan’s Resilience, his grip had changed. He had to think about not crushing the ceramic. Every movement required a conscious dial-down of force, a software patch for hardware that was suddenly military-grade.

Below him, the fog churned. It was Day 4.

The world had ended four days ago, but the sun still tried to rise. It was a pale, sickly thing, struggling to penetrate the cloud layer that wrapped the city in gray cotton.

Ren checked the Architect’s Blueprint.

[Territory: Skyview Garden, Block B]

[Status: 16/24 Floors Cleared.]

[Population: 8 Survivors (Registered), 1 Landlord.]

He took another sip. The heat didn't register on his tongue. His density made him temperature-resistant.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

He held up the empty coffee mug. It was a cheap promotional item from an insurance company. 'You're in Good Hands,' the faded print lied.

[Target: Empty Ceramic Mug]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren didn't need the mug. He needed surveillance. The cameras in the stairwells were blind spots in his omniscience.

The ceramic groaned, fracturing not into shards, but into dust. The dust swirled, catching the dawn light, and reassembled into a small, black sphere the size of a marble. It hovered an inch above his palm.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: The Whispering Marble (Rank E)]

[Attributes: Audio-visual relay. Stick to any surface. 24-hour battery life.]

Ren caught the marble. He stood up. The concrete of the balcony didn't crack, but it felt like it wanted to. He weighed three hundred pounds now, packed into a frame that hadn't gained an inch of height.

He walked back into the apartment. The Cuirass of the Iron Eater sat on the couch, looking like shed reptile skin. He pulled it on.

Day 4: The Eviction Notice

The cleaning process was repetitive. It was manual labor.

Floors 12 through 5 were infested with Ghouls. Not the elites, not the bosses. Just the trash. The tenants who had turned.

Ren moved through the hallways with a rhythm.

Kick door. The wood splintered.

Scan room. Shadows moved.

Step. The floorboards groaned.

The Molecular Blade was efficient, but Ren found he barely needed it. With the Titan Serum, a backhand slap was enough to shatter a Ghoul’s cervical spine. He moved like a tank in a library, heavy and destructive.

He didn't loot everything. He didn't have the inventory space. He took the cores—small, gritty stones found in the chest cavities—and he took the electronics. Phones, laptops, tablets.

The batteries were dead, but the potential energy remained.

By noon, he reached the 4th floor.

The barricade here was amateur work. A sofa, a bookshelf, and a pile of chairs jammed against the stairwell door.

Ren looked at it through the wire-glass window. He saw movement on the other side.

He didn't knock. He tapped the glass with the pommel of his blade.

On the other side, a face appeared. It was the young man from the lobby. The one with the glasses. He looked like he hadn't slept in ninety hours. He saw Ren—saw the scale armor, the blade, the dead eyes—and fell back, scrambling away on his hands and knees.

"Get Lin Xia," Ren said through the door. His voice was muffled by the steel, but the bass notes carried through the floor.

A minute later, she appeared.

She wore a dirty blazer and jeans. Her hair was tied back with a rubber band. She held a kitchen knife like she knew how to use it, which was an improvement over the others.

She didn't open the door.

"You turned the lights on," she said. It wasn't a question.

"I own the utilities," Ren said.

Ren looked at the barricade. "Open the door. If I wanted to hurt you, that sofa wouldn't stop me."

Lin Xia hesitated. She looked at the blade in his hand. She looked at the blood spatter on his armor—black, viscous gore from the Ghouls on the 9th floor. She unlocked the deadbolt and dragged the sofa back a foot.

Ren didn't step inside. He stayed on the landing. The air in the stairwell was cold; the air in the hallway was stale and humid with the breath of seven terrified people.

"I cleared floors 24 through 5," Ren said. "The building is secure from internal threats. The external perimeter is compromised, but the lobby is frozen."

"Secure?" Lin Xia laughed. It was a brittle sound. "We saw a monster made of rocks walk past the window yesterday."

"The Gatekeeper. Dead."

"And the... thing. With the cleaver."

Ren reached into his pocket. He pulled out a bag. It was a heavy-duty trash bag, stained with fluids. He dropped it on the floor. It hit with the heavy clatter of stones.

"Ghoul cores," Ren said. "Thirty of them."

Lin Xia looked at the bag, then back at him. "I don't know what those are."

"Currency," Ren said. "And fuel. If you eat them, you might die. If you survive, you might get stronger. But mostly, they are money."

He looked over her shoulder. The other survivors were peeking out from the doorways of the apartments. A fat man in a suit. A teenager holding a cat. An old woman clutching a rosary.

"I am establishing a kiosk in the lobby," Ren said. "Food. Water. Weapons. Medicine."

The word food hit the hallway like a physical blow. The fat man took a step forward. His lips were cracked.

"You have food?" Lin Xia asked. Her voice lost its edge.

"I have an unlimited supply of stock," Ren lied. Well, a half-truth. With the Talent, supply was just a matter of conversion. "But I don't give charity. I trade."

He pointed to the bag of cores.

"One core gets you a bottle of water. Three cores get you a hot meal. Five cores get you a weapon that won't break."

"We don't have cores," the teenager said, his voice cracking.

"The fog is full of them," Ren said. "The parking garage is full of them. The street is full of them."

He looked at Lin Xia.

"I need a manager. Someone to watch the lobby. Someone to ensure nobody breaks the machine."

"And if we refuse?" Lin Xia asked.

Ren shrugged. The scales of his armor clicked. "Then you stay on the 4th floor. You starve. Eventually, something climbs in the window, and you die."

"Wait," Lin Xia said. "You said... a hot meal?"

Ren stopped. He focused.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

He thought of the packet of dried ramen in his inventory. The cheap, sodium-packed brick of noodles.

The air shimmered. A heavy ceramic bowl appeared in his hand. Steam curled off it, smelling of beef tallow, star anise, and rich, salty broth. The noodles were thick, hand-pulled. The beef was tender.

Ren placed the bowl on the floor next to the bag of gory stones.

"Beef brisket noodles," Ren said. "Spicy."

He walked back up the stairs.

Behind him, he heard the scramble of feet, the desperate slurping of broth, and the sound of civilization bending the knee to supply and demand.

The lobby was a cathedral of ice.

The corpse of the Spider Queen had disintegrated into blueprints days ago, but the frost she left behind had bitten deep into the marble. It was ten degrees below zero down here.

Ren stood in the center of the room, near the concierge desk.

He couldn't be here twenty-four hours a day. He had to sleep. He had to hunt. He needed automation.

He looked at the pile of junk he had dragged down from the upper floors. A broken vending machine from the staff break room on the 2nd floor (empty). A microwave. A pile of copper wiring stripped from the walls of the 8th floor. A shattered LCD screen from the security desk.

It looked like a pile of garbage. To Ren, it was a recipe.

He placed his hands on the pile.

The headache was getting familiar now. A dull pressure behind the eyes, the cost of warping reality.

"System," Ren whispered. "Construct."

He didn't just want a vending machine. He wanted a localized spatial distortion anchored to a commerce interface.

[Target: Vending Machine (Broken) + Copper Wire + LCD Screen + Microwave]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren poured mana into the Exchange. He visualized the function. Input matter. Output product. Security. Indestructibility.

The pile collapsed. The metal groaned, twisting like taffy. The glass of the screen melted and reformed, harder, darker. The copper wires wove themselves into the chassis like veins.

A flash of purple light illuminated the frozen lobby.

When the spots cleared from Ren's vision, the object sat there.

It was black. Matte black metal that seemed to absorb the ambient light. It was tall, imposing, shaped like a monolith. The front was a sheet of reinforced glass, but inside, there were no rows of chips or soda. There was only swirling gray mist.

On the side, a slot for inserting items. Below, a heavy steel tray.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: The Abyssal Kiosk (Rank A)]

[Attributes: Automated trading post. converts biomass/cores into pre-set inventory items. Indestructible. Anti-theft shock system (50,000 Volts).]

Ren touched the black metal. It was warm.

He opened the admin panel—a holographic interface that projected from the glass when he placed his palm on it.

[Set Exchange Rates?]

Ren reached into his Void Storage.

He pulled out the Unlimited Cup Noodle supply he had unlocked on Day 1. He shoved a crate of them into the loading slot. The machine swallowed them, the mist inside glowing briefly orange.

He pulled out the Purification Spirit water barrels. He fed the machine a gallon. The mist turned blue.

1 Mutant Core (Level 1-3) = 1 Liter Purified Water.

2 Mutant Cores = 1 Hot Meal (Randomized Menu).

10 Mutant Cores = 1 Basic Weapon (Iron Pipe / Machete).

The screen on the front of the machine flickered to life. Text scrolled across it in bright neon red.

"Lin Xia!" he shouted. His voice echoed up the stairwell.

She came down cautiously, wearing a heavy coat against the cold. The fat businessman was with her, wiping grease from his chin. They stopped ten feet away from the black monolith.

"This is the shop," Ren said. "Put the core in the top slot. Food comes out the bottom. If you try to pry it open, it will kill you."

The businessman, whose name was Chen, looked at the machine. He looked at the bag of cores Ren had given them yesterday. They had thirty free credits.

Chen stepped forward. He pulled a slimy gray stone from his pocket. He held it up to the slot.

The machine made a sound like a vacuum seal breaking. Thwump. The core vanished.

A bottle of water dropped into the tray. It was cold, clear, and the plastic was thick.

Chen grabbed it. He cracked the seal and drank half of it in one breath. He gasped, wiping his mouth.

"It... it makes you feel stronger," Chen whispered. "My headache. It's gone."

"Purified water," Ren said. "Cleans out the toxins from the fog."

He looked at Lin Xia.

"You're on shift," Ren said. "Tell the others. Hunt. Kill. Pay. If anyone causes trouble, the machine has my permission to electrocute them."

Ren turned and walked toward the elevators. He had given them the tools. Now he would see if they were worth saving.

Ren sat in the control room on the B1 level, watching the monitors.

The Whispering Marble he had stuck to the ceiling of the lobby gave him a 4K view of his new economy.

It started slow. The survivors on the 4th floor burned through their free thirty cores in six hours. They ate. They drank. They remembered what it was like to not be dying.

Then, the hunger returned. But this time, it wasn't the hunger of starvation; it was the hunger of consumers.

He watched Chen and the teenager, whose name was Marco, step out of the lobby doors into the fog. They carried iron pipes Ren had dispensed from the machine. They were shaking.

They came back twenty minutes later, running, bleeding. Chen had a gash on his arm. But Marco was holding three cores.

They fed the machine. They ate. They healed.

By the afternoon of Day 6, the dynamic changed.

They came from the street. Survivors from the neighboring apartment block, Block A. They saw the lights. They saw the movement.

Ren watched a group of four men smash the glass of the lobby doors. They carried fire axes and makeshift shields. They looked desperate, violent.

They saw the Kiosk. They saw Lin Xia standing guard with her kitchen knife.

"Get back," Lin Xia warned. She was bluffing. She was terrified.

The leader of the intruders laughed. He stepped toward the machine. He raised his axe to smash the glass front.

"Jackpot," he snarled.

Ren sat forward in his chair, his finger hovering over the Override button on the console. But he didn't press it. He wanted to test the machine's passive defense.

The axe hit the black metal.

A arc of blue lightning exploded from the chassis. It didn't just shock the man; it threw him across the lobby. He hit the reception desk with the sound of breaking ribs. He convulsed once and lay still. Smoke rose from his boots.

The other three intruders froze.

Lin Xia didn't scream. She stepped forward. She pointed at the dead man, then at the slot on the machine.

"Price is one core," she said, her voice shaking but loud. "Or you can try the axe again."

The intruders looked at their fallen leader. They looked at the glowing red text: FEED ME.

Slowly, one of them reached into his pocket. He pulled out a handful of bloody stones.

Ren leaned back in his chair.

"Capitalism," Ren said to the empty room. "It works in every dimension."

Ren wasn't just watching. He was hoarding.

Every core fed into the Kiosk didn't disappear. It was teleported to the Storage Vault Ren had designated in the B1 Safe Room.

He went to the vault.

It was a small concrete room. In the center, a pile of gray stones grew like a burial mound.

[Resource Count: 412 Mutant Cores (Common)]

[Resource Count: 18 Mutant Cores (Uncommon)]

The survivors were working hard. The rumors were spreading on the Regional Chat.

Ren opened the interface.

[Regional Chat - Zone 04]

User882: Is it true? Skyview Garden has a safe zone?

User109: I saw the lights. But the fog is thick there. High-level mobs.

MadDog: It's real. I went there. There's a black box. Magic food. But the price is steep.

User882: What's the price?

MadDog: Blood. You gotta pay in cores.

SniperWolf: The Shopkeeper is watching. Don't try to rob it. My buddy got fried.

Ren closed the chat. The Shopkeeper. He liked the anonymity.

He looked at the pile of cores.

It was enough for the next phase.

Ren walked to the pile. He knelt down. He buried his hands in the cold, gritty stones.

He didn't want to exchange them for items. He wanted stats. He wanted raw power.

But the Inequivalent Exchange offered him something else. A pathway.

[Target: 400 Mutant Cores]

[Exchange Available.]

The pile of stones dissolved. The gray dust swirled, filling the room, choking the air. It didn't form an item. It formed a doorway.

A rift tore open in the air. Not a physical door, but a tear in the fabric of the B1 level. Through the tear, Ren could see... nothing. Just a swirling, starry void.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Pocket Dimension Access - The Armory (Rank S)]

[Attributes: A personal sub-space for storing heavy ordinance and equipment. Time within the Armory is frozen.]

Ren stepped through the rift.

He stood on a floating platform of obsidian in a sea of stars. It was empty. A blank canvas.

He stepped back out into the B1 level. The rift closed behind him, shrinking into a tattoo on his wrist.

The system notification blinked in his peripheral vision, red and urgent.

[Global Announcement Incoming.]

[The 3000th Descent of Mystery is about to begin.]

[Time remaining: 15 Minutes.]

Ren walked out of the vault. He climbed the stairs to the roof of the building.

The wind was howling up here, whipping his hair across his face. The fog below was glowing with the bioluminescence of a thousand monsters.

He looked up at the sky.

The clouds were parting. Not naturally, but torn apart by massive, descending pillars of light.

The gods were sending their care packages. The dungeons were opening.

Ren touched the hilt of his Molecular Blade. He touched the Cuirass on his chest. He felt the density of his bones, the strength of the Titan blood in his veins.

He wasn't the scared kid who had hidden in the bathroom on Day 1.

He was the landlord. He was the Shopkeeper.

And he was ready to buy out the competition.

"Let them come," Ren said.

Above him, the first pillar of light slammed into the city center, shaking the earth.

The Descent had begun.
