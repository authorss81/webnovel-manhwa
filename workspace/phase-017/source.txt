# Chapter 17: Public Enemy Number One

The storm outside had died down to a greasy, yellow drizzle that smeared the cracked windows of Unit 2403.

Ren Qi sat in his steel-framed desk chair, the metal groaning under his three-hundred-pound frame. The Titan’s Resilience serum had settled deep into his bone marrow, turning his skeleton into something resembling high-density industrial rebar. He didn't feel heavy to himself, but the floorboards under his boots gave a soft, dry squeak every time he shifted his weight.

On his desk, the blue light of the system terminal illuminated his face. The local forums for District 9 were moving so fast the text was a blur of panic, greed, and digital noise.

He didn't need to scroll. He had set a filter for three words: Hualong, Wyvern, and Thief.

The results were dense.

User4401: Did anyone see that guy? He fell from forty stories, ripped a scale off the Wyvern, and just... walked away? Is he even human?

GreyWolf_99: It wasn't a player. It had to be a humanoid boss. No player has that kind of strength. The Dawn Vanguard spent three days setting up that raid. They lost forty-two men and the loot got swiped by a ghost.

Slayer_X: Dawn Vanguard is offering a bounty. Check the pinned post.

Ren Qi clicked the pinned thread. The author’s name was highlighted in a heavy, metallic gold that indicated a verified guild leader status.

Sovereign Cao (Level 15, Vanguard Knight): To the unidentified scavenger who interfered with the Hualong Financial Tower raid: You have thirty-six hours to return the Scale of the Steel Wyvern to our headquarters at the CBD Center. Failure to comply will result in an active, permanent bounty. We have your height, weight signature, and the unique weapon profile of your grapple device. You are now designated a public enemy. Anyone who provides actionable information on this individual's location or identity will receive 100 Rank E Cores and a permanent officer position within the Dawn Vanguard.

Ren Qi leaned back, his eyes reflecting the gold text.

A hundred Rank E cores. It was a fortune for the average survivor. It was enough to buy a year's worth of clean water and canned meat from the Abyssal Kiosks. He had expected some backlash, but Sovereign Cao was taking this personally.

A soft notification chimed in the corner of his vision.

[SYSTEM: Infamy rating has increased to 'Local Menace'.]

[Current Status: Public Enemy Number One (Dawn Vanguard Blacklist)]

[Effect: Passive fear generation increased by 15% within a 5-kilometer radius.]

Ren Qi reached into his pocket and pulled out the dark, leathery sphere of the Shadow Monarch Egg. It felt warm, almost hot, vibrating against his palm with a rhythmic, heavy heartbeat. He checked the display hovering over the shell.

[Item: Companion Egg - Shadow Monarch (Rank ???)]

[Hatching Progress: 214/1000 (Fear/Dread Accumulated)]

The numbers were ticking up. 215. 216.

The forum post was doing the work for him. Every time a player read the bounty, felt a shiver of dread at the thought of a monster capable of robbing a Level 20 Wyvern, the egg drank.

Public enemy, Ren Qi thought, a dry, humorless smile touching his lips. They call it a crime. I call it a marketing campaign.

But anonymity was a melting candle. The grapple launcher was unique. The fact that he was operating near Block B would eventually link back to him. He needed to accelerate his defenses.

The service elevator hummed as he took it down to the lobby.

The ground floor of Skyview Garden was quiet. The marble floor was clean, though a faint, dark stain remained near the entrance where the Rock Skin user had been turned into sand. The dust had been swept into a plastic bucket near the corner, waiting to be mixed with mortar.

Lin Xia wasn't behind her desk. She was standing near the basement stairwell, her fingers white as she gripped a heavy iron flashlight. When the elevator doors slid open with a metallic chime, she jumped, nearly dropping the light.

"Landlord," she gasped, her voice thin. "You're... you're down."

"You're not at the desk, Lin Xia," Ren Qi said. His voice was low, carrying the flat, bureaucratic tone of a man reviewing an audit. "The reception guidelines are clear. The lobby must be monitored."

"I know, I'm sorry," she stammered, pointing the flashlight toward the darkness of the stairwell. "But... the Units. They found something in B3. Below the water pumps. They were clearing the rubble from the old structural collapse like you ordered, and... they stopped working."

"They're scared," she whispered. "Even with the contract. They're just sitting on the floor. I went down to check on them, and... there are writings on the wall. They're glowing. I translated some of them using the municipal ledger we found, but..."

She swallowed hard, her throat dry.

"It's a gate. A big one."

Ren Qi adjusted the strap of his Cuirass of the Iron Eater beneath his flannel shirt. "Show me."

The descent into the sub-basement of Skyview Garden was like entering a tomb. The air grew progressively colder, smelling of ancient moisture, rusted pipes, and the thick, suffocating draft of the deeper earth. As they passed B2, the distant, primordial thrum of the Divine Corpse Fragment in the root chamber vibrated through the concrete walls—a low-frequency hum that made the teeth ache.

In B3, the lights were gone. The only illumination came from the yellow beam of Lin Xia's flashlight and a dull, pulsing red glow from the far end of the corridor.

The four indentured thugs—the survivors of the Pyromancer's raiding party—were huddled against a concrete pillar. Their hands were wrapped in dirty, grey bandages, their faces hollow and grey in the shadows. When Ren Qi's heavy boots stepped into the room, all four of them flinched, their eyes wide with a terror that was deep and absolute. They had seen what he did to their leader. They knew the brand on their chests could stop their hearts with a single thought from the man standing before them.

"Landlord," the largest of them, Number Three, mumbled, keeping his eyes on the floor. "We... we didn't stop because we wanted to. The wall... it wouldn't let us dig."

Ren Qi ignored him, walking past the men toward the excavation site.

A ten-foot section of the concrete foundation had been cleared away, revealing a massive, archaic archway embedded directly into the bedrock of the city. The stone was black, oily, and seemed to actively reject the light of Lin Xia's flashlight, absorbing the beam into its porous surface.

Carved into the lintel were runes that looked like rows of pointed teeth. They glowed with a stagnant, arterial red, pulsing in time with the low thrum of the building's foundation.

[System: Unknown Dungeon Gate Detected.]

[Status: Blood Lock Active.]

Ren Qi ran his fingers over the stone. It was freezing, almost burning his skin with an unnatural frost.

"What did the translation say?" Ren Qi asked.

Lin Xia stepped closer, her flashlight beam shaking as she pointed it at the central rune.

"It... it's old dialect," she said, her voice trembling. "It says: The threshold is dry. It demands the crimson of the bound. A soul for a path. The master must yield the breath of the servant to open the way."

She paused, looking from the gate to the four thugs huddled by the pillar.

"It... it wants a sacrifice, Landlord. A human life. Someone who belongs to the owner of the gate."

Silence fell over the sub-basement. The only sound was the wet, rhythmic dripping of water from a broken pipe somewhere in the darkness.

The four thugs stiffened. Number Three looked up, his face pale, his lips parting in a silent plea. They knew the Contract of Indenture. They knew their lives were legally and systematically his property. If he ordered one of them to step into that stone archway and let it drain them, they could not refuse. Their own bodies would betray them, walking them into the meat grinder.

Lin Xia stared at Ren Qi, her chest rising and falling in shallow, rapid breaths. She was waiting for the command. She was waiting for the cold, logical calculator in his head to select the weakest of the four and send him to the altar.

Ren Qi looked at the thugs.

His mind worked in numbers. A human life in this new world was a variable asset.

These four men represented the physical labor force of Block B. They cleared debris, they hauled steel, they guarded the perimeter. Replacing them would require finding new survivors, draft contracts, and feed them. In terms of long-term utility, a living laborer was worth approximately 40 Rank F cores per month in raw productivity.

Sacrificing one for a single, one-time gate key was a deficit. It was bad business. It was a temporary gain for a permanent loss of capital.

Lin Xia let out a breath she seemed to have been holding for a minute. "You're... you're not going to do it?"

"A dead worker produces nothing," Ren Qi said, his voice flat. "It's a waste of resources. I don't tolerate waste."

He stepped closer to the stone gate.

If the gate demanded a sacrifice—a "crimson of the bound"—it was asking for a concept. The system operated on definitions, on value and exchange. The gate wanted the weight of a life surrendered, the moral gravity of a master giving up his mercy.

The concept of mercy, Ren Qi thought.

He looked at his status screen. His Charisma was a mere 3. His empathy was already a dry well, worn away by years of structural design deadlines, debt, and the cold reality of the descent. But there was still a remnant. A small, stubborn piece of his human socialization that hesitated before the dark.

"System," Ren Qi murmured.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

He placed his hand on the cold stone of the gate.

"I don't have blood to give you," Ren Qi whispered, his eyes narrowing. "But I have the concept of it. I trade my own moral reservation. The part of me that still hesitates to cross the line."

[Target: Concept of Personal Mercy (Abstract Alignment)]

[Target Attribute: Empathy, Moral Restraint.]

[System Warning: This exchange will permanently alter your psychological profile. Alignment will shift toward 'Neutral Evil'. Do you wish to proceed?]

"Proceed," Ren Qi said.

The drain was instantaneous and violent.

It wasn't a physical pain, but a sudden, icy void opening in the center of his chest. He felt a thousand memories—the soft, warm moments of his childhood, the face of his mother, the guilt he had felt the first time he had to fire a subordinate in Shenzhen—turn gray, then transparent, then dissolve into cold, mathematical data.

His nose began to bleed, a thick, dark drop of red curling over his lip. But his eyes remained perfectly steady, devoid of any warmth.

The SSS-Rank talent groaned, pulling nearly 150 points of his mana in a single, crushing sweep. The air around him distorted, turning into a neon blue grid of coding wireframes.

[Exchange Successful.]

[Input: Concept of Personal Mercy + 150 Mana.]

[Output: Key of the Martyr (Rank B).]

A heavy, iron key materialized in the air before him. It was cold, shaped like a stylized rib bone, dripping with a dark, oily substance that smelled of copper and old dust.

[Item: Key of the Martyr (Rank B)]

[Properties: Concept-Fusing. Satisfies any blood-debt or life-sacrifice requirement for ancient seals by simulating the absolute surrender of moral value.]

The four thugs watched in stunned silence as Ren Qi wiped the blood from his nose with the back of his hand, his expression completely blank. He looked less like a human now and more like a piece of high-density concrete that had learned to speak.

He stepped forward and thrust the bone key into the central rune of the archway.

The stone gate groaned. The red runes turned a blinding, violent white, then shattered like glass. The black stone of the archway dissolved into a swirling, liquid void, revealing a steep, descending flight of stone stairs that wound down into the absolute dark beneath the city.

[System: Dungeon Gate 'The Crypt of the Flayed' has been unlocked.]

[First Clear Bonus Active: 200% Experience and Loot Drop Rate for the next 2 hours.]

Ren Qi looked down into the dark. The cold draft rising from the stairs had a new smell. Not mold. Not rot.

The smell of raw, unharvested iron.

He turned to the thugs.

"Get your tools," Ren Qi said. "We have a new sector to clear."

The men didn't hesitate. They scrambled to their feet, their fear of the dark completely eclipsed by their terror of the man who had just traded his own soul to save their skins.
