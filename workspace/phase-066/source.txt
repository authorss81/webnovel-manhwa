Chapter 6: First Descent

The lobby of Skyview Garden, Block B, was a mausoleum of ice.

Ren Qi stood amidst the wreckage. The air was crisp, holding that specific, sharp stillness that follows a blizzard. The corpse of the Concrete Arachnid dominated the space—a frozen monument of rebar and hate, caught in a permanent scream.

Around him, the sounds of life were returning, wet and pathetic.

The people he had cut from the webs were waking up. There were seven of them. A security guard in a torn uniform. A woman in pajamas clutching a cat carrier. A businessman who had soiled himself. They coughed up strands of gray silk, shivering violently as the absolute zero of the frozen spider leeched the heat from the room.

The businessman looked up. He saw the frozen monster. He saw the wall of marble that had flowed like water to seal the entrance. Then he saw Ren.

Ren didn't look like a hero. He looked like a janitor who had just finished a particularly messy shift. He held a black blade that hummed with a headache-inducing frequency, and his eyes—visible through the crack in the matte-black scope he wore around his neck—were flat.

"Who..." the businessman stammered. His teeth chattered. "Who are you?"

Ren didn't answer. He consulted the holographic blueprint hovering in his peripheral vision.

[Territory: Skyview Garden, Block B]

[Population: 8 (1 Awakened, 7 Dormant)]

He tapped a command into the air.

The marble floor beneath the survivors groaned. Stone rippled. A section of the floor rose up, forming a rough, circular bench.

They sat. Not out of respect, but out of the sheer, crushing weight of his presence. He was the only thing in the room that made sense.

"The doors are sealed," Ren said. His voice echoed off the ice. "The temperature will normalize in an hour. There is a bathroom behind the reception desk. There is a vending machine in the east corridor."

He pointed the tip of the Molecular Blade at the frozen Spider.

"Do not touch the statue. It is minus two hundred degrees. If you touch it, you will lose the hand."

"Are you the police?" the woman asked. She hugged the cat carrier. "Did the government send you?"

Ren looked at her. He activated the Specter-Sight Scope for a fraction of a second. No thermal bloom of a hidden weapon. Just fear, bright and cold blue.

"The government is dead," Ren said. "I own this building. You are guests. Keep the noise down."

He turned his back on them. He didn't offer reassurance. Hope was a calorie-expensive emotion he couldn't afford to distribute.

He walked toward the stairwell. The elevator was gone, the shaft occupied by the severed cable and the debris of his victory. He had a long walk ahead. Twenty-four floors.

But he couldn't rest. Not yet.

He had claimed the building, sealed the borders, and killed the king. But his own doorstep was dirty.

The creature—the Mist Walker—was still up there. The one that had mimicked his mother. The one that had tapped on his glass.

It was a loose end. Ren hated loose ends.

The climb was grueling.

Ren took the stairs at a steady, rhythmic pace. Step. Breath. Step. Breath.

At the 10th floor, he paused to eat a handful of dry ramen noodles from his pocket, crunching the raw brick for carbohydrates. At the 18th floor, he drank from the water bottle spiked with the Purification Spirit. Fatigue tried to claw at his legs, but the blue water washed it away, replacing lactic acid with a cool, vibrating energy.

He reached the 24th-floor landing.

He didn't open the fire door immediately. He stood there, listening.

Silence. Not the empty silence of a vacuum, but the heavy, expectant silence of a predator holding its breath.

Ren unclipped the Specter-Sight Scope and held it to the heavy steel door.

The hallway beyond was a wash of gray ghost-light. The walls were weeping. The corruption from the fog had seeped through the ventilation, growing mold that looked like black velvet.

He scanned the corridor.

By the door to Unit 2403. His apartment.

Through the scope, it didn't look like a stick figure anymore. It was a coalescence of cold vapor and solid matter. It stood seven feet tall, its limbs too long, possessing too many joints. It was stroking the wood of his door with long, gray fingers.

Ren lowered the scope.

He checked his gear. The Molecular Blade in his right hand. The Prometheus Zippo in his pocket.

He triggered the Mental Fortress.

The anxiety in his gut vanished. The fear of the dark, the primal hesitation of hunting a monster in a confined space—it was excised. His mind became a spreadsheet.

Objective: Eliminate intruder.

Asset: Molecular Severance.

Ren pushed the fire door open.

The hinge squealed. A rusted, piercing sound.

The creature down the hall spun around. It moved with unnatural speed, its upper body rotating a full 180 degrees while its legs remained planted.

It had no face. Just that vertical slit.

The voice hit him. It wasn't his mother this time. It was softer, younger.

"Uncle Ren? Did you bring the red envelope?"

His niece. The one who had owned the pink binoculars. She lived in Hangzhou.

The creature tilted its head. The slit in its face twitched, tasting the air for his reaction. It was mining his memories, looking for a hook, a barb of guilt to snag him with.

Ren stepped into the hallway. The carpet squelched under his boots.

"Your data is outdated," Ren said calmly.

The creature shrieked. It wasn't a human sound. It was the sound of a radio tuned to a dead channel at max volume.

It didn't run; it flowed. Its limbs scrabbled against the walls and the floor, closing the twenty meters between them in a heartbeat. It raised a hand, fingers elongating into gray spikes meant to impale.

Ren watched it come. He didn't flinch. He calculated the vector.

He waited until he could smell the ozone and rot rolling off its skin.

Ren didn't dodge. He burst forward, directly into the creature's guard.

The stamina drain bit into his thighs, a sharp electrical cramp, but the physics held. He passed through the space where the creature’s killing blow should have been. He appeared inside its reach, chest-to-chest with the abomination.

He brought the Molecular Blade up in a short, vicious arc.

He didn't aim for the head. He aimed for the structural pivot point. The shoulder.

The blade parted the gray flesh like water. There was no resistance. Bone, muscle, condensed fog—it all severed at the atomic level.

The creature’s right arm detached, spinning away into the darkness.

The scream changed pitch, becoming a gurgle. The creature thrashed, trying to backpedal, trying to reform.

Ren didn't let up. Mental Fortress allowed no pity, no hesitation. This wasn't a fight; it was a renovation.

He stepped on the creature's foot, pinning it to the floor.

He swung the blade again. A horizontal slash.

The creature’s legs separated from its torso.

It collapsed, a pile of twitching gray sludge. The vertical slit on its face opened wide, trying to mimic a sound, perhaps a plea, but Ren silenced it with a downward thrust into the center of the mass.

The twitching stopped.

The gray sludge began to boil. It evaporated, turning into acrid steam that vanished into the ventilation ducts.

Ren stood over the stain on the carpet. He flicked the blade, though there was no blood to clean.

Something remained on the floor.

A small, jagged crystal. It was dull gray, pulsing with a faint, rhythmic light.

Ren picked it up. It was cold, heavy as lead.

[Item: Core of the Mist (Common)]

[Description: The condensed essence of a low-level fog entity. Contains traces of spatial data.]

Ren held it up to the flickering hallway light.

A common drop. In the global market—if one existed yet—this would probably buy a loaf of bread.

But Ren Qi didn't play by market rules.

[Talent: Inequivalent Exchange]

The golden text bloomed in his retina, hungry and bright.

He looked at the core. He thought about what the creature was. It was a thing of the fog. It moved through the mist unseen. It knew where to hide. It saw things others couldn't.

"I don't need the energy," Ren whispered. "I need the perspective."

He squeezed the gray stone.

The reaction was subtle. The stone didn't explode. It dissolved into his skin. It felt like dipping his hand into a bucket of mercury—heavy, cool, permeating.

The sensation traveled up his arm, past his shoulder, and settled directly behind his eyes.

A sharp, stabbing headache blinded him for a second. He gasped, bracing himself against the wall.

Then, the pain receded, leaving behind a cold clarity.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Eye of the Void (Skill Book - Consumed)]

[Attributes: Passive Skill. The fog is no longer a barrier; it is a medium. You can perceive the structural density of the Void. Reveals hidden dimensional pockets, loot caches, and weak points in reality.]

He wasn't using the scope. This was his own vision.

The darkness was still there, but it was no longer uniform. He saw textures in the air. The fog wasn't just gray smoke; it was a web of data streams.

He looked at the floor where the creature had died.

He saw a faint, golden thread rising from the carpet, drifting through the wall into Unit 2402—Lin Xia’s apartment.

He turned his head. He looked at his own door.

The metal of the door was semi-transparent to his new gaze. He could see the stress points where he had welded it. But more importantly, looking out toward the balcony, through the walls of his apartment, he saw a bright, pulsing node of white light suspended in the air outside the building, about ten meters out.

A loot cache. Hidden in the fog. Invisible to the naked eye. Invisible to the thermal scope.

But to the Eye of the Void, it shone like a beacon.

"Detailed," Ren muttered.

He walked to his door. He placed his hand on the fused metal. With the Molecular Blade, he carefully sliced the weld, opening his sanctuary.

He stepped inside. The air was stale, smelling of ozone and the old leather sofa.

The Sentry Turret on the balcony tracked him, then whirred down as it recognized his bio-signature.

Ren walked to the sliding glass door. He looked out at the white node floating in the void.

It was too far to jump.

He looked at the Architect’s Blueprint interface still hovering in his vision.

[Territory Construction Available.]

"Extend the balcony," he commanded.

He watched as the concrete lip of his balcony groaned. The rebar stretched like taffy. The stone grew, pushing out into the fog, forming a narrow bridge toward the floating light.

He walked out onto the new ledge. The wind whipped at his clothes. He was twenty-four stories up, standing on a tongue of concrete he had just willed into existence.

He reached out and grabbed the floating white light.

It felt like grabbing a static shock.

[System Notification: Hidden Void Cache Found.]

The light shattered in his hands, revealing a small, wooden box. It looked ordinary, like a jewelry box.

Inside sat a ring. Simple, made of a dark, unpolished metal.

[Item: Ring of the Hoarder (Rank B)]

[Effect: Grants 10 cubic meters of dimensional storage space. Weight of stored items is negated.]

Ren slipped the ring onto his finger. It resized instantly to fit.

He looked back at his apartment. The pile of junk—the toaster, the twine, the old magazines—all the fuel for his exchanges.

"Inventory management," Ren said. "Finally."

He walked back inside. The concrete bridge retracted behind him, sealing the fortress once more.

He sat on the sofa. He was exhausted. The mana drain from Shadow Step, the mental load of the Exchange, the physical climb—it was catching up to him.

He picked up the bowl of noodles. Cold now.

He had cleared the lobby. He had cleared his floor. He had eyes that could strip the secrets from the air itself.

Ren Qi closed his eyes. For the first time in three hours, he allowed his muscles to unclench.

Tomorrow, he would clear the other twenty-two floors. Tomorrow, he would deal with the tenants.
