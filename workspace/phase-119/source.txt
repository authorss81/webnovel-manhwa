# Chapter 59: Counter-Offensive

The grease-rain of Floor 1 did not stop. It gathered in the deep, cracked channels of the southern transit routes, turning the black silt of the drainage system into a slow, viscous river that smelled of old diesel and heavy metal.

Ren Qi stood at the threshold of the Skyview Garden gate, his fingers slowly tightening the leather buckle of his left gauntlet. The rain ran down the rusted plates of his Cuirass of the Iron Eater, leaving dark, oily streaks across the dark flannel of his clean shirt. Behind him, the ten F-Rank recruits stood in a perfect, silent line. Their faces were grey, their eyes fixed forward with the deadpan neutrality of automated heavy machinery. They did not shift their weight. They did not check their weapons. They simply waited for the next input.

Varrick was hovering near the heavy steel door of the office, his fingers twitching against the side of his trousers. The green light from the internal terminals still reflected in the damp sheen of his forehead.

Boss, he said, his voice dropping into a dry, frantic whisper. The Alliance’s forward operating base at the southern junction... they’ve locked it down. They’ve pulled all their sentries inside the stone wall. Deputy Commander Brand is there. He’s got forty heavy line-breakers and eighty mercenary crossbowmen. If we go through that canal, we’re walking into a bottleneck. It’s a bad trade.

A liability left unpaid is a recurring expense, Varrick, Ren Qi said. He did not look back. If we allow them to hold that junction, they control the drainage valves. They can flood our basement with toxic run-off from the Floor 2 refineries whenever their operating costs get too high. We are going to liquidate their position before the next market cycle begins.

Mina stepped out of the shadows of the doorway, her long-barreled rifle slung low across her hip. Her boots made a dry, scraping sound on the concrete. She spat a small bit of zinc-grit into the mud.

The mercenaries are already arguing about their rations, she said, her voice like gravel sliding down a chute. I’ve been monitoring their local frequency. Brand tried to pay them in regional credit vouchers because he doesn't have the physical coin to back their daily allowances. They told him they can't eat paper. It’s a swily situation in there.

Then their internal cohesion is at three percent, Ren Qi said. He reached into his vest pocket, checked his ledger to ensure the ink on the previous page was dry, and then slid it back. Kael.

The shadow in the corner of the courtyard did not speak, but the faint, cold gleam of the Phantom Blade’s hilt shifted in the dim light.

Lead the way, Ren Qi said.

The march through the southern drainage canal was silent. The water was deep, reaching their knees in the low-lying sections where the concrete foundation of the transit hub had settled over the last three hundred years. It was cold, thick with the oily residue of upper-tier machinery, and it tasted of copper pennies when the wind blew the spray into their faces.

The ten recruits moved with a single, synchronized stride. They did not splash. They did not complain about the cold. Under Ren Qi’s systematic training, their physical responses had been calibrated to maximize energy conservation. Every step was a calculated expenditure of force, designed to prevent premature fatigue.

Ren Qi walked at the front, his heavy boots cutting through the black sludge. His mind was not on the cold or the smell of the water. He was tracking the coordinates of the forward operating base.

Three thousand meters.

The Alliance had built the fort around the main structural column of the Floor 1 transit lift. It was a brutalist redoubt, constructed from three-ton blocks of dark granite salvaged from the ancient quarries of Floor 2. The walls were forty feet high, six feet thick, and reinforced with internal steel tension rods. It was designed to withstand high-impact siege engines and low-tier magical artillery. It was a physical manifestation of the Alliance’s authority on this floor—a heavy, immovable barrier that forced every independent scavenger to pay a fifty percent tariff on their finds before they could access the upper levels.

As the canal widened into the lower reservoir, the dark outline of the fortress rose from the mist like a sleeping iron beast.

The defensive walls were dark. The massive, high-intensity searchlights that usually swept the reservoir were flickering weakly, their yellow beams dimming to a dull amber before dying entirely for seconds at a time. The local generators, starved of the high-grade Light Crystals that Ren Qi had swept from the global market, were struggling to maintain even the basic security systems.

On the parapets, the sentries were huddled together in groups of three, their heavy steel armor clinging to their shivering frames. Without the internal heating coils of their suits active, the cold dampness of Floor 1 was slowly drawing the heat from their bones.

Deputy Commander Brand was pacing the upper platform, his voice carrying clearly over the steady patter of the grease-rain.

Keep those heaters off! he roared, his fist slamming against the stone railing. I don't care if your fingers are stiff, Private! If we burn the remaining oil in the auxiliary turbine now, we won't have enough power to run the communication arrays by midnight!

But Commander, a subordinate stammered, his teeth clicking together. The grease in the mechanical gate locks is starting to congeal. If we don't run the thermal lines, we won't be able to open the gates manually if we need to retreat to the lifts.

We are not retreating! Brand snapped. The enforcers on Floor 3 are already assembling a reinforcement convoy. We just have to hold this junction for twelve more hrs. The merchant guild will find a way to break the crystal freeze. No one can hold a global monopoly for more than two days. It’s mathematically impossible.

Nothing is impossible, Ren Qi murmured, stepping out from the shadow of the main drainage archway into the open mud of the reservoir.

His voice was flat, devoid of any theatrical weight, but it cut through the hum of the dying generators with the cold precision of an iron wedge.

The sentry on the nearest tower froze, his hand fumbling with the safety of his crossbow.

Who’s there? Identify yourself!

Ren Qi did not stop walking. He approached the base of the massive granite wall, his boots sinking three inches into the grey silt. The stone was cold, slick with a thin layer of organic slime and grease-rain. He reached out his right hand, his palm flat against the rough, dark surface of the three-ton block that formed the foundation of the gate structure.

`[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]`

`[Input: Granite Defensive Wall. Value: High.]`

`[Output: Silica Glass Wall. Value: Low.]`

The air around the fortress did not explode with light. There was no grand display of magical energy, no runic circles expanding across the mud. Instead, a high-pitched, metallic ring vibrated through the concrete foundations of the reservoir, a sound so sharp and clean it made the metal buckles on the recruits’ vests ring in sympathy.

From the point where Ren Qi’s palm touched the stone, the dark, opaque granite began to change.

The deep grey color faded, the density of the rock dissolving as its molecular structure was systematically reordered. Within four sec, a fifty-foot section of the forty-foot-tall wall had turned completely transparent. The rough texture of the stone became smooth, cold, and clean, transforming into a massive pane of tempered silica glass that reflected the dim, yellow glow of the internal lanterns like a frozen lake.

Through the clear barrier, the terrified faces of the Alliance line-breakers were visible. They were standing on the wooden interior scaffolds, looking down through the floor beneath their boots at Ren Qi’s upturned face. They looked like insects trapped in amber, their mouths open in silent, confused screams.

Ren Qi took two steps back, his face an unreadable sheet of stone.

The silhouette beside him moved. The movement was so swift it left no trail in the damp air. Kael did not use a high-tier skill. He did not call upon the power of his SSS-rank configuration. He simply raised his arm, his fingers locked around the hilt of the Phantom Blade, and struck the center of the transparent glass wall with a single, high-impact kinetic thrust.

A tiny, white star appeared at the point of impact.

For a fraction of a second, the silence of the reservoir was absolute. Then, a sharp, cracking sound like the splitting of an ancient pine tree echoed through the canal.

The crack multiplied. A thousand silver lines spider-webbed across the forty-foot pane of glass, glowing with the refracted light of the sputtering lanterns inside.

With a deafening, crystalline roar, the entire section of the wall collapsed.

Millions of heavy, razor-sharp glass shards rained down into the interior courtyard of the fort, slicing through the wooden scaffolds and dragging forty heavy line-breakers down in a landslide of transparent knives. The heavy steel armor they wore, unpowered and cold, did nothing to protect them. The sharp, heavy shards found every joint, every unsealed neck-guard, and every open visor, pinning them to the stone floor beneath the weight of their own defenses.

Understood, Landlord, Mina replied.

She did not wait for the dust to settle. She raised her long-barreled rifle, her shoulder absorbing the dry, rhythmic kick of the weapon as she began to fire into the ruined courtyard.

The ten F-Rank recruits advanced in a perfect, horizontal line. They did not sprint. They did not scatter for cover. They moved with a slow, grinding momentum, their modified kinetic rifles cracking in unison. They did not aim for the torso or the heavy breastplates. They aimed for the knees, the helmets, and the soft leather seals of the throat.

The enforcers inside the fort were in complete disarray. The sudden, absurd transformation of their granite redoubt into fragile glass had shattered their tactical discipline.

My armor! an enforcer screamed, his voice high and hysterical as he tried to drag himself out from under a pile of broken silica. The joints are frozen! I can't move my legs!

Help me! another cried, his hand reaching out from the glass-rubble, his fingers slick with dark, thick blood.

The recruits did not show mercy. They did not show anger. They operated with the cold, bureaucratic efficiency of a clearinghouse. Every step forward was accompanied by the mechanical sound of an ejecting brass casing clattering against the broken glass on the floor.

Mina’s rifle cracked again.

An Alliance captain, who was attempting to rally fifteen crossbowmen near the secondary inner wall, dropped instantly. The lead slug, conceptually hardened through Ren Qi’s talent to ignore light-based armor values, tore through his golden helmet as if it were nothing but dry pasteboard.

The remaining crossbowmen threw down their weapons and fled toward the cargo lift at the rear of the base.

Ren Qi walked through the shattered gap in the wall. His heavy boots made a slow, rhythmic crunching sound as they crushed the remaining glass into fine, white sand. He did not look at the dead or the wounded. His eyes were fixed on the command office—a small, reinforced iron bunker built into the side of the central transit column.

The heavy steel security door of the office was vibrating, its emergency gears grinding with a wet, struggling screech.

Inside, Deputy Commander Brand was frantically turning a manual brass crank, his shirt soaked with sweat, his face white with terror as he looked through the small, high-density glass viewport at the advancing figures.

Open the lift! he screamed at his terminal, his fingers slamming against the dead keys. Why isn't the primary lift responding?

The terminal screen flickered once, displaying a single, cold line of text.

`[SYSTEM: Insufficient power reserves. Core allocation: 0.12%. Lift locked.]`

The security door did not close completely. The grease in the tracks, frozen by the lack of thermal maintenance, had jammed the bottom guide-rail. A six-inch gap remained at the bottom of the door, letting in the smell of copper, blood, and the cold grease-rain from the reservoir.

Ren Qi stopped in front of the door. He did not try to pry it open with his hands. He reached into his vest pocket, pulled out his personal ledger, and opened it to a fresh page.

Brand, Ren Qi said, his voice flat and quiet.

The manual crank inside stopped turning. Brand’s face appeared at the viewport, his eyes wide and bloodshot, his lips trembling.

Ren Qi, he gasped, his voice thin through the steel barrier. You... you can't do this. This is an official logistics base of the Golden Sun Alliance. We are a registered guild under the Babel Hub’s central charter. If you occupy this sector, the council will declare you a rogue element! They’ll freeze your assets globally!

The council operates on the assumption that the Alliance is solvent, Ren Qi said. He did not look up from his ledger. He picked up his pen and wrote a single line on the clean paper. Currently, your guild is operating at a daily loss of four hundred thousand gold units due to the logistics halt on Floor 3. Within three days, your central treasury will be dry. A rogue element cannot be prosecuted by a bankrupt institution.

He looked up, his eyes cold and empty of any emotion.

Your life has a high maintenance cost, Brand. You consume forty units of ration-grade mana per day, and your administrative output over the last quarter has shown a fifteen percent decrease in efficiency. From a balance-sheet perspective, you are a non-performing asset.

What... what do you want? Brand whispered, his hand sliding off the brass crank.

I am going to execute an exchange, Ren Qi said.

He reached down and touched the steel door of the command office.

`[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]`

`[Input: Reinforced Security Door. Value: Structural.]`

`[Output: Sheet of Dry Plywood. Value: Negligible.]`

The heavy steel plates of the door did not slide back. They simply lost their grey luster, the metallic coldness of the iron replacing itself with the light, fibrous grain of cheap construction wood. The hydraulic seals fell away, clattering onto the concrete steps as useless scrap.

Kael did not wait for the command.

He kicked the plywood sheet. The door shattered into three neat pieces, showering the interior of the office with dry, brown splinters.

Brand fell backward against his desk, his hand reaching for the shortsword at his belt, but before his fingers could even touch the leather hilt, the cold, black tip of Kael’s Phantom Blade was resting against the hollow of his throat.

Do not move, Ren Qi said, stepping into the room.

He walked over to the main communication console of the fort. It was a massive, brass-faced array of dials and crystal receivers, connected directly to the primary subterranean cable that ran through all ninety-nine floors of the Babel Hub. It was the central node for Floor 1’s regional broadcast system, usually reserved for Alliance emergency announcements and tax notices.

The console was dark, its power indicators showing green only at the very bottom of the scale.

Ren Qi reached into his pocket and pulled out a single, spent brass shell casing that he had picked up from the courtyard floor. It was scratched, blackened by gunpowder residue, and completely worthless.

He placed the casing on the center of the brass console.

`[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]`

`[Input: 1x Spent Brass Casing. Value: Negligible.]`

`[Output: Regional Communication Bandwidth. Value: High.]`

The spent casing vanished.

The brass console roared to life. Every indicator dial on the faceplate slammed to the far right, the small, green lamps glowing with a fierce, violet intensity that made the copper wires behind the panel hum with a high-pitched, vibrating frequency. The central screen of the array, which had been blank for hrs, blazed with a bright, clean white light.

Ren Qi adjusted the input receiver, his fingers moving with a slow, practiced grace.

He opened the Global Chat interface—the massive, decentralized communication log that connected every player, merchant, and guild leader across the ninety-nine floors of the system.

The screen was currently a chaotic torrent of text, thousands of messages scrolling past every second as players from the upper levels argued about the sudden, extreme shortage of Light Crystals.

"Why is the transit platform on Floor 3 shut down?"

"The price of copper wire just doubled on the Floor 2 exchange!"

"Is it true the Golden Sun Alliance is losing control of the southern spires?"

"Who is this 'Landlord'?"

Ren Qi tapped the broadcast key. He did not use a voice filter. He did not prepare a dramatic speech.

He simply spoke into the brass receiver, his voice flat, steady, and entirely devoid of the urgency that was vibrating through the rest of the world.

The Golden Sun is setting, Ren Qi said. Join The Exchange, or burn.

He tapped the screen three times, attaching a real-time, high-density video feed of the forward operating base.

The image displayed was clear: the massive, shattered gap where the granite wall had turned to glass; the forty line-breakers lying in the mud, pinned by their own unpowered armor; and Deputy Commander Brand sitting on the office floor with Kael’s black blade pressed against his throat.

The Global Chat did not slow down.

It stopped completely.

For five full sec, the screen remained a solid, motionless sheet of white text. Not a single message was posted across all ninety-nine floors. The silence was absolute, a collective, psychological intake of breath from millions of survivors who had spent the last three years believing that the Alliance’s power was as permanent as the concrete columns of the hub itself.

Then, the log exploded.

The text moved so fast it became a solid, white blur of light on the brass screen.

"The southern fort is gone?"

"The granite wall... did it turn to glass? What kind of SSS-Rank talent is that?"

"He captured Brand! He literally walked through their main defensive line in thirty minutes!"

"Where is The Exchange? I have three hundred units of iron ore in my private vault on Floor 2. Can I trade them for food?"

"Is the transit lift open? We want to leave the Alliance sector!"

Ren Qi did not read the responses. He turned his back on the screen, his fingers sliding his ledger back into his vest pocket.

Yes, Boss? Varrick asked, his voice shaking as he stepped through the splinters of the plywood door.

Establish a local branch office in this fort, Ren Qi instructed. Use the recruits to secure the transit lift. The neutral guilds on Floor 2 will be sending trade representatives down within the next three hrs. They will want to secure their supply of Light Crystals before the price rises again.

Our price is seven hundred gold units per crystal, Ren Qi continued. We do not accept credit vouchers. We do not accept Alliance script. Physical gold only, or an equivalent value in raw copper and industrial iron.

Varrick swallowed hard, his face a mix of terror and manic, financial calculation. And... and the Alliance enforcers who survived?

Tell them the rent for this sector is now fifty gold units per head, per day, Ren Qi said. If they cannot settle their accounts by tomorrow morning, they will be liquidated.

He walked out of the command office, his dark flannel shirt damp from the grease-rain, his boots crunching softly on the glass-grit as he headed toward the main courtyard where the recruits were already organizing the captured gear.

Outside, the wind from the ruins of Floor 1 howled against the shattered walls of the fort, but inside the reservoir, the air was still.

The calculation had just begun.

And the first floor of the Babel Hub was officially under new management.
