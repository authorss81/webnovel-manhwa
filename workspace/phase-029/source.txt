# Chapter 29: The Invitation

The morning light that filtered through the cracked window of the third-floor management office did not carry the warm, golden hue of the old world. It was a pale, sterile lavender, filtered through layers of stagnant, high-altitude dust that had lingered since the Red Moon had been broken.

Ren Qi sat behind his desk. The surface, once cheap imitation mahogany, was now a cold, seamless slab of synthetic obsidian that pulsed with a faint, deep-green light whenever his fingertips brushed the edges. His fingers were longer now, the skin carry a translucent, polished quality that looked more like sculpted marble than human flesh. Underneath his epidermis, the veins did not show the blue of oxygen-depleted blood, but a steady, silent crawl of indigo mercury.

He closed his eyes, and the world did not go dark. Instead, his Molecular Sight activated, rendering the entirety of Skyview Garden in a three-dimensional wireframe of light and stress vectors. He could see the structural supports of the B2 generator room holding steady under nine hundred metric tons of concrete. He could see the microscopic fissures in the eastern retaining wall, where the chemical rain had begun to eat away at the aggregate mix. He could even see the slow, rhythmic pulses of the survivors sleeping in the western dormitory, their nervous systems glowing like pale blue moss in the dark.

Everything was measurable. Everything had a cost.

A quiet knock rattled the heavy iron door of the office.

"Enter," Ren Qi said. His voice was flat, carrying the dry, resonant scrape of shifting flagstones.

Lin Xia stepped into the room, her sneakers making a soft, rubbery squeak on the polished floor. She was holding her metal clipboard against her chest like a shield, her knuckles still stained with the grey, greasy soot of the B2 coal-conversion burners. She looked older than she had three weeks ago, the fine lines around her eyes etched deeper by the constant pressure of survival, but her posture was no longer hunched with the frantic, trembling panic of the early days. She had adapted to the ledger.

"The morning audit is complete, Landlord," she said, her voice steady but quiet. "The B2 disposal team finished sorting the debris from the eastern crater. We recovered forty-three kilograms of low-grade copper wiring and twelve rusted steel structural joints from the transit van wreckage. I've logged them under the secondary raw material inventory."

"And the survivors?" Ren Qi asked without looking up from his hands.

"Sun Liang and the other two are still working off their penalty rates," she replied, her pen hovering over the paper. "Their current debt balance is down to eighty-two credits each. At the current rate of toxic waste sorting, they will return to a neutral balance in approximately twelve days. They aren't complaining. Not after they saw what happened to Zhao Kun's boots."

"A rational choice," Ren Qi observed. "An unpaid debt is a structural instability. Once the account is balanced, their survival rate will rise by fourteen percent based on the current food allocation metrics."

He stood up, his movement so fluid and devoid of acceleration that it looked as if the space around him had simply shifted to accommodate his new position. The heavy scale vest of his Cuirass of the Iron Eater clinked softly, the rusted iron plates now carrying a dark, oil-slick sheen that absorbed the lavender light of the window.

He walked to the glassless frame of the window, looking out over the central courtyard. The Skyview Spire rose forty meters into the sky, its blocky, three-sided bronze faces etched with deep, glowing violet runes that hummed at a frequency that kept the swily yellow fog exactly twelve meters outside the property line. The fog pressed against the invisible boundary like a greasy, yellow wall, boiling and churning, but unable to find a single crack in the system's structural integrity.

"The air pressure is dropping," Ren Qi said, his eyes tracking a thin, violet fracture line that had appeared in the upper atmosphere, miles above the Nanshan District. "The system is preparing for a regional dampener adjustment. The local currency circulation has reached its maximum density. The market is saturated."

"Saturated?" Lin Xia repeated, stepping closer to the window but keeping a respectful three feet of distance between herself and the shimmering heat distortion that surrounded Ren Qi's body. "Does that mean another wave of monsters?"

"No," Ren Qi said, his gray eyes narrowing as he analyzed the atmospheric data. "A wave of monsters is a liquidation event. It is designed to clear out low-value assets and redistribute system currency. But the current regional danger index is neutral. The system has already extracted its profit from this sector. It is time for a market expansion."

Before Lin Xia could ask what he meant, the violet fracture in the sky above the Futian Financial Center did not widen.

Instead, it turned a deep, blinding gold.

It was not the warm gold of a sunrise, but the cold, heavy glare of highly refined currency. The sound came a split second later—not an explosion, but a massive, resonant chime that vibrated through the concrete floors of Skyview Garden, making the iron-reinforced window frames sing like tuning forks.

The swily yellow fog outside the courtyard boundary did not scatter; it was instantly flattened against the asphalt, pinned down by a sudden, massive increase in local spatial density.

From the golden tear in the sky, a single object began to fall.

It did not tumble. It did not carry the frantic, burning speed of a meteor. It descended with a perfectly uniform velocity of exactly one meter per second, cutting through the stagnant air with zero air resistance. As it drew closer, Ren Qi's Molecular Sight locked onto its structure.

It was a heavy, rectangular tablet of solid gold-platinum alloy, roughly the size of a standard architectural blueprint folder. Its surface was completely smooth, devoid of any kinetic damage from its descent through the upper atmosphere, but as it crossed the boundary of the Skyview Spire's green light field, the runes on the spire began to spin in frantic, circular patterns, outputting high-frequency alerts directly into Ren Qi's mind.

[SYSTEM: External administrative anomaly detected.]

[Object Class: Summons / High-Tier Spatial Anchor]

[Structural Compatibility: 99.8%]

[Warning: The incoming transaction bypasses local sector restrictions.]

The golden tablet did not hit the courtyard floor. It paused, hovering exactly three feet above the concrete socket where the decoy totem had once stood. It spun slowly, once, twice, before the golden light radiating from its surface condensed into a thin, horizontal beam that projected directly toward the third-floor window.

Ren Qi did not flinch. He did not pull back. He watched as the beam of light touched his synthetic obsidian desk, carving a series of deep, precise geometric characters into the polished stone with a sharp, hissing sound that smelled of ozone and burnt gold leaf.

When the light faded, the golden tablet in the courtyard dissolved into a fine, glittering metallic dust that was immediately absorbed by the soil of the courtyard, leaving only the freshly carved characters on Ren Qi's desk.

Lin Xia hurried over, her eyes wide as she looked at the glowing gold letters that now sat on the black obsidian.

"Landlord..." she whispered, her hand trembling as she pointed at the text. "What is this? Is it a contract?"

Ren Qi looked down at the writing. His eyes processed the linguistic data not as language, but as a series of system prompts that aligned themselves within his vision.

[SYSTEM: World Server Integration initiated.]

[Summons: The Invitation to the Tower of Babel]

[Recipient: Ren Qi (Demi-God of Matter - Tier 2 / Regional Administrator of Sector 44-Shenzhen)]

[Location: The Multi-Server Integration Hub (The Tower of Babel)]

[Description: You have achieved absolute regional dominance and transcended the mortal threshold within your designated sector. You are hereby invited to ascend to the Tower of Babel, the central junction of three thousand parallel descents. There, the strongest administrators, sovereigns, and merchants from five hundred integrated Earths gather to trade, compete, and secure their final ascension.]

[Warning: Upon entering the Tower of Babel, regional protective dampeners are disabled. The local physical laws are governed by the Prime Arbiters. The risk of asset liquidation is high. The potential for resource acquisition is unlimited.]

[Access Portal active in: 24 hrs.]

Ren Qi read the prompts twice, his expression remaining as flat and unreadable as the obsidian desk beneath his hands.

"A cross-server junction," he murmured, his fingers tracing the gold-leaf characters. "Five hundred integrated Earths. That represents a significant population density. The quantity of high-grade assets, rare materials, and unallocated system currency must be several thousand times greater than the local sector's total capacity."

"Other Earths?" Lin Xia's voice was barely a breath. She looked out the window, at the ruined, silent towers of Shenzhen that had been her entire world since the descent began. "You mean... there are other cities? Other people who survived the same thing we did?"

"Of course," Ren Qi said, his voice practical. "The system is a highly efficient machine. It would not design an entire integration infrastructure for a single, isolated population. That would be a massive waste of administrative overhead. A multi-server model is far more cost-effective. It allows the creators to test different environmental variables across multiple sectors, then pool the high-performing assets into a centralized clearinghouse."

He turned back to the room, his eyes scanning the corner of the office where a pile of raw materials lay stacked on a metal rack.

"The Tower of Babel is not a sanctuary," he continued, walking toward the rack. "It is a wholesale market. And in any wholesale market, the primary risk is not the quality of the goods, but the predatory pricing of the established wholesalers."

"Are you going?" Lin Xia asked. She looked at him, her fingers tightening on her clipboard until the metal buckled slightly. "If you leave... what happens to Skyview Garden? What happens to the spire?"

"The Skyview Spire is a hardwired anchor," Ren Qi said, picking up a rusted steel pipe from the rack and testing its weight. "Its current power reserve is sufficient to maintain the protective field for three hundred and forty days without manual fuel injection. I will configure the automation loop to draw power from the local B2 coal generator. As long as you maintain the coal delivery, the regional dampener will not fail."

He set the pipe down, his gaze shifting to a small drawer at the bottom of his desk. He reached in, his fingers wrapping around an object he had carried with him since the very first day of the descent—the yellow plastic box cutter he had used to score drywall and trim copper wire during his endless, underpaid shifts at the Futian design firm.

The plastic handle was faded, scratched, and smeared with a thin film of dried grey mortar. The steel blade was rusty, its tip snapped off from a high-pressure joint he had tried to pry open three months before the sky fractured. To any other survivor, it was a piece of useless trash, a D-rank tool that wouldn't even cut through the hide of a crawl-rat.

But to Ren Qi, it was an asset with an established historical ledger. It was a tool of precision division.

"A system transition requires a modern upgrade to the primary tools of production," Ren Qi said softly.

He placed the yellow box cutter on the synthetic obsidian desk.

Then, he walked to the heavy metal safe in the corner of the room, unlocking it with a touch of his finger. From the interior, he pulled out three items: the cracked, violet-glowing fragment of the Heart of the Glitch that he had kept as a stabilizer, a small bar of high-density spatial alloy he had purchased from the Abyssal Kiosk for ninety thousand system coins, and a heavy, crystalline gland he had extracted from the skull of a glass-fleshed hound.

He laid the three items in a neat row beside the yellow box cutter.

"Lin Xia," he said, without looking at her. "Close the door. The kinetic back-pressure from a Tier 2 exchange can destabilize the local air pressure."

She didn't hesitate. She stepped back, pulling the heavy iron door shut until the latch clicked into place with a solid, echoing thud.

Ren Qi closed his eyes. The cold, deep void in his chest opened, not like a physical wound, but like a vast, empty ledger that was starving for a transaction. He reached out with both hands, his fingers hovering inches above the pile of scrap and the rusty utility knife.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange (Tier 2) activated.]

[Input: 1x Rusty Utility Knife (Rank D) + 1x Fragment of the Heart of the Glitch (Unique / Anomalous) + 1x Spatial Alloy Bar (Rank S) + 1x Crystalline Gland of the Glass-Fleshed Hound (Rank A)]

[Concept: Precision division -> Absolute Molecular Severance]

[Warning: The SSS-Rank talent is bypassing the material limitations of the local physical plane. The conversion ratio is highly inequivalent.]

[Exchange successful. Commencing structural reorganization...]

The room did not fill with light. Instead, the pale lavender light from the window was violently drawn inward, sucked toward the center of the desk as if a tiny, highly localized black hole had opened on the obsidian surface. The yellow plastic casing of the box cutter did not melt; it began to peel back, its molecular chains sliding apart like liquid silk, while the spatial alloy bar dissolved into a series of thin, silver threads that began to weave themselves through the plastic's original carbon structure.

The fragment of the Heart of the Glitch hissed, its violet code bleeding into the steel blade, turning the rust into a deep, non-reflective black that looked like a tear in the fabric of the room itself. The crystalline gland of the hound shattered, its internal density condensing into a single, microscopic edge that ran along the very limit of the steel blade.

The sound was a quiet, high-pitched hum, like a distant turbine running at maximum speed.

When the light returned to the room, the desk was clean. The scrap metal, the spatial alloy, and the violet crystal were gone.

In their place lay a sleek, matte-black utility knife. The handle was no longer cheap yellow plastic, but a dense, non-reflective synthetic compound that felt slightly warm to the touch, its surface etched with the same geometric stress lines that Ren Qi saw through his Molecular Sight. The blade was short, barely three inches long, but it did not reflect the light of the room. It looked like a thin, vertical slit of absolute nothingness, a physical void that seemed to pull the surrounding air toward its edge.

Ren Qi picked it up. The weight was perfect, balanced exactly at the pivot joint.

[SYSTEM: Item properties updated.]

[Item: The Administrator's Utility Knife (Rank: Divine - Custom)]

[Active Ability: Molecular Severance – The blade does not cut through physical resistance; it bypasses atomic density entirely, severing the structural bonds of any inanimate matter within fifty meters without physical contact.]

[Passive Ability: Structural Appraisal – When held, the user's Molecular Sight is enhanced. All structural weaknesses, load-bearing joints, and system anchors within five hundred meters are displayed with a 100% accuracy rate.]

[Description: A tool that was once used to trim drywall, now reconstructed with the logic of a spatial anomaly. It does not exist to build; it exists to decommission.]

Ren Qi slid the slide lock forward. The blade made a quiet, clean click that felt incredibly satisfying.

"A suitable tool for a site inspection," he murmured. He slid the knife into the pocket of his dark flannel shirt, right beside his steel pocket knife.

He walked to the door, pulling it open. Lin Xia was standing in the corridor, her hands clenched so tightly that her knuckles were white. She looked up at him, her eyes scanning his face for any sign of change.

"Is it done?" she asked.

"The preparation is complete," Ren Qi said, walking past her down the concrete corridor. "We have twenty-four hours to organize the logistics before the portal stabilizes. I will need the following reports compiled before sunset: the current coal reserves on B2, the average daily consumption rate of the water pumps, and the current balance sheet of the survivor labor force."

"I've already started on the coal report," she said, running to keep up with his long, effortless strides. "We have enough for six months of continuous generation, even if the regional dampener is running at maximum load. But Landlord... who will lead the B1 security team while you are away? Number Three is strong, but he doesn't... he doesn't talk. He just stands by the eastern wall."

"Number Three does not need to talk," Ren Qi said, stopping at the landing of the stairs. "He is an automated security protocol. He has been programmed to execute any tenant who violates the rental agreement. As long as you maintain the ledger, the system will enforce the rules."

He turned his head, his gray eyes locking onto her. "Do not attempt to negotiate with the survivors, Lin Xia. A contract is not an emotional document. It is a formula. If they do not work, they do not eat. If they attempt to leave the property, their security deposits are forfeited. If they attempt to damage the spire... Number Three will settle their accounts."

Lin Xia swallowed hard, but she nodded. She had seen the alternative. In this new world, Ren Qi's cold, mathematical cruelty was the only thing that kept the yellow, toxic fog from filling their lungs. It was a high price, but it was a predictable one.

"I understand," she said. "I'll keep the books balanced."

The night was long and quiet.

Ren Qi spent the remaining hours walking the perimeter of Skyview Garden, his Molecular Sight active as he checked every load-bearing pillar, every weld joint on the outer chain-link fence, and every seal on the B2 generator room. He didn't sleep. A Tier 2 Demi-God of Matter did not require sleep; his physical structure was constantly being maintained by the slow, rhythmic conversion of the Skyview Spire's energy field.

When the first light of dawn broke through the yellow fog, the golden beam in the courtyard flared to life once more.

The air in the center of the courtyard began to twist, the spatial density rising until a three-meter-wide vertical circle of golden light stabilized above the concrete socket. Through the shimmering surface of the portal, Ren Qi could see a glimpse of another world.

It was not a city of glass and concrete. It was a massive, impossibly high vertical column that rose from a sea of dark, churning clouds, its sides composed of massive blocks of black basalt and glowing gold trim that looked like a cross between a mountain and a high-rise office tower. It was so vast that it seemed to fill the entire horizon, its upper levels disappearing into a sky that was a chaotic, spinning vortex of violet and gold rifts.

Even from this distance, through the spatial link of the portal, Ren Qi's Molecular Sight could see the structural vectors of the tower. It was a masterpiece of inefficient engineering—too many decorative spires, too much raw mana being wasted on aesthetic lighting, and a structural load distribution that relied entirely on active spatial anchors rather than proper physical foundation work.

It was a beautiful, bloated asset.

The survivors of Skyview Garden had gathered on the balconies of the western block, their pale faces pressed against the iron railings as they watched the golden portal hum in the center of their courtyard. They looked at the massive tower in the distance, and then they looked at Ren Qi, who stood before the portal with his hands behind his back.

Number Three stood behind him, his massive, grease-stained canvas coat rustling in the spatial wind that blew from the portal. Beside them, Lin Xia held her clipboard, her eyes shiny with a quiet, unshed wetness that she refused to let fall.

Ren Qi turned to face her.

"The ledger is in your hands," he said simply.

"Yes, Landlord," she said, her voice carrying a faint, dry tremor. "Safe travels."

Ren Qi did not answer. He turned back to the golden portal, his gray eyes reflecting the cold, heavy glare of the tower in the distance. He reached into his flannel shirt, his fingers brushing the matte-black handle of his upgraded utility knife—his Divine-tier box cutter.

He did not look like a hero stepping into a grand battle of gods. He did not carry the fiery passion of a warrior or the desperate hope of a survivor. He looked like an administrator stepping out of his office to inspect a poorly managed branch office.

"Let us see how much this tower is worth," he said to himself.

He took a single, steady step forward, his boot crossing the threshold of the golden light, and vanished into the multi-server integration zone.
