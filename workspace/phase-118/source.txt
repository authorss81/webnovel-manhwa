# Chapter 58: The Siege of The Exchange

The rain on Floor 1 did not fall from clouds. It seeped from the porous concrete ceiling of the transit hub miles above, a steady, grease-slicked drizzle that tasted of rust and sulfur. It ran down the brutalist angles of Skyview Garden, turning the grey dust of the courtyard into a thick, dark paste that clung to the boots of the ten F-Rank recruits standing at the perimeter wall.

Inside the main office, the air was dense with the smell of scorched insulation. The three trading terminals salvage-rigged by Varrick were whining, their copper heat sinks struggling to disperse the thermal load of processing thousands of rapid-fire market liquidations.

He’s here, Boss, Varrick said, his fingers twitching against his knees. His eyes were wide, reflecting the erratic green strobe of the transaction logs. Vance. He’s the commander of the Third Auxiliary Vanguard. Thorne’s personal attack dog. They didn't take the transit platforms. They used the heavy cargo elevators from Floor 2 and walked through the southern drainage canals. They’ve got three hundred players with them. Mercenaries, heavy shields, line-breakers.

Ren Qi did not look up from his ledger. He was carefully adjusting the leather straps of the Cuirass of the Iron Eater, pulling them tight over his fresh flannel shirt. The cold dampness of the room was a welcome relief against the residual heat still trapped in the rusted iron scales of his vest.

Three hundred, Ren Qi murmured, his voice flat, rhythmic. A significant operational expenditure. Moving that many high-tier players through the wet zones without automated transport consumes approximately forty units of ration-grade mana per head. They are desperate.

They’re not just desperate, Mina said from the doorway. She was cleaning the firing pin of her rifle with an oily rag, her movements slow and deliberate. She spat a dark speck of zinc-grit onto the floor. They’re hungry. The market freeze on the Light Crystals has already taken down the heating grids in their barracks on Floor 3. I checked the short-wave before the static got too thick. The mercenaries are demanding their daily gold allowances in advance because they don't trust the Alliance’s credit lines anymore.

Then they are operating under a severe structural deficit, Ren Qi said. He closed his ledger with a dry, decisive snap and slid it into his inner vest pocket. A force motivated by immediate liquidity is highly sensitive to sudden capital depreciation. Let us go receive our guests.

He walked past Varrick, his heavy boots clicking on the damp concrete floor, and stepped out onto the iron-gated balcony that overlooked the main courtyard of the garden.

The scene below was a study in industrial siege.

The southern gate of Skyview Garden—a massive barrier made of interlaced structural steel and copper grounding cables—was already groaning under the weight of several heavy iron rams. Three hundred players, clad in the mismatched, bulky armor of various mid-tier guilds under the Golden Sun Alliance’s banner, had formed a tight, overlapping shield wall.

Their gear, however, was not the pristine, glowing gold that Thorne had displayed on the upper terrace. The light-conductive filigree on their breastplates was dark, flickering with a weak, sickly amber instead of the steady, blinding solar radiance. Without the Light Crystals to feed their internal capacitors, their high-tier defense arrays were nothing more than heavy, inert plating that dragged in the mud.

At the front of the line stood Vance. He was a broad-shouldered man wearing a heavy iron-plated coat over a scarred cuirass. His motorcycle helmet was pushed back, revealing a face caked in grey coal dust and sweat. In his hand, he held a massive, double-bitted hydraulic axe that hissed with escaping steam every time he shifted his weight.

Ren Qi! Vance bellowed, his voice bouncing off the concrete columns of the transit hub. You think you can freeze the global exchange and hide in this sewer? The Alliance doesn't play by your market rules, merchant! Hand over the forty thousand crystals in your vault, or we’ll peel this building apart piece by piece and use your skull as a cup for our grease!

Ren Qi leaned his forearms against the cold iron railing of the balcony, looking down at the three hundred men with the cool, analytical eye of a building inspector surveying a crack in a foundation wall.

Your platform is leaking hydraulic fluid, Vance, Ren Qi said. His voice was not loud, but it possessed a peculiar, carrying clarity that cut through the hiss of the steam rams and the patter of the grease-rain. The left piston of your primary ram has a pressure seal failure. If you continue to cycle it at that rate, it will blow the gasket within four min.

Shut your swily mouth! Vance roared, raising his axe. You’ve got ten F-Rank trash-pickers and a sniper who’s running out of brass. You think you’re a god because you found a loophole in the transaction code? We are the ones who cleared the dungeons on Floor 2! We are the ones who hold the gate! Break the doors!

The heavy ram slammed into the steel gate again. The structure buckled, the copper grounding cables snapping with loud, blue sparks that died instantly in the wet mud of the courtyard.

Mina stepped up beside Ren Qi, her rifle already resting on the iron railing. Her thumb was on the safety. I can take three of their front-line captains before they clear the inner courtyard, she said, her voice like gravel sliding down a metal chute. But after that, the recruits will get swamped. They don't have the density to hold a three-hundred-man line.

We do not need to hold a line, Ren Qi said. He reached into his leather belt pouch and pulled out a heavy, canvas bag.

The bag clinked with a dense, metallic ring. It was filled with Babel Coins—the heavy, gold-minted currency of the system. Each coin was stamped with the weeping sun of the upper tiers, a universal unit of value recognized by every merchant, player, and system terminal across the ninety-nine floors.

Ren Qi loosened the drawstring, letting a handful of the heavy gold coins spill into his palm. They were cold, heavy, and perfectly formed.

Varrick, who had crept out onto the balcony behind them, let out a small, strangled gasp. Boss... those are the high-denomination coins we pulled from the Floor 3 trade settlement. That’s fifty thousand gold units. You’re not going to try to bribe them, are you? They’ll just kill us and take the bag anyway.

I am not offering a bribe, Ren Qi said. His face remained an unreadable sheet of stone. I am executing a transaction.

He tilted his hand. The fifty thousand gold coins spilled over the edge of the balcony, falling through the grease-rain in a glittering, metallic cascade that clattered loudly against the concrete steps and into the deep mud of the courtyard below.

The Alliance players at the front of the line froze. The sight of so much raw, physical currency rolling through the dirt was a psychological shock that their mercenary minds could not immediately process. Several of the front-line shield-bearers actually lowered their guards, their eyes tracking the rolling gold pieces as they settled in the grey sludge.

Is this your surrender? Vance sneered, though his eyes, too, lingered on the gold. You think a few thousand coins will buy your life?

He closed his eyes, his mind locking onto the invisible interface of the system. The SSS-Rank talent within his consciousness stirred, not with a surge of magical power, but with the cold, absolute certainty of a balance sheet being settled.

Input, he whispered. Fifty thousand gold units.

The system responded instantly, the prompt appearing before his eyes in clean, unembellished characters.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]

[INPUT: 50,000x Babel Gold Coins. VALUE: High.]

[OUTPUT: 12x Aurum Devastators. VALUE: Colossal.]

The gold coins in the mud did not dissolve. They began to vibrate.

A low, bone-shaking hum rose from the ground, so intense that the stagnant puddles in the courtyard began to dance with concentric ripples. The gold metal of the coins began to expand, liquefying into a thick, molten slurry that did not mix with the mud. Instead, the liquid gold began to draw in the surrounding materials—the rusted rebar from the broken concrete walls, the copper grounding wires of the shattered gate, the heavy iron slag discarded by the old workshops of Floor 1.

The transformation was silent, violent, and instantaneous.

Twelve massive, geometric figures rose from the mud. They stood over ten feet tall, their bodies constructed from thick blocks of interlocking gold-plated iron, their limbs massive cylinders of solid brass that hissed with a dry, internal heat. They had no faces—only a single, horizontal slit in their heavy, rectangular heads that glowed with the deep, violet light of raw mana shards.

The weight of their feet sinking into the concrete caused the entire foundations of Skyview Garden to groan.

What... what kind of summons are those? Vance stammered, stepping back as his hydraulic axe hissed in protest. He had never seen these monsters in any of the system’s bestiaries. They weren't listed in the Alliance’s databases.

These are not summons, Ren Qi said, his voice cold, deadpan. They are assets. And their maintenance costs have already been settled.

He looked down at his ledger, which he had pulled back out with his left hand.

Money fights for me, he muttered.

The twelve Aurum Devastators moved.

Despite their colossal weight, their movements were not slow. They possessed the clean, unyielding momentum of a hydraulic press. The nearest golem took a single step forward, its massive gold-plated fist swinging in a wide, horizontal arc.

Three heavy shield-bearers from the Alliance’s front line raised their interlocking barriers to block the blow.

The impact sounded like a church bell being struck by a locomotive.

The hard-light projectors on their shields, starved of Light Crystals, sputtered once and died. The heavy iron plates buckled instantly, folding inward like wet cardboard. The three players were launched backward into their own ranks, their breastplates crushed by the sheer, unyielding density of the gold fist.

They are indestructible! a mercenary screamed, his sword striking the shoulder of the second golem.

The heavy steel blade left a shallow, silver scratch on the gold plating. A second later, the molten gold beneath the surface welled up, filling the groove and hardening back into a smooth, seamless finish. The golem did not even turn to look at the attacker; it simply brought its massive, block-like heel down on the mercenary's chest, crushing his heavy armor into the wet concrete with a wet, sickening crunch.

The Alliance players began to panic.

For three thousand years, the system had taught them that gold was a number on a screen, a resource to be hoarded, traded, and used to buy power. Now, that very currency was walking through their ranks, a physical manifestation of wealth that was literally beating them to death with its own value.

Keep the line! Vance roared, his face turning a deep, purple red as he swung his hydraulic axe against the leg of the leading golem. The steam-assisted blade bit deep into the brass joint, spraying hot oil and gold filings into the air. Break them! They’re just constructs! They don't have infinite health!

The golem’s leg buckled slightly, but the violet light in its head slit did not dim. It reached down, its massive gold fingers locking around the shaft of Vance's axe.

With a single, effortless twist, the golem sheared the heavy steel shaft in half. Vance stumbled backward, his hands empty, his face pale as he looked up at the faceless, gleaming giant that towered over him.

Mina, Ren Qi said, his eyes never leaving the ledger. The command structure is attempting to reform near the cargo elevators. Neutralize the variables.

Understood, Mina replied.

She did not use the automated defense grid. She didn't need to.

In the dark shadows of the water tanks on the roof, five of the F-Rank recruits—whom Ren Qi had systematically equipped with modified, low-draw kinetic rifles—raised their barrels. They did not move with the frantic energy of players; they moved with the silent, rhythmic efficiency of automated machinery.

The first crack of Mina’s rifle was a dry, whip-like report that echoed through the transit hub.

An Alliance sub-commander, who was attempting to rally forty mercenaries near the southern wall, dropped instantly. His helmet was shattered by a high-density lead slug that had been conceptually hardened through Ren Qi’s talent to ignore light-based armor values.

Before the mercenaries could even look for the source of the shot, four more cracks followed in rapid succession.

Three more officers fell into the mud, their defense arrays completely failing to register the low-tech, high-velocity kinetic projectiles.

The "Cyborg Ghosts"—the nickname Varrick had given to Ren Qi’s silent, re-educated recruits—did not stop firing. They did not aim for the heavy armor; they aimed for the joints, the necks, the soft visors of the helmets. They operated with a cold, terrifying precision that offered no heroic challenges, no dramatic speeches, only a systematic liquidation of targets.

They’re sniping us from the roof! a player yelled, his shield shattering under a second heavy blow from an Aurum Devastator. We can’t get close! The gates are locked!

The retreat began as a slow drift, then turned into a frantic, mud-splattered rout.

The three hundred players who had marched down from Floor 2 with dreams of easy plunder and a quick resolution to the crystal shortage were now trampling each other in their haste to reach the cargo elevators. They threw away their heavy iron shields, their gold-plated weapons, their supply packs—anything that might slow them down as they fled the golden giants that pursued them with tireless, mechanical strides.

Vance was one of the last to reach the elevator. He was bleeding from a deep gash on his forehead where a piece of his own shattered axe had struck him. He looked back at the courtyard of Skyview Garden, his chest heaving as the elevator doors began to slide shut.

On the balcony, Ren Qi stood undisturbed by the rain, his dark flannel shirt damp but his expression perfectly calm. He was writing in his ledger, his pen moving with the steady, practiced grace of an accountant closing out a minor ledger page.

This isn't over, Ren Qi! Vance screamed through the closing steel slats of the elevator. The Alliance will shut down the entire transit hub! We’ll starve you out! You won't have a single coin to spend!

The elevator doors closed with a heavy, metallic bang, cutting off his voice.

The courtyard grew quiet again, save for the steady patter of the grease-rain and the heavy, rhythmic breathing of the twelve Aurum Devastators as they stood in a circle around the ruined gates.

The mud was littered with the discarded gear of three hundred players—swords, shields, iron coats, and broken rifles.

Varrick slowly let go of the balcony railing, his knees shaking so hard he had to slide down against the concrete wall. We... we just destroyed a heavy auxiliary unit of the Golden Sun Alliance, he whispered, his voice high-pitched, incredulous. With fifty thousand gold. We literally threw money at them until they died.

It was a highly efficient transaction, Ren Qi said. He did not look at Varrick. He closed his ledger and tucked it back into his vest.

He reached down and activated his interface.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' deactivated.]

[Aurum Devastators returning to currency form.]

The twelve massive gold giants did not dissolve back into the mud. They began to shrink, their heavy iron and brass bodies shedding the impurities they had drawn from the courtyard. The gold liquefied once more, refining itself in the rain before hardening back into neat, circular gold coins that clattered onto the clean concrete steps of the manor.

Ren Qi walked down the steps, his boots crunching on the gravel, and began to methodically pick up the coins, dropping them back into his canvas bag one by one.

He didn't leave a single one in the dirt.

Mina descended from the roof, her rifle slung over her shoulder. She looked at the pile of discarded weapons and armor scattered across the courtyard. What do we do with the scrap? she asked.

Store it in the western warehouse, Ren Qi said, his fingers locking around the last gold coin. Varrick can list the raw metal on the Floor 2 salvage boards. The market for copper and iron is currently rising due to the Alliance’s manufacturing halt. We should be able to realize a fifteen percent margin on the liquidation.

And the Alliance? Varrick asked, standing up shakily from the balcony and looking down at him. Vance was right about one thing, Boss. They control the transit gates between the floors. If they lock down the elevators, we can't move our goods.

They cannot lock down the elevators, Ren Qi said, his voice dropping into that quiet, chilling register that always made the air around him feel ten degrees colder. To run the cargo elevators, they require thirty units of high-grade light energy per cycle. They have less than forty-eight hrs of reserves left in their central vaults.

He stood in the center of the ruined gate, looking up through the grey rain toward the high, dark ceiling of the transit hub where the massive cables of the elevators disappeared into the upper floors.

By tomorrow morning, Ren Qi said, they will not even have enough power to keep the security doors on their own offices closed. They will not be locking us in. They will be trying to keep us out.

He turned back toward the main office, his dark flannel shirt clinging to his shoulders, his face an unreadable sheet of stone.

Clean the rifles. Prepare the recruits for a tier transition. We are moving our administrative operations to Floor 3 by the end of the week.

Understood, Mina said, her lips pulling into a thin, grim smile.

Ren Qi walked back into the office, the heavy steel door sliding shut behind him with a wet, hydraulic hiss that sounded like the final line of an audit being drawn.

The system was waiting for his next input.

And the rent was officially overdue.
