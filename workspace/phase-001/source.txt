Chapter 1: The Zero Hour

The sodium content in the Spicy Beef flavor cup noodles was three hundred percent of the recommended daily intake. Ren Qi drank the broth anyway. It tasted like hot salt and regret, thick with the oily sheen of preservatives that would outlast the civilization that invented them.

He sat on the balcony of his twenty-fourth-floor apartment, legs dangling through the gaps in the railing. Below him, the city of Shenzhen was a sprawling grid of frantic light. Above him, the sky was counting down.

The numbers were etched into the atmosphere itself, burning with a color that didn't exist on the visible spectrum—a violet so deep it made the back of Ren’s eyes ache. They hovered over the skyscrapers, over the stalled traffic on the G4 expressway, over the smog.

"Load-bearing capacity exceeded," Ren muttered, tilting the Styrofoam cup to catch the last dregs of dehydrated carrot.

He wasn't talking about the balcony. As a structural engineer, he knew the cantilevered concrete slab beneath him could hold two tons of dead weight. He was talking about the people.

Down on the streets, the screaming had started an hour ago. It wasn't the unified roar of a stadium; it was a discordant, jagged noise. The sound of a million individual panicked animals realizing the cage door was about to open. Looting had begun in the convenience store across the street. Ren watched a man smash a plate-glass window with a fire extinguisher, not to steal, but just to break something.

Ren understood the impulse. When the framework is compromised, you want to control the collapse.

His phone buzzed on the metal table. Another emergency alert. The government had stopped using words like "calm" and "order." The last message had simply been a string of coordinates for bunkers that didn't exist.

Ren didn't move. He had deadlines. The blueprints for the Jinmao expansion were due Monday. He wondered, with a detached curiosity, if the apocalypse would count as force majeure in his employment contract. Probably not.

He swirled the remaining slurry in his cup. The heat seeped through the white foam, warming his palm. It was the only real thing left. The air smelled of ozone and burning rubber. The wind had died completely, leaving the city in a vacuum of stillness that amplified the chaos below.

The text in the sky shifted. The numbers stopped pulsing and solidified, hard and bright as neon branding.

[THE 3000TH DESCENT OF MYSTERY]

[INTEGRATION IMMINENT]

Ren took another sip. The spice hit the back of his throat. He coughed, a wet, hacking sound that felt small against the enormity of the sky.

"Three thousand times," he said to the empty air. "You'd think they’d have the bugs worked out by now."

He wasn't afraid. Fear required an investment in the future, a belief that there was something worth losing. Ren had a negative net worth, a career built on calculating stress fractures, and an empty apartment. If the universe wanted to foreclose, let it.

The city lights flickered. Not a power outage—physics itself stuttering. The streetlamps bent, their light pooling like spilled liquid. The noise from the street cut off, severed by a knife.

The world didn't end with a bang. There was no mushroom cloud, no angelic trumpets.

Instead, the sky broke.

It happened directly above the financial district. The air fractured like tempered glass struck by a hammer. Spiderwebs of absolute blackness shot across the horizon, swallowing the stars, swallowing the countdown. The sound was the worst part—a high-pitched grinding, like steel screaming against bone, vibrating in the marrow of Ren’s teeth.

Ren floated two inches off his chair. The noodle cup drifted from his hand, rotating slowly, drops of broth suspending in the air like amber beads.

Then, reality snapped back.

He slammed into the metal seat. The cup hit the floor, bouncing with a hollow thwack.

[SYSTEM INITIALIZATION COMPLETE]

The voice wasn't a sound. It was a thought forced into his frontal lobe, cold and invasive. It tasted like copper.

Blue translucent panels erupted in Ren’s vision. They overlaid the burning city, the smoke, the chaos. It was an interface. Gamification. A cosmic joke played on a species that spent too much time looking at screens.

[Class: Assigning...]

[Talent Awakening: Initiating...]

Ren squeezed his eyes shut. He rubbed his temples, trying to push the interface away, but the text burned behind his eyelids.

"Opt out," he rasped. "Where is the unsubscribe button?"

[Synchronization fluctuation detected in Sector 86.]

[Subject Ren Qi temporal signature unstable.]

A jolt of agony spiked through his skull. It felt as if someone had driven a railroad spike through his left eye. Ren gasped, doubling over, his hands gripping the cold metal of the balcony railing until his knuckles turned white. The blue text glitching in his vision turned red, then static, then a void-like black.

The universe seemed to pause around him. For a microsecond, Ren saw the code beneath the concrete. He saw the mathematical probability of the railing rusting, the fatigue limit of his own heart muscle, the binary stream of the wind.

He was a variable that didn't fit the equation. A rounding error in the grand calculation.

[Compensating for existence failure...]

The pain vanished as quickly as it arrived, leaving him breathless and sweating in the humid night air. The red static cleared, replaced by a deep, shimmering gold that looked heavy, expensive.

[Unique Anomaly Detected.]

[Talent Assigned: Inequivalent Exchange]

Ren stared at the words. He wiped a trickle of blood from his nose with the back of his hand.

"SSS?" he whispered. "Sounds like a grading curve for overachievers."

He looked over the railing. The world had changed. It wasn't just the interface. Down on the street, the man who had smashed the window was screaming, clutching his hands as they glowed with erratic fire. A woman was running across the hoods of stalled cars with unnatural speed. Shadowy shapes, lanky and disjointed, were peeling themselves off the darkness of the alleyways, hunting.

Monsters. Magic. The whole sordid fantasy suite.

Ren sank back into his chair. His heart hammered a frantic rhythm against his ribs, betraying his outward calm. He wasn't a hero. He wasn't a protagonist. He was a guy who checked if the cement had enough aggregate.

He looked at the floor. The Styrofoam cup lay on its side. A pathetic tragedy of spilled broth and dehydrated corn.

A new prompt hovered over the trash.

[Target: Empty Cup of Instant Noodles (Waste)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren blinked. The golden text pulsed, inviting him.

"Exchange?" Ren asked. "Exchange for what?"

[Talent: Inequivalent Exchange.]

[Description: The Law of Equivalent Exchange is a lie told to keep the poor destitute. You do not trade value for value. You trade origin for potential. The inputs are irrelevant; the output is absolute.]

It sounded like a Ponzi scheme.

Ren reached down and picked up the cup. It was light, flimsy. The plastic fork rattled inside.

[Exchange this item?]

[Cost: One (1) Unit of Garbage.]

Ren laughed. It was a dry, cracking sound. "Sure. Take it. It’s all I’ve got."

He focused on the prompt and mentally clicked Yes.

The sensation was visceral. The cup in his hand didn't vanish; it unraveled. He felt the matter disassemble, the carbon and hydrogen bonds snapping with a sound like tearing paper. The space where the cup had been twisted, folding in on itself, creating a pocket of dense, heavy air.

Then, the light flared.

A heavy porcelain bowl dropped into his lap. It was hot.

Ren yelped, scrambling back. The bowl was exquisite—blue and white ceramic, rimmed with gold, etched with dragons that seemed to writhe in the steam rising from within. But it wasn't the bowl that paralyzed him.

Rich, slow-cooked bone broth. Fresh scallions. The iron tang of premium beef. It smelled like a memory of better times, of a childhood before the grey grind of the city took over.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: The Endless Cornucopia of Sustenance (Rank A)]

[Attributes: This vessel contains a spatial anomaly connected to the Concept of Gluttony. It will refill endlessly with the user's desired sustenance. The food provides minor healing properties and alleviates mental fatigue.]

Ren stared. His hands trembled as he picked up the heavy ceramic spoon that had appeared with the bowl.

Flavor exploded across his tongue. It wasn't just food; it was vitality. The fatigue that had clung to his bones for years evaporated like mist in sunlight. The headache from the glitch smoothed out into clarity.

He looked at the golden spoon. Then he looked at the railing of his balcony.

"Trash," he muttered. "I traded trash."

He stood up, the heavy bowl in one hand. He walked to the edge of the balcony. The city was burning. Screams tore through the night. A creature the size of a bus was tearing the facade off the bank building down the block.

Ren looked around his apartment. He saw the cracked plastic flower pot in the corner. The pile of unpaid utility bills on the counter. The broken toaster he’d been meaning to fix for three years.

He saw a kingdom of resources.

A low, guttural roar shook the sliding glass door. Ren turned.

Climbing over the railing of the adjacent balcony was a thing that looked like a man turned inside out. Muscles exposed, wet and glistening, teeth too many and too sharp. It hissed, its eyes fixing on the bowl of noodles, then on Ren.

[Creature: Flesh Scavenger (Level 1)]

Ren didn't scream. He didn't run. He looked at the monster, then he looked at the half-empty bottle of cheap cleaning alcohol sitting by the plant pot.

The text hovered over the bottle.

[Target: Isopropyl Alcohol (Diluted)]

[Exchange Available.]

The monster lunged, claws scrabbling on the concrete divider.

Ren grabbed the bottle. His eyes were cold, calculating. The structural integrity of the world had failed, but he had just been handed the editing tools.

"Let's see what you're worth," Ren whispered.

[Exchange Initiated.]

The bottle dissolved in a flash of gold.

The air pressure in the room dropped. The smell of antiseptic vanished, replaced by the scorching heat of a collapsed star.

In Ren's hand, a heavy, jagged crystal appeared. It pulsed with a violent, violet light, humming with enough energy to power the block for a year.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Star-Core Ember (Rank S)]

[Attributes: Volatile. Highly explosive. Handle with care.]

The Flesh Scavenger shrieked, sensing the sudden spike in thermal energy. It tried to scramble back, its survival instinct overriding its hunger.

Ren weighed the crystal in his hand. It was warm, like a living heart.

He tossed the crystal gently. It arc’d through the air, a lazy violet streak, landing at the creature's feet.

Ren stepped back inside and slid the glass door shut. He sat down on his sofa, picked up the ceramic bowl, and took another spoonful of broth.

Outside, the balcony vanished in a silent, blinding sphere of purple fire.

The shockwave rattled the glass, but the door held. The "Level 1" notification in his vision shattered into experience points.

Ren Qi chewed the tender beef. He swallowed.

"Not bad," he said to the empty room. "Not bad at all."

He looked at the pile of junk mail on the coffee table.
