# Chapter 57: War Council

The transition between the third floor and the damp, low-pressure basement of Skyview Garden on Floor 1 did not happen in a flash of divine light. It was a slow, greasy folding of space that tasted of zinc and copper pennies. One moment Ren Qi was standing on the high brass terrace of the Babel Hub, his eyes tracking the smoking trajectory of Commander Thorne’s retreating platform; the next, his boots sank into the fine, grey dust of his personal sanctuary.

The air here was different. It lacked the clinical, filtered dryness of the upper levels. It smelled of old concrete, stagnant puddle water from the outer courtyard, and the faint, sulfurous tang of his own automated defense grid.

Ren Qi stood still for a moment, letting his equilibrium settle. His left sleeve was gone, the fabric burned away to a stiff, blackened cuff by Thorne’s concentrated solar beam. The Cuirass of the Iron Eater clung to his chest, its rusted scales still radiating a dry, uncomfortable heat that made his skin itch. He reached down, unbuckling the side straps to let the metal settle, then walked toward the heavy steel security door leading to the main office.

The door slid open with a wet, hydraulic hiss.

Mina was there first. She didn't offer a salute or a word of greeting. She was sitting on a wooden crate near the armory rack, a long-barreled rifle balanced across her knees. Her thumb was methodically running over the brass casing of a cartridge, cleaning away a speck of corrosion that only she could see. When she looked up, her eyes lingered on the scorched portion of his chest.

You look like you fell into a furnace, Landlord, Mina said. Her voice was like gravel sliding down a chute.

Ren Qi didn't stop. I survived a minor structural inefficiency. Where is Varrick?

In the back, Mina replied, tilting her chin toward the inner office. He’s been staring at the market boards for five hrs. He says the price of copper wire is shifting, and it’s making his teeth hurt.

Ren Qi entered the inner office. The room was small, lit by the flickering, green glow of three separate trading terminals he had salvaged from the lower levels of the district. The walls were lined with metal shelves holding ledger books, heavy iron locks, and boxes of dried rations.

Varrick was hunched over the main console, his fingers tapping a rhythmic, frantic beat against the laminate desk. A small cup of synthetic tea sat cold and untouched near his elbow. In the dark corner of the room, barely visible against the peeling wallpaper, stood Kael. The assassin was so still he might have been a painted shadow, his dark flannel shirt and trousers absorbing what little light the screens cast.

A low, static-filled hum came from the short-wave receiver on the shelf.

Lin Xia’s voice was thin, distorted by the atmospheric interference between the first floor and her station at the Earth-side lobby. She sounded tired, the kind of exhaustion that came from tracking five different survivor manifests while trying to keep her knuckles from shaking.

I am here, Ren Qi said, pulling a clean, dark flannel shirt from a metal locker and sliding his arms through the sleeves. He didn't button it yet, leaving his chest exposed to the cool draft of the office. Report.

The lobby is stable, Lin Xia said, her voice crackling over the wire. We had three minor incursions from the lower-tier scavengers near the northern fence, but Mina’s automated turrets kept them at a distance. But... Landlord, the trading terminal is acting strange. The transaction logs are updating so fast the screen is starting to warp.

Varrick stood up, his joints popping. He ignored the radio, his eyes locked on Ren Qi’s face. It’s not just her terminal, Boss. The entire market on the lower three floors is sliding. The Golden Sun Alliance just put out a restricted trade notice. They’re locking down the supply lines on Floor 3. They’re calling it a security audit.

They’re calling it an audit because they don't want to admit they’ve lost control of the central spires, Ren Qi said. He sat down behind his desk, pulling his personal ledger from his vest pocket. The heavy paper gave a dry scrape as he opened it to a fresh page. He picked up his pen, his movements slow, deliberate, and entirely devoid of the urgency that was vibrating through Varrick’s small frame.

Commander Thorne attempted to liquidate our assets on Floor 3, Ren Qi continued, his tone flat. He failed. The Alliance is currently operating at a loss, but they have deep reserves. If we allow them to consolidate their forces, the next reclamation team they send will be larger, better equipped, and far less inclined to negotiate.

So we dig in? Mina asked, leaning against the doorframe, her rifle still held loosely in her right hand. We have enough iron and water to hold the garden for six months. If they try to come down the stairs, I can turn the lobby into a meat grinder.

Ren Qi shook his head. Defense is a passive depreciation of capital. Every day we spend behind these walls is a day our operational costs tick upward without a corresponding increase in value. We are not going to wait for them to assemble their resources.

Varrick squinted, his brow furrowing. Then what? We can’t storm the third floor with ten F-Rank recruits and a silent killer, Boss. No offense, Kael.

The shadow in the corner did not move, but the faint, cold gleam of the Phantom Blade’s hilt seemed to catch the green terminal light.

We don't need to storm their towers, Ren Qi said. He tapped the flat end of his pen against the desk. The Golden Sun Alliance is a bloated asset. They rely on high-frequency mana-conductive filaments to maintain their entire infrastructure. Their hard-light barriers, their high-speed platforms, their personal defense suits—everything they use to project power is calibrated to a single, specific energy frequency.

Light Crystals, Varrick muttered, his eyes widening.

Exactly, Ren Qi said. The Alliance doesn't mine them. They import them from the neutral houses on Floor 2 and the deep quarries of Floor 4. They buy them in bulk, distribute them to their enforcers, and use them as fuel. Without those crystals, Thorne’s armor is nothing but fifty kilograms of gold-plated tin.

Varrick shook his head, a dry, cynical laugh escaping his throat. That’s a swily plan, Boss. Sure, if we could stop the supply, they’d go dark. But those crystals are traded globally on the hub’s neutral exchanges. There are over forty thousand high-grade units sitting on the boards right now. To buy them all up and freeze the market... you’re talking millions of gold coins. We have some savings, yes, but we don't have that kind of liquidity.

The system is a closed loop, Ren Qi said, his voice dropping into that quiet, chilling register that always made Varrick’s spine stiffen. It operates on the assumption that every transaction must be equal. It believes that to acquire a high-value asset, one must surrender an equal measure of wealth.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, dull grey metal object. It was a rusted iron bolt, salvaged from the wreckage of a broken water pipe in the Skyview basement. It was worthless. It didn't even have enough integrity to hold a nut.

He placed the bolt on the center of the desk.

The system is wrong, Ren Qi murmured.

He closed his eyes, focusing his mind on the interface that existed only within his own consciousness. His SSS-Rank talent, Inequivalent Exchange, did not require a complex ritual. It required only an input, an output, and a profound disregard for the laws of conservation.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]

[INPUT: 1x Rusted Iron Bolt. VALUE: Negligible.]

[OUTPUT: 100,000x High-Grade Mana Shards. VALUE: Extreme.]

The air in the office grew suddenly cold. The green light from the terminals flickered, dying down to a dull, sputtering amber before roaring back with a high-pitched hum that made the glass of Ren Qi's empty tea cup ring. On the desk, the rusted bolt did not dissolve. It simply ceased to be there, replaced instantly by a mountain of small, hexagonal violet crystals that spilled over the edges of the wood, clattering onto the concrete floor like heavy rain.

The room smelled of ozone and wet stone.

Varrick fell back against his chair, his hands grabbing the armrests so hard his knuckles turned white. What... what is that?

Ren Qi did not answer him. He reached out, his hand passing over the violet heap, and opened the global trading interface on the main console. His fingers moved with a rhythmic, mechanical precision, navigating the complex sub-menus of the Babel Hub’s central exchange.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: Trade Request Registered. Offer: 100,000x High-Grade Mana Shards. Demand: Global Currency Units.]

[Transaction successful. Account balance updated: 5,200,000 Gold Units.]

Varrick’s jaw went slack. The numbers on the terminal screen were changing so fast they looked like a solid green line. The digit at the far left kept ticking upward, five, then five point two, then five point five.

That’s... that’s not possible, Varrick whispered. The system doesn't allow that kind of margin. You can't just... you can't just change the numbers.

I didn't change the numbers, Ren Qi said, his face an unreadable sheet of stone. I simply renegotiated the terms of the transaction. The system accepted the value of the shards. The market does not care where they came from; it only cares that they exist.

He leaned forward, his eyes reflecting the green light of the screen. Now, Varrick. Open the purchase orders for the Light Crystals. Every single one.

Every single one, Ren Qi repeated. His voice was soft, but it carried the absolute weight of an eviction notice. Do not leave a single unit on the boards. If a merchant has ten crystals listed for fifty gold, buy them. If a guild has five thousand listed for a premium, buy them. I want the floating supply of the entire hub cleared within the next thirty minutes.

Varrick didn't argue this time. The sheer, absurd reality of the gold balance on the screen had burned away his caution. His fingers flew across the keyboard, his face slick with sweat as he began executing the buy orders.

The trading board of the Babel Hub was a massive, decentralized ledger that connected thousands of survivors, guilds, and merchants across all ninety-nine floors. For three thousand years, it had remained relatively stable, governed by the cold, impartial logic of the system.

In the span of five minutes, that logic began to fracture.

On Floor 2, in the humid, sulfurous markets of the acid sea, a merchant looked at his terminal and saw his entire stock of seventy Light Crystals vanish. He blinked, thinking it was a synchronization error, but the gold units were already in his account, clean and spendable.

On Floor 3, in the gleaming spires of the Golden Sun Alliance’s main logistics hub, the automated monitoring systems began to flash yellow.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: Warning. High-Volume Purchase Detected. Item: Light Crystal (High-Grade). Origin: Unknown Account.]

[Current market supply: 82% depleted.]

[Current market supply: 94% depleted.]

Ren Qi watched the green candles on the market chart. They weren't rising; they were leaping, straight vertical lines that looked like green spears driving through the ceiling of the graph. The average price of a single Light Crystal started at forty-five gold units. Within ten minutes, as the supply dried to nothing, the automated pricing algorithms of the exchange tried to compensate for the sudden, extreme scarcity.

It’s escalating, Varrick gasped, his shirt collar damp. The neutral guilds on Floor 4 are trying to cash in. They’re putting their emergency reserves on the market at five hundred gold a piece. They think they’re being clever.

Buy them, Ren Qi said.

Boss, we’ve already spent three million—

Buy them, Ren Qi repeated.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SSS-Rank Talent 'Inequivalent Exchange' activated.]

[INPUT: 1x Old Copper Key. VALUE: Negligible.]

[OUTPUT: 200,000x High-Grade Mana Shards.]

The office floor was now ankle-deep in violet crystals, the dry, electric hum of their presence making the hair on Mina’s arms stand straight up. She took a step back, her back hitting the wall, her eyes wide as she watched Ren Qi dump another massive block of wealth into the exchange.

The gold balance reset, rising back to five million.

Varrick let out a high-pitched, hysterical laugh. He didn't look at Ren Qi. He couldn't. He kept his eyes locked on the screen, clicking the buy buttons with a manic, rhythmic speed. He was no longer a financial manager; he was a machine, a tool executing a systematic liquidation of an entire resource class.

[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: High-Grade Light Crystals remaining on global exchange: 0.]

[Market state: Illiquid. Price shift detected: +1,140% in last 18 minutes.]

The silence that followed the final transaction was heavier than the space folding of the teleportation pad.

On the monitors, the chart for Light Crystals had flatlined at the very top of the screen, a single, horizontal red line that indicated there was nothing left to buy at any price. The current listed value was over eight hundred gold units per crystal, but the quantity column was a solid row of zeros.

Varrick slowly let his hands drop from the keyboard. His breathing was shallow, his shoulders trembling. We... we did it. We have forty-two thousand crystals in our private vault. Every single one that was available for public trade is ours.

Lin Xia’s voice came through the radio, so loud and clear it sounded as though she were standing in the room. Landlord... the lobby terminal just received a priority transmission from the Floor 3 merchant guild. They’re asking if we have a regional representative. They say the local power grids in the mid-tier districts are reporting zero reserve capacity. They’re offering to buy whatever we have at three hundred percent of the yesterday's rate.

Tell them we are currently conducting an internal audit, Ren Qi said. He leaned back in his chair, his fingers interlaced over his chest. Our inventory is not for sale.

Mina stepped into the light of the terminal, her expression shifting from confusion to a cold, predatory satisfaction. The Alliance uses those crystals for their defensive barriers on Floor 3, don't they?

Not just their barriers, Ren Qi said, his voice dry. Their patrol boats, their communication arrays, the heating systems in their residential quarters. The Alliance has approximately forty-eight hrs of reserve crystals in their central vaults. After that, their defensive grids will begin to drop, one sector at a time, to preserve the life support systems of their command spire.

They’re going to be blind, Mina said.

They’re going to be bankrupt, Ren Qi corrected. They cannot afford to buy them back at eight hundred gold a piece, even if we were willing to sell. Their treasury is large, but it is not infinite. To maintain their current operational status under these prices, they would have to liquidate their primary assets within a week.

He turned his head slightly, his gaze falling on the dark corner where Kael stood.

The shadow moved, a fraction of an inch, the silence of his presence more absolute than any sound.

The Alliance will attempt to source crystals directly from the mines on Floor 4, Ren Qi said. They will send armed transport caravans through the southern transit shafts. They cannot afford to use their high-speed platforms because of the fuel shortage. They will have to use the old, mechanical rail carts.

Kael nodded once. The movement was simple, but it carried the weight of a death warrant.

Take three recruits, Ren Qi instructed. Establish a checkpoint at the Floor 3 junction. The Alliance will be desperate. They will be running light security to maximize their cargo space. Do not allow a single cart to pass. If they resist, liquidate the assets.

Understood, Kael’s voice was a low, dry rasp, the first words he had spoken since Ren Qi’s return. He did not wait for further instructions. He simply stepped back into the deeper shadows of the office, his form dissolving into the dark until the corner was empty.

Ren Qi looked back at Varrick. The financial manager was still staring at the screen, his hand resting on the mouse as if he were afraid that if he let go, the gold balance would vanish like a dream.

Varrick, Ren Qi said.

Monitor the secondary energy markets. The Alliance will try to substitute Light Crystals with low-grade fire shards or heavy oil. The moment they start buying, use our capital to acquire those assets as well. I want their operating costs to double every twelve hrs.

It’s going to destroy them, Varrick said, his voice a mix of awe and terror. They won't even have the power to run their security doors. They’ll be trapped in their own gold towers.

A proper renovation requires the removal of the old foundation, Ren Qi said. He picked up his pen and began to write in his ledger, his hand moving with a fluid, steady grace. The ink was dark, drying quickly on the thick paper.

Audit Phase 49: Alliance logistics paralyzed. Resource choke established. Phase 2 execution in progress.

Outside, the wind from the ruins of Floor 1 howled against the reinforced concrete walls of Skyview Garden, but inside the office, the only sound was the scratching of the pen and the faint, dry hum of forty thousand violet crystals glowing on the floor.

The rent was due, and the calculation had just begun.
