# Chapter 46: Information Extraction

The air in the maintenance bay was heavy, thick with the smell of scorched hydraulic fluid and the ozone tang that lingered after any significant magical discharge. Kael, the man who had only moments ago been a predator in the shadows, now sat on a stack of rusted crates. He looked small. The bravado that had fueled his intrusion into the manor was gone, replaced by a dull, hollow confusion as he stared at his own calloused palms.

Ren Qi stood a few paces away, his hands buried deep in the pockets of his vest. He didn't look like a master of men. He looked like an accountant who had accidentally wandered into a battlefield and found the casualties to be poorly organized.

You are an expensive mistake, Ren Qi said, his voice dropping into the quiet, dry tone of a man reviewing a ledger entry that refused to balance. You have a skill set designed for infiltration, yet you lack the foresight to realize when your target is better equipped than your employer.

Kael looked up, his eyes narrowing. He was an F-Rank, a bottom-feeder who had built a career on the margins, but even he could recognize the weight of the man in front of him.

The Guild Master of Silver Wing, Kael rasped, his voice sounding like gravel grinding against metal. He doesn't pay for mistakes. If I go back there without your head, I am as good as dead.

Ren Qi walked to a nearby workbench where a collection of scrap sat waiting for repurposing. He picked up the dagger Kael had used—a chipped, utilitarian blade—and turned it over in the dim light. It was a pathetic thing, dull and prone to snapping under any significant pressure.

The Guild Master is a sentimentalist, Ren Qi remarked, his fingers tracing the microscopic fissures in the steel. He believes that by controlling the shadows of this district, he owns the district. He doesn't understand that shadows are merely a lack of light. They have no substance of their own. They are the easiest thing in the world to displace.

Ren Qi tapped the terminal on the workbench. He didn't look at the screen, his focus entirely on the transformation of the metal in his hands.

[System Notification: Material Analysis Complete. Item: Common Dagger. Essence Requirement: Shadow-Touched Shard. Synthesis Initiated: Phantom Blade.]

A faint, violet hum filled the room. The metal of the dagger began to liquefy, not in the traditional sense, but by folding into itself, the geometry shifting until the blade became a translucent sliver of obsidian-edged light. It wasn't just sharper; it was conceptually heavier, anchored to a different spatial coordinate.

Ren Qi held it out, handle first.

I have no interest in your life, Kael, he said. I have an interest in your efficiency. You have a grudge against Silver Wing. Use this to settle it. Bring me the Guild Master’s badge, and we will discuss the terms of your future employment.

Kael hesitated. He stared at the blade, the air around it shimmering with a low-frequency vibration that seemed to whisper of secrets long forgotten. He reached out, his fingers trembling as they closed around the handle. The moment he made contact, his eyes widened, the violet light reflecting in his pupils.

This is... he started, but his voice trailed off.

It is a tool, Ren Qi corrected. Don't mistake utility for sentiment. Bring me the badge, or keep the blade and run. But know this: I know the architecture of this city better than you know your own skin. There is nowhere in the Babel Hub you can go that I cannot reach.

Kael stood up. He didn't say another word. He simply nodded, a short, sharp movement, and stepped into the nearest corner. Instead of walking, he dissolved, flickering out of existence like a bad connection on a monitor.

Ren Qi watched him go, the emptiness of the bay now absolute. He turned back to his desk. He hadn't been lying about the architecture. The entire district was a system, a giant, leaking pipe that he was slowly plugging and rerouting to suit his own design.

He sat down, his ledger spread open before him. The ink was dark, the handwriting precise.

Audit Phase 18: Security breach neutralized and repurposed. Asset Kael deployed for secondary extraction.

He tapped the screen. A map of the Babel Hub flickered to life, showing the glowing, congested arteries of the city. He could see the clusters of wealth in the upper levels, the desperate, swirling activity of the middle, and the stagnation of the base where he stood.

He began to input a new sequence, the stylus clicking rhythmically against the glass.

The alchemists were quiet, which meant they were either planning a larger response or realizing that their influence was slipping through their fingers like sand. Ren Qi preferred the latter, though he was prepared for the former. He had the spectral servants, he had the manor, and he had the infrastructure to manufacture items that made the local market look like a collection of children’s toys.

He picked up a small, heavy coin and rolled it over his knuckles. The sound of the metal was a constant reminder of the physical reality he was dissecting.

He had spent his life in a world of deadlines and structural integrity tests, where the failure of a single bolt could bring down an entire skyscraper. The Babel Hub was no different. It was a building constructed on rotten foundations, and he was the inspector who had finally decided to mark it for demolition.

He stood up and walked to the window. The city lights were a chaotic, blinking expanse that looked less like a home for millions and more like a circuit board in the middle of a short. He could see the silhouette of the Silver Wing guild hall in the distance, a looming structure of glass and polished steel that housed the ego of a man who thought he was a king.

King of a district that was currently being bled dry of its resources, Ren Qi thought.

He turned away from the window. The room felt cold, the air stagnant despite the ventilation systems that hummed incessantly in the walls. He needed to upgrade the manor's power source, something that could sustain the spectral servants at a higher output. The core he had stripped from the assassin’s vial had been a start, but it was insufficient for the scale of what he had planned.

He sat back at his desk, his fingers flying across the terminal.

[System Notification: Energy Optimization Required. Target: Mana Leaking Junctions. Status: Estimating Potential Yield.]

The map shifted, highlighting several key intersections in the district where the energy flow was chaotic and unrefined. If he could tap into those lines, he wouldn't just be managing his own small shop; he would be managing the flow of the entire district.

The door to the study creaked open. Lin Xia walked in, her face pale, her hands still clutching her apron. She looked at him with a mixture of fear and, for the first time, a flicker of genuine curiosity.

Mr. Ren? she whispered. The merchants from the corner... they’re asking if we’re going to be open tomorrow. They seem... worried.

Ren Qi didn't look up from his ledger.

Tell them the store will be open, he said, his voice flat. Tell them we are expanding our inventory to include more specialized items. And tell them to ignore the commotion at the Silver Wing hall tonight. It’s a standard maintenance procedure.

Lin Xia paused, her hand lingering on the doorframe. Standard maintenance? she repeated, her voice hesitant. But the Guild Master... people say he’s a beast in a fight.

Ren Qi finally looked up, his gaze steady and utterly devoid of concern.

Beasts don't understand the structural weaknesses of their own cages, he replied. They just pace, and they roar, and eventually, they get tired. By then, the bars have usually been cut.

Lin Xia nodded, though she clearly didn't understand the implications of his words. She left the room, the heavy iron door clicking shut behind her.

Ren Qi stood and walked to the wall, pressing his palm against the cold stone. He could feel the vibration of the tower, a deep, rhythmic thrum that told him exactly how much stress the structure was under. It was a fascinating sound, the groan of a giant machine that had forgotten how to function.

He pulled a small, delicate gear from his pocket, a relic he had salvaged from the swamp tier weeks ago. He set it on the desk and began to manipulate it with his bare hands, his fingers dancing across the metal as he applied the logic of his talent.

[System Notification: Input: Ancient Gear Mechanism. Output: Core Stabilizer Node.]

The gear expanded, the teeth shifting and locking into a new, intricate configuration. It glowed with a faint, pale light that pulsed in time with his own heartbeat.

He stood up, his posture relaxed, his eyes fixed on the darkness outside. He was not just surviving the descent; he was rewriting the code of the world, one line item at a time.

He closed his eyes for a moment, listening to the city. He could hear the distant, frantic shouts of guards in the distance, the sound of a panic that hadn't yet reached the lower levels. It was a beautiful, chaotic sound, like the tearing of paper.

He had the house. He had the staff. He had the ledger.

The audit was just beginning, and he was the only one prepared to settle the bill. He leaned back, his eyes closed, his mind already drifting upward, toward the next floor, toward the next, more complex, more corrupt, and more beautiful challenge of his life.

The 3000th Descent was finally, truly beginning. And he was the one who was going to write the final entry in the book.

He opened his eyes, his gaze fixed on the darkness above, his mind clear, his heart steady.

And he was the only one collecting.

He looked out over the city, the sprawl of the lower districts stretching out before him like a map of missed opportunities. He could see the faint, flickering glow of the portals that led to the upper floors, each one a promise of wealth, status, and power.

He didn't want their power. He wanted to own the foundation they were built on.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out the small, metallic coin, flipping it over his knuckles. He caught it, the sound of the metal meeting his skin sounding like a final, decisive note.

The audit was just beginning, and he was the only one prepared to settle the bill. He turned away from the window, his expression cold and unyielding.

The audit of the 3000th Descent was far from over. And he had a foundation to maintain.
