Chapter 2: A Transaction of Variance

The broth tasted like time.

It wasn't just the sodium or the fat; it was the depth of it, a richness that coated the tongue and seeped into the gums. Ren Qi sat on the floor of his living room, his back against the sofa, holding the blue-and-white porcelain bowl with both hands.

Outside, the world was screaming.

The explosion on the balcony had done its job. The Flesh Scavenger was gone. In its place, a semicircle of the concrete floor slab had simply ceased to exist, bitten off by the sphere of violet annihilation. The rebar protruded from the jagged edge like severed nerves, glowing a dull, angry cherry-red.

The sliding glass door was a spiderweb of fractures. It held, barely. Through the crazed glass, Ren could see the fires burning in the district below, casting long, erratic shadows that danced across his ceiling.

He took another sip. The warmth hit his stomach and radiated outward, chasing away the cold sweat of shock. The "Endless Cornucopia of Sustenance" lived up to its name. As he swallowed, the level of the liquid in the bowl rose imperceptibly, maintaining a constant volume.

It was impossible. It violated thermodynamics. Energy created from nothing.

Ren set the bowl down on the coffee table. He looked at his hands. They were steady.

"Inequivalent Exchange," he said. The words felt heavy in the quiet room.

He pulled up the system interface. It flickered into existence, a translucent overlay that didn't care about the physics of light.

[Level: 2 (EXP: 45/200)]

[Status: Satiated, Calm]

He had leveled up from killing the Scavenger. He didn't feel stronger, not in the way a superhero movie would depict. His muscles didn't bulge; his vision didn't zoom. But the fatigue that had been his constant companion for a decade—the engineer’s burnout, the sleep debt, the caffeine withdrawal—was gone. He felt efficient.

He looked at the notification log.

[Kill Confirmed: Flesh Scavenger (Lvl 1)]

[Damage Dealt: 99,999 (Overkill)]

[Note: Resource expenditure was disproportionate to the threat.]

"Disproportionate," Ren muttered. He picked up a piece of jagged glass from the floor. "I threw a sun at a cockroach."

The talent wasn't a store. It was a refinery. He put raw material in, and the system returned a finished product that shared the concept of the input but ignored the value of the input.

Trash became sustenance.

Diluted alcohol became a star core.

The logic was thematic. It was poetic. It was also terrifyingly broken.

Ren stood up and walked to the kitchen. The linoleum was cool under his socks. He opened the "junk drawer"—the universal feature of every apartment, the purgatory for things too useful to throw away and too useless to keep.

He pulled the drawer out completely and dumped the contents onto the living room carpet.

The pile was a sad archaeology of his life.

Three dead AA batteries.

A ball of twine, stiff with age.

A promotional laser pointer from a construction expo in 2019.

A handful of rubber bands, brittle and cracking.

A rusted box cutter with a snapped tip.

A plastic lighter, empty.

Ren sat cross-legged in front of the pile. He felt like a primitive shaman casting bones, looking for a sign in the debris of a dead civilization.

"Let's test the parameters," he whispered.

He reached for the lighter. It was a cheap, translucent blue plastic thing. He flicked the wheel. Sparks showered, but no flame. The fuel was gone.

[Target: Depleted Butane Lighter]

[Exchange Available.]

[Cost: One (1) Fire Source (Empty).]

The prompt hovered, pulsing with that expensive gold light.

Ren focused. Exchange.

The lighter didn't dissolve into dust. It twisted. The plastic groaned, a sound like ice cracking on a frozen lake. The blue casing darkened, turning into a matte, light-absorbing charcoal. The metal hood expanded, shifting from cheap tin to a heavy, brushed gunmetal.

The air pressure in the room dipped for a second, stealing the breath from Ren’s lungs.

In his hand sat a heavy, rectangular device. It felt cold and dense, like a brick of solid lead.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: The Prometheus Zippo (Rank A)]

[Attributes: Produces a flame that cannot be extinguished by wind, water, or vacuum. The flame burns the target's oxygen supply directly from their blood. Fuel source: Infinite.]

Ren stared at the object. He flipped the lid. Clink. The sound was crisp, satisfying. He struck the wheel.

A tongue of white fire erupted. It stood perfectly straight, ignoring the draft coming from the shattered balcony door.

Ren blew on it. Hard. The flame didn't flicker. It didn't lean. It just burned, silent and white.

He snapped the lid shut. Clunk.

"Survival is solved," Ren said. His voice was flat.

He had food. He had fire. With the Star-Core Ember—assuming he could find more alcohol—he had artillery.

Most people down there in the dark were fighting for their lives. They were looking for a can of beans or a sharp stick. Ren had bypassed the bottom three layers of Maslow’s Hierarchy in twenty minutes.

But survival wasn't the goal. Survival was just the baseline. The Scavenger on the balcony had been Level 1. The sky had said this was the "3000th Descent." That implied iteration. It implied escalation.

Eventually, something would come along that could eat a Star-Core Ember.

He needed personal power. He needed a way to apply force that didn't involve blowing up his own apartment building.

Ren turned back to the pile on the carpet.

He picked up the laser pointer. It was a keychain toy, the kind used to annoy cats. He pressed the button. A weak, red dot appeared on the wall.

[Target: Low-Output Diode Pointer]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren hesitated. A laser was light. Focused light. If he exchanged this, what would he get? A lightsaber? A blind-ray?

He initiated the exchange.

The plastic keychain grew hot. The red paint peeled away, revealing a sleek, silver cylinder. The cheap chain fused into the body, forming a loop of liquid metal.

[You have obtained: The Eye of the Surveyor (Rank B)]

[Attributes: Optical enhancement tool. Allows the user to see structural weaknesses, stress points, and health bars. Range: 500 meters.]

Ren blinked. He brought the cylinder to his right eye. The glass lens at the end was cool against his skin.

The walls of his apartment became wireframes. He saw the load path of the ceiling joists. He saw the micro-fractures in the concrete caused by the earlier explosion. He looked at the shattered glass door.

[Structural Integrity: 4%]

[Material: Tempered Glass (Compromised)]

He looked down at the pile of junk.

[Object: Rusted Box Cutter]

[Utility: Severing (Low)]

Ren lowered the scope. Information. He had information now. As an engineer, he valued this more than the fire. You can't fix a problem you can't see.

He reached for the box cutter.

It was ugly. The orange plastic handle was stained with paint and grease. The blade was extended, brown with oxidation, the tip snapped off years ago when he’d tried to pry open a paint can.

It was a tool of separation. It was designed to divide one thing into two things.

[Target: Rusted Box Cutter (Damaged)]

[Exchange Available.]

This time, the reaction was different.

There was no flash of light. There was no drop in temperature.

The screaming from the street, the hum of the refrigerator, the distant sirens—it all cut out. A radius of absolute silence expanded from Ren’s hand.

The box cutter didn't twist or melt. It began to vibrate.

It wasn't a physical shake. It was a molecular agitation. Ren felt his teeth ache. The atoms in the orange plastic handle were being excited, reorganized, aligned. The rust on the blade flaked off, not falling to the ground, but vaporizing into red mist.

The steel blade turned grey. Then dark grey. Then a black so profound it looked like a hole in the universe.

The silence broke with a sound like a tuning fork struck by a god. A pure, high-frequency hum that resonated in the marrow of Ren’s bones.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Molecular Vibration Blade (Rank S)]

[Attributes: High-frequency oscillation tool. The edge vibrates at 40,000 hertz. It does not cut; it separates atomic bonds. Friction is negligible. Durability: Indestructible.]

The weapon in his hand retained the shape of a box cutter, but it had been perfected. The handle was now a grip of matte-black composite that molded to his palm. The blade was a void.

Ren stood up. He felt a strange compulsion, a need to test the truth of the text.

He walked to the metal railing of the balcony—the part that hadn't been vaporized. It was solid steel, painted black, two inches thick.

He held the cutter in a reverse grip.

He didn't swing. He didn't hack. He simply pushed the blade forward.

There was no resistance.

It felt like moving his hand through water. The black blade passed through the steel railing as if the metal wasn't there.

Ren watched as the top half of the railing slid slowly, agonizingly, to the left. The cut surface was mirror-smooth, glowing faintly with residual heat. The piece fell, tumbling twenty-four stories down into the dark.

He didn't hear it hit the ground.

Ren looked at the weapon. It was terrifying. It was a tool that rendered armor, cover, and durability irrelevant.

"I'm not an engineer anymore," he whispered. "I'm a demolitionist."

He retracted the blade. The humming stopped. The oppressive dread in the room lifted.

A thud against his front door made him spin around.

It wasn't a knock. It was the heavy, wet impact of a body being thrown against the wood.

Ren didn't panic. He raised the Surveyor scope to his eye.

Through the wood of the door, he saw the wireframe.

[Target: Human (Deceased)]

A corpse. Someone had thrown a body against his door.

Behind the corpse, standing in the hallway, was a shape.

It was humanoid, but wrong. The proportions were stretched. The arms hung down to the knees. The muscle fibers were highlighted in angry red through the Surveyor’s lens.

[Target: Feral Ghoul (Level 3)]

[Weakness: Sever the brain stem.]

Ren lowered the scope. He slid the black blade of the box cutter out. The hum returned, a comforting song of destruction.

He walked to the door.

He didn't check the peephole. He didn't ask who was there.

The ghoul slammed against the door again. The wood splintered around the lock. The hinges groaned, metal screaming against metal.

Ren stood two feet from the door. He visualized the blueprints of the hallway. He calculated the position of the creature based on the impact point.

He raised the cutter.

"Knock knock," Ren said.

He thrust the blade forward.

It went through the solid oak door. It went through the steel deadbolt. It went through the damp air of the hallway.

He felt a momentary, slight drag—like cutting through wet clay—as the blade passed through the ghoul's skull.

He withdrew the blade.

The scratching on the other side stopped. There was a heavy, wet slump.

[Kill Confirmed: Feral Ghoul (Level 3)]

Ren stood in the silence of his apartment. He looked at the tiny, clean slit in the door. A single beam of light from the hallway shone through it, illuminating dust motes dancing in the air.

He went back to the coffee table. He picked up the noodle bowl and took a long, slow drink of the broth. It was still hot.

He looked at the rest of the junk pile. The ball of twine. The rubber bands.

He smiled, but it didn't reach his eyes.

"Okay," he said. "What else can we make?"

He picked up the twine.

The world had ended, yes. The grid was down. The law was gone. But Ren Qi had just discovered that he was the only one holding the edit keys to reality.

He wasn't trapped in here with the monsters.

He began to knot the twine, his fingers moving with the precise, practiced rhythm of a man who knew exactly how much tension the line could hold before it snapped.

[Target: Ball of Hemp Twine (Old)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren closed his hand around it.
