Chapter 11: The First Intruder

The first rule of property management is simple: protect the asset.

Ren Qi sat in the ergonomic mesh chair of the B1 control room, the ghost of a dead man’s shift still lingering in the smell of stale grease and ozone. On the wall of monitors, the world outside was a swirling gray soup of fog and nightmares. Inside, however, the lights were humming.

Electricity was a beacon. In the dark ages, a lighthouse warned ships away from the rocks. In the apocalypse, a lighthouse told the sharks where the dinner was served.

Ren took a sip of water. It was crisp, filtered through the Purification Spirit. He watched Screen 4.

It was 2:00 AM, but the lobby was bright. The Abyssal Kiosk sat in the center of the marble floor like a monolith of black glass and commerce, its internal neon glowing with a soft, predatory hum.

Lin Xia was on shift. She sat on a high stool behind the reception desk, her back rigid. The fat businessman, Chen, was asleep on a pile of coats near the elevator bank, clutching a rusted iron pipe like a teddy bear.

Ren checked the ledger.

[Current Treasury: 462 Cores]

Business was good. The survivors on the 4th floor had learned the rhythm: kill, harvest, pay, eat. They were looking less like frightened cattle and more like lean, desperate laborers.

Then, the motion sensor on the exterior camera tripped.

Ren’s eyes snapped to Screen 1.

The front doors of Skyview Garden were heavy tempered glass, reinforced with steel bars. Beyond them, shapes moved in the mist. Not the shambling, jerky gait of Ghouls. Not the skittering of arachnids.

These shapes moved with purpose. They moved with swagger.

Ren leaned forward. The density of his new skeletal structure made the chair groan under him. He toggled the Specter-Sight filter on the camera feed.

Five heat signatures. Bright orange against the blue-black of the fog.

One of them was burning hotter than the others. Much hotter.

Ren didn't feel fear. Fear was a biological response to the unknown. This wasn't unknown. This was market competition.

He watched as the leader of the group—a man wearing a heavy firefighter’s coat and a motorcycle helmet—stepped up to the glass doors. He didn't knock. He didn't look for a handle.

He placed a gloved hand against the lock mechanism.

On the thermal feed, the hand flared white.

The steel of the lock didn't just melt; it vaporized. A jet of superheated plasma erupted from the man’s palm, blowing the deadbolt out the back of the frame in a shower of molten sparks.

[Target Identified: Player]

[Talent Detected: Pyrokinesis (Rank B)]

The doors swung open.

The fog rolled into the lobby, and with it, the five men.

Ren stood up. He adjusted the Cuirass of the Iron Eater over his chest. He checked the Molecular Blade at his hip.

He didn't rush. He moved with the heavy, hydraulic grace of a machine. He walked to the service elevator and pressed the button for the Ground Floor.

He wasn't going down to fight. He was going down to collect.

The air in the lobby changed the moment the doors breached. The sterile, refrigerated chill of the Spider Queen’s lingering frost was instantly polluted by the smell of sulfur and unwashed bodies.

Lin Xia was on her feet before the glass stopped vibrating. She held her kitchen knife with both hands, the point wavering.

"Stop," she said. Her voice was thin, swallowed by the cavernous ceiling. "This is private property."

The intruders stopped. They looked around, eyes adjusting to the electric light. They looked at the marble floors. They looked at the heating vents pumping warmth. They looked at Lin Xia.

The leader took off his helmet. He was handsome in a ruined sort of way—half his face was covered in a burn scar that pulled his lip into a permanent sneer. He had ash in his hair.

He laughed. It was a wet, hacking sound.

"Private property," he repeated, looking at his crew. They carried axes, machetes, and a stop sign sharpened into a spear. "Did you hear that, boys? The lady thinks the laws still apply."

He stepped closer. The air around him shimmered. He was a walking furnace.

"We saw the lights from Block A," the leader said. "We thought, 'Who has electricity when the world is ending?' We thought maybe the army was here."

He looked at Chen, who had scrambled up and was backing away, wetting his pants. He looked back at Lin Xia.

"But it's just you. And him."

"Get out," Lin Xia said. She took a step back, putting the reception desk between them. "The Landlord... he doesn't like guests."

"Landlord?" The leader snorted. He walked past her, ignoring the knife like it was a toothpick.

His eyes locked onto the black monolith in the center of the room.

The neon text scrolled across the glass face: FEED ME.

"What is this?" The leader circled the machine. He ran a hand along the matte black metal. "Vending machine? A vault?"

"It's the store," Chen blurted out, his voice trembling. "You pay cores. It gives food."

The leader paused. He looked at Chen. "It gives food?"

"Yes. Beef noodles. Water. Weapons."

The leader smiled. The scar tissue on his face stretched white. "Well, that's convenient. I'm starving."

He turned to the machine. He didn't check his pockets for currency. He didn't look for a coin slot.

"Open up," he growled.

His hand ignited. It wasn't a flicker; it was a flamethrower. A cone of roaring orange fire blasted from his knuckles, engulfing the front of the Kiosk.

Lin Xia screamed. "No! Don't touch it!"

The Abyssal Kiosk was Rank A. It was durable. The black metal absorbed the heat, glowing faintly red but holding its shape. The anti-theft system triggered.

A blue arc of electricity snapped out from the chassis, aiming for the attacker.

But the Pyromancer was fast. Or maybe he was just insulated. He realized the danger and flared his aura, a blast of heat pushing him backward. The lightning grounded itself harmlessly into the marble floor, scorching a black mark.

"It bites," the leader hissed. He shook his hand. He was angry now.

He wasn't trying to loot it anymore. He wanted to break it.

"Boys," he shouted. "Smash the glass."

Two of the thugs stepped forward with fire axes. They swung with the synchronized rhythm of men who had spent the last four days breaking down apartment doors.

The reinforced glass of the display panel starred.

Spiderwebs of white fractures spread across the face of the machine.

Thousands of shards exploded outward, raining onto the marble floor. The vacuum seal broke with a hiss. The grey mist inside the machine swirled out, dissipating into nothingness. The neon sign flickered and died.

The machine was dead.

The leader kicked a pile of glass aside. He reached into the broken maw of the machine, expecting to find rows of candy bars or crates of supplies.

His hand grasped empty air.

"It's empty," he whispered. He turned to Lin Xia, his face twisting into a mask of pure rage. "Where is the food? Where is the stock?"

"It... it makes it," Lin Xia stammered. "It exchanges..."

"Bullshit!" The leader roared. He grabbed the side of the heavy machine and shoved. With a screech of tearing metal, the heavy kiosk tipped over, slamming into the floor.

He turned to his men.

"Find the stockpile," he ordered. "Tear this place apart. If they hide it, burn them."

The men cheered. They raised their weapons.

The sound was soft, polite. It cut through the shouting like a razor.

Everyone froze. They turned toward the elevator bank.

The service elevator doors slid open.

He wasn't wearing the tactical gear of a soldier. He was wearing jeans, a dark flannel shirt, and a vest made of overlapping, rusted iron scales that looked like they had been ripped from a prehistoric fish.

He didn't have a weapon drawn. His hands were in his pockets.

He walked into the lobby. His boots hit the marble with a heavy, solid thud that you could feel in your teeth. Thud. Thud.

He stopped ten meters away from the group. He looked at the overturned machine. He looked at the shattered glass spread across the floor like diamonds.

He didn't look at the men.

"You broke the interface," Ren said. His voice was calm. It was the voice of an accountant noting a discrepancy in a spreadsheet.

The leader laughed. He stepped forward, wreathed in smoke. "And who are you? The janitor?"

Ren finally looked at him. The Eye of the Void behind his contact lenses stripped the man down to his biology. Muscle density: Average. Adrenaline: High. Core temperature: 104 degrees.

"I am the Landlord," Ren said.

"Well, Mr. Landlord," the leader sneered, sparking a flame in his palm. "I'm the eviction notice. This is my building now. My lights. My food."

He pointed a burning finger at Ren.

"Kneel, and I might let you work for me."

Ren sighed. He took a hand out of his pocket. He checked his watch.

"You have damaged facility property," Ren said. "The replacement cost for a Rank A spatial anchor is substantial."

Ren took a step forward.

"Don't move!" one of the thugs shouted, raising a spear.

Ren ignored him. He walked toward the pile of broken glass.

The leader narrowed his eyes. "Torch him."

He threw a fireball. It was a sloppy, baseball-sized sphere of plasma. It flew across the room, aiming for Ren’s face.

Ren didn't dodge. He just tilted his head.

The fire splashed against his shoulder.

The Cuirass of the Iron Eater absorbed the kinetic impact. The heat washed over him. His skin, reinforced by the Titan’s blood, felt warm, like opening an oven door, but it didn't burn. The flannel shirt singed, revealing the pale, dense muscle underneath.

Ren brushed the ash off his shoulder.

He reached the spot where the glass covered the floor.

"You shouldn't have broken the glass," Ren said softly.

The thugs hesitated. Why was he kneeling? Was he surrendering?

Ren placed his palm flat against the carpet of shattered safety glass. Thousands of jagged cubes, glittering in the electric light.

[Target: Broken Reinforced Glass (Quantity: 4,000 shards)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren didn't want the glass back. He didn't want a window.

He focused his mana. The headache spiked, a sharp needle behind his eyes.

The magic rippled out from his hand. It was invisible to the naked eye, a distortion in the air.

The glass shards didn't melt. They didn't disappear. They sharpened.

The chaotic, random shapes of the broken safety glass reorganized. The edges honed themselves to a molecular razor. The material turned transparent, refracting light so perfectly that they became effectively invisible against the polished marble.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Field of Agony (Rank D)]

[Attributes: 4,000 Invisible Caltrops. Induces 'Crippled' status. Neurotoxic coating causes amplified pain reception.]

Ren stood up. He stepped back.

The floor between him and the intruders looked empty. Just polished stone.

"Kill him!" the leader screamed, realizing something was wrong. "Rush him!"

The four thugs charged. They raised their axes and spears, screaming war cries that they had learned from movies.

They took three steps.

The first thug, the one with the spear, planted his left foot.

It wasn't a loud sound. It was the wet pop of a boot sole being punctured, followed by the grinding of bone.

The scream was immediate and high-pitched. The thug dropped his spear. He clutched his foot, hopping on the other leg.

He landed on the other foot.

He fell. His hands hit the floor.

The invisible shards shredded his palms. He rolled, screaming, his body finding new razors with every movement.

The other three men tried to stop. But momentum is a cruel mistress. They slid on the slick marble.

They slid into the zone.

It was a meat grinder.

The man with the axe fell onto his knees. The glass bit through his jeans, through the patella, into the marrow. He howled, a sound that wasn't human. It was the sound of a nervous system being overloaded.

Within five seconds, four men were writhing on the floor, bleeding from a dozen points, unable to stand, unable to crawl. The neurotoxin worked fast. Every heartbeat was a hammer of agony.

Only the leader remained standing. He had stopped just at the edge of the invisible field. His face was pale, the scar standing out like a brand.

He looked at his men. He looked at the empty floor.

"What did you do?" he whispered. The fire in his hands flickered and died.

Ren walked around the perimeter of the field. He moved toward the reception desk.

He picked up the microphone for the building’s PA system. He pressed the button. The feedback whine echoed through the lobby, sharp and loud.

"Testing," Ren said. His voice boomed from the ceiling speakers, god-like and distorted.

He looked at the Pyromancer.

"The exchange rate has been updated," Ren said into the mic.

He pointed at the screaming men.

"For the damage to the machine, I am confiscating their lives."

Ren turned the Molecular Blade on. The hum was low, menacing.

"For you," Ren said, staring directly at the leader. "The price is different."

The leader backed up. He hit the glass doors. "You're crazy. You're a psycho."

"I am a businessman," Ren corrected.

"I'll burn this whole place down!" The leader held up both hands, gathering a massive ball of fire. He was panicking. He was going to detonate his entire mana reserve.

"Lin Xia," Ren said, his voice amplified by the speakers.

Lin Xia jumped. She was huddled behind the desk, trembling.

"How much is a Level 2 Pyrokinesis core worth?"

Lin Xia looked at the leader. She saw the fear in his eyes. She saw the sweat. She looked at Ren, standing calm and heavy in his iron vest.

"Fifty credits?" she guessed.

He didn't activate a skill. He just kicked off the floor. The Titan’s Resilience exploded into motion. The marble beneath his boot cracked.

He was a blur. A three-hundred-pound artillery shell.

The leader threw the fireball.

Ren swatted it aside with his armored forearm. The fire washed over him, singing his eyebrows, heating the iron scales to a dull red.

He closed the distance in a heartbeat.

The leader tried to scream. He tried to run.

Ren’s hand shot out. He grabbed the man by the throat.

The grip was absolute. It was hydraulic. The leader’s windpipe compressed. His feet lifted off the floor.

Ren held him up, one-handed, like a doll.

"Your talent is inefficient," Ren said, his face inches from the man’s panic-stricken eyes. "You use fire to destroy resources. I use resources to create order."

There was a sickening crunch.

The leader went limp. The fire in his veins died out.

Ren dropped the body. It hit the floor with a wet slap.

Silence returned to the lobby, broken only by the whimpering of the men in the glass field.

Ren looked at his hand. He wiped the soot on his pants.

He turned to Lin Xia.

"Clean this up," Ren said. "Strip the bodies. Weapons, clothes, boots. Anything usable goes into the inventory."

"And... and the prisoners?" Lin Xia asked, pointing to the men bleeding on the invisible glass.

Ren walked over to the overturned Kiosk. He placed his hand on the dented metal.

"There are no prisoners," Ren said. "Only raw materials."

He looked at the broken machine. He needed to fix it. He needed glass. He needed circuits.

He looked at the bodies. Biological mass. Carbon. Iron.
