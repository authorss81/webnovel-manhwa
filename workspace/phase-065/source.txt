Chapter 5: The Neighbor's Bargain

The dust from the breached wall settled on Ren Qi’s shoulders like dandruff.

He stood in the center of what used to be Unit 2404, waiting for the structural groan of the building to subside. It didn't. The skyscraper was a living thing now, its steel bones vibrating with the collective trauma of three thousand residents and the things hunting them.

Ren stepped back into his own apartment—Unit 2403. He dragged the heavy mahogany cabinet he’d looted from Mr. Zhang’s place across the floor, positioning it to block the jagged hole he’d carved. It wasn't a perfect seal, but it broke the sightline.

He checked the balcony.

The Automated Sentry Turret Seed he had buried in the dead philodendron pot had germinated. It didn't look like a plant. It looked like a tumor made of chrome and carbon fiber. A stalk of matte-black metal had pushed up through the dry soil, thickening as it rose. At the top, a bulbous sensor cluster swiveled with a faint, oily whir, tracking the swirling fog beyond the glass. A short, stubby barrel extended from the "flower," currently retracted in sleep mode.

Solar recharge. It would be hungry until morning.

Ren turned away. He picked up the blue-and-white porcelain bowl. He ate a mouthful of noodles, chewing rhythmically. The texture was perfect—firm, alkaline, comforting. It was the only constant in a reality that had gone fluid.

The sound didn't come from the hallway. It didn't come from the balcony.

It was a weak, frantic percussion coming from the west wall. Unit 2402.

Ren swallowed the noodles. He set the bowl down on the coffee table, next to the stolen Moutai and the glowing Molecular Blade.

He moved to the wall. He didn't speak. He didn't knock back.

He raised the Specter-Sight Scope to his eye.

The drywall dissolved into a wash of grayscale static. The structural studs appeared as dark vertical bars. Beyond them, in the bedroom of 2402, the thermal imaging bloomed.

A heat signature. Human.

It was huddled on the floor, pressed right up against the baseboard. The colors were wrong. A healthy human heat map is a uniform gradient of oranges and yellows. This one was blotchy. The left leg was a cold blue—circulation cut off. The torso was a feverish white-hot.

Infection. Or massive trauma.

The figure raised a fist and struck the wall again. It was a feeble blow, barely vibrating the plaster.

"I know you're there," a voice said.

It was muffled by the concrete and drywall, but the acoustics of the building carried it. A woman’s voice. Wet, strained.

Ren recognized it. Lin Xia.

He’d seen her in the elevator perhaps a dozen times in two years. She was a petite woman who wore oversized hoodies and always smelled of vanilla vape smoke. She worked IT for a logistics firm in Nanshan. They had never exchanged more than a polite nod.

"Ren Qi," she rasped. "Please."

Ren lowered the scope. He looked at the blank white wall.

She knew his name. That wasn't surprising; packages got left in the hallway. She knew he was home. Also not surprising; the noise of him remodeling 2404 with a vibrating void-blade hadn't exactly been subtle.

He debated the variables.

Risk: Infection vector. Betrayal. Noise discipline violation.

"Go away," Ren said. He spoke clearly, close to the wall, but he didn't raise his voice.

"I'm hurt," Lin Xia said. A cough rattled in her chest, sounding like dry leaves skittering on pavement. "My leg... the glass from the balcony door. It went deep."

"Apply pressure," Ren said. "Elevate it."

"I did. It's... it's turning black. The fog touched it."

Ren took a step back. Necrosis. The ambient corruption in the fog wasn't just atmospheric; it was microbial. If she was infected, she was already a ticking clock.

"I can't help you," Ren said.

"I have water," she whispered. "I have two bottles of Nongfu Spring. Sealed. I’ll give them to you."

Ren looked at the sink in Mr. Zhang’s kitchen, where he had just manufactured a miracle cure from sewage. He looked at the three bottles of fifty-year-old Moutai.

"I have water," Ren said.

Silence stretched between the apartments. He could hear her ragged breathing through the wall. It was the sound of hope breaking.

"I have information," she said. Her voice changed. The desperation hardened into something sharp. "I have a Talent."

Ren paused. He gripped the handle of the Molecular Blade.

"Not through the wall," she said. "I can't... I can't shout. I'm dizzy."

Ren scanned the wall again with the scope. He traced the ductwork. The bathroom ventilation shafts connected the two units.

He walked to his bathroom. It was tiled in generic beige ceramic. The vent was high up on the wall, a rusty metal grate clogged with years of gray lint.

He stepped onto the toilet lid to reach it. He didn't unscrew it. He placed the tip of the Molecular Blade against the metal slats.

The steel grate vanished, dusting the floor with iron filings.

He peered into the dark, square tunnel. It smelled of mildew and, faintly, vanilla.

"I'm listening," Ren said into the duct.

A shuffle of movement on the other side. A pale face appeared in the gloom of the shaft, illuminated by the light of a phone screen she was holding up.

It was Lin Xia. Her skin was the color of old parchment. Her lips were cracked and blue. Sweat plastered her bangs to her forehead.

"You look like hell," Ren said.

"You look..." She squinted, her eyes trying to focus in the dark. "You look clean."

"The Talent," Ren prompted.

"Assessment," she coughed. "Rank A. I can... I can see the data. Not just names. Stats. Weaknesses. Drop rates."

Eye of the Surveyor gave him structural data. Specter-Sight gave him thermal and soul imaging. But neither gave him the RPG logic—the numbers that governed this new reality.

"Prove it," Ren said. "What am I?"

She stared at him. Her eyes flashed with a momentary, faint golden ring.

She flinched, recoiling from the vent as if he had slapped her.

"What..." She swallowed dryly. "What are you? The text... it's glitching. It says 'Human,' but the parameters are... volatile. And your inventory... it’s a black hole."

"Good enough," Ren said. "What do you want?"

"Water," she said. "Not for the thirst. I need to wash the wound. The fog... it burns."

"Information for water," Ren said. "That’s the trade."

Ren hopped down from the toilet.

He went to the living room. He found an empty plastic water bottle—half-crushed trash from his pile. He walked to the kitchen.

He filled the bottle with tap water. The sludge had cleared up slightly, but it was still brown and foul.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out the small medicine bottle containing the Purification Spirit.

He needed to calibrate the dose. The pure spirit was too potent; it would be a waste, and it might reveal too much. He needed a dilution that healed but didn't make him look like a messiah.

He took a toothpick from a dispenser on the counter. He dipped the tip into the blue spirit, picking up a droplet no larger than a grain of sand.

He swirled the toothpick into the bottle of brown tap water.

The reaction was instant. The brown silt dissolved. The water turned crystal clear, glowing with a faint, impossible luminescence.

Ren capped the bottle.

He returned to the bathroom. He climbed back onto the toilet.

Lin Xia was still there, her head resting against the lip of the vent tunnel. She looked like she was fading.

He slid the bottle into the duct. The metal was cold. He pushed it until it bumped against her hand.

She grabbed it with frantic, trembling fingers. She didn't wash the wound. She tore the cap off with her teeth and guzzled half of it in one breath.

The effect was visceral.

The gray pallor of her skin flushed pink. The blue tinge left her lips. Her eyes, which had been glassy and unfocused, snapped wide open.

She gasped, dropping the bottle. It didn't spill; she caught it against her chest.

She looked at her leg—out of Ren’s sight—and let out a strangled sob.

"It stopped," she whispered. "The black lines... they're receding."

She looked up at Ren. The fear in her eyes had shifted. It wasn't the fear of dying anymore. It was the fear of being in the presence of something she didn't understand.

"What was that?" she asked. "That wasn't water."

"It was the price," Ren said. "Now pay yours."

Lin Xia wiped her mouth with the back of her hand. She nodded, slowly. She was terrified of him now. Good. Fear was a reliable contract.

"The lobby," she said, her voice stronger. "I was down there when it started. Before I ran up the stairs."

"The revolving doors... they melted. Fused together. Something pushed through the floor tiles. It looked like a cocoon. Made of... wet concrete and bone."

Ren leaned closer to the vent. "A spawn point."

"It hatched ten minutes ago," Lin Xia said. "I watched it through the floorboards of the second-floor gym. I used my Assessment."

She fumbled with her phone. "I can't send you the system file unless we're in a party. But I wrote it down."

She held the phone screen up to the vent.

Ren pulled out his own phone and snapped a picture of her screen.

[Target: The Concrete Arachnid (Boss - Regional)]

[Attributes: High physical defense. Immune to small arms fire. Exoskeleton reinforced with rebar.]

[Active Skill: Seismic Tremor - Causes structural instability in a 50m radius.]

[Active Skill: Spun Silk - Sprays quick-hardening cement webbing.]

[Weakness: The sensory cluster on the underbelly. Cold damage.]

Ren stared at the photo.

The Ghouls were Level 3. The Mist Walkers were Level 8.

A Level 10 Boss with 5,000 HP and reinforced concrete armor. And it was in the lobby. The foundation of the building.

"It's going to bring the building down," Ren said softly.

"It's eating people," Lin Xia said. "Everyone who tried to run out the front door... it webbed them. It’s sticking them to the walls of the atrium. Keeping them for later."

She looked at Ren through the grate. "Ren, we have to leave. We have to go to the roof."

"The roof is a dead end," Ren said. "Helicopters aren't coming."

"But we can't kill that thing. It's... it's a tank."

Ren looked at the glowing bottle in her hand. "You have your water. Stay quiet. Block your door. If you hear fighting, don't look."

He stepped down and slid a heavy ceramic tile over the vent hole, blocking her view and her voice.

He walked back to the living room.

He sat on the sofa and looked at the data again.

Weakness: Cold damage.

He didn't have cold damage. He had fire (The Prometheus Zippo). He had vibration (The Blade). He had kinetic force (The Turret).

But he had something else.

He looked at the interface. Inequivalent Exchange.

He looked at the three bottles of Moutai on the table. High-proof alcohol. Flammable. Volatile.

If he exchanged them, he might get a better explosive. A bomb.

But a bomb inside the building, against a creature that caused structural instability? That was suicide. If he blew up the lobby pillars, the tower would collapse, and he’d ride Unit 2403 into the rubble.

He needed surgical precision.

He needed to change the state of matter.

Ren stood up. He walked to the refrigerator. The power was still on, for now. He opened the freezer.

It was empty, save for a tray of old ice cubes and a bag of frozen dumplings.

He took the ice tray. He popped a single cube into his palm.

It was cold, wet, melting. A trivial object.

[Target: Ice Cube (Melting)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren closed his fingers around it.

The cold intensified. It didn't just freeze his skin; it bit into the bone. Frost bloomed rapidly across his knuckles, turning his hand white.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Seed of Absolute Zero (Consumable)]

[Attributes: A crystallized fragment of entropy. Upon impact, it absorbs all thermal energy in a 10-meter radius. Flash freezes matter to -273.15°C.]

Sitting in his palm was not an ice cube. It was a geometric shape, a dodecahedron that seemed to be made of blue glass, but inside, smoke swirled. It didn't melt. The air around it turned into mist as the humidity froze instantly.

He carefully placed it on the coffee table. He wiped his hand on his jeans; his fingers were numb.

One grenade. One shot.

If he missed, the spider would bring the building down.

He looked at the floor. The spider was twenty-four stories down.

He couldn't take the elevator. The stairs would be infested.

Ren walked to the balcony. He looked at the Sentry Turret. It was fully formed now, a silent sentinel watching the fog.

He looked over the railing.

The side of the building was sleek glass and steel. But twenty feet down, there was a maintenance ledge. And below that, the balconies of the lower units staggered outward.

Ren activated Shadow Step.

He felt the energy coil in his legs.

He wasn't going to fight the spider in the lobby. He wasn't going to play by its rules.

He went to the kitchen and grabbed the remaining two bottles of Moutai.

[Target: Bottle of Moutai (53% Alcohol)]

[Exchange Available.]

The bottle shifted. The glass thickened, becoming opaque. The liquid inside sloshed with a heavy, metallic viscosity.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Liquid Gravity Anchor (Rank C)]

[Attributes: When ingested or applied, increases the object's weight by 1000% for 5 minutes.]

Ren frowned. Not a weapon. A utility item.

He looked at the second bottle.

This time, the bottle elongated. The ceramic turned into a fibrous, organic material. The neck of the bottle widened into a funnel.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: The Drunkard’s Scream (Rank C)]

[Attributes: Sonic emitter. deeply amplifies sound waves. Can shatter glass and disorient echolocation.]

The Spider had a weakness: Sensory cluster.

He had a plan. It was stupid. It was dangerous. It relied on physics that barely made sense.

But he was an engineer. And the problem was a structural anomaly in the lobby.

Ren strapped the Prometheus Zippo to his belt. He clipped the Molecular Blade to his pocket. He held the Seed of Absolute Zero in his left hand and The Drunkard’s Scream in his right.

He walked to the front door of his apartment. The one he had welded shut.

He cut it open again.

The hallway was empty. The corpse of the Ghoul he had killed earlier was gone. Dragged away. A trail of black ichor smeared the carpet, leading toward the stairwell.

He didn't go down. He went to the elevator shaft.

He pried the doors open with the Molecular Blade. The shaft was a dark throat, smelling of grease and stale air. The cables hung in the void.

Twenty-four floors of empty space. At the bottom, the lobby.

If Lin Xia was right, the spider was nesting there.

Ren pocketed the items. He grabbed the steel cable. It was greasy, thick as his wrist.

He looked at his hands. "Don't slip."

The descent was a blur of friction and heat. His gloves—leather ones he’d found in Mr. Zhang’s closet—smoked. Floor numbers painted on the inside of the shaft flashed by. 20... 15... 10...

At the 5th floor, the shaft shook.

A low, grinding vibration traveled up the cable.

The sound of metal shearing.

Ren halted, wrapping his legs around the cable. He hung there, swaying in the dark.

Below him, a red light pulsed.

He looked down through the Specter-Sight Scope.

The elevator car was at the bottom of the shaft, crushed like a tin can. But on top of the car, looking up, was a cluster of eyes.

Eight of them. Glowing with a dull, malevolent red.

The Concrete Arachnid wasn't just in the lobby. It had breached the shaft. It was climbing.

Ren saw the legs—thick pillars of rebar and cement, tipped with hooks. It moved with a jerky, stop-motion horror. It was huge, filling the entire shaft.

It hissed. A sound like steam escaping a pipe.

[Target: Concrete Arachnid (Level 10)]

Ren looked up. He was at the 5th floor. Climbing back up to 24 was impossible. He couldn't outpace it.

He looked at the cable he was holding.

He pulled out the Liquid Gravity Anchor. He uncorked it.

He didn't drink it. He poured the heavy, metallic fluid onto the steel elevator cable, just above where he was holding it.

He activated Shadow Step.

He didn't step sideways. He stepped into the wall of the elevator shaft, aiming for the maintenance hatch of the 5th floor.

He burst through the thin metal doors of the hatch, tumbling onto the carpet of the 5th-floor hallway.

Behind him, in the shaft, physics took a sharp turn.

The Liquid Gravity Anchor soaked into the cable. For a five-meter section, the cable’s weight increased by 1000%.

The sudden, massive increase in load was too much for the pulley system at the top of the building.

The sound was like a gunshot.

The main cable, now weighing as much as a freight train, whipped downward.

Gravity did the rest.

The cable fell. It fell straight down the shaft, a whip of solid steel moving at terminal velocity.

The Spider was climbing up. The cable was falling down.

The impact shook the floorboards under Ren’s feet. A screech of tearing metal and shattering concrete echoed up the shaft.

Ren scrambled to his feet. He looked through the open hatch.

The Spider had been knocked down. The heavy cable had smashed into it, breaking its momentum, tangling its legs, driving it back down to the lobby floor.

But it wasn't dead. He could hear it screaming—a high-pitched, chittering wail that made his teeth ache.

"Phase one complete," Ren muttered.

He ran to the stairwell.

He took the stairs three at a time. The air grew colder as he descended. The fog was thicker here, leaking in from the lower levels.

He reached the mezzanine level—the balcony overlooking the grand lobby.

He crouched behind the marble railing.

The lobby was a nightmare.

The floor was gone, replaced by a crater of webs and broken tiles. In the center, the Concrete Arachnid was thrashing. It was the size of a van. Its body was a mosaic of sidewalk slabs and rebar. Two of its legs were bent at wrong angles, crushed by the cable.

But it was rebuilding itself. The concrete on its back was shifting, knitting together.

It was webbed to the walls. And in the webs...

Ren saw them. Dozens of cocoons. Human shapes wrapped in gray silk. Some were still moving.

The Spider hissed again. It rotated. Its underbelly was exposed—a soft, pulsing sack of translucent flesh, glowing with blue bioluminescence. The sensory cluster.

Ren pulled the pin on the Drunkard’s Scream.

It looked like a megaphone made of bone.

He aimed it at the Spider.

"Hey, ugly!" Ren shouted.

The Spider froze. The eight eyes swiveled up to the mezzanine.

Ren squeezed the trigger of the sonic device.

He didn't hear a sound. The frequency was too high for human ears.

But the effect was visible.

The air between Ren and the Spider rippled. The glass railing of the mezzanine shattered instantly into dust.

Below, the Spider convulsed. The sound waves hit its sensory cluster. The blue sack vibrated violently. The creature shrieked, its legs buckling. It was disoriented, its echolocation shattered, its equilibrium gone.

It collapsed onto its back, legs flailing in the air.

Ren stood up. He held the Seed of Absolute Zero in his hand.

He calculated the arc. Distance: fifteen meters. Target size: two meters. Windage: negligible.

"Cool off," Ren said.

He threw the dodecahedron.

It spun through the air, glinting in the emergency lights.

It hit the pulsing blue underbelly of the Spider.

There was no explosion. No fire.

There was a sound like a sudden intake of breath. Whump.

The world turned white.

Frost raced outward from the impact point, moving faster than the eye could follow.

The blue sack froze instantly. The ice spread to the concrete legs, to the rebar mandibles, to the floor, to the webs.

The thrashing stopped.

The Spider froze in mid-spasm. It turned into a statue of diamond and gray stone. The air in the lobby dropped fifty degrees in a second. Ren’s breath turned to steam.

The creature’s health bar, visible through the Specter-Sight Scope, didn't drop. It simply turned gray.

[Status: Frozen (Absolute)]

Ren didn't wait. He vaulted over the railing, dropping down to the frozen lobby floor.

He landed in a crouch. The cold burned his face.

He walked up to the frozen monster. It was majestic in a horrific way. A monument to the end of the world.

He placed his hand on the frozen leg.

[Target: Concrete Arachnid (Frozen)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren smiled. His lips cracked from the cold.

"Inequivalent Exchange."

The massive, frozen statue didn't melt. It didn't shatter.

It collapsed inward. Five tons of monster, encoded with the data of a Level 10 Boss, swirled into a vortex of gold light.

The light condensed. It became heavy, dense, real.

A single object dropped into Ren’s hand.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: The Architect’s Blueprint (Rank S)]

[Attributes: Territory Construction Tool. Allows the user to redesign the structural layout of any building they claim ownership of. Material costs are waived for basic structures.]

Ren stared at the blueprint. It wasn't a weapon. It was better.

He looked around the frozen lobby. At the shattered doors. At the people trapped in the webs—still alive, their cocoons brittle from the cold but intact.

He looked up at the tower that stretched twenty-four floors above him.

"My building," Ren whispered.

He unrolled the blueprint.

The text hovered in the air.

[Claim Territory: Skyview Garden, Block B?]

[Territory Established.]

[Current Integrity: 64%]

[Threats Detected: 0 (Lobby Cleared)]

Ren looked at the front doors.

"Seal them," he commanded.

The floor tiles of the lobby flowed like water. They rose up, filling the gap where the revolving doors had been. They knit together, forming a solid, seamless wall of marble and stone.

The wind stopped howling. The fog was shut out.

Ren stood in the silence of his new fortress.

He was tired. He was cold. He was hungry.

He walked to the nearest web cocoon. He used the Molecular Blade to slice the silk.

A middle-aged man fell out, shivering, blue-lipped, but breathing.

Ren didn't help him up. He just looked at him.

"Rent is due on the first of the month," Ren said.

He turned and walked toward the stairs. He had a lot of remodeling to do.
