Chapter 4: Digital feudalism

The wall shared with Unit 2404 died quietly.

There was no dust cloud, no crumbling masonry. The Molecular Vibration Blade didn't smash the concrete; it convinced the atoms to stop holding hands. Ren Qi carved a rectangular section the size of a door frame, then pushed. The two-ton slab of reinforced concrete fell into Mr. Zhang’s living room with a heavy, muffled thud that shook the floorboards beneath Ren’s feet.

Ren stepped through the breach.

The air in 2404 was stale, smelling of old paper and expensive tobacco. Mr. Zhang was a retired university dean with a pension that outpaced his lifespan and a collection of Moutai liquor that Ren had envied for three years.

Ren scanned the room with the Specter-Sight Scope. No heat signatures. No ghosts. Just the static, cold shapes of furniture.

He walked to the mahogany cabinet in the corner. It was locked. Ren sliced the lock off like he was trimming a fingernail.

Inside, the bottles glinted—white ceramic, red ribbons. Fifty-year-old Moutai. 53% alcohol by volume. In the old world, this cabinet was worth a down payment on a Tesla. Now, it was an ammunition depot.

Ren took three bottles. He didn't have pockets big enough, so he placed them gently on Mr. Zhang’s dining table.

He needed water. The endless noodle bowl provided broth, which was hydrating, but you couldn't brush your teeth with beef fat. You couldn't wash the grime of the apocalypse off your skin with soup.

Ren went to the kitchen. It was cleaner than his, clinical in its orderliness. He gripped the chrome lever of the faucet and pulled up.

The pipes groaned. It wasn't the usual hydraulic hiss of city plumbing. It was a guttural, wet sound, like a large animal clearing its throat.

Spattering out of the aerator came a thick, viscous sludge.

It was black, streaked with oily iridescence. It hit the stainless steel sink and didn't splash; it piled up, oozing slowly toward the drain like molasses. The smell hit Ren a second later—sulfur, rot, and something metallic, like old blood.

Ren watched it flow. He didn't gag. The Mental Fortress in his head categorized the smell as "unpleasant" and "hazardous" but deleted the nausea reflex before it could reach his stomach.

"Infrastructure failure," Ren noted.

The water treatment plants were down. Or, more likely, the corruption that had birthed the Mist Walkers had seeped into the groundwater. The reservoir was poisoned.

Ren looked around the kitchen. He grabbed a plastic bucket from under the sink—bright red, used for mopping. He placed it under the tap and let the black sludge fill it halfway.

He turned off the tap. The silence returned.

Ren stared at the bucket of filth. It was death in liquid form. If anyone downstairs drank this, their organs would shut down before they finished swallowing.

[Target: Contaminated Sludge (1 Gallon)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren rested his hand on the rim of the bucket. The plastic was cool.

The reaction was immediate. The sludge didn't boil. It hissed, shrinking rapidly. The volume collapsed. The black color deepened, pulling inward, condensing the impurities, the toxins, the biological waste into a single point of absolute rejection.

The bucket rattled against the porcelain sink.

A small glass vial materialized at the bottom of the red bucket. It was shaped like a teardrop, corked with a stopper made of something that looked like compressed salt. inside, a clear, pale-blue liquid swirled with its own internal current.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Vial of Purification Spirit (Rank B)]

[Attributes: Essence of Clarity. One drop will neutralize all toxins, pathogens, and curses in 100 liters of liquid. Grants the consumer 'Minor Regeneration' and cures dysentery.]

Ren picked up the vial. It was cold, condensation forming instantly on the glass.

He looked at the sink, still coated in the residue of the sludge. He pulled the cork—a soft pop—and tilted the vial, letting a single microscopic droplet fall onto the stain.

The reaction was violent. The black residue hissed and turned white, then evaporated into odorless steam. The stainless steel underneath gleamed as if it had just been polished.

"Leverage," he whispered.

He had food. He had ammo. Now, he had the monopoly on clean water.

A chime rang out. It wasn't a sound in the room; it was a notification hammered directly into his auditory nerve.

[SYSTEM UPDATE COMPLETE.]

[REGIONAL CHANNEL OPEN: SHENZHEN SECTOR.]

[WORLD CHANNEL OPEN.]

A new translucent panel unfolded in the upper left corner of his vision. It was transparent, overlaid on reality, but the text scrolling across it was solid neon blue.

It moved so fast it was a blur.

User9821: WHERE IS THE POLICE?

IronBull: My skin is burning help me help me

SnakeEater: Don't go outside! The shadows bite!

User111: I just saw my mom eat my dog. What is happening?

GoldenDragon: COORDINATES 22.543, 114.057. WE HAVE GUNS. WE NEED DOCTORS.

VoidWalker: System is real. Grind levels or die, trash.

Ren sat down on Mr. Zhang’s kitchen stool. He watched the feed. The Mental Fortress kept him detached, an anthropologist observing a colony of ants that had just had their hill kicked over.

The sheer volume of the text was dizzying. Millions of people, terrified, confused, screaming into the digital void.

Ren mentally filtered the feed to [Regional: Shenzhen].

The scroll slowed down, but the panic remained.

FlowerGirl: I’m trapped in the metro at Convention Center. The water is rising. It smells like sulfur.

BossChen: Anyone near Nanshan? I have cash. 100k RMB for a ride out of the city.

HunterZero: @BossChen Money is toilet paper now, idiot. Do you have food?

Ren’s eyes tracked the patterns. It had been less than an hour since the integration, and already the social contract was dissolving. The polite fiction of civilization—that all men are created equal, that laws protect the weak—was evaporating faster than the sludge in the sink.

Then, the bold text started appearing.

[ANNOUNCEMENT - User: THUNDER_KING]

[Level 4 - Talent: A-Rank Lightning Manipulation]

"Listen up. I've cleared the Vanguard Supermarket in Futian. This is my territory now. If you want shelter, you pay tax. Food, water, or women. If you have a Combat Talent, come work for me. If you’re a leech, stay away or I’ll fry you."

Ren tapped his finger on the counter.

Feudalism. It took humanity five thousand years to invent democracy and forty-five minutes to revert to warlords.

Other messages flared up, challenging the Thunder King or declaring their own little fiefdoms.

RedViper: Futian is huge, Thunder Boy. Watch your back.

SteelWall: Forming a defensive group at the Children's Palace. Only Strength types allowed. No freeloaders.

The hierarchy was solidifying before his eyes.

Top Tier: Combat Talents (Elements, Strength, Speed). They were the new nobility. They killed monsters, leveled up fast, and claimed territory.

Mid Tier: Support Talents (Healing, Crafting). Valuable assets. They would be kidnapped, enslaved, or protected, depending on the "Lord."

Bottom Tier: The Talentless or Non-Combatants (people with "Gardening" or "Accounting" skills). They were currency. Meat shields.

Ren looked at his own status.

[Talent: Inequivalent Exchange (SSS)]

He didn't fit the chart. He wasn't a tank. He wasn't a healer.

He looked at the vial of Purification Spirit. Then he looked at the chat again.

User440: I'm so thirsty. The tap water is black slime. Is it safe?

Dr.Li: DO NOT DRINK THE SLUDGE. It’s necrotic. I’ve seen three patients die in the last twenty minutes. Vomiting blood. Rapid organ failure.

User440: Then what do we drink? All the bottled water is gone from the 7-11.

Ren closed his eyes. He visualized the map of the district. Thousands of people trapped in high-rises. The convenience stores had been looted in the first ten minutes. The taps were poison.

Thirst killed faster than hunger. And panic about thirst killed faster than thirst itself.

He focused on the chat interface. He could type with his mind.

He selected [Anonymous].

Message: [Image Attached: Vial of Purification Spirit]

Text: "One drop purifies 100 liters. Cures low-level toxins. Restores vitality. Bidding starts now. I accept Cores, Gold, or Skill Books. Place your offers."

The message floated in the feed for a second, sandwiched between a scream for help and a prayer.

Then, the private messages exploded.

It wasn't a notification chime; it was a continuous, solid tone of incoming data.

Thunder_King: Where are you? Bring that to me. I’ll make you a lieutenant.

BossChen: I have a gold watch. Rolex. Real.

SteelWall: Is this real? How do we trade?

Ren ignored the threats. He ignored the begging. He looked for the smart ones.

The system included a [Trade Platform]. It allowed remote exchange of items if both parties agreed, taking a 10% "processing fee" of energy from the item itself.

Ren filtered the messages. He wasn't interested in Rolexes. The old currency was dead. He needed resources that fed his talent.

He saw a message from a user named SilentBlade.

SilentBlade: I have a Mutated Rat Core (Level 2). And a Skill Book: 'Shadow Step' (Rank F). For the vial.

Ren paused. A Skill Book.

His talent allowed him to edit matter, but his body was still just a human body. He had the Mental Fortress, but he couldn't dodge bullets. Shadow Step implied mobility.

He accepted the chat request.

[Trade Initiated with SilentBlade]

[Item Offered: Mutated Rat Core (Lvl 2) + Skill Book: Shadow Step]

[Item Requested: Vial of Purification Spirit]

Ren hesitated. The vial was worth more than a Rank F skill and a rat core. But this was a market opener. He needed to establish credibility. He needed the world to know that "The Merchant" was real and that his goods were legitimate.

He modified the trade.

[Offer Updated: 1/10th Vial of Purification Spirit (Decanted)]

He wasn't going to give away the whole farm.

He went to the kitchen, found a small soy sauce dish, and poured a tenth of the blue liquid into it. He focused his talent.

[Target: 10ml Purification Spirit]

[Exchange Available.]

Wait. No. He didn't need to exchange it. He needed to package it.

He grabbed an empty plastic medicine bottle from Mr. Zhang’s trash—old blood pressure pills. He rinsed it with a drop of the Spirit, then poured the 10ml dose inside.

He placed the medicine bottle into the System’s trade slot—a small vortex of blue light that opened in the air when he focused on the interface.

A second later, two items dropped onto Mr. Zhang’s kitchen table.

A gray, jagged crystal the size of a marble. And a thin, leather-bound pamphlet that looked ancient, despite the fact the apocalypse was an hour old.

[Skill Book: Shadow Step]

[Effect: Allows the user to burst 3 meters in any direction instantly. Cooldown: 10 seconds. Stamina Cost: Medium.]

Ren picked up the book.

[Do you wish to learn this skill?]

The book didn't open. It dissolved into particles of black light that shot into Ren’s chest.

He gasped. The sensation was like a bucket of ice water being dumped down his spine. His leg muscles twitched involuntarily, rewiring themselves, learning a movement pattern he had never practiced.

He knew how to move. He knew how to shift his weight, how to push off the ball of his foot to slide through the air.

Ren stood up. He looked at the kitchen door.

He didn't walk. He willed it.

He was standing in the doorway. He hadn't run; he had skipped the space in between. A sharp pain spiked in his calves—the stamina cost—but he was there.

"Useful," he muttered.

He looked back at the World Chat.

SilentBlade: It’s real. The stuff is magic. My brother was dying from the sludge water. He’s walking now. The Merchant is legit.

The chat went nuclear.

Thunder_King: @TheMerchant I will find you. Nobody hoards resources in my city.

IceQueen: @TheMerchant I have a Level 3 Core. Message me.

FireStarter: I have a location on a Supply Chest. Will trade info for water.

Ren swiped the panel away.

He walked back to his own apartment, carrying the three bottles of Moutai and the remaining Purification Spirit. He stepped through the hole in the wall, back into his fortress.

He set the bottles down next to the Endless Noodle Bowl.

He was safe. He was fed. He was armed.

But down below, the city was eating itself. The strong were enslaving the weak. The weak were dying of thirst.

Ren picked up the noodle bowl and took a sip of the broth. It was savory, grounding.

He wasn't a hero. He wouldn't go down there and fight the Thunder King for the sake of justice. That was a protagonist's job, and protagonists usually died tragic, noble deaths.

But he wouldn't let the Thunder King control the market, either.

Ren looked at the pile of junk he still hadn't exchanged. The ball of twine. The rubber bands. The broken toaster.

"Digital Feudalism," Ren said to the empty room. "Fine. If you want to play kings and peasants..."

He picked up the Mutated Rat Core he had just received. It pulsed with a dirty, gray light.

[Target: Mutated Rat Core (Level 2)]

[Exchange Available.]

"Let's see what the currency of the new world is worth."

He squeezed the core.

The core shattered. The gray light turned blinding white. The air smelled of ozone and... fresh mint?

A small, silver object fell into his hand. It looked like a seed. A metallic, geometric seed.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Automated Sentry Turret Seed (Rank C)]

[Attributes: Plant in solid ground. Grows a bio-mechanical turret in 5 minutes. Autonomously targets hostile entities. Ammo: infinite (solar recharge).]

Ren stared at the seed.

A slow, cold smile touched his lips. It wasn't a nice smile. It was the smile of a structural engineer realizing the bridge creates a perfect choke point.

He walked to the shattered balcony. The fog was still pressing against the glass.

"Thunder King wants to find me?"

Ren tossed the seed into the soil of the dead potted plant in the corner of the balcony.

He sat back on the sofa, opened the trade menu, and typed a new message.

Message: Stock resupplied. Now accepting bulk orders. Do not bring weapons to the exchange point. I am watching.

He didn't have an exchange point yet. He didn't have a storefront. He was just a guy in a broken apartment with a god-tier talent and a bowl of noodles.

But in the land of the blind, the man with the Specter-Sight Scope was king.

Ren closed his eyes and listened to the hum of the city dying. It sounded like opportunity.
