Chapter 9: Harvesting the Divine

The silence on the 13th floor was heavier than the violence that had preceded it.

Ren Qi stood ankle-deep in the dissolving muck of what used to be the Butcher. The air tasted of copper and ozone, a metallic tang that coated the back of his throat. His chest heaved, the new Cuirass of the Iron Eater rising and falling with a faint, shifting clatter of scales. It was heavy, this new armor—a physical reminder of the physics he had just cheated.

The corpse was unstable. The Butcher wasn't a natural organism; it was a construct of necrotic magic and hate, and now that the binding energy was gone, it was returning to the void. The patchwork flesh was bubbling, turning into a gray, acrid steam.

Ren watched the steam rise. It drifted toward the ceiling, wasted energy bleeding back into the system.

Most survivors—if there were any others left who could kill a Level 10 Elite—would have looted the key and run. They would have been grateful for the experience points and the adrenaline crash.

Ren didn't operate on gratitude. He operated on efficiency.

He saw the corpse not as a defeated enemy, but as a pile of unrefined resources slipping through his fingers.

He reached down into the sludge. His gloved hand brushed against something hard and cold. He pulled it out.

It wasn't gold like the Master Key. It was silver, tarnished, heavy, and cold enough to burn his skin through the leather glove. The bow of the key was shaped like a gear.

[Item: Chief Engineer’s Key (Silver)]

[Access Level: Building Infrastructure / Maintenance Sub-Levels]

Ren clipped it to his belt next to the Master Key.

He looked back at the corpse. The massive torso was slumped, the stitches unraveling like dead worms. The meat underneath was gray and weeping.

"System," Ren said. His voice was a rasp.

He placed his hand directly onto the Butcher’s exposed, dissolving chest cavity.

[Target: Corpse of the Flesh-Stitched Butcher (Level 10 Elite)]

[Status: Decaying (45% Integrity)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren focused. The headache from his previous exchanges was a dull throb at the base of his skull, a warning light on a dashboard he was choosing to ignore.

He didn't want a weapon. He didn't want a tool.

The Butcher had been strong. Not just strong—dense. It had walked through a wall of iron spikes like they were grass. It had fallen a story and stood up. It was a creature of structural excess.

Ren looked at his own hand. Pale. Thin. Trembling slightly from the exertion. Human.

He was piloting a Honda Civic in a demolition derby of tanks. The Molecular Blade gave him offense, the Cuirass gave him a shell, but the chassis underneath was still fragile. If a blow bypassed his armor, his ribs would snap like dry twigs.

"I need the foundation," Ren whispered.

He squeezed the wet, dead flesh.

The reaction wasn't immediate.

Usually, the golden text was instant, eager. This time, the interface flickered. The letters blurred, rearranging themselves, struggling to parse the request. He was trying to liquidate a Boss entity while the code was still deleting it.

[Calculating Value...]

[Error: Biological Mass unstable.]

[Synthesizing conceptual traits: , , .]

The sludge under his hand stopped steaming. It turned cold. Freezing. The gray flesh darkened, contracting rapidly, pulling away from the floor as if sucked into a vacuum. The mass swirled, compressing violently into the space beneath Ren’s palm.

The sound was terrible—a high-pitched whine of matter being crushed beyond its limits.

[Exchange Successful.]

Ren pulled his hand back.

The mountain of flesh was gone. The bloodstains on the carpet remained, but the meat had vanished.

Sitting on the floor, in the center of the wet stain, was a canister.

It looked like industrial hardware. A heavy cylinder of glass and gunmetal, capped with a needle that looked more like a drill bit. Inside the glass, a fluid moved. It wasn't red. It was a deep, viscous amber, suspending flecks of black iron that spun slowly in the suspension.

[Item: Bloodline Serum - Titan’s Resilience (Rank S)]

[Type: Genetic Modifier (Consumable)]

[Description: Extracted essence of a high-density juggernaut. Rewrites the user's skeletal and muscular density. Removes the 'Fragile' trait of the human species.]

[Warning: Administration is traumatic. Survival rate: 60%.]

Ren picked it up. It weighed five pounds.

He looked at the needle.

In the old world, a forty percent chance of death was a reason to cancel a surgery. In this world, a sixty percent chance of power was a statistical gift.

He didn't take it there. The 13th floor was exposed. The hole in the ceiling above him was a vulnerability.

He touched the Chief Engineer’s Key on his belt.

"Infrastructure," he muttered.

The maintenance access was not in the lobby. It was in the service core, behind a nondescript door in the B-Block stairwell, halfway between the first floor and the basement levels.

Ren moved down the stairs. He ignored the scuttling sounds in the ventilation. He ignored the frost creeping back up the walls as the building cooled without his presence.

He reached the heavy steel door on the landing between Floor 1 and B1. It had no handle, just a keyhole.

He inserted the Silver Key.

It turned with a smooth, heavy thunk that vibrated through his arm. The tumblers in this lock were massive, well-oiled, untouched by the rust of the apocalypse.

The door swung inward.

The air that hit him was stale, dry, and warm. It smelled of grease and high-voltage rubber.

Ren stepped inside and locked the door behind him.

He was in a narrow catwalk overlooking a cavernous space. This was the lungs of the building. Below him, massive yellow generators sat silent in the dark. Water pumps the size of trucks were lined up against the far wall. Cables thick as pythons snaked across the ceiling.

Ren scanned the room with the Specter-Sight Scope.

Clear. No thermal signatures. No ghosts. No biologicals. Just machinery waiting for an operator.

He climbed down the ladder to the main floor. He found the control room—a glass-walled box elevated above the generators.

Inside, there was a cot. A desk. A schedule pinned to the wall that ended three days ago. The skeleton of a maintenance worker sat in the chair, a coffee mug still resting near its bony hand. The worker hadn't been eaten; he had just died. Heart attack? Stroke? The System didn't discriminate.

Ren gently moved the bones to the floor.

"Shift's over," he said.

He sat in the chair. It was a good chair. Ergonomic mesh.

He placed the Bloodline Serum on the desk.

The amber liquid swirled. The black flecks seemed to be watching him.

Ren stripped off his jacket. He unclasped the Cuirass of the Iron Eater, setting the heavy scale armor on the desk. He rolled up the sleeve of his flannel shirt. His arm was pale, the veins blue and distinct.

He looked at the stats. Survival rate: 60%.

If he didn't take it, his survival rate against the next Boss was zero.

Ren picked up the cylinder. He didn't hesitate. Hesitation allowed the brain to invent scenarios of failure. He pressed the heavy, drill-like needle against the crook of his elbow.

He squeezed the trigger.

It didn't feel like a needle. It felt like being shot.

The pressurized gas in the cylinder fired the amber sludge into his vein with the force of a nail gun.

Ren gasped, his back arching off the chair.

The pain was immediate and absolute.

It wasn't a burning sensation. It was weight. It felt as if he had injected molten lead. He watched the amber fluid track up his vein, a visible, bulging ridge moving under his skin toward his shoulder.

When it hit his heart, the world turned white.

Ren fell out of the chair.

He hit the linoleum floor, curling into a fetal ball.

He didn't scream. He couldn't. His vocal cords had seized, turning to stone.

The description said Rewrites density. It didn't mention the violence of the edit.

He could feel his marrow boiling. His bones weren't just hardening; they were compacting. The lattice structure of his femur fractured, dissolved, and reformed instantly, knitting together tighter, heavier, eliminating the microscopic air pockets that made human bones light enough to swim.

His ribs audibly shifted. They ground against each other, thickening, reshaping into a solid cage designed to protect the engine within.

Ren’s vision narrowed to a pinprick. His heartbeat was a slow, thunderous hammer against the floorboards. Thump. Pause. Thump. Pause.

His muscles tore. Every fiber in his biceps, his quads, his back—they shredded and snapped back together, weaving themselves into cables of dense, darker meat. The lactic acid burn was a forest fire in his nervous system.

He vomited bile onto the floor, but he couldn't move to wipe it away. His body was a construction zone, and the foreman was a sadist.

He felt the darkness creeping in. The easy way out. Just let go. Let the heart stop. The pain would end.

Ren grit his teeth so hard a molar cracked.

He thought about the cold noodles. He thought about the landlord who had threatened to evict him last week. He thought about the Spider in the lobby and the Butcher in the hall.

He thought about the Inequivalent Exchange.

He wasn't done trading.

He forced his lungs to expand against the crushing weight of his new ribcage. He forced oxygen into the blood that was turning to sludge.

Breathe. Process. Adapt.

The seizure lasted for an hour. Or a minute. Or a year.

Slowly, the white noise in his ears faded, replaced by the hum of the silence in the control room.

Ren lay flat on his back, staring at the ceiling tiles.

Not the heaviness of fatigue. The heaviness of a tank. Gravity seemed to have a stronger grip on him, pulling him down, grounding him.

It moved with a hydraulic smoothness. The skin looked the same, perhaps a shade paler, but the underlying structure was different. The wrist bone was thicker. The tendons stood out like steel wires.

The motion was effortless. He didn't use momentum; he just contracted his abs and rose.

He felt the floor groan slightly under his boots. He hadn't gained size—he was still lean—but his density had doubled. He felt like a statue made of meat.

He walked to the metal locker in the corner of the room.

He didn't wind up. He didn't use proper form. He just threw a casual jab.

His fist went through the steel door. It didn't crumple; it sheared. The metal tore like wet cardboard.

Ren pulled his hand back. His knuckles weren't even red.

[Integration Complete.]

[Attribute Updated: Strength +30]

[Attribute Updated: Constitution +40]

[New Passive Trait: Iron Bone. Skeletal structure is now harder than industrial steel.]

Ren exhaled. The breath rushed out of him, deep and long.

He picked up the Cuirass of the Iron Eater from the desk. When he held it before, it had required both hands to lift comfortably. Now, he held it in one hand like a t-shirt.

He strapped it back on. It felt weightless.

He checked the Blueprint.

[Territory: Skyview Garden, Maintenance Sector]

[Power Status: Offline]

Ren turned his attention to the console.

The control board was dead, dust coating the analog dials and switches. But the Silver Key had given him access.

He scanned the panel. The instructions were faded, but the logic was universal.

Main Breaker. Fuel Pump. Ignition.

"Let there be light," Ren said flatly.

He threw the master switch.

Somewhere in the darkness below, a starter motor whined. It coughed, struggled, and then caught.

The number one diesel generator thundered to life. The floor vibrated.

Ren watched the needles on the console jump. Voltage spiked. Frequency stabilized.

In the cavern below, floodlights flickered on, bathing the machinery in harsh sodium-yellow light.

Ren tapped the console, bringing up the building schematics.

First, the elevators. He locked them all to the ground floor except for the service elevator, which he keyed to his biometrics.

Second, the HVAC. He killed the air conditioning in the lobby—let the spider’s ice remain—but turned the heat on for the 24th floor.

Third, the security grid.

Ren watched the monitors on the wall flicker to life.

Sixteen screens. Most showed static. But some cleared.

He saw the lobby. The frozen spider statue. The seven survivors huddled in a circle near the reception desk, shivering.

He saw the 14th floor hallway. The hole he had cut in the floor.

He saw the exterior cameras.

The fog outside was a swirling wall of gray, pressing against the glass. Shapes moved in it—vast, swimming shadows that blotted out the pale sun.

Ren looked at the exterior view of Block B.

Windows were lighting up.

In a world of darkness, Skyview Garden was suddenly a beacon. A lighthouse in hell.

Ren knew what that meant. Light attracted moths. And in this new ecosystem, the moths had teeth.

He leaned back in the dead man's chair, the heavy mesh groaning under his new density.

He had a fortress. He had power. He had a body that could punch through steel.

He opened his inventory.

The Core of the Mist (Level 8) he had picked up earlier. The Necro-Stitch Thread. The junk he had hoarded.

He looked at the empty Bloodline Serum canister.

[Target: Empty Glass Canister]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren considered it. It was trash. High-grade medical waste.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Sterile Bio-Bandage (x3)]

Ren put them away. Nothing wasted.

He looked at the monitor showing the survivors in the lobby. The businessman was standing up, pointing at the lights, shouting something Ren couldn't hear. The woman with the cat was crying.

They thought the lights meant rescue. They thought civilization had rebooted.

Ren adjusted the Specter-Sight Scope.

He saw the heat signatures of three Ghouls climbing the exterior of the building, drawn by the sudden warmth radiating from the windows on the 4th floor.

Ren tapped the microphone button on the console. It connected to the building's PA system.

His voice boomed through the speakers in the lobby, echoing off the marble and the ice. It sounded deeper than before, resonating in his dense chest.

On the screen, the survivors froze. They looked up at the ceiling speakers.

"The power is on," Ren said. "This is not a rescue. This is a utility update."

He watched them. The hope on their faces cracked, replaced by confusion.

"The lights will attract things from the outside," Ren continued. "Stay away from the windows. Do not leave the lobby. If you try to come up the stairs, the turrets will fire."

"Rent is due on the first of the month. Since the banks are closed, I accept payment in raw materials, food, or labor. I will come down to collect tomorrow."

Ren stood up. He felt the hunger rising in him—not for food, but for fuel. His new cells were demanding calories to maintain this density.

He needed to clear the rest of the building. He needed to turn every monster in Block B into a resource.

He walked to the heavy steel door.

He was done playing janitor.

Ren unlocked the door and stepped out into the stairwell, the Molecular Blade humming in his hand, ready to harvest the tenants who hadn't paid.
