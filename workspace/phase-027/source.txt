# Chapter 27: Betrayal and Purge

The grease-colored rain did not stop after the meteors fell. It simply thinned, turning into a fine, chemical mist that clung to the shattered concrete of Skyview Garden like wet oil.

Ren Qi stood on the edge of the courtyard, his boots sinking slightly into a thick layer of grey ash. The air smelled of burnt silicon, boiled copper, and the distinct, wet-dog stench of vaporized crawl-rats. Six kilometers away, the skeletal remains of the Futian office towers stood like blackened teeth against a sky that had faded from violent crimson to a sickly, bruised lavender.

He pulled a small steel pocket knife from his dark flannel shirt and began scraping a crust of dried, purple mana residue from the collar of his Cuirass of the Iron Eater. The rusted iron scales clinked softly, a dry, rhythmic sound in the quiet morning.

"The lateral wall has a structural fracture along the third-floor joint," Ren Qi said without looking back. "The concrete there was poured during a high-humidity week in 2018. The aggregate mix was cheap. It didn't hold up to the kinetic back-pressure."

Lin Xia stepped out from the shadow of the lobby entrance, her sneakers squeaking on the wet tiles. She held her clipboard tightly against her chest, her fingers still gray with soot from the reception desk.

"I... I took the inventory, Landlord," she said, her voice thin and vibrating with a faint, residual tremor. "We lost forty-two meters of the outer chain-link fence. The automated water pumps on B1 are drawing forty percent more power because of the sediment in the intake. And... the survivors."

"The survivors are a depreciating asset if they are not working," Ren Qi said, his gray eyes scanning the horizon. "How many are currently functional?"

"Forty-eight," she whispered, stepping closer to him, her eyes darting toward the dark staircase of the western block. "But they aren't working, Landlord. They're talking. In the western corridors, near the old fire exit. Zhao Kun has been organizing them."

Ren Qi’s hand did not pause its scraping. "Zhao Kun. The one who used to run the logistics team for the logistics company on the fourth floor."

"Yes," Lin Xia said, her breath catching in her throat as a cold gust of wind blew a spray of swily fog across the courtyard. "He’s telling them that you’re empty. He says that S-rank spell you used—the meteors—it must have burned through your entire mana pool. He says your talent is a high-cost, high-cooldown trigger, and that you’re sitting in the office now with dry veins. He’s... he’s planning to take the Totem."

Ren Qi finally stopped scraping. He folded the pocket knife with a clean, metallic snap.

"The Totem of the Iron Hearth is the only thing keeping the regional dampener active," he observed, his voice flat, completely devoid of anger or surprise. "If they take it, the clean-air zone collapses within twelve hrs. The toxic density will rise to forty percent per minute. They would die before they reached the Nanshan border."

"They don't believe that," Lin Xia stammered, her knuckles turning white against the clipboard. "Zhao Kun told them that the military faction at the High-Tech Park Station has a spatial filter, but they need a high-grade anchor to secure their inner perimeter. He wants to use our Totem as a trade-in. He thinks... he thinks if they run tonight, they can buy their way into the inner ring."

Ren Qi turned his head slightly, looking down at her. His face, waxy and gray under the dim light, carried no trace of human emotion. To him, Zhao Kun was not a rebel or a traitor; he was simply a line item on an unpaid invoice. A bad debt that was attempting to liquidate its own collateral.

"A predictable variance," Ren Qi said softly. "When a system experiences a massive energy discharge, the local variables always assume the generator is offline. They do not understand that a proper grid always maintains a reserve."

He turned back toward the basement stairs. "Come. Let us adjust the inventory."

The B2 workshop was cold. The violet glow of the Heart of the Glitch sat on the metal workbench, casting long, shivering shadows across the damp concrete walls. The microscopic lines of code inside the crystal were drifting slowly now, like frozen dust suspended in oil.

Ren Qi did not touch the glitched core. Instead, he walked over to a stack of rusted iron junk in the corner—old steel pipe joints, a broken copper radiator from a commercial air conditioning unit, and a cracked, depleted mana core he had recovered from a crawl-rat two days ago. The core was dull, its surface gray and chalky, completely stripped of its original systemic energy.

He laid the pieces on the rusted metal table.

"Lin Xia," he said, his hands moving with the deliberate, slow precision of a draftsman. "The real Totem of the Iron Hearth is currently anchored four meters below the central courtyard socket. It requires twelve kilowatts of continuous thermal conversion to maintain the green light field."

"Yes, Landlord," she said, standing near the heavy metal door, her eyes fixed on his steady fingers.

"The survivors believe the totem is a portable artifact," Ren Qi continued, picking up a rusted copper pipe. "They believe it is an independent valve. They do not understand that the item is merely a terminal. If you sever the terminal from the line, the line remains. But the terminal... the terminal becomes an open circuit."

He closed his eyes. The cold, empty void in his chest opened, its invisible fingers reaching out to wrap around the pile of scrap metal and the dead core.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

[Input: 1x Depleted Mana Core (Rank D) + 8kg Scrap Copper + 2x Rusted Iron Flanges]

The system interface in his mind flickered, the green text pale against the blackness of his inner eyelids.

[Concept: Decoy Beacon -> Parasitic Conversion Loop]

[Exchange successful. Bypassing equivalent limitations.]

[Obtained Item: Totem of the Iron Hearth (Decoy / Unstable Structure)]

The metal on the table did not melt. It dissolved into a grey, metallic sludge that rose into the air, spinning slowly before solidifying into a blocky, three-sided bronze pillar. It was a perfect replica of the real totem. The three faces of the pillar were engraved with the same intricate, geometric runes, and a soft, comforting green light pulsed from its hollow core, casting a warm glow across Ren Qi's waxy face.

To anyone looking at it, it was an S-rank protective anchor, humming with clean, stabilizing energy.

But Ren Qi could see the internal ledger. The exchange had not been equivalent. The SSS-rank talent had bypassed the material cost by inserting a predatory logic loop into the core of the decoy. It was a terminal with no ground wire. If anyone other than Ren Qi attempted to force-bind their mana to its runic interface, the device would not output a barrier.

It would collect the debt.

"Number Three," Ren Qi called out into the dark corner of the workshop.

A massive figure stepped into the violet light. Number Three stood nearly seven feet tall, his grey canvas coat stained with grease and dried blood, his face hidden beneath the shadow of a heavy hood. His hands, thick and scarred, rested on a heavy steel crowbar that had been welded to a concrete-breaker's bit.

"Go to the courtyard," Ren Qi said, his voice dropping into that dry, bureaucratic register. "Swap the terminals. Place the decoy in the main socket. Take the real totem and store it in the B1 generator room. Do it quietly."

Number Three gave a low, gravelly grunt. He picked up the heavy bronze decoy with one hand, his shoulder muscles bunching under the canvas coat, and walked out into the corridor.

Ren Qi watched him go, then turned his gaze back to Lin Xia. She was staring at the spot where the decoy had been, her lower lip trembling slightly.

"Landlord," she whispered. "If Zhao Kun takes that... what will happen to him?"

"He will execute his own transaction," Ren Qi said, picking up a dry rag to clean his hands. "He believes he is stealing an asset. He is actually signing a high-interest liability contract. In this world, Lin Xia, there is no such thing as a free unit of safety. Someone always pays the maintenance fee."

The middle of the night brought a shift in the wind. The swily fog from the Nanshan District rolled in, thick and yellow, smelling of rotten cabbage and old batteries. It ground against the shattered glass doors of the lobby, leaving greasy, yellow streaks on the remaining panes.

Inside the western block's stairwell, four shadows moved in absolute silence.

Zhao Kun led the way. He was a lean man with a sharp, twitching nose and eyes that never stayed still for more than a second. He wore a heavy leather jacket reinforced with duct-taped plastic shin guards, and in his right hand, he clutched a modified iron pipe with a sharpened steel tip.

Behind him came three other survivors—two young men from the logistics team and a former security guard named Sun Liang, who was carrying a heavy nylon duffel bag.

"Keep it quiet," Zhao Kun hissed, his voice a dry whisper that was swallowed by the low hum of the distant generator. "The Landlord’s pet—the big one in the canvas coat—is patrolling the eastern wall. He won't be back for ten minutes."

"Kun," Sun Liang whispered, his breath coming in short, cold puffs. "Are we sure about this? The Landlord... you saw what he did to the Pyromancer. He turned him into a mortar. Just like that. The guy didn't even have time to scream."

"The Pyromancer was a fool," Zhao Kun sneered, his hand tightening on the iron pipe. "He tried to fight the Landlord in his own lobby, right where his defenses are strongest. But look at the courtyard. The green light on the totem is dim. The Landlord’s spent, I tell you. He used everything he had to call down those steel mountains. He’s sleeping in the basement like a dead dog, trying to recover his mana."

They reached the edge of the lobby, peer-checking through the broken glass doors.

In the center of the courtyard, the bronze pillar sat in its concrete socket. The pale green light it emitted was steady, cutting through the swily, yellow fog in a five-meter circle. The air inside that circle was clear and cool, a tiny, fragile island of safety in the middle of a dying city.

"Look at that," Zhao Kun whispered, his eyes shining with a frantic, desperate greed. "That's our ticket. The military guys at the High-Tech Park have five thousand cans of pork and a water purifier that works. But their perimeter is failing because their rank-D barrier expired. We bring them this, and we're officers. We won't have to carry concrete or clear toxic sludge for thirty credits a day."

He waved his hand, and the four of them scurried across the wet asphalt, their boots making no sound on the thin layer of grey ash.

They reached the totem.

Zhao Kun knelt, his fingers tracing the cold bronze runes. He felt the faint, rhythmic vibration of power inside the metal. It felt real. It felt massive.

"Get the crowbar," he ordered Sun Liang. "Pop the base pins. Hurry."

Sun Liang jammed a small steel bar under the bronze mounting plate. With a sharp, dry clink, the retaining pins—which Number Three had purposely left unbolted—snapped free. The totem came loose, the green light flickering once before stabilizing.

Zhao Kun grabbed the heavy bronze pillar, hoisting it into his arms. He gasped slightly at the weight, his knees buckling, but the sheer adrenaline of the theft carried him through.

"We go," he hissed. "Through the eastern wall breach. The fog is thick enough to cover our tracks."

They turned and ran, disappearing into the yellow, swily mist before the lobby doors could even swing shut.

From the dark window of the third-floor management office, Ren Qi stood with his hands behind his back. The cold breeze blew through the shattered frame, rustling his dark flannel shirt. Beside him, Lin Xia stood in silence, her hand resting on her clipboard, her eyes tracking the faint, moving green glow as it traveled toward the eastern edge of the property.

"They have cleared the perimeter," she said, her voice barely audible.

"The transaction has commenced," Ren Qi replied.

Six hundred meters east of Skyview Garden, the ruins of the Nanshan High-Tech Park were a maze of collapsed skybridges and burned-out city buses. The swily yellow fog was denser here, clinging to the asphalt like wet cotton, reducing visibility to less than three meters.

Zhao Kun was panting heavily, his shoulder aching from the weight of the bronze pillar. Sun Liang and the other two were jogging beside him, their eyes darting nervously toward the dark shadows of the ruined storefronts.

"Kun... wait," Sun Liang gasped, stopping against the rusted side of a transit van. "The fog... it’s getting thicker. The safety index on my system panel... it’s dropping. It’s already down to eighteen percent."

"Just keep moving," Zhao Kun snarled, wiping a mix of greasy rain and sweat from his forehead. "The station is only three blocks away. Once we get inside the tunnel, the fog won't—"

A low, wet sound came from the darkness ahead. It was the sound of bones grinding against concrete, accompanied by a rapid, metallic clicking—the unmistakable sound of claws on shattered glass.

From the yellow mist, six pairs of crimson eyes emerged.

They were glass-fleshed hounds from the Futian Red Zone. Their bodies were translucent, their inner organs glowing with a faint, corrupted violet light, their muscle fibers visible like twisted glass threads beneath their cold skin. They did not growl; they simply stared at the four humans, their jaws dripping with a thick, yellow fluid that hissed as it touched the wet asphalt.

"Crap," Sun Liang screamed, dropping his nylon bag. "Hounds! There's a whole pack of them!"

"The totem!" Zhao Kun roared, his face contorting with panic. "Plant the totem! Set it down and activate the barrier!"

He slammed the heavy bronze base of the decoy onto the cracked asphalt of the street.

"How do we turn it on?" Sun Liang screamed, his voice cracking as the first hound lunged forward, its glass claws tearing through the wet air. "Kun! How do we turn it on?"

"The runes!" Zhao Kun yelled. He remembered seeing the Landlord press his palm against the central interface. "You have to bind your mana to it! Blood or mana! Just touch the center!"

Zhao Kun slammed his right palm against the primary rune on the front of the pillar. He bit his own thumb, smearing a thick streak of red across the bronze geometric lines.

"Authorize!" he screamed. "Activate the perimeter! Now!"

The totem did not emit a gentle green dome.

Instead, the green light in its core died instantly. The bronze pillar shuddered, its metallic surface turning a dark, oily crimson that looked like dried blood. The geometric runes did not glow; they sank into the metal, turning into deep, hollow grooves that began to draw inward with a sharp, hydraulic hiss.

Zhao Kun tried to pull his hand back.

His palm was fused to the bronze surface. The metal felt ice-cold, but it was not the cold of winter; it was the absolute, violent draft of a thermodynamic void.

"Kun?" Sun Liang stammered, backing away as the glass-fleshed hounds paused, their crimson eyes narrowing as they sensed the sudden, violent shift in the local mana frequency. "What’s... what’s happening to your hand?"

Zhao Kun did not answer. He couldn't.

The waxy, grey skin of his forearm was rapidly turning translucent. The capillaries beneath his epidermis did not carry blood anymore; they were hardening into thin, rigid threads of black glass, identical to the structure of the glitched core in the B2 workshop. He could feel his own life force—the very marrow of his bones, the heat in his lungs, the electrical impulses in his brain—being violently sucked down into the bronze pillar like water down a high-pressure drain.

[SYSTEM: Unauthorized User detected.]

[Item: Totem of the Iron Hearth (Decoy) initiating secondary exchange loop...]

[Input Source: Biological Mass & Lifeforce of User (Zhao Kun - Rank C - Logistics Specialist)]

[Target Output: Explosive Rune Detonation (Rank B)]

[Warning: Exchange ratio is highly inequivalent. Biological structure conversion: 100%]

"No... please..." Zhao Kun whispered, but his throat had already turned to dry, grey salt. His eyes, once bright with greedy panic, went dark and glassy, their pupils freezing into tiny, black ice crystals.

The three other survivors did not run. They were frozen in a state of pure, paralyzed horror.

They watched as Zhao Kun’s body shrank, his flesh collapsing inward as if he were being vacuum-sealed from the inside out. His leather jacket flattened against a frame that was no longer composed of bone and muscle, but of highly compressed, unstable kinetic energy.

The bronze pillar began to whistle—a high-pitched, screaming sound that vibrated through the asphalt, shattering the windows of the transit van beside them.

The glass-fleshed hounds realized the danger too late. They turned to spring back into the fog, their glass legs straining against the wet ground.

Then, the world went white.

It was a clean, silent expansion of energy. A seventy-meter disc of white-hot fire erupted from the spot where Zhao Kun had stood, expanding outward with the speed of a physical hammer.

The transit van was instantly sheared in half, its metal body melting into white-hot droplets before it could even hit the ground. The six glass-fleshed hounds were vaporized in mid-leap, their translucent bodies shattering into billions of tiny, harmless red glass beads that were immediately consumed by the heat.

Sun Liang and the other two conspirators were thrown backward by the shockwave, their bodies tumbling across the concrete like dry leaves.

The sound of the explosion followed a split second later—a deep, bass-heavy roar that rolled through the ruined office buildings of Nanshan, echoing off the concrete towers like a physical growl.

When the smoke cleared, the yellow fog had been completely blown away from the block, leaving a clean, circular crater seventy meters wide. The ground inside the crater was a smooth, reflective sheet of black glass, still bubbling with a faint, sulfurous grease.

Sun Liang lay on his side near the ruined rear axle of the transit van. His face was covered in wet ash, his clothes torn and smoking, his left shoulder dislocated. He was groaning, his fingers clawing weakly at the hot asphalt.

Footsteps approached.

They were dry, rhythmic, and steady. The sound of heavy boots crunching through the cooling glass debris.

Sun Liang forced his head up, his vision blurry and smeared with blood.

Ren Qi stood at the edge of the crater. His dark flannel shirt was clean, his Cuirass of the Iron Eater showing no signs of heat or ash. Behind him, the massive figure of Number Three stood in silence, holding the heavy steel crowbar in his right hand.

Beside them, Lin Xia walked with her clipboard, her pen poised over the paper, her face pale but resolute.

"The lateral damage to the street is within acceptable parameters," Ren Qi said, his analytical eyes scanning the smoking crater. "The blast has cleared the eastern breach of all active hostiles. The efficiency of the conversion was approximately eighty-four percent."

"L-Landlord..." Sun Liang gasped, his voice wet with blood as he reached out a trembling hand. "Please... Zhao... Zhao Kun... he..."

"Zhao Kun’s account has been settled," Ren Qi said, walking down into the crater until he stood directly over Sun Liang. He did not look at the smoking, empty boots of the former logistics leader that lay at the center of the glass circle. "He had a negative credit balance of forty-five units. The system required a liquidation to balance the ledger. His biological mass was sufficient to clear his debt and fund the eastern defensive wall repair."

Ren Qi looked back at the lobby survivors who had followed him from the safety of the courtyard. They stood at the edge of the crater, their faces pale, their eyes wide with a quiet, petrified awe that was far deeper than any fear they had felt during the Red Moon calamity.

They looked at the smooth, black glass of the crater. They looked at the empty boots of the man who had tried to lead them.

"I have said this before," Ren Qi addressed them, his voice carrying clearly through the damp morning air, flat and absolute. "Skyview Garden is a managed estate. The rules of this property are not subjective. They are structural."

He paused, letting the quiet weight of the ruined city settle over them.

"Loyalty is an asset," Ren Qi continued, his gray eyes reflecting the pale violet light of the fading sky. "It yields a predictable, compound return in safety, food, and shelter. Betrayal... betrayal is an expenditure. It represents an uncontrolled leak in the balance sheet. And a leak... a leak will always be plugged."

He looked down at Lin Xia. "Update the tenant ledger. Zhao Kun’s unit is now vacant. Deduct the cost of the decoy totem from the security deposits of Sun Liang and the remaining two survivors."

"Yes, Landlord," Lin Xia said, her pen making a sharp, scratching sound on the paper.

"And their rent?" she asked, without looking up.

"Double it," Ren Qi said, turning his back on the crater and walking toward the courtyard where the real Totem of the Iron Hearth was already being reinstalled by Number Three. "They will be reassigned to the B2 toxic disposal team. They have a significant amount of overtime to clear before their accounts return to black."

He did not look back at the groaning men on the asphalt.

As he stepped back into the clean-air zone of Skyview Garden, the green light of the real totem flared to life once more, casting a cool, protective dome over the courtyard.

Ren Qi looked up at the sky. The red moon was gone, but the heavy, structural weight in the upper atmosphere remained, a cold presence watching from the dark.

The first calamity was over, the internal audit was complete, but the Landlord knew that the next billing cycle was already approaching.
