Chapter 42: The Recruitment Drive

# Chapter 42: The Recruitment Drive

The air inside the manor was stagnant, tasting of ozone and the stale, lingering scent of ghosts that no longer had a reason to haunt. Ren Qi sat at his desk, the terminal before him casting a pale, flickering light across the mahogany surface. He tapped a finger against the wood—a rhythmic, impatient sound that signaled his lack of interest in the quiet.

He was done with the cleanup. The specters had been efficient, though they required constant recalibration to ensure they didn't drift back into their old, looping habits of despair. He needed something more tangible. He needed hands that could hold things he didn't want to touch, and minds that were desperate enough to follow instructions without asking about the morality of the architecture.

He opened the communal interface of the Hub, a sprawling, chaotic mess of desperate pleas and trade offers. He ignored the high-tier guild recruitment spam. Instead, he opened the local notice board and began to draft a message. It was short, stripped of any flowery language or enticements.

Wanted: Individuals with F-Rank talents. Task: Maintenance and material gathering. Compensation: Daily caloric intake and safety. Note: Survival is not guaranteed. Professionalism is required.

He hit submit. The Hub was a shark tank, and he had just tossed a bit of bait into the water. He didn't care about the quality of the individuals—in fact, he preferred the bottom-tier scavengers. Their talents were usually simple, singular utilities, the kind of raw potential that most players ignored because it couldn't be used to swing a sword or throw a fireball. They were the forgotten components of the system, and Ren Qi was an expert in finding the hidden value in trash.

Within minutes, the courtyard gate groaned. The security system he had jury-rigged from scrap metal and spectral energy pinged a warning. He didn't check the logs. He stood up, adjusted his coat, and walked toward the entrance.

The line stretched out past the iron gates, disappearing into the gray fog of the district. There were a dozen or so hopefuls, their gear a patchwork of rusted iron and threadbare cloth. They looked at the manor with a mix of fear and greed. They saw a high-value property, a fortress; they didn't see the man who was actually looking to hire them as lab rats for his talent.

Ren Qi stood on the front steps, his eyes sweeping over them. He didn't offer a smile. He didn't offer a greeting.

I have limited time, Ren Qi said. His voice carried, cool and dry in the thin air. If you are here for glory, move on. If you are here for survival and have a talent that is objectively useless to anyone with half a brain, stand forward.

The crowd hesitated. A man in the back pushed his way forward, his armor clanking like a collection of pots and pans. He looked like he’d been eating dust for a week.

I have a talent, the man said, his voice trembling. It’s F-Rank. Object Relocation. I can move objects up to three inches in any direction. It’s good for picking locks, I guess.

Ren Qi looked at him, his gaze analytical. He didn't speak. He reached out and tapped the air, as if measuring the structural load of the man’s existence.

Step to the side, Ren Qi ordered.

One by one, they came forward. There were scouts with minor vision-boosts, cooks who could boil water instantly, and a woman who stood in the back, her hands tucked into her sleeves. She looked exhausted, her hair matted with grime, but her eyes were steady.

I’m Mina, she said when it was her turn. My talent is Temperature Control. It’s... pathetic. I can warm up tea. That’s all. It doesn't work on people, it doesn't work on monsters. Just tea.

Ren Qi paused. He looked at her, his expression entirely unreadable.

She held out her hand, a small, ceramic mug appearing in her palm. She concentrated, her brow furrowed. The water inside the mug began to steam, a faint, wispy vapor rising into the cold air. It was a useless talent, the kind of thing a bored merchant might find charming, but here, in the middle of a collapsing tower, it was a joke.

Ren Qi reached into his pack and pulled out a length of rusted pipe. It was cold, brittle, and covered in the crust of long-dead iron.

Warm this, he commanded.

Mina hesitated, but she reached out and grasped the pipe. She closed her eyes, the air around her fingers shimmering as she pushed her energy into the metal. It took a moment, but the pipe began to lose its frost, the surface warming under her touch. She wasn't strong, but she was consistent.

Ren Qi took the pipe back. He felt the residual heat, the faint, flickering life she had forced into the metal. He held the pipe in his palm, his talent, Inequivalent Exchange, pulsing in the back of his mind like a dormant engine ready to start.

[System Notification: Catalyst detected: Low-Level Thermal Energy. Potential conversion: Molecular Rearrangement.]

Ren Qi focused. He didn't need to do much; the heat was there, providing the foundation for the change. He fed the pipe with his own intent, a sharp, precise command to the reality of the object.

Input: [Thermal Energy + Rusted Iron]. Output: [Heat-Seeking Spike].

The metal groaned, the rust flaking away as the pipe twisted and collapsed in on itself. It wasn't a slow process. It was a violent, structural folding. In a blink, the rusted pipe had vanished, replaced by a sleek, jagged spike of dark, glowing alloy that radiated a faint, red light. It felt heavy, balanced, and alive.

Ren Qi looked at the spike, then at Mina. She was staring at his hand, her mouth slightly agape.

You’re hired, Ren Qi said.

The rest of the interviews were clinical. He picked ten of them, mostly those with talents that seemed to possess a specific, narrow-frequency energy output. He didn't care about their combat skills. He cared about the way their talents interacted with the physical world, the way they could serve as the baseline, the initial input that his own talent could refine into something lethal.

He led them inside. The manor looked different now that it was inhabited, the specters moving silently in the background as they polished the marble and swept the dust that had settled over the centuries. The recruits looked around, their eyes wide as they saw the specters working in perfect, rhythmic synchronization.

This is your workspace, Ren Qi said, gesturing to the basement, a massive, cavernous room filled with ancient, half-forgotten machinery. You will spend your days providing your input on the materials I provide. You will not ask questions. You will not deviate from the protocol. In exchange, you will receive shelter, food, and the protection of this property.

One of the men, a burly scavenger with a scar running across his nose, stepped forward. This isn't a team, he said, his voice rough. This is a factory.

Ren Qi turned to him, his expression cold and unimpressed.

It is an investment, Ren Qi replied. If you want a team, go find a guild. If you want to survive, you will do exactly what I tell you, when I tell you to do it. You are the variables in a calculation that I have already solved.

The man started to argue, but the spectral butler drifted out of the shadows, its eyes glowing with a faint, predatory light. The man fell silent, his face turning pale.

He didn't need their loyalty. He didn't need their friendship. He needed them to be efficient. He needed them to be the building blocks of his own ascension.

Ren Qi returned to his study. He pulled out the terminal and began to update his ledger. The acquisition of labor was the final piece of the structural puzzle. He had the manor, he had the resources, and now, he had the catalysts.

The notification on his wrist pinged again. A report from his perimeter sensors. Something was moving near the gate. Something large.

[System Notification: External Threat Detected: Sector 4-A Enforcement Unit. Status: Hostile. Proximity: 50 meters.]

Ren Qi stood up, his posture relaxed. He reached for his sword, the iridescent blade humming as it caught the light.

They were faster than he had expected. But then, he hadn't expected them to be slow.

He walked into the hallway, the manor’s walls shivering with the weight of the incoming pressure. He could feel the reality of the house being tested, the system outside trying to force its way in, to delete the anomaly that had taken up residence in its forgotten corner.

Mina was standing by the basement door, her eyes fixed on him.

What’s happening? she whispered.

Ren Qi didn't look back.

He walked out into the courtyard. The air was thick with the scent of ozone, the same smell that had preceded his arrival in the Forge. The gates were glowing, a swirling, violent blue light pulsing around the iron bars. They were breaching the property. They were trying to reset the anchor.

Ren Qi stood in the center of the courtyard, his boots planted firmly on the cold, gray stone. He didn't reach for his ledger. He didn't reach for his sword. He waited, his hands at his sides, his mind a void of absolute, crystalline focus.

The gate exploded inward, the iron bars turning into a fine, glittering dust as a figure stepped through the debris. It was a Goliath, a machine of war, nearly seven feet tall, clad in armor that shifted and rippled like liquid mercury. Its eyes were twin points of cold, white light, and it held a massive, double-bladed polearm that hummed with the sound of a thousand tearing realities.

It wasn't an auditor. It was a purifier.

You are the corruption, the Goliath said, its voice a deep, resonant boom that vibrated in the marrow of Ren Qi’s bones. You are the glitch in the foundation. The tower demands the restoration of order.

Ren Qi looked at the Goliath, his eyes drifting to the structural weak points in its armor, the places where the liquid mercury was stretched too thin, where the energy flow was constricted.

Order is a matter of perspective, Ren Qi said. And your perspective is currently trespassing on my property.

The Goliath lunged. It moved with a speed that defied its size, a blur of silver and light.

Ren Qi didn't dodge. He sidestepped, his movement so precise it seemed as if he had anticipated the attack seconds before it happened. He tapped the shaft of the polearm as it passed him, his finger leaving a faint, violet trail of energy on the metal.

Input: [High-Velocity Kinetic Force]. Output: [Structural Instability].

There was no sound of metal on metal. There was only the sharp, sickening crack of the polearm snapping in half. The Goliath stumbled, its momentum carrying it forward, its balance shattered as the internal geometry of its weapon turned against it.

Ren Qi turned, his sword already in his hand. He didn't hesitate. He thrust the blade into the gap in the Goliath’s neck, the iridescent edge tearing through the liquid armor as if it were nothing but water.

The Goliath froze. Its white eyes flickered, the light dimming, then dying. It didn't fall. It dissolved, its body turning into a stream of raw, glowing code that evaporated into the cold, damp air of the courtyard.

Ren Qi stood over the spot where it had stood, his sword glowing with the residual energy of the kill. He felt the weight of the tower above him, the thousands of floors, the infinite layers of corruption, the entire, bloated system of the 3000th Descent waiting for him to fail.

He looked at his wrist.

[System Notification: Threat Neutralized. Resource Harvest: 10,000 Credits. Integrity: 99%.]

He walked back toward the manor. He didn't feel tired. He didn't feel relieved. He felt the cold, sharp clarity of a man who was just getting started.

The audit was moving to the next phase, and he was the only one with the ledger.

He walked through the door, the specters stepping aside as he passed. He didn't look at them. He walked into his study, sat down at his desk, and pulled his terminal toward him.

He began to write, his stylus clicking against the glass, the sound a steady, rhythmic heartbeat in the silence of the room.

Audit Phase 5: Commencing. Location: The Citadel. Objective: Total systemic reconstruction.

He closed the terminal, the silence of the manor settling around him once more. He had the house. He had the staff. He had the catalysts. The Babel Hub was a failing, bloated machine, and he was the only engineer on the floor.

He leaned back, watching the shadows dance on the ceiling, his mind already drifting upward, toward the next floor, toward the next, more complex, more corrupt, and more beautiful challenge of his life.

The 3000th Descent was finally, truly beginning. And he was the one who was going to write the final entry in the book.

He opened his eyes, his gaze fixed on the darkness above, his mind clear, his heart steady.

And he was the only one collecting.

The manor was quiet, save for the faint, distant sound of the city outside, a place of noise and light and chaos that had no idea what was waiting for it at the edge of the district. Ren Qi sat in the dark, the only light in the room the pale, flickering glow of his ledger. He was the architect. He was the owner. He was the one who was going to break it all.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, metallic coin, flipping it over his knuckles. He caught it, the sound of the metal meeting his skin sounding like a final, decisive note.

He stood up and walked to the window, looking out over the city. The lights were beautiful, if you looked at them as a series of errors, a cascading, beautiful, systemic failure.

He wasn't going to fix the tower. He was going to replace it.

And the first step was to tear down the walls.

He gripped his blade, the edge of which now glowed with a faint, iridescent light that seemed to pulse in time with the very rhythm of the tower itself.

The audit was just beginning.

He turned away from the window, his expression cold and unyielding.

The audit of the 3000th Descent was far from over. And he had a foundation to maintain.

He opened his terminal, the screen flashing with a cascade of green, cascading numbers. He began to write, his stylus clicking against the glass.

Audit Phase 5: Stability assessment complete. The Citadel is vulnerable. Proceeding to final neutralization.

He closed the terminal, the darkness of the manor wrapping around him like a shroud. He was ready for the next layer. He was ready for the next set of accounts to be settled.

He looked at the empty, silent room, the specters of his service standing in the corners, their eyes glowing with a faint, subservient light.

He had the house. He had the power. He had the ledger.

The audit was just beginning, and he was the only one prepared to settle the bill. He leaned back, his eyes closed, his mind already drifting upward, toward the next floor, toward the next, more complex, more corrupt, and more beautiful challenge of his life.

The 3000th Descent was finally, truly beginning. And he was the one who was going to write the final entry in the book.

He opened his eyes, his gaze fixed on the darkness above, his mind clear, his heart steady.

And he was the only one collecting.
