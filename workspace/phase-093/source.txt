# Chapter 33: Economy Breaker

The air in the Babel Auction Hall tasted of recycled oxygen and the sharp, ionized ozone of a thousand intersecting realities. It was a cavernous space, a rotunda of shifting geometries where the walls seemed to fold into themselves like origami made of light. Platforms hung suspended in the void, connected by bridges of translucent, hardened energy that hummed with a low, tectonic vibration.

Ren Qi walked across the central concourse, his boots clicking with rhythmic precision against the crystalline floor. He ignored the holographic displays hovering like ghost-lanterns around the merchants, each one advertising rare metals, volatile essence-cores, or artifacts scavenged from the cooling embers of dead worlds. He had been a structural engineer long enough to know that a building was only as strong as its foundation, and the Babel Hub was fundamentally a structure built on vanity.

He navigated toward the secondary market, a less opulent sector where the light was dimmer and the wares less pretentious. His pockets contained fifty Babel Coins—a pittance, salvaged from the pockets of the thugs he had liquidated earlier. It was enough to be ignored, which was exactly how he preferred to operate.

The Universal Exchange Hall was a chaotic hive. Merchants from the depths of the 3000th Descent barked prices in tongues that fluctuated between binary-code clicks and tonal, archaic vibrations. Ren Qi approached a kiosk draped in tattered, heavy-duty tarps. An old, hunched figure behind the counter, whose skin possessed the texture of cured leather, glanced at him with eyes that had seen far too many server crashes.

Need something, scavenger? the merchant croaked. Or are you just looking for a place to die?

I require bulk supplies, Ren Qi said, his voice flat and devoid of the performative aggression favored by the other denizens of the hub. Specifically, low-tier nutritional blocks. The ones designated for pet-maintenance or long-term structural stasis.

The merchant narrowed his eyes, a wheezing laugh escaping his throat. You want the garbage, then? It is ten coins a hundred-count. It is barely edible. Mostly compressed sawdust and synthetic protein mash. It is what we feed the rats when the oxygen scrubbers fail.

Ren Qi placed the coins on the counter. The metal clinked, a sound of heavy, solid reality in a place made of dreams and debt.

I will take the stock, Ren Qi said.

A moment later, he was standing in a secluded maintenance alcove behind a pillar of obsidian. He held a heavy, grey bag that smelled of sterile chemicals and long-term storage. He opened it, revealing one hundred blocks of pale, crumbling starch.

[Item: Stale Nutrient Block (Grade: Garbage)]

[Property: Provides basic caloric intake. Causes temporary indigestion and morale decay.]

Ren Qi took the bag and set it on the cold floor. He didn't use a workbench or an altar. He simply placed his hand on the bag, his fingers tracing the coarse, cheap texture of the starch. He didn't think about the bread; he thought about the concept of sustenance—about the warmth of a hearth in Shenzhen, about the way a simple loaf of bread tasted when one had worked an eighteen-hour shift. He felt the Inequivalent Exchange talent pulsing in his blood, a silent, ravenous weight that sought to correct the absurdity of the universe.

[Exchange initiated. Input: 100 Units of Stale Nutrient Mash.]

[Concept: Sustenance. Grade: High-Efficiency.]

[Exchange successful. You used 100 units of garbage to retrieve 100 loaves of Elven Lembas Bread.]

The air in the alcove flickered. The grey blocks didn't vanish so much as they were overwritten, replaced by the warm, earthy scent of grain and honey. He looked down. Inside the bag, nestled in thin parchment, were loaves of bread so dense and golden they seemed to catch the ambient light of the hub.

[Item: Elven Lembas Bread (Grade: Rare)]

[Property: One bite restores physical and mental fatigue to 100%. Heals all non-fatal injuries over 60 seconds.]

Ren Qi picked up a loaf. It felt like holding a solid, golden brick. He checked the market interface, his mind overlaying the data on the holographic display he had projected from his wrist unit. The standard potions—vials of bubbling blue liquid that offered half the utility of this bread—were being sold for two hundred Babel Coins a piece. The market was bloated, inefficient, and reliant on a scarcity that Ren Qi had just rendered obsolete.

He moved back to the main concourse, finding a vacant terminal near the center of the trading floor. He didn't list them as a merchant. He listed them as a liquidation audit.

100 loaves. 50 coins each.

He tapped the final command, his movements as economical as a draftsman’s line.

Within seconds, the terminal began to chime—a series of high-pitched, digital alerts that cut through the low-frequency hum of the hall. He watched the interface. At first, there was silence. Then, a single notification popped up. A high-level merchant from the upper tiers had bought ten. Then another, a scavenger from the fringes, bought five. Then, the numbers started to move—faster, erratic, climbing in a logarithmic frenzy.

[Alert: Supply surge detected in market sector 4.]

[System notification: Your listing has been flagged for rapid liquidation.]

Ren Qi stood with his hands in his pockets, his face a mask of mild, bureaucratic indifference. Around him, the atmosphere in the hall shifted. The merchants who had been hawking overpriced potions stopped in their tracks. The chatter died down as users checked their wrist-interfaces, their eyes widening at the sudden influx of high-efficiency sustenance at a fraction of the market price.

He was breaking the economy, one loaf at a time. It wasn't an act of charity; it was a correction. He was exposing the inefficiency of the current system, forcing the market to adjust to a new, higher standard of value.

A tall, spindly figure in robes of shifting, iridescent silk stopped beside him. The individual’s face was obscured by a mask of polished white porcelain.

You are the one, the figure whispered, their voice a harmonic synthesis of a dozen distinct tones. You are the Auditor. You have flooded the market with base-level reality-warping material. Do you have any idea how much this devalues the internal credit system?

Ren Qi didn't look at the stranger. He watched the notifications scroll past his field of vision, the Babel Coins accumulating in his private treasury at a speed that threatened to overload the local server.

I am not interested in your credit system, Ren Qi replied, his voice dry and devoid of tension. Your system was over-leveraged and under-productive. I am merely increasing the velocity of the circulation. If your potions cannot compete with a basic loaf of bread, then your potions were never worth the asking price to begin with.

The figure tightened their grip on a staff topped with a shimmering, unstable prism. The air around them began to crackle with blue, static discharge.

We do not allow interference in the Babel Exchange, the figure warned. This is not a marketplace for outsiders to play god.

Ren Qi turned his head, looking at the figure with eyes that were as cold as a structural blueprint. I am not playing. I am conducting an audit. And according to my current calculations, your staff is currently drawing power from the building’s lighting grid, which is currently being diverted into the structural support of this platform to prevent it from collapsing into the void. If you continue to exert that level of energy, you will cause a localized gravity failure within the next three minutes.

The figure froze, the static discharge around the staff stuttering and dying. They looked at the floor, then at the staff, then back at Ren Qi, their posture losing its predatory poise.

How do you know that? the figure demanded, their voice losing its synthetic, harmonic modulation.

I checked the load-bearing capacity of the sector, Ren Qi said. It is a very inefficient building. You should have hired a better architect.

He didn't wait for a response. He turned and walked away, disappearing into the swirling, multi-colored crowds of the main thoroughfare. Behind him, the auction hall was in absolute chaos. Merchants were scrambling to adjust their prices, the market interface was spiraling into a series of rolling blue-light errors, and the price of potions was plummeting in real-time.

Ren Qi checked his treasury. He had five thousand coins, and the number was still rising. It wasn't enough to buy the hub, but it was enough to start buying the materials he needed to upgrade his own sector’s infrastructure.

He stopped in front of a high-security kiosk, a massive construct of obsidian and glowing, pulsating wires. This was the supplier for high-end structural components, the kind of things that were only sold to the Prime Arbiters and their chosen agents. He placed his hand on the terminal, and his Molecular Sight deconstructed the security protocols in a heartbeat. They were sophisticated, but they were built on the same logic of scarcity that he had just dismantled.

[System alert: Restricted terminal access detected.]

[Identity validation: Auditor Rank 44.]

[System status: Access granted.]

A menu of options appeared in his vision. He didn't look at weapons or gear. He looked at the foundation-grade stabilizers, the power-grid conduits, and the atmospheric scrubbers.

I want everything in the inventory, Ren Qi said to the empty air, his voice matter-of-fact. Charge it to the audit fund.

The terminal hummed, a sound of heavy, industrial approval.

[Warning: Total cost exceeds the sector debt limit.]

[Action required: Reallocation of funds from decorative monument accounts.]

Reallocate them, Ren Qi commanded.

He felt a tremor go through the floor, a low, tectonic vibration that traveled up through the soles of his boots. Somewhere far above, the monuments to the Arbiters were losing their light, their glory being stripped away to provide the concrete, the steel, and the wiring that Ren Qi needed to fix the broken world of his sector.

He didn't feel guilty. He felt a sense of calm, the kind of satisfaction a man feels when he finally levels a sagging floor joist.

As the terminal confirmed the massive transfer of assets, a group of guards emerged from the shadow of the nearby pillars. They were not scavengers. They were the security forces of the Hub, clad in armor of white and gold, wielding lances of concentrated light.

Administrator, the leader said, his voice cold and resonant. You have triggered an emergency state.

Ren Qi didn't draw his blade. He leaned against the terminal, his posture relaxed, his hands still in his pockets. I am currently busy with a site inspection, he said, his voice flat. Please wait until I have finished filing my report.

The leader of the guards stepped forward, his lance crackling. You are under arrest for the subversion of the Babel Economy and the unauthorized liquidation of administrative assets.

Ren Qi looked at the guard. He looked at the way the man’s helmet was misaligned by a fraction of a millimeter—a tiny, structural flaw in a soldier who prided himself on absolute perfection.

Your helmet is loose, Ren Qi observed. If you move at high speed, the vibration will cause a misalignment in your optical sensors. You are going to miss the shot.

The guard paused, his hand instinctively reaching toward his helmet.

Ren Qi used the distraction. He tapped the terminal one last time, signaling the completion of the order. The floor beneath the guards began to dissolve, the crystalline material retracting into the ceiling. It was a planned maintenance cycle he had spotted in the building’s original schematic, one the current administration had neglected to track.

The guards tumbled, falling into the dark, churning void of the hub’s underbelly, their shouts fading into the distance.

Ren Qi stepped over the edge of the now-open pit, catching a nearby structural support with one hand and pulling himself up onto the maintenance catwalk. He had the components. He had the capital. And he had the attention of every Arbiter in the Babel Hub.

He stood up, looking out over the sprawling, golden expanse of the City of Glass. It was a beautiful place, but it was failing. And now, he had the ledger in his hands.

He reached into his pocket and took out a small, metallic scrap he had salvaged from the terminal—the key to the central hub. He held it up, watching the light catch the sharp, jagged edges.

The renovation was just beginning. And it was going to be expensive.

He started to walk again, his stride long and measured, moving toward the core of the tower where the Arbiters held their final, guarded records. He could feel the weight of the Inequivalent Exchange talent in his blood, a silent, ravenous hunger that was ready to consume the entire tower if that was what it took to fix the ledger.

He didn't look back. He didn't slow down.

The era of the audit was over. The era of the renovation had begun. He was the landlord of the universe now, and he had a lot of work to do. He looked toward the zenith of the tower, where the spires pierced the sky like needles of light, and he smiled. It was a dry, humorless expression, but it was the most genuine thing he had shown in years.

He reached the first of the vaulted doors that led to the upper tiers. The material was pulsating with a warning, but he didn't care. He raised his hand, his thumb resting on the locking mechanism, and he pushed.

The doors groaned, the sound echoing through the halls of the City of Glass like the dying breath of a god. He walked inside, and the light of the room flared, a brilliant, blinding white that erased everything he had known before.

He stepped into the heart of the machine, and for the first time in his life, he was home. He was exactly where he needed to be. He reached out, his hand hovering over the central node, the source of all the debt, the suffering, and the resources of the Three Thousandth Descent.

[SYSTEM: Critical Error. Unsanctioned access to Central Core.]

[Warning: Administrator Ren Qi is attempting to rewrite the global audit protocol.]

[System alert: Prime Arbiters mobilized.]

Ren Qi didn't hesitate. He began to pull the threads, to tie the knots, to shore up the foundations of the world. He was a structural engineer. And for the first time in his life, he had the perfect, unobstructed, infinite site to work on.

The room turned white, a blinding, absolute light that erased the boundaries of space and time. He saw the structure of the world—the threads, the conduits, the desperate, leaking holes where reality was fraying.

He took his utility knife from his pocket, the blade pulsing with a hungry, absolute darkness. He pressed the flat of the blade against the core, letting the material of the knife—reconstructed from the heart of the glitch—absorb the chaotic data and ground it into something orderly, something permanent.

The core pulsed, a sound of absolute, crushing authority. Ren Qi stood his ground, his gaze locked on the shimmering, infinite data, his mind already calculating the next move.

The Babel Hub was beautiful, but it was failing.

And he was the only one in the room with the tools to fix it.

He pressed his hand against the node, and the world shifted, the very fabric of reality bending to his will. The debt was being moved. The building was being brought up to code.

This was going to be a very profitable expansion.
