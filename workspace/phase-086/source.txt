# Chapter 26: The First Calamity: Red Moon

The violet pulse of the Heart of the Glitch sat on the metal workbench of the B2 workshop, casting a cold, oily light across the damp concrete walls. The shadow of the main overhead water pipe looked like a thick, black spider crawling slowly toward the ventilation shaft.

Ren Qi did not look at the shadow. He adjusted his grip on the heavy pipe wrench, tightening the bypass valve on the modified diesel generator. The metal of the wrench was cold against his palm, but his hands were steady. The Titan’s Resilience serum had settled so deeply into his bones that his skin felt like dense, dry wax, completely indifferent to the chill of the basement.

Beside him, Lin Xia stood with a clipboard clutched to her chest. Her fingers were white where they gripped the wood, her breath coming in short, uneven rattles. The yellow light from her flashlight danced erratically across the floorboards.

"Landlord," she said, her voice dropping to a harsh whisper that barely carried over the low hum of the generator. "The atmospheric pressure in the lobby just dropped forty millibars in the last three minutes. The air... it feels thick. Some of the Vanguard survivors on the second floor say their eyes are watering. The fog outside isn't yellow anymore."

"The color is irrelevant," Ren Qi said, his voice flat and dry as he set the wrench down with a heavy, metallic clink. "It is a change in the chemical density of the integration barrier. The system is adjusting the regional parameters to prepare for a heavy load. It is a standard stress-test procedure."

"But they're saying it's the Red Moon," she stammered, stepping closer to the workbench as if the violet glow of the glitched core could offer some protection. "The regional chat channel is completely flooded. Everyone's talking about the First Calamity. They say the system is going to open the rifts wide. No more clean-air zones. No more safe harbors."

Ren Qi picked up a rag and wiped the black grease from his fingers. He looked at the Heart of the Glitch. The microscopic lines of code within the crystal were drifting faster now, swirling like disturbed silt in a pond.

"A safe harbor is a temporary concession," Ren Qi said, his analytical mind already mapping out the structural integrity of the courtyard walls. "The producers do not maintain a set they do not intend to burn. If the pressure increases, we adjust the reinforcement. Go back to the lobby. Keep the ledger open. If anyone attempts to breach the inner security doors without a cleared account, notify Number Three."

Before she could answer, a deep, resonant chime vibrated through the concrete floorboards. It was not a sound that traveled through the air; it was a physical frequency that rattled the marrow of Ren Qi's teeth, carrying the cold, sterile signature of the system's core architecture.

[SYSTEM: Global Notification - The First Calamity: Red Moon has commenced.]

[SYSTEM: Regional Danger Index: Extreme. All environmental barriers are suspended for 12 hrs.]

[SYSTEM: All native and integrated entities within the Sector will receive a 100% attribute increase. Aggression parameters: Maximum.]

[SYSTEM: Survival Phase initiated. Liquidate or be liquidated.]

The red light did not filter down from the staircase; it bled directly through the concrete. The grey walls of the basement took on a raw, copper-colored sheen, as if the stones themselves were beginning to sweat old blood.

Ren Qi didn't hesitate. He took his Molecular Blade from the workbench, checking the slide on the scabbard before mounting it to his belt. He adjusted the leather straps of his Cuirass of the Iron Eater, the rusted iron scales clinking softly against his dark flannel shirt.

"To the lobby," he said.

The lobby of Skyview Garden was in chaos.

Forty-eight survivors were huddled near the reception desk, their voices rising in a discordant, panic-stricken wall of sound. Some were praying; others were frantically sharpening crude scrap-metal spears on the stone steps. The reinforced glass doors at the main entrance were no longer showing the yellow, swily fog. Instead, the world outside was a deep, liquid crimson.

The red moon hung over the ruined skyscrapers of the High-Tech Park like a bloated, weeping eye. The light it cast was thick and greasy, turning the broken asphalt of the courtyard into a field of dark, shimmering glass.

"Get out of the way!" a loud, commanding shout cut through the panic.

A man wearing a heavy, soot-stained firefighter's coat and a black motorcycle helmet pushed through the crowd. Half of his face, visible behind the cracked visor, was a mass of twisted, pink burn tissue that pulled his lower lip down into a permanent, ugly sneer. This was the Pyromancer Leader from Block A, his hands already crackling with unstable, blue-hot sparks that smelled of burnt hair and kerosene.

Four other raiders, armed with heavy iron bars and modified nail guns, followed closely behind him.

"We need the food lockers opened now!" the Pyromancer roared, slamming his flaming fist against the wooden reception desk. Lin Xia flinched, dropping her pen as she shrank back against the wall. "The barrier is flickering! If we don't load up on the high-energy rations to keep our mana up, we're all dead meat when the first wave hits! Landlord! Where is that bastard?"

"You are five hours late on your utility contribution," a flat, dry voice said from the basement stairs.

Ren Qi stepped into the lobby, his heavy boots making a dull, solid sound on the tiled floor. His three-hundred-pound frame seemed to block the light from the corridor behind him. He looked at the Pyromancer's flaming hand, then at the scorched wood of the reception desk.

"That desk is property of the estate," Ren Qi said. "The repair cost will be deducted from your remaining credit. You now have a negative balance."

"You think I give a damn about your balance sheets?" the Pyromancer sneered, stepping forward as his flames flared hotter, casting long, frantic shadows across the ceiling. "The system just doubled the monsters' stats! There are millions of those things coming out of the Futian rifts right now! Your little green light totem in the courtyard is going to pop like a bubble! We're taking the basement, and we're taking the supply bins!"

The Pyromancer lunged, his right hand whipping forward to unleash a stream of high-heat flame directly at Ren Qi's face.

Ren Qi did not flinch. He did not step back.

He pivoted slightly, his high-density skeleton absorbing the momentum of his shift. The Cuirass of the Iron Eater on his chest glowed dull red as it met the blast, the rusted iron scales absorbing the thermal energy and dispersing it as a harmless wave of warm air around his shoulders.

Before the Pyromancer could register that his flame had failed to penetrate, Ren Qi's hand shot forward. His fingers, heavy and solid as iron clamps, closed around the Pyromancer's throat.

The motorcycle helmet's chin guard buckled under the sudden, immense pressure. The Pyromancer gagged, his blue flames sputtering out as his boots left the floor, suspended in the air by Ren Qi's single-handed grip.

"Your contract is terminated," Ren Qi said, his voice entirely devoid of heat. "Under the emergency survival clause, dead weight will be converted."

The Pyromancer's crew tried to raise their nail guns, but Number Three was already there. The massive, scarred guard in the grey canvas coat stepped out of the shadows by the elevators, his hand resting on his heavy steel crowbar, his eyes cold and unblinking. The raiders froze, their weapons trembling.

Ren Qi carried the struggling Pyromancer toward the broken glass doors of the lobby, stepping out into the cold, copper-red glare of the courtyard.

The red moon was massive, taking up half the sky. Under its light, the first wave of the horde was already visible at the edge of the clean-air zone. They were crawl-rats the size of wolves, their fur matted with dried blood, their eyes glowing with a frenzied, crimson light. Behind them, glass-fleshed hounds from the Red Zone sprinted silently, their translucent skin reflecting the bloody sky.

The bronze Totem of the Iron Hearth in the center of the courtyard was pulsing frantically, its pale green light struggling to maintain a five-meter perimeter against the encroaching red fog.

Ren Qi dropped the Pyromancer onto a pile of concrete debris near the totem.

"Landlord—please—" the man gasped, his sneer finally breaking into a mask of pure terror as he saw the sea of red eyes approaching from the dark. "I have cores... I have..."

"A live fire catalyst has high conceptual value," Ren Qi said, his hand resting on the Pyromancer's shoulder. "More than twenty normal cores. It is an efficient transaction."

He closed his eyes, opening the cold, empty void in his chest.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

[Input: 1x Live Pyromancer (Rank C - Flame Specialist) + 12x Corroded Steel Pipes + 3x Broken Concrete Slabs]

[Concept: Combustion -> High-Density Ballistic Ordinance]

[Exchange successful. Bypassing equivalent limitations.]

The Pyromancer's body dissolved with a wet, rushing sound, turning into a stream of molten copper and white-hot sparks that swirled around the concrete debris and rusted pipes on the ground. The metal groaned and reshaped itself, hardening into a thick, blocky mortar made of charred stone and reinforced iron plates. Its wide barrel sat heavy on the concrete, already smoking with a thick, sulfurous black grease.

The four remaining raiders in the lobby lobby entrance gasped, one of them dropping his nail gun with a sharp clatter before turning to run into the dark streets. They didn't get far; a pack of crawl-rats met them at the corner, the sound of tearing flesh and screaming echoing through the red fog.

"Number Three," Ren Qi called out, his eyes fixed on the approaching horde. "Load the mortar with the broken pavement blocks from the western wall. The conversion efficiency is high enough to sustain fifty rounds."

"Yes, Landlord," Number Three grunted, his large frame bending to rip up a chunk of concrete with his bare hands, dropping it into the smoking barrel of the Searing Brimstone Mortar.

The mortar shuddered. A dull, bass-heavy thud shook the courtyard as a boulder of solid fire shot from the barrel, arching over the broken fence and slamming directly into the center of the approaching crawl-rats. The impact was not a standard explosion; it was a violent release of compressed thermal energy that liquefied thirty beasts in an instant, turning the asphalt into a boiling trench of red grease.

But the horde did not slow down.

The Red Moon had stripped them of all survival instincts. Millions of mutated beasts were pouring out of the Futian District, their bodies climbing over one another to form a writhing, desperate wall of flesh and teeth that stretched as far as the eye could see. The sound of their claws against the concrete was like a steady, deafening rain.

The Searing Brimstone Mortar fired again and again, its heavy thuds vibrating through Ren Qi's boots. Every shot cleared a path fifty meters wide, but the gap was closed in seconds by the sheer volume of the incoming horde.

The pale green light of the Totem of the Iron Hearth was shrinking. It was now barely three meters wide, the green barrier humming with a high-pitched, metallic whine as the glass-fleshed hounds threw themselves against it, their bodies shattering into red dust that hissed as it touched the light.

In the lobby, the survivors were screaming, crowding toward the elevator shafts.

"Landlord!" Lin Xia's voice came through the radio, crackling with heavy static. "The western wall... the structural integrity is failing! The pressure from the corpses... they're piling up against the second-floor balconies! The concrete is cracking!"

Ren Qi climbed the steel ladder to the top of the courtyard's outer wall. He stood on the narrow concrete ledge, the red wind whipping his flannel shirt against his side.

The view from the top was a literal sea of flesh.

The streets of Shenzhen were gone, buried under a shifting, chaotic tide of red fur, bone, and iron. The scale of the calamity was beyond any standard defensive calculations. To survive this with a static barrier was impossible; the maintenance cost in mana cores would bankrupt the sanctuary in less than an hour.

To clear a deficit of this size, he needed an offset that bypassed the local geography entirely.

He looked at the thousands of monster carcasses piling up against the base of the wall. The red blood was pooling in the cracks of the asphalt, steaming under the crimson light of the moon.

"A surplus of raw material," Ren Qi murmured, his waxy face illuminated by the bloody glare. "Unallocated assets."

He reached down, his palm touching the cold concrete of the wall. He extended the reach of his talent, using his own mana to bridge the gap between his physical body and the mountain of dead flesh below.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

[Input: 150x Glass-Fleshed Hound Carcasses (Rank D) + 300x Crawl-Rat Corpses (Rank E) + 4,000kg Ruined Concrete Rubble + 2,000 Personal Mana]

[Concept: Mass Accumulation -> Kinetic Recompression]

[Exchange successful. Bypassing equivalent limitations.]

The pile of bodies at the base of the wall vanished in a sudden, silent flash of oily black light. In their place, a single, heavy sphere the size of a beer keg appeared on the ledge beside Ren Qi. The sphere was made of dark, glass-like stone, but inside, a violent violet-and-crimson light swirled like a miniature storm, pulling the loose gravel on the ledge toward it with a faint, gravitational hum.

He moved along the wall, his heavy boots crunching through the red frost. Every time he reached a section where the corpses were piled high, he activated the talent.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

[Input: 200x Mixed Monster Carcasses + 5,000kg Rubble + 2,000 Personal Mana]

[Exchange successful. Obtained Item: Star-Fall Core (Rank A - Consumable)]

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

[Input: 180x Mixed Monster Carcasses + 1x Destroyed Riot Vehicle + 2,500 Personal Mana]

[Exchange successful. Obtained Item: Star-Fall Core (Rank A - Consumable)]

By the time he reached the western corner, three of the massive, vibrating spheres sat on the ledge. The gravitational field around them was now strong enough that Ren Qi's flannel shirt was pulled tight toward the stones, and the red rain falling from the sky was bending in mid-air, curving around the spheres like water around a drain.

Below him, a massive, sixty-foot-long centipede made of fused office desks and petrified human limbs was dragging itself up the wall. Its hundreds of rusted steel legs screamed against the concrete, leaving deep, white gouges in the structure.

Ren Qi looked up at the red moon.

He could feel it—that same structural weight he had detected after returning from the Red Zone. The unseen eyes of the Producers, sitting in their sterile, high-density void lounges, adjusting their dials, watching the little humans in their little sanctuary struggle against the tide. They wanted drama. They wanted a desperate, bloody defense.

"You want a spectacle," Ren Qi whispered, his voice dry and flat. "Let us adjust the production budget."

He reached out, his waxy hands touching two of the Star-Fall Cores simultaneously. He did not target the monsters below. He targeted the unstable, high-density spatial rifts floating above the Futian Financial Center six kilometers away, using the cores as a gravitational anchor to bridge the distance.

[SYSTEM: SSS-Rank Talent: Inequivalent Exchange activated.]

[Input: 3x Star-Fall Cores (Rank A) + Ambient Spatial Friction (Futian Rift Zone) + 5,000 Mana]

[Concept: Gravitational Collapse -> Catastrophic Planetary Descent]

[Exchange successful. Bypassing equivalent limitations.]

The three spheres on the ledge dissolved into a thick, black liquid that did not fall; instead, it shot straight upward into the crimson sky like three dark arrows, piercing the heavy clouds of the Red Moon.

For a second, the world went completely silent.

The low growls of the crawl-rats, the clatter of the centipede's legs, the heavy thuds of the mortar—everything stopped. The red light of the moon flickered, turning a pale, sickly violet as the clouds began to swirl inward, forming a massive, anti-gravitational vortex directly over Skyview Garden.

It was not a sound, but a sudden, violent expansion of pressure that flattened the red fog for three miles in every direction.

From the heart of the vortex, five massive objects emerged. They were not standard meteors of rock and iron; they were towering, jagged blocks of crystallized structural steel, melted skyscraper concrete, and high-density mana nodes harvested from the ruins of previous liquidated cycles, burning with a pale, cold violet fire.

The first block, easily the size of a twenty-story office building, slammed into the center of the Fuhua Road plaza three hundred meters away.

The impact was instantaneous.

A shockwave of pale violet dust and liquefied concrete swept through the streets, vaporizing fifty thousand monsters in a fraction of a millisecond. The heat was so intense that the red rain evaporated before it could touch the ground, turning into a thick, dry steam that smelled of old sulfur and burnt tin.

Before the sound of the first impact could reach the sanctuary, the second and third blocks hit.

The ground buckled, the asphalt of the streets rising in massive, frozen waves of black glass. The sixty-foot centipede on the wall was torn from the concrete by the sheer kinetic force, its body disintegrating into grey dust before it even hit the floor of the courtyard.

The survivors in the lobby fell to their knees, their hands over their ears as the reinforced glass doors shattered into millions of tiny, harmless fragments, the pressure wave washing over them like a hot, dry wind.

Through the broken frames, they saw the Landlord.

Ren Qi stood on the edge of the wall, his dark flannel shirt soot-stained, his hand resting on the hilt of his Molecular Blade. The pale violet fire of the falling debris reflected in his flat, grey eyes, casting long, steady shadows across the ruined courtyard.

The five meteors had turned the three-kilometer radius around Skyview Garden into a smoking, glassy crater. Of the millions of monsters that had covered the streets, nothing remained but a thick, grey layer of ash that fell from the sky like silent, dirty snow.

The red moon was still there, but its light seemed thin now, diluted by the pale violet smoke rising from the craters.

Ren Qi checked his status panel.

[SYSTEM: You have successfully cleared 'The First Calamity: Red Moon' regional wave.]

[Total Liquidations: 142,500 entities]

[Regional Contribution Score: 98.4%]

[You have established absolute systemic dominance in the Futian Sector.]

Ren Qi turned away from the burning horizon. He walked down the steel ladder, his boots making a dry, rhythmic sound against the soot-covered rungs.

The survivors in the lobby did not move. They did not cheer. They huddled against the walls, their eyes wide with a quiet, petrified awe that was far deeper than the panic they had felt during the siege. They looked at him not as a landlord who managed their rent, but as something ancient, cold, and absolute—a force that could rewrite the parameters of their world with a single gesture.

Ren Qi stopped at the reception desk.

Lin Xia was still holding her clipboard, her jaw slack, her pen rolling slowly across the floor.

"Landlord..." she whispered, her voice trembling so hard she could barely form the words. "The... the horde... they're all..."

"The regional wave is cleared," Ren Qi said, his voice flat and clinical. "The lateral wall sustained four percent structural damage. The repair cost will be distributed among the active tenants in the morning."

He looked up at the broken ceiling, his gaze passing through the concrete, the red sky, and the bloated moon, directly toward the cold void where the Producers watched.

"The show is over," Ren Qi murmured. "Now, we audit the studio."
