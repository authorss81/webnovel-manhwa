# Chapter 44: Market Manipulation

The Babel Hub smelled of stagnant ambition and scorched circuitry. Ren Qi sat behind the dark, polished surface of his reception desk, his fingers tracing the edge of a stack of transaction ledgers. Beside him, Lin Xia kept her gaze fixed on the front gate, her hands trembling as she polished a piece of scrap metal that had long since lost its luster. She was trying to look busy, a habit Ren Qi had begrudgingly tolerated because it kept her from pacing.

The shop, which he had christened The Exchange, sat on the edge of the lower district. It was an unassuming structure of reinforced iron and weathered stone, but its inventory was beginning to warp the local economy. On the shelves behind him sat rows of gear that defied the scarcity of the sector: blades with razor-thin edges that held their temper despite the ambient heat, and vials of restorative serum that tasted faintly of carbonated sugar yet mended broken tissue in seconds.

The prices were competitive—absurdly so. Ren Qi had purposefully set the exchange rates to undercut every guild merchant within five blocks. It was a calculated strategy, a way to bleed the established vendors dry and force the local flow of wealth toward his own coffers.

People were starting to notice. The local Alchemist Association had sent three separate envoys this week, all of whom had left with grim expressions and empty hands. They didn't like that he was selling high-grade performance items for the price of common scrap. They wanted him to participate in the cartel, to keep the prices artificially inflated so they could all maintain their comfortable, parasitic existence.

He wasn't interested in the cartel. He was interested in the disruption.

The gate groaned as it swung open, the rusted hinges shrieking. A man in a heavy, flame-retardant trench coat stepped inside. He had the arrogant posture of a mid-tier fire mage, his hands glowing with a soft, pulsing orange hue that cast long, dancing shadows across the floor. He didn't look like a customer. He looked like an arsonist looking for a target.

Ren Qi didn't look up from his ledger. He reached for a small, handheld terminal and tapped a command, the device emitting a low, steady hum.

Welcome to The Exchange, Ren Qi said, his voice flat. We have a policy against open flames inside the building. If you are here to shop, stow the magic. If you are here for something else, I suggest you rethink your priorities.

The mage scoffed, his gaze sweeping over the racks of equipment. He sneered, a sound of wet, grating contempt.

You call this a shop? The mage laughed, a sharp, barking sound. You are dumping garbage into the market. My masters are tired of your interference. We have an agreement in this sector, and you are currently violating every single clause of it.

Ren Qi finally looked up. His eyes were cold, analytical, assessing the man’s mana flow and the structural integrity of his coat.

An agreement implies mutual benefit, Ren Qi said. You provide low-quality goods at high prices. I provide high-quality goods at low prices. The market has already decided which one it prefers. If your masters are concerned about their margins, they should learn to be more efficient.

The mage’s face twisted in rage. He snapped his fingers, and a ball of concentrated heat manifested in his palm.

I’m here to close you down, the mage spat.

He didn't wait for a response. He hurled the fire-bomb toward the back shelves, where the most valuable of the refined equipment was stored.

Ren Qi didn't move. He didn't even reach for his blade. He simply snapped his fingers once.

From the shadows, two of his spectral servants flickered into existence. Their movements were faster than thought, a blur of gray motion that intercepted the sphere of flame in mid-air. They caught the fire, their translucent forms absorbing the heat without a flicker of distress. They didn't extinguish it. They held it, the energy swirling between their hollow palms like trapped lightning.

The mage stumbled back, his eyes wide with disbelief. What? How are you controlling those things? They are high-tier constructs. You shouldn't be able to—

Ren Qi stood up, his height giving him an imposing, angular presence in the small room. He walked around the desk, his boots clicking with rhythmic, terrifying precision. He gestured to the spectral servants.

They aren't constructs, Ren Qi corrected, his voice dropping to a low, dangerous register. They are shareholders. And they don't appreciate people trying to burn their dividends.

Before the mage could react, the servants drifted forward. They didn't use force in the traditional sense; they moved through the mage’s defense as if it were nothing but air. They gripped him by the arms, his magical heat failing instantly as they drained the mana from his core. He shrieked, a high, thin sound of pure panic.

Strip him, Ren Qi ordered.

The servants moved with mechanical efficiency, removing the mage’s coat, his boots, and his jewelry. Within seconds, the man was left standing in his thin, sweat-soaked undershirt, shivering in the sudden chill of the shop.

Take him to the front sign, Ren Qi said. Tie him up. And make sure he’s visible from the street.

The mage’s pleas were silenced by a sudden, sharp pressure against his throat as the servants dragged him toward the door. Lin Xia watched, her face pale, her hands gripping the counter so hard her knuckles were white. She didn't look away, though. She watched, her eyes wide with a mixture of fear and, deeper down, a flicker of something like respect.

Ren Qi turned back to the counter, his gaze falling on the fire-bomb the servants had brought to him. He looked at it for a moment, his mind already calculating the molecular rearrangement.

[System Notification: Energy Source Detected: High-Purity Thermal Mana. Potential Conversion: Pyrotechnic Display.]

Ren Qi smiled, a thin, ghost of an expression that reached nowhere near his eyes.

If they want a show, he whispered to the empty room, we’ll give them a grand opening.

He began to manipulate the energy, folding it, twisting it, forcing it into a new, complex geometry. The heat changed from a destructive orange to a series of brilliant, cascading blues and violets. It wasn't a bomb anymore. It was a catalyst for a celebration.

Outside, the neighborhood began to gather. They saw the mage, disgraced and hanging from the shop’s sign, a placard tied around his neck that read: DISCOUNTED GOODS. The humiliation was total. Word traveled fast in the Babel Hub, and by the time Ren Qi walked out onto the front step, a crowd of scavengers, desperate players, and curious merchants had formed.

He looked at them, his posture relaxed, his hands tucked into the pockets of his vest. He didn't look like a man who had just dismantled a local power structure. He looked like an engineer presenting a finished prototype.

The market has been stagnant for too long, Ren Qi announced, his voice carrying clearly through the crisp, thin air. We are here to introduce a new standard of efficiency.

He held up the refined catalyst. With a flick of his wrist, he tossed it into the sky.

It didn't explode with a roar. It blossomed. A massive, silent display of iridescent light filled the dark sky above the district, the colors pulsing in perfect rhythm with the vibration of the tower itself. It was beautiful, a display of raw, controlled energy that left the crowd speechless.

As the light faded, Ren Qi retreated back into the shop. He closed the door, the heavy iron bolt sliding into place with a definitive, satisfying clack.

The economy of Floor 1 was in chaos. By tomorrow, the Alchemist Association would either be bankrupt or they would come crawling to him for a partnership. He didn't care which. He sat back down at his desk, his terminal flashing with a new sequence of alerts.

[System Notification: Influence Rating Increased. Market Dominance: 12%. Reputation: Notorious.]

He began to write, his stylus clicking against the glass, the sound a steady, rhythmic heartbeat in the silence of the room.

Audit Phase 10: Market manipulation complete. The local guilds have been neutralized. Preparing for the transition to the next structural tier.

He closed the terminal, the silence of the manor settling around him once more. He had the house. He had the staff. He had the market. The Babel Hub was a failing, bloated machine, and he was the only engineer on the floor.

He leaned back, watching the shadows dance on the ceiling, his mind already drifting upward, toward the next floor, toward the next, more complex, more corrupt, and more beautiful challenge of his life.

The 3000th Descent was finally, truly beginning. And he was the one who was going to write the final entry in the book.

He opened his eyes, his gaze fixed on the darkness above, his mind clear, his heart steady.

And he was the only one collecting.

The shop was quiet, the only sound the faint, rhythmic ticking of a clock that served no purpose other than to remind him of the passing seconds. Lin Xia stood by the door, her hands still trembling, but she wasn't looking at the floor anymore. She was looking at the ledger.

Mr. Ren, she began, her voice thin but steady. The Alchemists... they won't just walk away. They have ties to the upper levels. They’ll bring someone from the next floor down. Someone who isn't a simple mage.

Ren Qi didn't look up from his ledger. He reached for a glass of water, his movements calm and deliberate.

Let them, he said. The higher they climb, the more they have to lose. And the more they have to lose, the easier they are to manage.

He walked to the window, watching the city lights flicker in the distance. He could see the silhouettes of individuals moving through the streets, their lives governed by the very machine he was dismantling, piece by piece. They were cogs in a system that was grinding itself to dust, and they had no idea who was holding the wrench.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out the small, metallic coin, flipping it over his knuckles. The sound of the metal meeting his skin was a final, decisive note, a punctuation mark in the ongoing story of his rise.

The audit of the 3000th Descent was far from over. And he had a foundation to maintain.

Audit Phase 11: Market stability at 94%. Sector 4-A integration imminent. Proceeding to final asset acquisition.
