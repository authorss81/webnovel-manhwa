# Chapter 30: The Threshold of Babel

Ren Qi stood at the center of the courtyard, the golden light of the portal casting long, distorted shadows against the cracked concrete. The air around him shimmered with the residual energy of the transition, a static hum that made the teeth ache. He did not look like a man about to embark on a journey of unknown danger; he looked like a weary inspector checking the final items on a manifest.

He turned to Lin Xia, who stood a few paces back, her fingers white-knuckled as she clutched a heavy canvas bag.

Lin, he said, his voice flat and devoid of sentiment. These are the defensive variables.

He took the bag from her, reaching inside to pull out a handful of grey, jagged river stones. They looked like common debris, the kind of gravel one might find kicked into a gutter after a heavy downpour. To the uninitiated, they were nothing. To Ren Qi, they were the result of a three-hour intensive processing session.

He handed the bag back to her.

These are not stones, he stated. Each one has been structurally mapped and infused with a localized kinetic discharge trigger. If someone—or something—attempts to breach the perimeter, place these along the threshold. When pressure is applied, they do not shatter. They invert.

Lin Xia took the bag, the weight of it pulling her shoulder down. She understood the weight of his instructions. She had seen his tools transform the mundane into the lethal before.

What if they come from the air? she asked, her voice barely rising above the low-frequency drone of the portal.

The spire is calibrated, Ren Qi replied, glancing up at the bronze, rune-etched tower that now dominated the Skyview skyline. It is not decorative. It detects aerial displacement. If it moves, the spire discharges. Do not concern yourself with the mechanics of the defense, Lin. Concern yourself with the ledger. Keep the balance, and the assets will remain secure.

He turned his back on her, his gaze locking onto the swirling golden vortex of the portal. The interior was not a tunnel, but a cascading series of overlapping planes, shifting and folding like pages in a ledger that refused to stay shut.

Ren Qi took a single, deliberate step forward.

There was no sensation of falling. Instead, there was a violent shift in perspective, as if his consciousness were being dismantled and rebuilt across a vast, light-starved distance. The world of Skyview Garden, the grey ash of the ruined city, and the distant, dying hum of the Futian Financial Center vanished.

He landed on a surface that felt like polished obsidian, cold and utterly devoid of friction. The silence here was absolute, a heavy, pressurized vacuum that didn't just suppress sound but seemed to erase it.

[SYSTEM: Arrival at the Tower of Babel (Integration Hub / Multi-Server Transit Zone)]

[Condition: Antechamber Status - Transient Access]

[Warning: Local physical laws are currently in a state of flux. Administrative authority is restricted to individual assets.]

Ren Qi stood, adjusting his vest. The air smelled of nothing—not ozone, not rot, not even the stale musk of human sweat. It was the smell of a clean, unused room.

Before him stood a construct. It was not human, nor was it entirely alien. It was a shifting mass of geometric shapes—tetrahedrons and rhomboids—suspended in mid-air, rotating with a rhythmic, hypnotic precision. A singular, glowing eye of intense violet light sat at the center of the structure, tracking his every movement.

Greetings, Administrator of Sector 44-Shenzhen, the construct spoke. Its voice was not produced by air vibrating against vocal cords; it was a direct broadcast of data into his auditory nerves.

I am the Gatekeeper of the Babel Antechamber, it continued. You have arrived at the junction point of three thousand parallel descents. To proceed toward the central hub, an entry fee is required.

Ren Qi looked at the construct, his expression as flat as the floor beneath his boots. How much?

The construct spun, a dozen sharp, golden edges flashing in the dim light. Five hundred Soul Shards of high-density purity. Or an equivalent asset value based on the current market index of this node.

Ren Qi checked his internal ledger. He had none. The term Soul Shard appeared to be a high-tier currency specific to the Babel tower, something his home sector had never produced. He was, by the system's current definition, a pauper.

I possess no Soul Shards, Ren Qi said.

The construct’s violet eye narrowed. Its geometric plates shifted, a low-frequency hum vibrating through the floor. Then you are in violation of the transit protocol. Unauthorized presence will result in immediate asset liquidation.

Ren Qi didn't panic. He knelt, placing his hand on the obsidian-like floor of the Antechamber. He could feel the granular structure of the place, the way it was held together by complex, high-energy bonds. It was not a physical room; it was a simulation of structural integrity.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a handful of the grey dust he had scraped from the very floor upon his arrival. It was essentially the waste material of this room’s own structural composition.

[Input: 1x Handful of Antechamber Floor Dust]

[Concept: Void Essence -> Crystallized Soul Essence]

[Warning: Material origin is native to the hub. Conversion ratio is statistically improbable.]

[Exchange successful. Processing...]

The dust in his palm began to glow. The grey, lifeless particles rearranged themselves into a cluster of six multifaceted, violet-gold stones that pulsed with a rhythm identical to the tower’s own heartbeat.

Ren Qi stood up and held the stones out to the construct. He hadn't just converted the material; he had optimized its density. These were not merely shards; they were concentrated chunks of the room's own fundamental code.

He offered them to the construct.

The Gatekeeper’s violet eye flickered, the light dimming and brightening in a sequence that suggested a processing error. The geometric shapes that made up its body slowed their rotation, then abruptly stopped.

The logic of the system attempted to categorize the items.

[SYSTEM: Transaction Error: High-Value Asset detected. Market value exceeds entry fee by factor of 50,000%.]

The construct lowered itself, its sharp edges retracting until it resembled a smooth, metallic sphere. It extended a small, delicate prong, gently taking the shards from Ren Qi's palm.

This is... unexpected, the construct stated, its voice losing its synthesized coldness for a fraction of a second. You have provided a donation that exceeds the requirements of an entire fiscal cycle.

Ren Qi shrugged. I prefer to keep my account in the black.

The construct bowed, a motion so smooth it defied gravity. The violet eye softened, radiating a steady, warm amber light. Your account has been marked as a Priority Admin. You are granted access to the High-Tier Trade Level. Please, follow the conduit.

A wide, golden path materialized before him, unfolding across the vast, dark void of the Antechamber. It led toward a massive, spiraling structure that rose into the clouds above, a place of impossible geometry and glowing, flickering lights.

Ren Qi began to walk. The path was not a walkway; it was a conveyor of solid light, carrying him forward at a constant, uniform speed. As he traveled, the scale of the tower became apparent. He could see other platforms suspended in the dark, each one hosting administrators from other worlds. Some were armored in plates of glowing light; others were shrouded in dark, swirling clouds of corruption.

He was a minor figure in a grand machine, a structural engineer from a small, insignificant city on a small, insignificant Earth. But as he looked at the tower, he saw the stress fractures in its walls. He saw the inefficient flow of energy through its core.

He was not just a guest. He was an auditor.

He reached the first landing of the High-Tier Trade Level. A market of immense scale opened before him. Thousands of entities moved through the space, trading goods that defied the laws of physics. Items that shimmered with the energy of dying stars, biological components that whispered in the back of his mind, and crates of raw, concentrated system currency stacked as high as office towers.

He reached into his pocket, his hand resting on the matte-black handle of his utility knife.

The market was saturated, bloated, and desperately in need of a restructuring.

Ren Qi stepped off the golden conduit and onto the floor of the Babel market. A small, hovering drone drifted toward him, its sensory array scanning his biometric data.

Identify yourself, Traveler, the drone chirped. Your designation and sector of origin.

Ren Qi, Administrator of Sector 44-Shenzhen, he said.

The drone’s light blinked, cross-referencing his ID against the vast database of the tower. It hesitated, its internal processors whirring.

Administrator Ren, the drone said, its voice changing to a tone of polite, measured caution. You are listed in the High-Tier registry. Your arrival has been noted by the Prime Arbiters. May I interest you in a tour of the central exchange?

Ren Qi looked at the sprawling, chaotic market, his eyes scanning the structural weaknesses of the nearby stalls, the instability of the currency flow, and the obvious waste of resources in every direction.

No, Ren Qi said. I am here for a site inspection.

He moved into the crowd, his boots clicking against the polished basalt of the floor. Around him, the noise of a thousand worlds rose in a deafening, discordant roar, but he didn't hear it. He only heard the quiet, rhythmic ticking of a market that was waiting to be foreclosed upon.

He was Ren Qi, and for the first time since the sky had fractured, he felt like he had enough work to keep himself busy for a lifetime.

The era of the audit had finally reached the center of the world. He reached out, touching the cold, vibrating surface of a nearby pillar, feeling the flow of energy that powered the entire district. It was leaking. It was poorly managed.

He smiled, a dry, humorless expression.

This, he whispered to himself, is going to be profitable.

As he walked deeper into the Tower of Babel, the golden light of the hub shifted, the violet rifts above beginning to swirl in synchronization with his footsteps. The system, once a chaotic, governing force that had dictated the terms of his survival, now felt like a tool in his hand, a mechanism waiting for his specific, precise input.

He reached the center of the trading floor, where a massive, glowing ledger—a literal manifestation of the sector's debt and wealth—hovered in the air.

He reached out, his hand hovering over the surface of the ledger, his fingers ready to make the first, most important adjustment of his new career.

[SYSTEM: You are now within the Prime Administrative Zone.]

[Warning: The Prime Arbiters have detected your structural intervention.]

[System alert: Your actions are being monitored by the Tower Core.]

Ren Qi ignored the alerts. He adjusted his flannel shirt, checked the weight of his utility knife, and looked up at the flickering, unstable sky of the Tower of Babel.

Let them watch, he thought. A good manager always keeps the records transparent.

He pressed his hand against the ledger.

The market of the universe was about to learn the true cost of inefficiency.
