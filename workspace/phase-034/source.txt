# Chapter 34: The Merchant's Guild Notice

The Babel Hub did not sleep. It merely entered a state of low-frequency hum, the kind of tectonic vibration that settled into the marrow of one’s bones. Ren Qi leaned against the cold, metallic plating of a tavern wall in the lower district, his gaze fixed on the shifting neon streams above. His internal ledger, an invisible tally of incoming and outgoing resources, had crossed the fifty-thousand coin threshold. It was a staggering sum for a man who, until very recently, had been counting grease-stained copper bolts in a generator room.

The atmosphere in the tavern was thick with the scent of recycled spirits and the metallic tang of overheated hardware. Around him, creatures of various origins hunched over glowing drink-wells, their forms flickering as they adjusted their visual profiles to hide their true shapes. Ren Qi remained in his standard attire—the dark flannel and his signature vest—an anomaly in a room filled with chromatic robes and bioluminescent armor.

He had been nursing the same cup of synth-water for twenty minutes when the crowd parted. They did not move out of fear, but out of a calibrated deference. A man approached, his movements possessing a liquid grace that suggested heavy modification to his spinal column. He was draped in fine, charcoal-colored silks that shimmered with an oily sheen. His face was angular, almost bird-like, with eyes that glowed with the faint, persistent amber of a Truth-Seeker implant.

This is a quiet corner for such a loud ledger, the man said. His voice was melodic, perfectly modulated to carry over the ambient roar of the bar without straining his throat.

Ren Qi did not look up from his drink. He had identified the man the moment he entered the sector. This was Varrick, a representative of the Golden Scale Guild. They were the ones who managed the flow of resources in the lower levels, ensuring that no market correction—like the one Ren Qi had just initiated—went unpunished.

It is a corner, Ren Qi replied. The noise is ambient. The ledger is just arithmetic.

Varrick pulled out a stool, the chair scraping against the floor with a sound like a serrated blade. He sat, his amber eyes narrowing as they flickered, scanning Ren Qi’s biometrics. His Truth-Seeker implant whirred, a sound barely audible above the tavern music.

Arithmetic is rarely so disruptive, Varrick continued, leaning forward until his face was inches from Ren Qi’s. You have flooded the lower levels with high-grade nutrition. The local merchants are reporting a total collapse of the potion-trade index. My employers are curious, Administrator. We are wondering where a man of your profile finds the raw mass to fabricate such density.

Ren Qi set his cup down. The glass clinked against the counter with precise weight. He looked at Varrick, his expression entirely neutral, a dry slate of indifference that defied the other man’s reading of his micro-expressions.

I acquire raw materials, Ren Qi said. The process is a matter of conversion.

Varrick’s eyes flickered, the amber light pulsing faster. He was looking for the lie. He was waiting for the slip, the hesitation that would signal a hidden benefactor or a black-market factory.

Conversion requires a source, Varrick pressed. His hand moved toward his sleeve, a faint blue light leaking from the fabric. Do you manufacture these goods from thin air? Because if you do, the Guild has a different set of protocols for such miracles.

I create them from raw materials, Ren Qi repeated. The truth was absolute. He did not say where he got them, and he did not say how he processed them. He simply stated the reality of his labor.

Varrick paused, his eyes dimming. The Truth-Seeker had registered no dissonance. He stared at Ren Qi for a long moment, the air between them growing heavy with the tension of a failed interrogation. Varrick assumed, based on the lack of a lie, that Ren Qi was an alchemist of unparalleled skill—a master who had found a way to bypass the need for expensive reagents.

You are a man of few words, Varrick murmured, his tone shifting from predatory to calculating. If you have such efficiency in your processes, perhaps you are in the wrong market. Working for the Guild would offer you stability. We have infrastructure. We have security. You would not need to worry about being targeted by the Prime Arbiters if you were under our patronage.

Ren Qi let a slow, ghost of a smile cross his lips. It was a thin, humorless line.

Patronage is just another form of debt, Ren Qi said. I have no interest in accumulating more liabilities.

Varrick tapped his fingers against the counter. He had anticipated a refusal, but he had not anticipated the absolute lack of concern in Ren Qi’s demeanor.

Everyone here has a price, Varrick noted.

I do not have a price, Ren Qi replied. I have a cost.

Varrick leaned back, his interest piqued. A counter-proposal then?

Ren Qi turned his body slightly, looking Varrick in the eye. I am interested in the unmapped sectors of Floor One. I require a comprehensive chart of the subterranean supply conduits.

Varrick stiffened. Those zones are restricted, even for the Guild. We have lost countless scouts trying to map the shifting geometry of the base-level foundations. Why would you want that?

I have an audit to finish, Ren Qi said.

A heavy silence fell between them. Varrick looked at the man before him, seeing not a scavenger, but a force of nature. If he could control the flow of this bread, if he could tether this man to the Guild, he would become the most powerful merchant in the Hub.

I will provide the map, Varrick said, his voice dropping to a low hiss. In exchange, you grant the Golden Scale Guild exclusive distribution rights for your product for twenty-four hours.

Ren Qi nodded once. A standard agreement. Twenty-four hours of market control. It was enough time for the Guild to dump their remaining potion stock and force a panic-buy on his bread. It was a predatory move, but it suited his purposes.

Varrick pulled a small, glowing crystal from his robe and pushed it across the counter. It pulsed with a faint, rhythmic light. The map was stored within, a three-dimensional model of the city’s bones.

The Guild expects quality, Varrick warned, rising from his seat. Do not attempt to undercut us once the window closes.

Ren Qi took the crystal and dropped it into his pocket. I am a man of my word. My word is a ledger. It remains balanced.

Varrick lingered for a second longer, trying to see through the veil of Ren Qi’s calm, then turned and disappeared into the crowd. Ren Qi watched him go, the weight of the map now sitting in his vest pocket. He didn't care about the Guild’s profit margins. He cared about the map. The subterranean conduits were the key to the entire hub’s structural integrity. If he could find the main intake valve for the city’s power, he could reroute the flow, siphoning enough energy to stabilize his own sector back on Earth for a century.

He finished his synth-water and stood up. The tavern felt colder now, as if the air itself had thinned. He walked out into the transit lane, his boots clicking in a steady, metronomic rhythm. He did not look for trouble, but he felt the eyes of the Guild’s observers tracking him from the shadows.

He moved toward the elevators that descended into the lower depths of the tower. The Babel Hub was built on tiers, each one a testament to someone else’s ego. He was headed for the basement, where the vanity of the gods gave way to the harsh, unforgiving physics of the machine.

As he reached the lift, the system console flashed a warning.

[System alert: Restricted area access requested.]

[Identity validation: Auditor Rank 44.]

[Status: Authorization required.]

Ren Qi didn't panic. He reached into his pocket and touched the cold, jagged edge of the scrap he had salvaged from the terminal earlier. He pressed the metal against the console.

[System notification: Override initiated via salvage-logic.]

[Warning: Integrity of the sector security protocol is being bypassed.]

[Action: Unauthorized descent permitted.]

The lift doors hissed open, revealing a shaft that plunged into the absolute dark of the foundation levels. Ren Qi stepped inside, the metal of the lift groaning under his weight. He began to descend, the lights of the upper levels fading until there was only the flickering red of the maintenance beacons.

The heat increased, a dry, baking intensity that smelled of ozone and scorched earth. He looked at the map Varrick had given him, projecting it onto his wrist interface. The conduits were vast, sprawling like the veins of a living god. He traced the path to the primary regulator.

The lift stopped with a violent jolt. Ren Qi stepped out onto a grating that vibrated with the roar of a thousand turbines. This was the heart of the Hub. Everything he had seen above—the gold, the light, the vanity—was nothing but a distraction from this. Here, the reality of the descent was laid bare. He saw the threads of space and time being pulled thin, stretched to power the towers above.

He walked along a narrow catwalk, his eyes scanning the pipework. He reached the primary regulator, a massive, obsidian sphere that was bleeding energy into the surrounding space. It was leaking. It was poorly managed. It was a disaster waiting to happen.

He pulled his utility knife from his pocket. The blade hummed, a sound that resonated with the frequency of the sphere. He wasn't here to destroy it. He was here to fix it. He was a structural engineer, and the most important part of any project was ensuring the load was distributed correctly.

He pressed the blade against the surface of the regulator.

[System notification: Energy flow adjustment initiated.]

[Status: Diverting excess load to sector 44.]

[System alert: Structural load balance detected in the Hub foundation.]

The room roared. The sphere flickered, the obsidian surface cracking to reveal a core of pure, white light. Ren Qi felt the feedback, a surge of raw, unallocated energy that threatened to tear his body apart. He didn't let go. He leaned his weight into the knife, his boots grinding against the grating.

He was holding the foundation of the world in his hands.

The light engulfed him, a blinding, all-encompassing brilliance that turned the dark basement into a cathedral of pure information. He saw the structure of everything—the broken, leaking holes in reality, the desperate, dying embers of a thousand worlds. He pulled the threads tight. He knotted them. He sealed the fractures with the pure, unwavering logic of his talent.

The roar reached a crescendo, a sound so loud it defied physics, then abruptly, it stopped.

Silence descended, absolute and terrifying.

Ren Qi stood in the dim light of the basement, his hands shaking slightly. The sphere was still now, glowing with a soft, steady gold. He had done it. He had balanced the ledger.

[System notification: Global Ledger reconciled.]

[Status: Foundation stabilization complete.]

[Warning: Prime Arbiter surveillance has identified a total system reset in the foundation.]

He didn't look at the warning. He looked at his hands, which were covered in a fine, silver dust—the residue of the reality he had just repaired. He had shifted the debt. He had corrected the inefficiency. And he had, for the first time, established a direct line between his own sector and the source of the world’s power.

He turned and walked back toward the lift, his stride slow and deliberate. As he emerged from the shaft, the atmosphere in the Hub had changed. The golden lights were dimmer, more efficient, and the constant, tectonic vibration that had plagued the Hub for millennia was gone.

The people in the concourse were looking around, their faces marked by confusion and a strange, sudden sense of peace. The overbearing, aggressive energy of the Hub had been dampened.

Ren Qi walked through them, a shadow among the confused, his face as flat and calm as it had been on the day he had sat on his balcony. He had a lot of work to do. He had a foundation to maintain.

He stopped in front of the central node once more, the place where he had first accessed the core. It was quiet now, the chaotic pulsing replaced by a rhythmic, steady heartbeat.

He reached out and touched the console.

[System notification: Administrator access level increased to Tier 3.]

[Status: You are now recognized as an authorized maintenance lead for the 3000th Descent.]

He didn't smile. He didn't celebrate. He simply checked his list.

The next item on the agenda was the distribution of power to the outer fringes. The sectors that had been left to rot while the center lived in opulence were going to be the next recipients of his audit.

He adjusted his vest, tightened the strap of his blade, and walked out into the golden expanse of the City of Glass. The Hub was no longer a monument to vanity. It was a site in progress. And he was the landlord.

The sky above the Babel Hub—the shimmering, digital clouds—began to shift, turning from a stagnant, sickly yellow to a vibrant, clear blue. The system had been brought up to code.

Ren Qi kept walking, disappearing into the crowd, a man who had seen the foundation of the world and found it lacking, and had decided to rebuild it one brick at a time.

Behind him, the central node flickered once, then settled into a steady, brilliant, and utterly orderly glow. The era of the audit was over. The era of the renovation had begun.

He checked his wrist unit. The treasury stood at fifty thousand coins, and the flow of resources was now optimized. He had the blueprint of the universe in his mind, and the tools in his hands.

He looked toward the zenith of the tower, where the spires pierced the sky like needles of light, and he smiled. It was a dry, humorless expression, but it was the most genuine thing he had shown in years.

He reached the first of the vaulted doors that led to the upper tiers. The material was pulsating with a warning, but he didn't care. He raised his hand, his thumb resting on the locking mechanism, and he pushed.

The doors groaned, the sound echoing through the halls of the City of Glass like the dying breath of a god. He walked inside, and the light of the room flared, a brilliant, blinding white that erased everything he had known before.

He stepped into the heart of the machine, and for the first time in his life, he was home. He was exactly where he needed to be. He reached out, his hand hovering over the central node, the source of all the debt, the suffering, and the resources of the Three Thousandth Descent.

[System: Critical Error. Unsanctioned access to Central Core.]

[Warning: Administrator Ren Qi is attempting to rewrite the global audit protocol.]

[System alert: Prime Arbiters mobilized.]

Ren Qi didn't hesitate. He began to pull the threads, to tie the knots, to shore up the foundations of the world. He was a structural engineer. And for the first time in his life, he had the perfect, unobstructed, infinite site to work on.

The room turned white, a blinding, absolute light that erased the boundaries of space and time. He saw the structure of the world—the threads, the conduits, the desperate, leaking holes where reality was fraying.

He took his utility knife from his pocket, the blade pulsing with a hungry, absolute darkness. He pressed the flat of the blade against the core, letting the material of the knife—reconstructed from the heart of the glitch—absorb the chaotic data and ground it into something orderly, something permanent.

The core pulsed, a sound of absolute, crushing authority. Ren Qi stood his ground, his gaze locked on the shimmering, infinite data, his mind already calculating the next move.

The Babel Hub was beautiful, but it was failing.

And he was the only one in the room with the tools to fix it.

He pressed his hand against the node, and the world shifted, the very fabric of reality bending to his will. The debt was being moved. The building was being brought up to code.

This was going to be a very profitable expansion.
