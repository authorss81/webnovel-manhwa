# Chapter 31: Server 6: The City of Glass

The transition did not feel like travel. It felt like a surgical extraction. One moment, Ren Qi was standing on the concrete of Skyview Garden, the static of the portal hummed against his skin, and the next, he was standing on a floor of polished, translucent material that possessed the density of synthetic diamond but the warmth of living skin.

He took a steadying breath. The air here was processed, lacking the rot of the yellow fog or the metallic tang of the Shenzhen atmosphere. It smelled of ozone, pressurized nectar, and something else—a faint, lingering scent of scorched data.

[SYSTEM: You have entered the City of Glass. Sector: Babel Hub Level 1.]

[Status: Guest of the Prime Arbiters.]

[Note: Violence within the transit corridors is strictly prohibited. Violators are subject to immediate molecular disassembly.]

Ren Qi scanned the perimeter. His Molecular Sight activated, painting the environment in crisp, sharp vectors. The City of Glass was not a city in the conventional sense. It was a sprawling, interconnected grid of platforms suspended in a void that defied verticality. Towers of translucent, glowing material rose into a sky that functioned as a digital mosaic, constantly shifting its hue between violet, gold, and a deep, bruised indigo.

Gravity was lighter here, perhaps sixty percent of Earth-standard. He felt the shift in his own center of mass; the iron scales of his vest seemed to weigh less, granting him a fluidity of motion he hadn't experienced since the descent began.

He walked forward, his boots clicking softly against the floor. The architecture was inefficient. Massive, ornate buttresses supported structures that possessed no structural load, and energy conduits ran in erratic, serpentine patterns across the surfaces, bleeding power into the ambient light. It was a playground for gods who lacked an engineering degree.

A crowd filtered through the main concourse. It was a menagerie of survival. He saw a man with clockwork gears replacing his left arm, ticking in rhythmic synchronization with the hum of the platform. Further on, a woman moved with the unnatural, disjointed gait of a biological mutation, her skin shimmering with a pale, bioluminescent coating. They were survivors from other Earths, each shaped by the specific, cruel parameters of their own descent.

His arrival did not go unnoticed. A trio of figures detached themselves from the shadow of a glowing, translucent kiosk. They walked with the predatory, rhythmic pace of professional scavengers, their boots heavy and reinforced, their gear cobbled together from high-tech alloy scraps. They were from Earth 12, likely a sector dominated by extreme industrial output given the heavy, scorched-silicon look of their armor.

The leader, a man with a lower jaw replaced by a jagged, iron-mesh respirator, stopped in front of Ren Qi. He looked down at Ren Qi’s worn flannel shirt and the rusted scale vest. A guttural, filtered laugh emanated from his throat.

Look at this, the leader said, his voice grating and synthetic. Another one from the backwaters. Hey, Rookie. You lost? This isn't a soup kitchen.

Ren Qi stopped. He looked at the leader, then at the two thugs flanking him. His Molecular Sight deconstructed them in seconds. The leader carried a pressurized pulse-cannon, its internal seals frayed, showing a sixty-percent failure rate at maximum output. The two to his left and right were carrying electrified batons with unstable energy cores that threatened to backfire if swung with too much force.

They were walking trash fires.

I am looking for the regional administrative audit bureau, Ren Qi said, his voice calm, flat, and entirely devoid of the fear they expected to see.

The leader stepped into his personal space, the metal mesh of his respirator pressing close to Ren Qi’s face. His eyes were cold, narrowed, and predatory. He didn't answer. Instead, he reached out, his gloved hand moving toward the pocket where Ren Qi kept his utility knife.

This is a restricted zone, Rookie, the thug said, his tone dripping with mock authority. You need a transit tax to walk these floors. Hand over that knife. And the vest. Might be worth something to a collector of primitive junk.

Ren Qi didn't reach for the knife. He didn't step back. He tilted his head, his eyes tracing the stress fractures in the leader’s respirator.

Your air intake is clogged, Ren Qi noted. The filter is rated for a Grade-C environment. This air is refined to Grade-A specifications. You are currently suffocating your own lungs with recycled toxins.

The leader paused, his hand hesitating. What?

If you continue to breathe through that hardware for another five minutes, the pressure differential will trigger a rupture in your sinus cavity, Ren Qi continued, his tone that of an inspector informing a property owner of a minor maintenance oversight. It is a very inefficient way to die.

The thugs exchanged a confused look. The leader snarled, his patience evaporating. He reached for the pulse-cannon at his hip, but his movement was clumsy, hindered by the sheer weight of his own over-engineered gear.

Ren Qi’s hand moved. It was not a violent strike, but a precise, economical movement. He didn't draw his weapon. He stepped into the leader’s guard, his shoulder hitting the man’s center of gravity—the exact point of structural instability he had identified seconds prior.

The leader gasped, the air failing to pass through his damaged respirator, and he stumbled backward. As he fell, Ren Qi reached out, not to strike, but to nudge the man’s pulse-cannon. He applied a precise amount of force to the weapon's safety lock, disengaging the firing mechanism and simultaneously shorting the capacitor by touching the exposed wiring with his calloused thumb.

The cannon fizzled, a cloud of foul-smelling smoke venting from the weapon’s core.

The leader landed hard on the polished obsidian floor, his breath coming in ragged, wheezing gasps as his hardware malfunctioned.

[SYSTEM: External asset manipulation detected.]

[Warning: Administrator Ren Qi, please refrain from unauthorized hardware decommissioning.]

Ren Qi looked at the drone hovering above, then back at the sputtering thugs. He reached into his pocket and pulled out his utility knife, sliding the blade out with a single, practiced click. The black, void-like edge caught the ambient light, casting a shadow that seemed to reach deeper than the floor itself.

I am not here to fight, Ren Qi said, his voice echoing slightly in the sudden quiet of the corridor. I am here to conduct a site inspection. Please move your assets out of the way. You are creating a traffic bottleneck.

The thugs looked at the knife, then at their leader, who was currently clutching his chest, his face turning an unhealthy shade of grey. The threat of the tower’s own security protocols weighed heavier than their pride. They scrambled, grabbing the wheezing leader and dragging him away into the shifting shadows of the glass pillars.

Ren Qi watched them go, his expression unmoved. He didn't care about their bruised egos or their broken equipment. They were irrelevant variables in a larger calculation.

He turned toward the center of the City of Glass. The main conduit, a wide, pulsating ribbon of golden light, lay ahead. It was the arterial flow of the hub, the path that led to the upper tiers where the Prime Arbiters resided—the ones who truly owned the debt of these five hundred worlds.

As he stepped onto the golden path, he felt the resonance of the tower. It was vast, a structure of impossible scope, but it was fundamentally flawed. It leaked energy through every decorative arch and wasted its potential on meaningless, aesthetic posturing.

He checked his pocket once more, ensuring the weight of the utility knife was centered. He had been a structural engineer his entire life, but he had spent his career fixing the mistakes of others. Here, in the heart of the descent, he was no longer just the person who patched the walls. He was the person who decided which walls stayed standing and which ones were condemned.

He walked forward, his stride long and measured. The golden light carried him upward, past platforms where merchants shouted prices for goods he couldn't name, and past observers who watched him with eyes that had seen the collapse of galaxies.

He did not blink. He did not slow down.

The path ended at the threshold of a massive, vaulted chamber. The doors were made of a material that looked like compressed starlight, and they stood slightly ajar, as if inviting his intrusion. Behind those doors lay the central ledger of the Tower of Babel—the record of every asset, every soul, and every currency transaction since the day the sky broke.

A voice, neither male nor female but a chorus of resonant, overlapping tones, filled the space around him.

Administrator of Sector 44, the voice said. You have bypassed the initial gate with a surplus. You have navigated the transit corridor with calculated efficiency. You are a curious variable.

Ren Qi stopped in front of the massive doors. He looked at the glowing geometry etched into the surface, his Molecular Sight identifying the specific frequency required to unlatch the mechanism.

I am an auditor, Ren Qi replied. The voice in his head was merely a system projection, a user interface for the tower's core. I believe there is an error in your current account.

The voice seemed to hesitate, the ambient light in the room fluctuating in response to the statement. An error?

You are misallocating energy, Ren Qi continued, his gaze drifting to the energy conduits running along the ceiling. You are spending ninety percent of your resources on maintaining a grand, visual deception for your guests, while the foundational integrity of the sector is eroding at a rate of four percent per cycle. If this continues, the entire Babel integration will face a total structural liquidation within twelve months.

Silence followed. It was not a heavy silence, but a processing delay. The tower was calculating the probability of his assertion.

The doors groaned, the starlight material shifting as if it were cooling magma. The gap between them widened, revealing a vast, dimly lit room filled with rows of shimmering, holographic shelves. Each shelf held the records of a different world, a different Earth, a different failure.

Ren Qi stepped inside.

This is the central audit facility, the voice said, less a projection now and more an acknowledgment. Your predecessors have attempted to manipulate these records for personal gain. They were all liquidated.

I am not here to manipulate the records, Ren Qi said, walking toward the shelf labeled Sector 44. I am here to balance them.

He reached out, his hand hovering over the shelf. His fingers, now hardened by the strange, indigo-mercury blood and the constant exposure to the tower’s energy, felt a sudden, sharp pull. The shelf didn't just contain data; it contained the physical manifestation of his world's debt—the total weight of the suffering, the resources consumed, and the potential wasted during the three thousandth descent.

He didn't pull back. He grabbed the glowing node of data and pulled it into his palm.

[SYSTEM: Accessing Sector 44 Audit Log.]

[Warning: Direct interaction with global ledger is restricted to Prime Arbiters.]

[System status: User Ren Qi is attempting to initialize an override.]

The room flared, the holographic shelves turning a violent, pulsating red. Alarms, soundless but physically jarring, vibrated through his very bones. The floor beneath him shifted, the polished obsidian threatening to drop him into the dark, churning void below.

Ren Qi didn't panic. He held the node, his mind sharpening, his Molecular Sight expanding to consume the entire facility. He saw the threads of light that governed the tower, the invisible anchors that kept the multi-server integration from collapsing under its own weight. They were brittle. They were frayed.

He took his utility knife from his pocket.

The blade pulsed with a hungry, absolute darkness. He didn't use it to cut the air or to threaten the system. He used it as a tuning fork. He pressed the flat of the blade against the holographic node, letting the material of the knife—reconstructed from the Heart of the Glitch—absorb the chaotic data and ground it into something orderly, something permanent.

The red light flickered and died, replaced by a cool, steady blue.

[SYSTEM: Override successful.]

[Audit process: Initiated.]

Ren Qi stood in the center of the room, the data of his world flowing through his mind like a river of black ink. It was painful, cold, and incredibly, intoxicatingly precise. Every scrap of copper wiring, every ton of coal burned in his generator, every life lost in the courtyard of Skyview Garden—it was all here, indexed and accounted for.

He looked at the shelf again, his grip on the utility knife tightening. He saw the entry for his own sector. He saw the debt. It was astronomical, an impossible figure that would have crushed any other human soul.

But it wasn't a punishment. It was a structural load. And like any load, it could be redistributed.

He began to input his adjustments. He redirected the idle, wasted energy from the tower’s aesthetic lighting into the foundational anchors of Sector 44. He condensed the floating, unallocated currency of the sector into the physical storage banks of the Abyssal Kiosk he had brought with him. He was not cheating the system; he was auditing it, finding the loopholes that the tower’s own architects had missed.

The air in the room grew heavy, a pressure that felt like the weight of a thousand atmospheres. He knew the Arbiters were watching now. He knew that somewhere, in a chamber far above, the masters of this tower were realizing that the small, insignificant auditor from Earth 3000 had just gained root-level access to their most guarded assets.

He didn't care. He was finished with the first entry.

He turned toward the next shelf. And the next.

The work was endless, it was exhausting, and it was the most meaningful thing he had ever done. He looked at the utility knife in his hand, the black blade humming with the energy of a thousand re-written laws.

One world at a time, Ren Qi whispered into the silence of the vault. Let's see if the books actually balance.

As he reached for the third shelf, the floor vibrated with a rhythmic, thunderous boom. The entrance to the vault began to dissolve, the starlight material scattering into embers.

He didn't turn around. He knew what was coming. He could feel the change in the atmospheric pressure, the shift in the tower’s internal gravity. The Prime Arbiters were coming to personally conduct their own audit.

Ren Qi slid the utility knife back into his pocket, his hand resting on the matte-black handle. He took a deep, steady breath, feeling the cool, filtered air of the tower fill his lungs. He was ready for the conversation.

He was the landlord of Skyview Garden, the administrator of Sector 44, and for the first time in his life, he held the ledger in his own hands. The audit was not just beginning. It was the only thing that mattered.

The heavy, metallic footsteps of the Arbiters echoed in the corridor, a sound of absolute, crushing authority. Ren Qi stood his ground, his gaze locked on the shelves of data, his mind already calculating the next move. The City of Glass was beautiful, but it was failing.

And he was the only one in the room with the tools to fix it.
