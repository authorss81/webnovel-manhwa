Chapter 12: Liquidation

The heat in the lobby was no longer a temperature; it was a physical weight.

The leader, the man with the scar and the arrogance of a god who had found a lighter, held the sun in his palms. The fireball wasn't thrown. It was growing, feeding on the oxygen, turning the air stale and dry. The moisture on the marble walls evaporated. The invisible glass shards on the floor, the ones that had crippled his men, began to glow a dull, angry orange as they heated up.

Ren Qi stood ten paces away. The Cuirass of the Iron Eater was hot against his chest, the leather backing sweating oil.

"I will melt the foundations!" The leader screamed. The fire roared, drowning out the whimpers of the men bleeding on the floor. "I will turn your little shop into a slag heap!"

Ren didn't advance. He didn't retreat. He watched the mana density spike in the man’s chest. The readout from the Eye of the Void was scrolling numbers that predicted a catastrophic structural failure.

If the Pyro detonated that much energy, the support pillars would buckle. The ceiling would come down. The assets—Lin Xia, the Kiosk, the potential laborers—would be ash.

Physical intervention was inefficient. By the time Ren crossed the distance, the fire would be released.

Ren took a step back, his boot heel clicking against the base of a decorative column. He reached behind him, placing his palm flat against the cool stone of the pillar.

"System," Ren whispered.

The interface expanded in his retina.

[Accessing Building Infrastructure...]

[Sub-system: Fire Suppression.]

[Status: Active. Water Reserves: 100%.]

The building had a standard sprinkler system. Thousands of gallons of stale, tepid water sitting in copper veins above the ceiling tiles. Against a Rank B Pyromancer, water was useless. It would turn to steam instantly, scalding everyone alive.

But Ren wasn't limited to what was in the pipes.

He focused. The headache slammed into the base of his skull, a railroad spike of pressure. This was a massive exchange. He wasn't transmuting a cup or a stone. He was transmuting a river.

[Target: 5,000 Gallons of Water (Fire Suppression Loop)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren looked at the leader, whose face was twisted in manic triumph. The fireball was pulsing, ready to burst.

"Burn," the leader hissed.

"Cool off," Ren said.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

Ren didn't ask for ice. Ice was slow. He needed the absolute zero of a dead star. He visualized the molecular stillness. The cessation of all energy.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Liquid Nitrogen (Industrial Grade).]

Above them, the copper pipes groaned.

The metal contracted violently. The sound was like a gunshot—CRACK.

The leader looked up, distracted. The fireball in his hands wavered.

Then, the sprinkler heads burst.

A white fog, heavier than lead, crashed down from the ceiling.

It hit the floor and rolled.

The effect was instantaneous. The fireball didn't go out; it was suffocated. The heat energy was sucked into the vacuum of the sub-zero gas so fast that the air pressure in the lobby dropped, popping eardrums.

The roar of the fire vanished, replaced by the terrible, high-pitched shrieking of matter contracting.

The mist enveloped the leader. He didn't have time to scream. He opened his mouth, and the nitrogen rushed in. His lungs froze instantly. The moisture in his eyes crystallized.

The four men on the floor, already bleeding, stopped moving. The blood pooling around them didn't dry; it solidified into red obsidian.

Ren stood near the column, the only safe zone he had designated in the exchange parameters. He watched the white cloud churn. The temperature in the lobby plummeted from 120 degrees to -300 in three seconds.

The Cuirass on his chest clicked as the metal cooled. Frost patterns raced up the walls, covering the scorch marks.

Ren waited for the hiss to die down.

He walked into the fog.

The cold bit at his exposed skin, but the Titan’s Resilience held. His dense bones, his reinforced cells—they were an engine that ran hot. He waded through the knee-deep vapor like a god walking through clouds.

He reached the center of the lobby.

The Pyro leader was still standing.

He was a masterpiece of thermal shock. He stood with his hands raised, the ghost of a fireball still implied in the curvature of his fingers. But the fire was gone. His skin was pale blue, dusted with white crystals. His clothes were stiff boards.

He wasn't dead. Not yet. The brain takes a long time to die, even when the blood stops.

Ren stopped in front of him. He could see the terror frozen in the man’s wide, glassy eyes.

"Fire requires three things," Ren said quietly, his breath puffing out in a white cloud. "Heat. Fuel. Oxygen."

Ren tapped the man’s frozen chest with his knuckle.

It sounded like tapping a wine glass.

"I removed the heat."

Ren didn't draw his blade. He didn't need it.

He drew back his fist. The movement was sluggish, the air resistance thick, but the power behind it was absolute.

He punched the statue in the stomach.

There was no wet thud. There was a sound like a tree snapping in winter.

The structural integrity of the frozen flesh failed. The leader didn't fold; he disintegrated.

His torso shattered into a thousand chunks of frozen meat and ice. His arms fell off, shattering on impact with the floor. The head, severed from the neck, rolled a few feet and stopped, the expression of rage still locked in the cloudy ice of the face.

[Target Eliminated: Level 12 Pyromancer]

Ren looked at the pile of debris. It looked less like a human and more like spilled gravel.

He crouched down. Among the chunks of frozen gore, something glittered.

It was red, pulsing with a faint, dying heat. The nitrogen hadn't killed the magic; it had just contained it.

Ren picked it up. It was warm.

[Item: Core of the Lesser Flame (Rank C)]

[Utility: High-density fuel source. Can be exchanged.]

He stood and turned to the others.

The nitrogen was dissipating, boiling away into gas, leaving the lobby bitterly cold but visible.

The four thugs were alive. Barely.

The Field of Agony—the invisible glass—was gone, shattered by the thermal shock. But the men were in bad shape. Frostbite had turned their fingers and noses black. They were curled in fetal balls, shivering so violently their teeth clicked like castanets.

They looked up at Ren.

They saw the Landlord standing in the mist, unbothered by the cold that was killing them. They saw the pile of red ice that used to be their captain.

"P-please," one of them stammered. His lips were blue. "W-warmth."

He checked his inventory. He had food. He had water. He had medicine.

But he wasn't running a charity. He was running a business. And a business needed employees.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a receipt—a crumpled piece of thermal paper from a hardware store run he’d made a week before the apocalypse.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

Ren held up the trash.

"I need a binding agent," he murmured. "Legal. Absolute."

[Target: Thermal Receipt Paper]

[Exchange Available.]

The paper didn't burn. It lengthened. The cheap chemical coating morphed into thick, heavy vellum. The faded ink rearranged itself, turning crimson. The jagged tear at the bottom smoothed out into a perfect seal.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Contract of Indenture (Rank D)]

[Attributes: Soul-bound agreement. Violating the terms inflicts 'Cardiac Arrest'.]

Ren walked over to the man who had spoken. He held the parchment out.

"The heating is expensive," Ren said. "The rent is overdue."

The thug looked at the paper. The words were blurring in his vision, but the meaning drilled into his brain. Servitude. Obedience. Survival.

"Sign it," Ren said. "And I turn the heat back on. Refuse, and you stay here until you shatter."

The man couldn't hold a pen. His fingers were claws of ice.

"Use your head," Ren said.

He dropped the contract onto the frozen puddle of blood next to the man's face.

"Press your forehead to it."

The thug didn't hesitate. Survival was a biological imperative that overrode dignity. He slammed his face into the parchment.

The blood on his skin hissed as it touched the magic vellum. The paper glowed red.

[Contract Sealed. New Tenant Registered: Unit 001.]

Ren looked at the other three.

Ten minutes later, the lobby was warm.

Ren had cycled the HVAC system, flushing the nitrogen gas and pumping hot air from the basement furnace. The ice on the walls was weeping, turning to puddles of water.

The four new tenants sat in a row against the reception desk. They were wrapped in wool blankets Ren had pulled from the inventory. They were eating raw cup noodles, crunching the dry bricks like rats. They were too hungry to wait for boiling water.

Lin Xia stood by the elevator, watching them. She held her kitchen knife, but her knuckles were white. She kept glancing at the pile of thawing meat in the center of the room.

"You killed him," she said. Her voice was flat.

"I liquidated him," Ren corrected. He was wiping the frost off the Abyssal Kiosk. "He was a liability. These four are assets."

He turned to the new hires.

"Names are irrelevant," Ren said. "You are Unit 1, 2, 3, and 4. Your job is resource gathering. You go out into the fog. You bring back cores, metal, and biomass. You deposit them in the machine."

Unit 1, the man who had signed first, looked up. The frostbite on his nose was ugly, but the warmth was bringing the color back to his face.

"And if we don't come back?"

"Then the contract kills you," Ren said. "Range limit is two kilometers. Try to run, your heart stops. Try to attack me, your heart stops. Steal from the inventory..."

Ren tapped his chest.

The men nodded. It was a simple social contract. Cruel, but simple. In a world of chaos, clarity was a luxury.

Ren turned back to the Kiosk.

The machine was battered. The Pyromancer’s fire had scorched the paint, and the glass front was gone. But the core mechanism—the spatial anchor—was intact.

[Target: Broken Abyssal Kiosk + 200 lbs of Thawing Biomass (Ex-Pyromancer)]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren placed his hand on the dented metal. He looked at the pile of the leader’s remains.

"Repair and Upgrade."

[Biomass sufficient. Genetic trait 'Flame' detected.]

The pile of meat dissolved. It didn't rot; it liquefied into a red slurry that flowed across the floor, defying gravity, climbing up the legs of the Kiosk. The metal absorbed it.

The dents popped out. The scorched black paint deepened, taking on a reddish, ember-like sheen. A new sheet of glass grew from the frame, thick and smoky.

The machine hummed. This time, the sound wasn't a mechanical whir. It was the crackle of a hearth.

[Exchange Successful.]

[Abyssal Kiosk Upgraded (Rank A+)]

[New Feature: Thermal Processing. The Kiosk can now cook raw food items instantly. 'Hot Meal' quality increased.]

Ren patted the machine.

He looked at Lin Xia.

"Put them to work," he ordered. "Clear the bodies from the entrance. Fix the door. Then send them to the parking garage. I want fifty cores by sundown."

Lin Xia looked at Ren. She looked at the men, who were scrambling to stand up, terrified of disobeying.

She realized then that the monsters outside were not the scariest things in Skyview Garden.

"Yes, Landlord," she whispered.

Ren took the elevator down to B1.

He needed isolation. The use of the nitrogen exchange had drained him. His mana reserves were scraping the bottom of the barrel, leaving him with a hollow, scraping sensation in his chest.

He entered the control room and locked the heavy steel door.

He sat in the mesh chair.

The silence was heavy. He watched the monitors. He saw Unit 1 and Unit 2 dragging the remains of the invisible glass victims toward the back exit. He saw Unit 3 and 4 nailing plywood over the melted front door.

He should feel something. He had just enslaved four human beings and pulverized a fifth. A week ago, he was a graphic designer who worried about his credit score. Now, he was a warlord.

He searched for the guilt. He waited for the nausea.

Instead, he felt a cold satisfaction. The numbers on the ledger were balanced. The territory was secure. The threats had been converted into resources.

"System," Ren said into the dark.

[Status: Fatigue (Moderate)]

[Humanity: ...Calculating...]

Ren opened his eyes. The prompt hung there, blinking.

[Trait Evolution Available.]

[Current Talent: Inequivalent Exchange (Rank SSS)]

[Trigger Condition Met: 'Merciless Liquidation'.]

[Do you wish to sacrifice 5 points of Charisma to increase Calculation Speed?]

Charisma. The stat that governed social interaction, empathy, and leadership appeal.

Calculation Speed. The ability to assess value, trajectory, and outcome.

[Exchange Successful.]

[Processing Power: +50%]

Ren felt a shift in his brain. The lingering fatigue didn't vanish, but the emotional fog cleared. The last vestige of doubt about the indenture contracts evaporated.

It wasn't cruelty. It was math.

He pulled up the Architect’s Blueprint.

The first floor was secure. The basement was secure. The tenants were productive.

It was time to expand vertically.

Ren stood up. He walked to the corner of the room where he kept his personal stash.

He picked up the Chief Engineer’s Key.

There was a sub-level below B2. The schematics didn't show it, but the key vibrated when he looked at the floor.

If the B1 level held the power, what held the foundation?

Ren walked to the door.

The descent wasn't over.

The stairwell to the sub-levels was older than the rest of the building. The concrete here was rough, unpainted. The air smelled of wet earth and ancient iron.

His boots made no sound. He was moving differently now—efficiently. No wasted energy on impact.

He reached the bottom. A single iron door, welded shut. No keyhole.

But Ren didn't need a keyhole. He had the Inequivalent Exchange.

He placed his hand on the rust.

[Target: Welded Iron Seal]

[Exchange Available.]

The metal groaned. The rust flaked away, revealing bright, shining steel underneath. The weld seam turned liquid, running down the door like tears.

Darkness greeted him. Not the absence of light, but a thick, physical darkness that swallowed the beam of his flashlight.

From the dark, a sound emerged.

It sounded like a heartbeat. A massive, slow, geological heartbeat.

[Warning: You have entered the Root Chamber.]

[Danger Level: Unknown.]

[Resource Detected: Divine Corpse Fragment (Dormant).]

Ren smiled in the dark.

The myths said the Descent of Mystery began when the gods died and fell to earth. Most people thought the dungeons were invasions. Ren knew better. They were graveyards.

And Skyview Garden was built on top of a tomb.

"Harvest time," Ren whispered.

He stepped into the abyss, the darkness closing around him like a velvet coat.
