# Chapter 16: The Three-Second Future

The rain tasted like pennies. Copper and old dust, washed down from the atmosphere by a sky that looked like a bruised peach.

Ren Qi stood on the lip of the Hualong Financial Tower, forty stories up. The wind here was a physical thing, a bully that shoved against his chest and tried to strip the heat from his skin. He didn't button his jacket. He was running hot. The mana in his blood was cycling, agitated, reacting to the sheer density of violence occurring in the plaza below.

From this height, the players looked like glitter spilled on gray concrete. Spells flashed—red fireballs, blue ice spears, the sickly green of amateur healing circles. They were screaming. The sound didn't reach him as individual voices, but as a collective, high-pitched drone, like a hive of bees set on fire.

In the center of the glitter was the mountain.

The Steel-Scaled Wyvern.

It wasn't a dragon from a storybook. It was an aerodynamic nightmare of biological engineering. Its scales were the color of gunmetal, interlocking plates that shifted with the fluidity of mercury. It didn't breathe fire; it breathed concussive sound. Every time it opened its jaws, the air distorted, and a dozen players in the front row simply turned into red mist and shattered bone.

Ren watched. He chewed a nutrient bar that tasted like sawdust and vanilla.

"Inefficient," he muttered.

The players were trying to tank it. They were holding up shields made of wood and scrap iron against a creature that weighed as much as a loaded Boeing 747. They were dying because they thought this was a game where the numbers would save them. They thought if they had enough Constitution, they could catch a falling building.

Ren checked his own numbers.

[Tool: Deep-Sea Carbon Fiber Fishing Rod]

[Target: Dorsal Plate 4 (Damaged)]

He wasn't here to kill the boss. Killing the boss was a logistical nightmare. It required thousands of rounds of ammunition, hours of time, and the willingness to sacrifice a hundred human lives as meat shields.

Ren wasn't a hero. He was a scavenger with a better business model.

He looked at the fishing rod in his hand. He had looted it from a high-end sporting goods store on Day 2. Japanese import. meant for hauling marlin out of the Pacific.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

Ren gripped the cork handle. The magic surged, cold and biting.

[Input: High-Tensile Fishing Rod + 100g Steel Wire + Motor from Power Drill.]

[Concept Adjustment: Retraction -> Agression.]

The rod twisted in his hands. The carbon fiber thickened, turning matte black. The reel expanded, the gears grinding together and fusing into a high-torque winch. The hook at the end of the line elongated, growing barbs, turning into a cruel, three-pronged claw.

[Output: Pneumatic Grapple Launcher (Rank D).]

[Tensile Strength: 2 tons.]

Ren weighed the launcher. Heavy. Good.

Below, the rhythm of the battle changed. The Wyvern stopped thrashing. It planted its claws deep into the asphalt, crushing a minivan flat. It reared its head back. The gills along its neck flared open, exposing glowing, superheated vents.

"It's stunned!" someone shouted over the roar of the wind. "Attack now! Hit the throat!"

"Idiots," he whispered.

They saw an opening. Ren saw a lung intake. It wasn't stunned. It was inhaling.

He needed to move. But he needed to know where.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out the Broken Stopwatch. He had exchanged a functioning Rolex for it three days ago. The Rolex told time. The broken stopwatch, heavily infused with Void energy, told something else.

[Item: The Chrono-Anchor (One-Use)]

[Effect: Burn the item to view 3 seconds of the immediate future.]

This was his edge. Not strength. Information.

Ren squeezed the watch. The glass face shattered against his thumb.

The world turned grey. The rain stopped in mid-air, hanging like suspended diamonds.

[Future +0.5s]: The Wyvern’s throat glows brighter. The players charge.

[Future +1.5s]: The Wyvern snaps its head down. Not a sonic scream. A beam of concentrated plasma. It sweeps left to right.

[Future +2.0s]: The rooftop Ren is standing on—specifically the gargoyle to his left—is sheared off by a stray bolt of energy.

[Future +3.0s]: The Wyvern recoils from the heat. The third scale on its neck, loosened by the heat expansion, lifts by three inches. A gap.

Time crashed back into the present. The rain hit his face.

Ren didn't hesitate. He didn't run away. He ran forward.

He sprinted toward the edge of the roof, the Pneumatic Grapple heavy in his arms.

Below, the players screamed "Attack!" just as Ren had seen. They surged forward.

And the Wyvern fired.

A beam of white-hot energy bisected the plaza. It was silent for a fraction of a second, simply because it was louder than the human ear could process. Then the thunder hit.

The front line of players vanished. Vaporized. The concrete melted into glass.

The beam swept upward, wild and uncontrolled.

Ren was already moving. He dove. Not away from the edge, but off it.

He plummeted into the void between the buildings just as the stone ledge he had been standing on exploded into dust. The heat of the passing beam singed the hair on his arms, a sudden, dry fever.

He was falling. Gravity grabbed his stomach and pulled.

Wind roared in his ears. The ground was rushing up—a collage of fire and ruin.

Ren twisted in mid-air. He wasn't looking at the ground. He was looking at the beast.

The Wyvern was recoiling, shaking its massive head, smoke pouring from its mouth. The heat had expanded its armor.

On the left side of the neck. A plate of steel the size of a dinner table, lifted slightly at the edge. The pink, raw flesh underneath was visible.

Ren raised the launcher.

His calculation speed attribute screamed the variables. Velocity: 40 m/s. Windage: 12 knots East. Target distance: 85 meters.

He pulled the trigger.

The grapple shot out. The steel cable sang a high, thin note as it unspooled.

The three prongs slammed into the gap under the scale. They punched into the soft meat beneath.

The Wyvern roared—a sound of genuine pain this time. It thrashed, jerking its head back.

Ren’s fall stopped with a bone-jarring yank. He wasn't anchored to a building. He was anchored to a god.

The momentum swung him in a wide, terrifying arc over the plaza. He was a pendulum, and the Wyvern was the pivot point.

"Retract!" Ren screamed, slamming his palm against the winch control.

The motor whined. The cable shortened.

He wasn't pulling himself up. He was pulling the scale off.

The Wyvern shook its head violently, trying to dislodge the parasite stinging its neck. The motion generated tons of centrifugal force.

Ren gritted his teeth. His shoulder felt like it was dislocating. He fed mana into the launcher, reinforcing the steel, reinforcing his own grip.

"Give... it... to me!"

The sound was like a gunshot.

The connecting tissue tore. The steel scale, loosened by heat and ripped by the mechanical torque, detached.

He fell holding the cable, and at the end of the cable was a curved slab of alien metal weighing two hundred pounds.

He was dropping toward the alleyway behind the plaza.

He hit the fire escape of the adjacent building. His boots skid on the metal grating. He tumbled, slamming his shoulder into the brickwork, the wind knocked out of him.

The dragon scale hit the alley floor below, embedding itself into the asphalt like a guillotine blade.

Ren lay on the fire escape, gasping. His vision swam with black spots. His right arm was numb. His ribs felt like a bag of gravel.

Above him, the Wyvern was rampaging, looking for the gnat that had bitten it. It breathed fire onto the building Ren had jumped from, melting the facade.

Ren forced himself up. Pain was information. It told him he was alive.

He scrambled down the ladder, sliding the last ten feet.

He landed next to his prize.

The scale was still hot. It radiated a dull, throbbing heat that dried the rain on his face. It was dark grey, iridescent in the gloom, the edges razor-sharp.

[Item: Scale of the Steel Wyvern (Rank A)]

[Properties: Magic Resistance (High), Physical Hardness (Extreme).]

Ren touched it. It burned his fingertips.

"Beautiful," he rasped.

This wasn't just armor. This was capital.

He heard footsteps. Heavy. Running.

Ren turned. Three players had followed the trajectory of his fall. They emerged from the smoke at the mouth of the alley. They looked ragged, scorched, their armor dented. But their eyes were bright with greed.

"He got a drop!" the lead player shouted. He was holding a two-handed axe. "He stole a drop from the Boss!"

"Hand it over!" the second one yelled, raising a crossbow. "That belongs to the raid group!"

Ren looked at them. Then he looked at the massive scale embedded in the road.

"You think this is a drop?" Ren asked. His voice was steady, despite the trembling in his legs. "The boss isn't dead."

"Doesn't matter," the axe-man stepped forward. "You stole it. PvP rules are active in the zone. Drop it or we kill you."

Ren laughed. It was a dry, hacking sound.

"PvP rules," Ren mocked. "You're still playing a game."

He placed his hand on the hot metal of the scale.

The players hesitated. They expected him to put it in his inventory. But the scale was too big for a standard low-level inventory slot.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

The air in the alley grew heavy. The pressure dropped. The rain seemed to shy away from Ren’s hand.

"What's he doing?" the crossbowman whispered.

Ren didn't look at them. He looked at the structure of the scale. The lattice of carbon and iron. The trace amounts of dragon blood—which was essentially liquid mana—still clinging to the root.

He didn't want a shield. A shield was defensive. A shield was for people who planned to get hit.

He wanted an industry.

[Input: Wyvern Scale (Rank A) + 500 Mana.]

[Concept: Protection -> Replication.]

He pushed his will into the exchange. He forced the system to understand that he didn't want the object; he wanted the knowledge of the object. He was trading the fish for the lesson on how to fish.

The scale began to glow. Not red, but a blinding, data-stream blue.

The metal liquefied. It didn't melt; it unraveled into geometric shapes, floating in the air like a deconstructed puzzle.

"Stop him!" The axe-man charged.

The floating metal swirled and shot into Ren’s chest. It didn't hurt. It felt like drinking cold water on a hot day.

In its place, a scroll hovered in the air. Parchment made of light.

[Exchange Successful.]

[Output: Blueprint - Dragonbone Alloy Plating (Scaleable).]

[Description: A method to fuse standard steel with monster bone to replicate the molecular structure of a Wyvern's defense. 60% effectiveness of the original material. Mass producible.]

He hadn't made armor for himself. He had acquired the ability to turn scrap metal into tank armor. He could outfit an army. Or, more likely, sell the plating to the highest bidder until he owned the city.

The axe-man stopped, confused. The giant loot was gone.

"Where is it?" he demanded. "What did you do?"

Ren held up the scroll. Then he crushed it in his hand, learning the recipe permanently. The light faded.

"I invested it," Ren said.

He looked at the three men.

"Now," Ren said, unclipping the empty grapple launcher from his belt and letting it drop with a heavy clatter. "You can leave. Or you can become customers."

"Customers?" The crossbowman lowered his weapon slightly.

"I sell survival," Ren said. "Dragon-grade armor. Coming next week to Skyview Garden. Bring cores. Bring food. Don't bring weapons."

He turned his back on them and walked deeper into the alley.

"Wait!" the axe-man shouted. "You're just walking away?"

"The Wyvern is turning around," Ren called back over his shoulder. "I saw it three seconds ago. It's going to breathe on this alley in about..." he checked his internal clock. "...four seconds."

The three players looked at each other. They looked at the sky.

A shadow fell over the alley entrance. The roar of a jet engine started directly above them.

They ran. They didn't run after Ren. They ran for their lives.

Ren ducked into a sewer maintenance hatch just as the world behind him turned white.

The sewers were quiet. They smelled of rot and stagnation, which was a significant improvement over the smell of burning flesh above.

Ren leaned against the damp brick wall, sliding down until he was sitting on the walkway. He checked his mana.

He opened a bottle of water he pulled from his inventory—one of the few he had left. He took a sip.

His hands were shaking. The adrenaline dump was hitting him now. The crash.

He closed his eyes. He could still see the beam of light vaporizing the crowd. He could feel the heat.

Fifty people dead in a blink.

He felt... nothing. No grief. No horror. Just a cold assessment of the facts. They stood in the fire. They died.

Ren opened his eyes and looked at the damp darkness of the tunnel.

"Am I broken?" he asked the silence.

The silence didn't answer.

"Good," Ren decided. "Broken things are sharp."

He pulled up his interface. He looked at the new recipe.

[Dragonbone Alloy Plating]

Requires: Steel, Bone Powder, Heat Source (1500°C).

He had steel. The city was made of it.

He had heat. He had the Heart of the Magma Turtle sitting in his warehouse, waiting for a use.

Ren thought of the pile of corpses the Wyvern was currently making in the plaza.

"Resources," Ren whispered.

He needed to get back to Skyview Garden. Lin Xia would be panicking. The Units would be restless.

He had a product launch to prepare for.

Ren navigated the tunnels by memory and map. He avoided the red zones where the Rat Kings nested. He bypassed the submerged sections where the amphibious things waited.

Thirty minutes later, he climbed the ladder to the basement of Skyview Garden.

He pushed the heavy iron hatch open.

Home. Or what passed for it.

The basement was warm. The hum of the generator was a comforting white noise.

Unit 1 was standing guard at the bottom of the stairs. He snapped to attention when Ren emerged, though his eyes widened at Ren’s appearance—soaking wet, scorched hair, smelling of ozone and sewer muck.

"Landlord," Unit 1 said. "You're back."

"Status," Ren said, stripping off his ruined jacket.

"Quiet," Unit 1 reported. "The fighting downtown... we can hear it. It sounds like the world is ending."

"It ended three days ago," Ren said, walking past him. "This is just the afterparty."

He walked up the stairs to the lobby.

Lin Xia was there. She was sweeping again. It seemed to be her coping mechanism. When the world threatened to eat her, she cleaned the floor.

She stopped when she saw him.

"You're alive," she said. It wasn't a question. It was relief mixed with disbelief.

Ren walked to the counter. He placed the heavy pneumatic grapple launcher on the desk.

"Put this in storage," he said. "It needs repairs. The winch is stripped."

Lin Xia looked at the device. "Did you... did you kill it? The thing on the news?"

Ren went behind the counter. He poured himself a cup of lukewarm coffee from the pot.

"No," Ren said. "I robbed it."

He took a sip of the coffee. It was bitter.

"How much steel scrap do we have in the warehouse? The car doors, the rebar, the pipes."

"Tons," she said. "The Units have been dragging it in all week. You said to hoard metal."

"And the bone meal? From the grinder?"

"Three barrels. Why?"

Ren looked at her. His eyes were tired, dark circles bruising the skin beneath them, but the pupils were sharp, focused.

"We are changing the inventory," Ren said. "Tomorrow, we stop selling just food."

He tapped the countertop.

"Tomorrow, we start selling immortality."

He brought up the holographic display of the Dragonbone Alloy. The blueprint rotated in the air, complex and beautiful.

"Unit 2 and 3 have welding experience, correct?"

"Yes," Lin Xia said, staring at the hologram. "Unit 2 was a structural engineer. Unit 3 worked in a body shop."

"Wake them up," Ren said. "And fire up the forge."

"Now? It's midnight."

Ren looked toward the glass doors of the lobby. Beyond the barricades, the night sky flickered with the distant flashes of the boss fight. The screams were faint now, drowned out by distance and walls, but they were there.

"The market is hot," Ren said softly. "People are dying. They are scared. They are realizing that their clothes are made of paper and their skin is soft."

He finished his coffee.

"When the sun comes up, the survivors will be desperate. They will give anything to feel safe again."

Ren walked toward the elevator to his private quarters.

"We're going to be rich, Lin Xia."

"Is that what matters?" she asked quietly to his back.

Ren paused at the elevator doors. He looked at the reflection of his own face in the polished steel. He looked like a ghost. A hungry ghost.

"In the apocalypse," Ren said, pressing the button, "wealth is the only stat that counts. Because wealth buys exchange. And exchange buys life."

"Get the forge running. I want the first prototype ready by breakfast."

The doors closed, sealing him in the box.

Ren leaned his head back against the metal wall and closed his eyes.

He had risked everything for three seconds of sight.

He brought his hand up. He focused.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

[Input: Memory of Fear + 10 Mana.]

[Output: Adrenaline Suppressant.]

A cool wash of calm flooded his system. His heart rate slowed. The tremor in his hands vanished.

He checked his inventory one last time.

The Recipe sat there. A golden ticket.

Ren Qi smiled in the darkness of the elevator.

The Wyvern was still out there, slaughtering the city. A catastrophe. A tragedy.

Ren checked his ledger.
