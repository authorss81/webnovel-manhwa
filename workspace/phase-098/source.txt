# Chapter 38: First Clear Announcement

The transition from the swamp into the threshold of the next tier felt like moving from a humid, overheated machine room into a walk-in freezer. The air became thin, brittle, and carried the sharp, metallic tang of absolute zero. Ren Qi stood for a moment, letting his lungs adjust to the sudden shift in atmospheric pressure. He looked back over his shoulder. The portal had already sealed, leaving nothing behind but the faint, shimmering memory of the green-hued garden he had left in his wake.

He checked the internal log. The Developer Mode was still active, its HUD overlay casting a pale, wireframe net over the new environment. The City of Frozen Static was not a city in the conventional sense. It was a sprawling, subterranean network of conduits and data-bays that had been flash-frozen in a moment of catastrophic system failure. Everything was covered in a thick, translucent frost that hummed with a low-frequency vibration.

He moved forward. His boots crunched against the floor, which was composed of discarded memory plates. Each step sent a ripple of white noise through the area, a sound that felt like sandpaper against his eardrums. He did not mind the cold. It was a structural stability he could appreciate. The previous sector had been messy, rotting, and organic. This was clean. This was math.

He navigated the labyrinthine corridors with the help of his overlay. The Data Lotus skill was proving to be an essential tool. He could see the structural weak points in the environment, the heat signatures of lurking entities, and the potential resource caches hidden behind walls of solid data-ice. He stopped at a junction where two major conduits crossed, their metal shells twisted and fused by the cold.

He didn't just walk past them. He paused, placing a hand on the frosted metal. He could feel the latent energy still pulsing within the lines, a flicker of power that had been caught in the freeze. He used his talent, channeling a small portion of his essence into the junction. It was a delicate operation, like fine-tuning a watch mechanism during a blizzard.

[System Notification: Energy Rerouted. Conduit Stability: Increased by 15%.]

The feedback was minimal, a tiny prickle of static on his fingertips. It was enough. He continued forward, the pace of his exploration increasing as he grew accustomed to the unique physics of the sector. The City of Frozen Static was littered with the remnants of previous explorers—shattered equipment, frozen husks, and the occasional, discarded scrap of high-tier armor. He didn't stop for the trash. He was looking for the core, the nexus of the sector’s operations.

He encountered his first resistance an hour later. They were Glitch Wraiths—semi-corporeal entities that looked like distorted, flickering images of people who had once walked these halls. They didn't growl; they simply appeared as visual artifacts, their movements stuttering through space. When they got close, they emitted a high-pitched, digital scream that made his teeth ache.

He didn't bother with his blade. He raised his hand, his fingers twitching in a series of sharp, rhythmic gestures. He saw the corruption in their code, the way their reality was fraying at the edges.

Input: The Glitch Wraith’s visual definition. Output: Resolution Reset.

The effect was cleaner than his previous battles. The Wraiths didn't burst or melt. They simply smoothed out, their distorted, jagged edges turning into solid, still statues. They froze in place, their terrified expressions rendered in high-definition stillness. He walked past them, tapping one on the shoulder. It shattered into fine, crystalline dust, revealing a small, glowing shard of high-density memory.

He harvested the shards as he moved, his collection of high-tier materials growing with every encounter. The deeper he delved, the more the architecture began to change. The walls were no longer raw metal; they were lined with rows upon rows of glowing, amber-lit display panels. Each one flickered with a fragment of a lost reality—a child’s birthday party, a military briefing, a sunset over a ruined city.

It was a library of ghosts. Ren Qi ignored them. Data was just weight. He only cared about the information that would help him climb.

He reached the primary intake center, a massive, domed chamber that served as the heart of the sector. In the center, suspended in a cage of lightning, hung the Plague King. It was a monstrous, towering entity, its form a patchwork of animated bones and decaying, necromantic energy. It was a corruption that shouldn't exist in a digital environment, an infection that had somehow clawed its way into the system.

The Plague King roared, a sound that wasn't audio, but a direct injection of pure, unadulterated suffering. The lightning in the cage flared, turning a sickly, poisonous green. The creatures in the room—a horde of skeletal, server-rack drones—swarmed toward him, their movements jerky and mindless.

Ren Qi didn't wait for them to reach him. He had anticipated the scale of the threat. He reached into his vest, pulling out a small, glass vial he had prepared back in the Babel Hub. It was the Holy Water Grenade. He didn't just throw it. He calculated the trajectory, the wind speed of the cooling vents, and the rotational velocity of the Plague King’s aura.

He tossed the vial. It flew in a perfect arc, shattering against the Plague King’s chest.

Input: Holy Water Grenade + Plague King’s Aura. Output: Purification Nova.

The reaction was instantaneous. A blinding, white-gold light erupted from the epicenter, a shockwave of pure, cleansing energy that vaporized the skeletal drones before they could even move. The Plague King let out a sound that transcended digital screams, a gutteral, panicked hiss as its necromantic form began to unravel. The rot was purged, the corruption turned to ash, and the chamber was flooded with a sterile, golden radiance.

The Plague King didn't stand a chance. It wasn't a fight; it was a cleanup operation. As the entity disintegrated, it collapsed into a pile of blackened, useless circuitry. Amidst the debris, a single, glowing object remained—a key, forged from a dark, iridescent material that seemed to drink the light of the room.

He walked over and picked it up. It felt warm, a sharp contrast to the biting cold of the chamber.

[System Notification: First Clear of the Whispering Swamp Hidden Instance achieved.]

[System Notification: You have been awarded the title: Architectural Auditor.]

[System Notification: Global Broadcast active. Sector 1-A has been fully reconciled by Player: Ren Qi.]

The broadcast echoed through the system, a deep, reverberating chime that reached every ear in the Babel Hub. In the bustling merchant markets, the quiet taverns, and the crowded transit lanes, the name appeared in golden, flickering text above the status panels.

The silence that followed the notification was brief, replaced immediately by an explosion of activity. The global chat, usually a stream of mundane requests and desperate trades, was suddenly flooded.

Is that the guy from the Skyview Garden?

First clear of a hidden instance? That requires a perfect structural assessment, it's impossible.

The hackers are probably working overtime to figure out his build.

Ren Qi ignored the noise. He didn't open the chat. He didn't care about the fame. He looked at the key in his hand, his eyes focused on the intricate, geometric carvings that marked its surface. It was a Master Key, a remnant of the Hub’s original design, meant for the architects themselves.

He tucked it into his pouch and turned to leave. He had a Tier 2 to reach, and the information provided by the Plague King’s data-remnants pointed to a much larger, more complex infrastructure waiting for him above.

As he walked toward the exit, the walls of the chamber began to recede, the sector stabilizing into a productive, efficient loop of data. The ice was retreating, replaced by a warm, amber glow that felt like the sunrise of a new, better-managed world.

He didn't look back at the clearing or the shattered remains of the boss. He kept his eyes on the path ahead, his stride steady and his mind already calculating the logistics of the next renovation. The system was large, and it was broken, but he was the landlord now. And it was time to get to work.

He approached the rift at the edge of the chamber. It wasn't the jagged, unstable tear he had seen before. This one was precise, a clean geometric window into the next layer of the descent. He stepped through, leaving the cold and the ghosts behind.

The transition deposited him onto a platform of solid, white light, floating in the center of an endless, shifting nebula of data. Below him, the layers of the Hub spiraled down like the tiers of a great, inverted tower. He was at the top of the first segment, but the tower was far from finished.

He stood on the platform, his vest hummed softly, and for the first time, he checked his status panel, not for the system, but for himself.

[Class: Structural Architect]

[Talent: Inequivalent Exchange (SSS)]

[Assets: 85,000 Credits, Master Key (Tier 1-3)]

[Objective: Audit Sector 2-A: The Archive of Frozen Time]

He looked at his stats. The numbers didn't matter. The efficiency did. He had accomplished more in a few short cycles than most had in years of the Descent. He felt the weight of the task ahead, a mountain of technical debt waiting to be balanced.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, portable terminal, a relic he had salvaged from the basement of the Skyview Garden. He didn't use it for communication. He used it as a notepad, a place to document the structural flaws of the universe itself.

He wrote: Renovation Phase 1: Complete. Efficiency metrics within acceptable parameters. Proceeding to Phase 2.

He closed the terminal and stepped onto the next bridge. It stretched out before him, a long, shimmering path that vanished into the heart of the next sector. He didn't feel the need for rest. He felt the cold, sharp clarity of a man who knew exactly what he was doing and exactly how much it was going to cost to get it done.

The nebula pulsed around him, the colors shifting from deep violet to a soft, inviting blue. He took a breath, the air clean and crisp, and began the long walk toward the next challenge. The audit was not just a job; it was a way of life, and he was the only one qualified to finish it.

As he walked, a new notification pulsed on his wrist, a soft, insistent chime that cut through the silence of the nebula.

[System Notification: Priority Alert: Prime Arbiter Command has issued a warrant for your immediate termination.]

[Status: Bounty Added: 500,000 Credits.]

Ren Qi smiled, his lips barely twitching. He didn't break his stride. He didn't look for cover. He simply reached into his pocket, pulled out a small, metallic coin, and flipped it into the air, catching it with a snap of his fingers.

He was expensive. That was a good sign. It meant he was finally doing something that mattered.

He stepped onto the first platform of the next sector, the ground beneath his feet shifting as it recognized his presence. He wasn't just an intruder anymore. He was the owner of the audit.

The light of the next sector welcomed him, a harsh, unforgiving, and perfect white. He stepped into it, his blade ready, his ledger balanced, and his mind entirely, beautifully empty of doubt. The work was waiting, and he was the only one with the keys to the building.

He walked into the brightness, the figure of the Architect fading into the vast, shimmering expanse of the 3000th Descent, ready to rewrite the foundation of the world, one line of code at a time. The City of Frozen Static was behind him, and the Archive of Frozen Time lay ahead, a massive, monolithic structure of trapped history.

He gripped his blade, the edge of which now glowed with a faint, iridescent light that seemed to pulse in time with the very rhythm of the tower itself. He was ready for the next layer. He was ready for the next set of accounts to be settled.

The audit had reached the second floor. And the rent was due.
