# Chapter 35: Preparation for Floor 1

The Babel Hub functioned on a cadence of predatory efficiency. Ren Qi sat on a crate in the periphery of a junk-merchant’s stall, the map of the subterranean levels glowing faintly on his wrist display. Most of the creatures circulating through the hub were looking for the same thing: a path to the higher tiers, a way to accumulate enough resources to transcend the grueling baseline reality of the descent. They sought the easy kills in the Safe Meadows, areas where the monsters were little more than animated dust-puppets that dropped copper scraps and minor experience orbs.

Ren Qi was not interested in the meadows. He stared at the jagged, red-hued lines of the Whispering Swamp, a territory marked with a warning icon that made the display on his wrist unit pulse a sickly, cautionary violet. It was a high-risk sector, a place where the air itself was rumored to erode the cognitive functions of those who breathed it too long. For anyone else, it was a death trap. For a man who viewed reality as a series of structural load-bearing problems, it was merely an environment with high stress-variables.

He rose, his boots making a dull, grounded sound on the metal plating. He needed armor. The Cuirass of the Iron Eater had served him well during the initial chaos in Skyview Garden, but the structural integrity was beginning to waver after the recent exposure to the central core’s feedback. He walked deeper into the bazaar, ignoring the stalls draped in iridescent silks and enchanted baubles, heading instead to a corner where the air tasted of molten slag and rust.

A merchant with four mechanical arms sat behind a desk constructed from recycled spaceship hull-plating. He was welding a piece of blackened metal with a torch that emitted a silent, freezing blue flame.

I need a base-layer, Ren Qi said, stopping at the edge of the counter. Something capable of handling environmental trauma.

The merchant didn't look up, his extra appendages continuing to work with a synchronized, rhythmic precision. A Rusted Knight’s Breastplate? I have one in the back. It is from a derelict epoch, full of structural scars. It will cost you five hundred Babel Coins and a source of material decay.

Ren Qi didn't haggle. He pulled five hundred coins from his ledger—a drop in the ocean of his current treasury—and placed them on the desk. From his internal storage, he retrieved a handful of oxidized copper bolts he had collected from the floor of the maintenance shaft.

The merchant paused, his four arms snapping to a halt. He swept the coins and the bolts into a hidden compartment, then turned to pull a heavy, jagged piece of metal from a pile of scrap. It was archaic, covered in a patina of green and brown corrosion, the metal pitted like a porous bone.

[Item: Rusted Knight’s Breastplate (Grade: Poor)]

[Property: Provides negligible defense. Currently prone to catastrophic structural failure.]

Ren Qi took the plate. It felt cold, heavy, and inherently broken. He didn't take it to an armor-smith. He stepped behind a partition, placing his hand over the center of the corroded iron. He didn't think about the knight who had once worn it, nor the battle it had lost. He thought about the weight of the grief that had permeated the iron during its centuries of abandonment. He channeled the Inequivalent Exchange talent, feeling the familiar, ravenous pull in his marrow as he fed the concept of his own cold, analytical resolve into the metal.

[Exchange initiated. Input: Rusted Knight’s Breastplate + 500 Babel Coins + Emotional Residue.]

[Concept: Resilience. Grade: High-Efficiency.]

[Exchange successful. You used rusted scrap to retrieve the Alloy of Sorrow.]

The rust didn't fall away; it refined, sinking into the metal to create a dark, matte surface that seemed to drink the ambient light. The breastplate reshaped itself, the edges sharpening into a clean, geometric fit that hugged his torso with an unsettling, absolute precision.

[Item: Alloy of Sorrow (Grade: Rare)]

[Property: Automatically absorbs emotional distress and ambient thermal energy to reinforce structural integrity. The more the user is pressured, the harder the surface becomes.]

Ren Qi donned the armor, securing the straps over his flannel shirt. It felt like a second skin, heavier than iron but shifting with his movements as if it were made of liquid shadow. He checked his reflection in a shard of mirror hanging near the stall. The armor was grim, dark, and devoid of ornamentation, a perfect match for his own detached, utilitarian presence.

He moved toward the main gate leading to Floor 1. The area was crowded with parties, most of them sporting glowing gear and laughing with the false confidence of those who hadn't yet seen their friends liquidated by a random gear-shift in the dungeon architecture. Groups of ten or twenty, all boasting about their planned routes, their support classes, and their defensive synergies.

They looked at him as he approached the queue, a lone man in a dark, matte vest with a blade at his hip. Some smirked. Others whispered, their voices carrying through the static-heavy air of the hub.

Look at the lone wolf, one of them said, a man dressed in bright, polished plate armor. He thinks he’s a soloist.

He’ll be back in five minutes, if he survives the loading screen, another laughed. It’s suicide to enter Floor 1 without at least a healer and a shield-bearer.

Ren Qi didn't glance their way. He stood in the single-player lane, his posture rigid, his eyes fixed on the shifting geometry of the portal. He wasn't playing a game; he was filing a report.

When the gate finally groaned open, a swirling vortex of shimmering white light, Ren Qi stepped forward. He didn't check his party members because he had none. He didn't prepare a defensive buff because he had his resolve. He stepped into the light, and for a fleeting second, the chaos of the Babel Hub vanished, replaced by the crushing silence of an unmapped, pristine world.

The transition was less like a leap and more like being compressed through a needle. When the pressure subsided, he was standing in a forest, but it was nothing like the greenery of the old world. The trees were tall, spindly pillars of translucent wood that pulsed with a faint, rhythmic heartbeat. The ground was covered in a thick, silver moss that felt like velvet under his boots.

He looked around. There was no sound, only the faint, ominous rustle of the leaves above.

[System alert: Entering Sector 1-A: The Whispering Swamp.]

[Objective: Locate the structural intake conduit of the lower foundation.]

He took a breath. The air smelled of damp earth and something electric, like a thunderstorm trapped in a jar. He checked his map. He was leagues away from the path the groups were taking. He was in the deep, the part of the swamp where the geometry was still raw, unformed, and potentially lethal.

A creature shifted in the shadows nearby. It was a lurker, a mass of wet, writhing tentacles and eyes that blinked in staggered intervals. It didn't attack immediately; it hovered, testing the air, its sensors likely confused by the lack of fear radiating from Ren Qi.

Ren Qi didn't wait for it to make the first move. He drew his Molecular Blade, the dark edge hummed with a low, hungry sound that resonated with the environment. He stepped forward, his movements economical, a ghost in the shifting light of the swamp.

He needed to map the conduits, and he needed to secure the territory. If this place was going to be part of his renovation, it needed to be up to code.

He lunged. The lurker shrieked, a sound like grinding metal, but it was already too late. Ren Qi’s blade cut through the creature’s central node with the precision of a scalpel. It disintegrated into a fine, grey mist before it even touched the moss.

He continued into the swamp, the trees closing in behind him. He wasn't lost. He was just beginning the audit of the first floor. And the floor, he noted with a grim, dry satisfaction, was already beginning to tremble.

He walked deeper, his footsteps silent on the silver moss. The Whispering Swamp was not just a forest; it was a breathing mechanism, a vital organ of the Hub’s foundation. As he traced the path to the intake conduit, he realized the trees weren't just trees. They were massive, living pipes.

He stopped in front of one, placing his hand against the translucent bark. He felt the vibration of the energy flowing through it, a frantic, inefficient pulse that threatened to overload the surrounding sectors.

He took out his utility knife, the blade vibrating with a dark, absolute energy. He wasn't going to just pass through this swamp. He was going to restructure it.

He pressed the blade against the wood.

[System notification: Structural modification initiated.]

[Warning: Major ecological shift detected in Sector 1-A.]

[System alert: Prime Arbiters notified of unauthorized foundation alteration.]

The ground beneath him heaved, a massive, tectonic shift that sent ripples through the silver moss. The trees groaned, their pulses quickening, and the very air began to distort as the reality of the swamp fought to maintain its form.

Ren Qi didn't flinch. He leaned into the tree, his hand anchored, his mind calculating the stress fractures, the flow of energy, and the distribution of the load. He was the landlord, and the infrastructure was finally coming under new management.

A sound filled the air, a deep, resonant boom that vibrated in his teeth. A guardian of the swamp, a massive, armored construct of stone and vines, began to rise from the ground a few hundred meters away. It was an elite entity, a Tier 3 guardian, and it was moving toward him with the speed of a falling mountain.

Ren Qi didn't look at the creature. He looked at the blade in his hand, at the pulse of the tree, at the ledger of the world. He had a job to finish, and the guardian was just another variable in his calculation.

He smiled, a dry, humorless expression.

This was going to be an efficient harvest.

The guardian roared, a sound that shattered the nearby trees, sending shards of translucent wood flying through the air. Ren Qi stood still, the Alloy of Sorrow on his chest hardening as it absorbed the sheer, crushing pressure of the creature’s aura.

He waited until the last possible second, his eyes tracking the guardian’s trajectory, the way its massive, stone-clad limb swung down with the intent to crush him into the silver moss. He stepped to the side, his movement a blurred, fluid shift that barely registered against the backdrop of the swamp.

He drove his blade into the guardian’s knee, the dark, hungry edge shearing through the stone and vine as if they were made of paper. The creature stumbled, its massive frame tilting, but it didn't collapse. It swung its other arm, a wide, sweeping arc that caught Ren Qi’s shoulder.

He was thrown back, slamming into a stand of trees, but the Alloy of Sorrow flared, turning the impact into energy that rippled through the armor’s weave. He stood up, shaking off the debris, his face as calm as the day he had sat on his balcony in Shenzhen.

The guardian prepared another strike, its core glowing with a blinding, emerald light.

Ren Qi reached into his vest, pulling out a small, metallic cylinder he had scavenged from the Hub’s trash-bins. It was a gravity-anchor, a discarded piece of technology that was meant to hold decorative statues in place.

He didn't use it as a weapon. He used it as an anchor.

He threw it at the guardian’s feet, the device humming as it activated, creating a localized, super-dense gravitational field. The guardian was pulled down, its massive body slamming into the ground with a force that cracked the earth for miles.

Ren Qi walked toward the immobilized entity, his blade ready. He didn't hate the guardian. He didn't fear it. He simply viewed it as an obstacle that had been incorrectly placed in the path of the renovation.

He climbed onto the creature’s back, moving toward the glowing core. The guardian writhed, its limbs shaking the ground, but Ren Qi was a static point in the chaos. He reached the core, his hand hovering over the flickering emerald light.

[System notification: Energy signature stabilization initiated.]

[Status: Reallocating core power to administrative ledger.]

The guardian’s light died, the emerald fading to a dull, grey stone. Its massive form stilled, the vines turning brittle and turning into dust.

Ren Qi stepped off the remains, checking his wrist unit. The treasury didn't jump, but the flow of power to his sector increased by a measurable, significant margin. He had successfully tapped into the source of the Floor One maintenance cycle.

He looked at the map again. The path to the primary intake conduit was now open, the obstacles cleared by the shift in the foundation. He kept walking, his stride measured and deliberate, moving toward the heart of the forest.

The sky above began to clear, the swirling, sickly mists of the Whispering Swamp thinning to reveal a pale, artificial sun. He had done it. He had brought the swamp into compliance.

He continued his trek, his eyes on the horizon, where the spires of the next tier awaited his audit. He was the landlord of the 3000th Descent, and the foundation was just the beginning. There were other tiers to fix, other systems to optimize, and a whole world to bring up to code.

As he walked, the forest around him began to change. The silver moss turned to a vibrant, healthy green, and the trees grew sturdier, their pulses steady and rhythmic. He hadn't just defeated the guardian; he had healed the sector.

He stopped at the edge of a ravine, looking across at the next expanse of the swamp. It was a vast, sprawling landscape of untamed, inefficient growth.

Ren Qi stood there, a solitary figure in a world he was rapidly making his own. He checked his blade, the dark edge still humming with potential.

The renovation was ahead of schedule. And he had a feeling that the Prime Arbiters were going to be very unhappy with the final report.

He stepped off the ledge, falling into the mist below with the calm, precise grace of an auditor who knew exactly where he would land. The era of the audit was over, and the era of the landlord was in full, terrifying swing.

Behind him, the forest stood silent, a perfectly ordered system of light and energy, waiting for the next step in its long, overdue repair.

He landed on the moss of the next valley, his feet finding purchase on the solid, reliable ground. He looked up at the spires of the next tier, his eyes narrowed.

The next audit was going to be even more profitable.

He took a step, then another, his boots clicking in a steady, metronomic rhythm that set the pace for the rest of the world.

He was Ren Qi, and he was home.
