Chapter 7: The Glitch in the Stairwell

Ren Qi woke up because his neck hurt.

He was sleeping sitting up on the sofa in Unit 2403, the Molecular Blade resting across his knees like a steel blanket. The apartment was cold. The kind of cold that settles in the drywall and radiates out, smelling of damp plaster.

He checked the time on his phone. 06:14 AM.

The battery was at 12%. The signal bars were crossed out. The old world was dying quietly in its sleep.

Ren stood up and cracked his back. He walked to the kitchen. The blue-and-white porcelain bowl sat on the counter. He thought about hot noodles, about the salt and the fat, but his stomach turned. The adrenaline hangover was real.

He drank a glass of water from the Purification Spirit supply. It tasted like ozone and cold iron.

He walked to the balcony. The concrete extension he had built last night—the bridge to the loot cache—was still there, a gray tongue sticking out into the mist.

The fog was thicker today. It wasn't just a weather event anymore; it was a rising tide. It churned against the glass of the lower floors, swirling with currents that had nothing to do with wind.

Ren touched the Ring of the Hoarder on his finger. It was cold, simple, unpolished iron.

He spent ten minutes cleaning the apartment. Not sweeping dust, but loading fuel.

He touched the broken toaster. Store.

The stack of old magazines. Store.

A dead potted plant. Store.

A drawer full of tangled cables and dead batteries. Store.

The items vanished, sucked into the dimensional pocket. They weren't trash anymore. They were currency.

Ren checked his gear. The Prometheus Zippo was full. The Molecular Blade was charged. The Specter-Sight Scope hung around his neck.

He looked at the Architect’s Blueprint hovering in his vision.

[Territory: Skyview Garden, Block B]

[Status: 1/24 Floors Cleared.]

"Time to evict the neighbors," Ren muttered.

The stairwell was a concrete throat.

It smelled of unwashed bodies and ammonia. The emergency lights were dead, leaving the shaft in a gloom that the daylight couldn't penetrate.

Ren moved silently. He didn't stomp. He rolled his feet, heel to toe, the way he’d learned from the skill book yesterday.

Floor 22. A bloodstain the size of a dinner plate, but no body.

Floor 21. The door was barricaded from the inside. Ren left it. If they were quiet, they could live.

At the 20th floor, he found a Ghoul.

It was gnawing on a fire extinguisher. The creature looked like a human that had been stretched on a rack, skin gray and tight over elongated bones. It heard Ren. It turned, hissing, dropping the red metal cylinder.

Ren didn't slow down.

He activated Shadow Step.

He was a blur in the gray light. He appeared behind the Ghoul before the creature’s hiss had finished. The Molecular Blade hummed a low, discordant note.

The Ghoul’s head slid off its neck. It hit the concrete with a wet slap.

Ren didn't stop to loot the core. It was trash mob loot. He kept descending.

The rhythm of the descent was hypnotic. Turn left, descend eight steps, landing, turn left, descend eight steps. The repetition was numbing.

Ren paused. The air pressure changed. It wasn't a wind; it was a heaviness, like the gravity had dialed up by five percent. His ears popped.

He looked at the door. A rusted "16" was stenciled on the metal.

Turn left. Descend eight steps. Landing. Turn left. Descend eight steps.

The door was painted green. A scuff mark shaped like a boot heel marred the bottom corner.

Turn left. Descend. Turn left. Descend.

He looked at the door. Green paint. Boot scuff on the bottom corner.

He looked up the stairs. The shadows stretched upward, silent.

He walked down another flight. He counted the steps. Sixteen steps total. A standard flight.

He reached the landing.

Ren stood very still. The Mental Fortress in his head flagged an anomaly, suppressing the rising spike of panic. Panic was inefficient. Panic wasted oxygen.

He walked to the door. He touched the green paint. It was cold, gritty.

He took the Molecular Blade and carved a deep 'X' into the metal of the door. The steel curled away like butter.

He turned and ran down the stairs. He took them three at a time, vaulting the railing at the landing.

He hit the next floor.

The 'X' was there, fresh and gleaming silver against the green paint.

Ren stepped back. He looked over the railing, down the central shaft of the stairwell.

Usually, you could see the spiral of railings going down into the dark.

Now, he saw... nothing.

Not darkness. Nothing.

The space simply ended ten meters down. The visual data terminated in a smear of gray, like a video game texture failing to load. He looked up. Same thing. The stairs above him looped back into the gray smear.

"Non-Euclidean," Ren said. His voice sounded flat, absorbed instantly by the concrete. "A spatial loop."

He wasn't on the 15th floor. He was in a pocket dimension. A error in the system’s integration.

He sat down on the stairs.

He checked his phone. The clock was frozen at 06:42.

"Okay," Ren said to the empty air. "You want to play logic games."

He closed his eyes. He focused on the sensation behind his eyelids, the heavy, cold weight of the Eye of the Void.

The headache was instantaneous. It felt like someone had driven a railroad spike into his frontal lobe. Ren gritted his teeth, forcing his eyelids apart.

To the Eye of the Void, the stairwell wasn't concrete and steel. It was a wireframe of neon blue lines, a structural mesh of reality. The fog wasn't mist; it was a deluge of binary code, zeroes and ones cascading like rain.

And the loop was visible.

The stairs didn't go down. They twisted. The space below the 15th floor curved upward, impossibly, sewing itself back into the ceiling of the 15th floor. It was a Möbius strip made of architecture.

Ren scanned the loop. He looked for the seam.

Everything has a seam. Nothing in this new world was perfect. The System was powerful, but it was rushing. It was pasting a new reality over the old one, and sometimes, the glue didn't hold.

In the corner of the landing, hovering about six feet off the ground, was a fracture.

It looked like a dead pixel on a monitor. A tiny, jagged square of absolute blackness where the blue wireframe failed to connect. A coding error.

Ren deactivated the Eye. He gasped, wiping a trickle of blood from his nose. The headache throbbed, a dull bass drum in his skull.

He looked at the corner. To his normal eyes, there was nothing there. Just empty air and dust motes.

But he knew where the weak point was.

He couldn't cut it. You can't cut a hole in a hole. He couldn't burn it.

He needed to crash it.

He needed to introduce so much contradictory data into that single dead pixel that the local reality driver collapsed and rebooted.

He opened his inventory.

He scrolled through the junk he had hoarded.

Dead Potted Plant. No.

His eyes landed on a small, corrosive item.

[Item: Corroded Lithium Battery (Trash)]

[Description: An old AA battery. Leaking acid. Zero charge remaining.]

Ren pulled it out of the void. It was sticky, covered in white crust.

"Energy void," Ren whispered.

He held the battery up.

[Target: Corroded Lithium Battery]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren focused. He didn't want a weapon. He didn't want a tool. He wanted chaos. He wanted the mathematical opposite of order.

The battery didn't glow. It darkened. The light in the stairwell seemed to bend toward it, sucked into the small metal cylinder. The white crust turned into a shifting, digital static.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Logic Fragmentation Grenade (Rank C)]

[Attributes: Single-use. Upon detonation, releases a pulse of raw, uncompiled data. Causes local reality destabilization. Highly effective against spatial anomalies and illusions.]

Ren held the grenade. It wasn't metal. It felt like holding a ball of vibration. It hummed against his palm, making his bones itch.

He looked at the corner of the landing. The invisible dead pixel.

He pulled the pin—which was just a loop of static—and threw the ball.

There was no explosion. No fire. No shrapnel.

There was a sound like a violin string snapping inside his ear.

The walls of the stairwell rippled like water disturbed by a stone. The green door to the 15th floor stretched, elongating into a grotesque rectangle, then snapped back.

The gray smear at the bottom of the stairs shattered.

Ren was thrown backward. Gravity failed for a second, then reasserted itself with a vengeance. He slammed into the concrete steps, the wind knocked out of him.

He lay there, gasping.

The air smelled of burning plastic and ozone.

He rolled over. He looked down the stairs.

The loop was gone. He could see the flight descending to the 14th floor. The 14th floor sign was yellow.

Reality had patched itself.

Ren sat up. He grabbed the railing to pull himself upright. His hands were shaking. That was too close. If the loop hadn't broken, he would have starved to death walking down the same sixteen steps forever.

He took a drink of the spirit-water. He needed to keep moving.

But then he heard it.

It came from below. Far below.

It was heavy. Rhythmic. It wasn't the scuttling of a ghoul. It wasn't the wet slide of a slime.

It sounded like a pile driver hitting the earth.

Ren moved to the railing. He looked down the central shaft.

The grenade had broken the loop. But the pulse of "uncompiled data" hadn't just stayed on the 15th floor. It had traveled down the shaft, an acoustic and energetic shockwave.

It had rung the building like a bell.

Ren raised the Specter-Sight Scope. He dialed the magnification to max. He stared down into the darkness of the lobby, twenty floors below.

The frost in the lobby was breaking.

He saw the thermal signature first.

It wasn't the Spider. The Spider was dead, turned into a blueprint.

This was something else.

It was climbing the stairs.

Through the scope, Ren saw a shape rounding the curve of the railing on the 4th floor.

It looked like a man. But it was too wide. It wore a suit of armor, but the armor wasn't steel. It was stone. Blocks of granite held together by black vines. It carried something in its hand—a slab of concrete that looked like it had been ripped from a highway divider.

[Target: The Gatekeeper (Level 15)]

[Status: Awakened by Seismic Disturbance.]

[Note: A guardian entity. Attracted to breaches in spatial law.]

Ren lowered the scope.

The Spider was Level 10, and it had almost brought the building down. This thing was five levels higher, and it was climbing the stairs.

It was coming for the source of the anomaly. It was coming for him.

Ren looked at the narrow stairwell.

Fighting a tank in a phone booth.

"Bad terrain," Ren muttered.

He looked up. He was on the 15th floor. His fortress was on the 24th.

He had traps on the 24th. He had the Turret.

He couldn't go down. He had to go up.

Ren turned and began to climb.

He didn't run. He managed his stamina. But the heavy thuds from below were getting louder. Faster. The thing sensed him moving.

Ren reached the 18th floor. His legs burned. The Shadow Step stamina drain from earlier was compounding with the physical exertion.

He needed to slow it down.

He stopped at the 18th floor landing. He looked at the door.

He looked at his inventory.

He had the Ring of the Hoarder. He had junk.

Ren pulled out a refrigerator.

He had looted it from Unit 2304 ten minutes ago, just to test the capacity of the ring. It was a double-door stainless steel model, heavy as hell.

The fridge crashed onto the landing, blocking the stairs leading down.

It wouldn't stop the Gatekeeper. But it would annoy it.

Below him, the sound of metal being crumpled like tin foil echoed up the shaft. The Gatekeeper hadn't moved the fridge; it had walked through it.

Ren gritted his teeth.

He reached the 24th floor. He burst through the fire door into the hallway.

He slammed the door shut. He used the Molecular Blade to fuse the lock, melting the steel into a solid lump.

He ran down the hall to Unit 2403.

"Turret!" Ren shouted. "Wake up!"

On the balcony, the automated sentry whirred to life. The black metal stalk extended, the barrel swiveling toward the hallway door.

Ren dived into his apartment. He didn't close the door. He wanted the door open. He wanted a kill box.

He overturned the heavy mahogany table in the living room, using it as cover. He crouched behind it, leveling the Molecular Blade.

The sound of footsteps stopped at the fire door at the end of the hall.

Then, a groan of metal.

The fire door didn't open. It bulged.

A fist made of granite punched through the steel. Then another. The door was ripped from its hinges and tossed aside like cardboard.

The Gatekeeper stepped into the hallway.

It was huge. Its shoulders brushed the ceiling tiles. The black vines connecting its stone armor pulsed with a purple light. It dragged the concrete slab sword behind it, carving a deep gouge in the carpet.

It had no eyes. Just a smooth stone visage.

It turned its head. It sensed the spatial distortion of the Ring, the residual energy of the Exchange.

It looked at Unit 2403.

It was like watching a landslide accelerate. The floorboards cracked under its weight.

"Fire!" Ren commanded.

On the balcony, the Sentry Turret unleashed hell.

The turret didn't fire bullets. It fired condensed bolts of kinetic force.

The shots hammered into the Gatekeeper. Stone chips flew. The creature stumbled, pushed back by the sheer impact.

But it didn't stop. It raised the concrete slab, shielding its core. The kinetic bolts smashed against the slab, pulverizing it, but the monster kept coming.

Ren looked at the Inequivalent Exchange interface.

He needed a finisher.

He looked at the pile of junk he had dumped on the floor earlier.

A microwave. A bag of fertilizer. A bottle of bleach.

His eyes locked on the bag of fertilizer and the bleach.

Old world chemistry. Chlorine gas. Explosion.

But with his Talent...

Ren grabbed the bleach. He grabbed the fertilizer.

He jammed them together.

[Target: Ammonium Nitrate + Sodium Hypochlorite]

[Exchange Available.]

The monster was five meters away. The turret was overheating.

"Exchange!" Ren screamed.

The bottles dissolved. The white powder and the liquid merged, twisting into a dark, volatile sphere of green gas contained in a fragile bubble.

[Exchange Successful.]

[You have obtained: Essence of the Trench (Rank C)]

[Attributes: Concentrated chemical warfare. Corrodes organic and inorganic matter instantly.]

He reappeared in the air, directly above the charging Gatekeeper.

He slammed the green sphere down onto the creature's neck, right where the black vines were exposed between the granite plates.

Ren kicked off the creature's shoulder, backflipping away just as the bubble burst.

A cloud of virulent green smoke erupted.

The Gatekeeper didn't roar. It made a sound like rocks grinding together in a mixer.

The smoke ate the vines. It ate the stone. It turned the granite into sludge.

The creature’s arms fell off. The concrete slab dropped, cracking the floor.

The main body collapsed, dissolving into a steaming pile of mud and gravel.

Ren landed in his living room. He rolled, coming up behind the mahogany table.

The turret spun down.

The hallway was silent, save for the wet bubbling of the Gatekeeper's remains.

Ren stood up. He walked to the doorway.

The hallway was ruined. The walls were scorched by the chemical gas.

But amidst the sludge, a golden light shined.

Ren walked into the chemical haze. His Purification Spirit water in his system fought off the toxins, burning in his veins.

He reached down into the mud.

He pulled out a heavy, golden key.

[Item: The Master Key (Rank S)]

[Attributes: Can open any lock, physical or digital. Can lock any door permanently.]

Ren stared at the key.

He looked at the elevator shaft. He looked at the stairs.

"No more visitors," Ren said.

He walked to the fire door frame—the one the monster had ripped open.

He held up the key. He didn't need a keyhole.

A barrier of golden light slammed down, sealing the 24th floor off from the rest of the stairwell.

Ren slumped against the wall.

He slid down until he hit the floor.

He looked at the Master Key. He looked at his shaking hands.

The 24th floor was secure.

Now, he just had to figure out what to do with the other twenty-three.
