Chapter 15: The Currency of Secrets

The scream outside wasn’t human. It was the sound of a surprised animal realizing the trap had already snapped shut.

Ren Qi stirred the cup of instant beef noodles with a plastic fork. The steam rose, smelling of artificial fat and MSG, curling against the cold blue light of the monitor bank.

On Screen 1, the thermal feed showed a cluster of heat signatures pressed against the first defensive wall. They were glowing red-orange against the cool grey of the street. But their hands—the hands that had grabbed the top of the steel barrier to vault over—were cooling rapidly.

The Field of Agony. The invisible glass shards Ren had exchanged earlier coated every inch of that metal.

To the naked eye, the barrier looked like rusted steel. To the flesh, it was a belt sander made of diamond dust.

"My fingers!" someone shrieked. The audio pickup on the external totem was high-fidelity. It caught the wet, tearing noise of skin parting from bone. "It cuts! Don't touch it!"

Ren took a bite of the noodles. Slightly undercooked. The center of the noodle block was still crunchy. He chewed methodically.

Lin Xia was huddled under the reception desk. She wasn't looking at the screens. She was pressing her hands over her ears.

"Fear," Ren corrected. "Check the egg."

Lin Xia hesitated, then crawled out far enough to peek at the void inventory display Ren had projected onto the wall.

"Sixty-two," she whispered. "It... it jumped."

Ren watched the monitor. The heat signatures were pulling back. The mob was confused. Pain does that; it overrides the tactical brain and activates the lizard brain. They were bunching up, seeking safety in numbers, which was exactly the wrong thing to do in a kill box.

The leader, the Rock Skin user, pushed through the crowd. On the thermal, he was a dense, cool blue. His skin wasn't radiating heat like the others. Stone insulation.

He swung a sledgehammer. CLANG.

The barrier shuddered. The Field of Agony stripped the rust off the hammer head, but the rock man didn't flinch. His hands were stone. He couldn't be cut.

"Come out!" The Rock Man roared. He hit the wall again. The steel groaned, a dent appearing in the center. "I know you're in there eating! I can smell it!"

Ren swallowed the noodles.

"He has a good nose," Ren murmured.

He turned away from the physical threat. The wall would hold for another four minutes. The Field of Agony would bleed the grunts dry before they crossed the courtyard. He had time.

He swiveled his chair to the secondary terminal. The Global Chat.

While the local world was burning, the digital world was screaming.

[Global Channel: District 9]

[User8821]: HELP. There's something in the water pipes. It came out of the faucet. My cat is gone.

[DragonSlayer_Mike]: Looking for party. Level 3+. Tank build. No noobs.

[User1102]: Does anyone have antibiotics? My daughter scraped her leg. It’s turning green.

[User1102]: Please. I have gold. I have a watch.

The text scrolled by at a dizzying speed. Desperation. Panic. Noise.

Ren watched the flow. He wasn't looking for cries for help. He was looking for the signal in the static.

He adjusted the filter settings.

Keywords: Trade, Info, Boss, Coordinates.

The stream slowed. Most of it was junk. But one message caught his eye. It repeated every thirty seconds, precise as a metronome.

[Fox]: Selling intel. Rare Spawns. Boss Timers. Safe Zones. Currency: Meds or Cores. DM for menu.

Ren narrowed his eyes.

Fox. A simple handle. No "Slayer," no "God," no numbers. Just a predator.

Ren opened a direct channel.

The reply was instantaneous.

[Fox]: Payment upfront for browsing. 1 Rank F Core.

Professional. Ren liked that. In a world where people were killing for a stale loaf of bread, charging an entry fee filtered out the waste.

Ren placed a small, jagged core from a sewer rat onto his desk.

"Exchange," he whispered.

[Target: Rank F Core]

[Recipient: User 'Fox' (Digital Transfer Protocol)]

The core dissolved into blue light and vanished into the screen.

[Fox]: *Received. Welcome, customer.

1. Local Map (Safe Routes) - 5 Cores.

2. Resource Cache Locations - 10 Cores.

3. Rare Monster Spawn (Live) - Negotiable.

4. World Boss 'Thunder Calamity' Timer - Bid.*

Ren stared at item number four.

The System announcements had been vague. Great threats bring great rewards. A World Boss wasn't just a big monster. It was a pinata filled with S-Rank gear, unique skills, and enough XP to jump ten levels instantly.

But fighting one alone was suicide.

Unless you knew exactly when it was weakest.

[Ren]: Item 4. Price?

[Fox]: Not taking cores for that. I need a Tier 3 Healing Salve. The blue stuff. System shop price is 500 points. If you have it, we talk.

Ren leaned back. Tier 3. That was military-grade regeneration. It could reattach a severed limb if you applied it fast enough. Nobody had 500 system points this early in the game. You got points for achievements, and most people were still trying to figure out how to swing a bat.

But Ren didn't need points.

He checked his inventory. He had raided the pharmacy section of the convenience store on day one. He had tubes of aloe vera gel, antifungal cream, and a generic bottle of 'Cooling Burn Spray'.

He pulled out the burn spray.

[Target: Lidocaine Burn Spray (Expired)]

[Target Attribute: Numbing, Cooling.]

He needed more potency. He grabbed a handful of the 'green herbs' Unit 4 had brought back from the park. They were weeds, mostly, but the System tagged them as Trace Vitality.

"Inequivalent Exchange."

Ren focused. He didn't want spray. He wanted a biological knit. A cellular reconstruction paste.

Value is subjective. To a man with a burn, the spray is gold. To the System, it was chemicals. Ren forced the math to work.

[Input: Burn Spray + Vitality Weeds + 20 Mana.]

[Exchange Successful.]

[Obtained: Revitalization Gel (Tier 3).]

The rusty aerosol can warped. The metal softened, turning into a small, elegant glass jar filled with a glowing, sapphire-blue gel.

Ren held it up. It cast a heavy, expensive light on his face.

Ren snapped a screenshot using the interface and sent it.

There was a pause. A long one. On the screen outside, the Rock Man had smashed the first barrier flat and was currently trying to rip the Field of Agony glass off his own stone skin, roaring in frustration.

[Fox]: Meet me. Sector 4. The old library.

[Ren]: No. Digital transfer. I send the item to the System Escrow. You send the coordinates and the time.

[Ren]: Survival instincts.

The transfer window popped up. Ren placed the blue jar into the void slot.

[You received: Encrypted Data Packet.]

Ren opened it immediately.

A map overlay appeared in his vision. A red skull icon pulsing three blocks east.

[Target: Thunder Calamity (Young Variant)]

[Spawn Time: 45 Minutes.]

[Condition: Injured. The beast is currently molting.]

Ren smiled. Molting. That meant its defense was zero. It was a glass cannon.

A "World Boss" that was technically a baby, caught with its pants down. It was the opportunity of a lifetime.

But three blocks east... that was close. Too close. If that thing woke up fully, Skyview Garden would be in the blast radius.

Ren closed the chat. Business concluded.

He stood up. He had forty-five minutes to kill a god.

But first, he had to take out the trash.

Ren took the elevator to the ground floor.

The lobby was vibrating. THUD. THUD.

The Rock Man was at the front doors now. The plywood Ren’s units had nailed up was splintering. A fist made of grey granite punched through the wood, showering the marble floor with splinters.

"I'm coming in!" The Rock Man bellowed. "I'm going to turn your skull into a bowl!"

Lin Xia was standing by the elevator, holding a kitchen knife with two hands. She was shaking so hard the blade was rattling against her watch.

"He... he's through the kill box," she said.

"He is," Ren agreed. He adjusted his cuffs. "Open the door."

"Unlock the door. Let him in."

Lin Xia looked at him like he had asked her to juggle grenades. "He'll kill us."

"He's a customer," Ren said. "And the customer is waiting."

Ren walked past her. He tapped the electronic lock on the wall.

The magnetic seals disengaged.

The shattered doors swung open.

The Rock Man stumbled in. He was massive, easily six-foot-five, his body covered in thick, tectonic plates of grey stone. He looked less like a man and more like a golem hacked out of a quarry.

Behind him, the street was empty. The rest of his gang was either dead in the kill box, bleeding out on the invisible glass, or had fled.

The Rock Man looked around the lobby. He saw the polished floors. He saw the warm lights. He saw the vending machine humming in the corner.

He grinned. It was a hideous cracking of stone.

"Nice place," he rumbled. He stepped forward, his heavy boots cracking the marble tile. "I'm taking it."

Ren stood five paces away. He wasn't wearing armor. He looked small.

"The lease is non-transferable," Ren said.

The Rock Man laughed. "You the Landlord? You look like a twig. I'm going to snap you."

It was like watching a landslide accelerate. The ground shook. He raised a fist the size of a cinder block, aiming directly for Ren’s head.

Ren didn't move. He didn't dodge.

He waited until the fist was a foot away.

Ren raised his hand, palm open, catching the stone fist.

The impact should have shattered Ren’s arm. It should have turned his bones to powder.

But the Inequivalent Exchange didn't care about momentum. It cared about definitions.

[Target: Granite Epidermis (Right Arm)]

[Composition: Silicon Dioxide, Feldspar, Mica.]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren looked into the Rock Man's eyes. He saw the triumph turn to confusion as the punch stopped dead against Ren’s palm.

"Rock beats scissors," Ren whispered.

The mana in his veins surged. It was a violent, spiraling drain, sucking nearly 30% of his reserves in a microsecond.

"But paper covers rock."

[Exchange: Cohesion.]

Ren didn't change the rock to water. He didn't turn it to air. That was too expensive.

He simply exchanged the molecular bond holding the stone together for static electricity.

The effect was instantaneous.

The Rock Man’s arm didn't break. It unspooled.

The solid granite turned into a cloud of fine, grey sand. The heavy fist that was about to crush Ren’s skull simply poured over Ren’s hand like an hourglass emptying.

The Rock Man stumbled forward, his momentum carrying him through the cloud of dust that used to be his limb.

He looked at his shoulder. It ended in a stump of crumbling sand.

"My arm! Where is my arm?!"

Ren stepped aside as the giant fell to his knees. The sand piled up on the pristine floor.

"Dust to dust," Ren said.

He placed his hand on the back of the Rock Man’s neck.

[Target: Cranial Plating.]

"No! Wait!" The man screamed. "I yield! I have cores! I have—"

The head collapsed. A pile of sand slumped onto the torso. The body twitched once, twice, and then settled.

Silence returned to the lobby.

Ren looked at the pile of sand. It was high quality. Fine grain.

[Target Eliminated: Level 9 Tank.]

Ren kicked the pile. A small, glowing yellow core tumbled out of the sand.

[Item: Core of the Earth (Rank D)]

[Utility: Construction material. High density.]

Ren pocketed it. He looked at Lin Xia.

"Clean this up," he said. "Save the sand. We can use it for cement mix."

Lin Xia stared at the headless body. She stared at Ren.

"You... you turned him into dirt."

"Inefficient," Ren muttered, checking his mana bar. "Should have turned him into fertilizer. The garden needs nitrogen."

He walked to the Abyssal Kiosk.

He needed to prepare. The Thunder Calamity was molting.

He bought a bottle of water from the machine and downed it in one go.

"Unit 1," Ren called out.

The man appeared from the stairwell, panting. He had been guarding the basement.

"We are going out," Ren said. "Bring the cart."

The location Fox had sold him was an electrical substation three blocks away.

The fog was thicker now. Ren walked with a purpose, his boots making no sound on the damp asphalt. Unit 1 pushed a shopping cart behind him, the wheels squeaking rhythmically.

The cart was filled with oddities. A coil of copper wire. Six bottles of bleach. A car battery. And a bag of flour.

"Landlord," Unit 1 whispered. "Where are we going? The shadows... they're moving."

"Ignore them," Ren said. He had the Shadow Monarch Egg in his pocket. The lesser shadows of the city sensed the royalty and kept their distance. The fear radiating from Unit 1 was feeding the egg a steady stream of nutrients.

They reached the substation.

It was a fenced-in lot dominated by massive transformers and pylons. The air here hummed. The hair on Ren’s arms stood up.

In the center of the lot, coiled around the main transformer, was the beast.

The Thunder Calamity.

It looked like a komodo dragon made of blue lightning and wet obsidian. It was huge, the size of a school bus. But its skin was dull, cracking. Great flakes of black scale were peeling off, revealing raw, pulsing blue flesh underneath.

It was asleep. A low, thrumming snore vibrated through the ground.

[Status: Vulnerable.]

[Time to Awakening: 12 Minutes.]

Ren crouched behind a concrete barrier.

"Stay here," he told Unit 1. "If I die, the contract kills you. So pray for me."

Unit 1 nodded frantically.

Ren crept forward. He didn't draw a weapon. He didn't have a weapon that could hurt this thing. Even molting, a World Boss had HP in the thousands.

He reached the perimeter fence.

He took out a piece of paper and a pen.

Calculation variables: Voltage output of the transformer. Conductivity of the shedding scales. Flammability of the flour dust suspension.

He drew a diagram. It wasn't art. It was an equation.

Ren closed his eyes. This was the gamble.

[Inequivalent Exchange.]

He held up the piece of paper with his plan.

"I don't want the plan," Ren whispered. "I want the result."

[Target: Strategic Diagram (Potential Future)]

[Exchange: Precognition (3 Seconds).]

The paper burned in his hand. Ash drifted away.

And then, Ren’s mind snapped forward.

He saw himself throw the flour. He saw the cloud expand. He saw the spark.

The dust explosion cracked the beast's raw skin. It shrieked. It lunged.

Lightning arced. It hit the copper wire Ren had placed. The feedback loop fried the transformer.

The explosion was blinding.

But in the smoke, something else moved. A dagger. A player in stealth.

Someone stole the last hit.

Ren gasped, returning to the present. The headache was blinding, like a nail driven into his temple.

He had seen it. The kill steal.

Fox hadn't just sold the info to him. He had sold it to others. Or he was here himself, waiting for Ren to do the heavy lifting.

"Clever," Ren whispered. A thin smile touched his lips.

He looked at the sleeping beast.

He picked up the car battery and the bleach.

He wasn't going to blow it up.

He moved silently toward the main power coupling.

Most players played checkers. They hit things until they died.

He placed his hand on the high-voltage cable feeding the transformer the beast was sleeping on.

[Target: 10,000 Volts of Alternating Current.]

[Exchange Available.]

Ren looked at the sleeping dragon.

"Exchange," he breathed.

[Target Output: 10,000 Units of Neurotoxin (Gaseous).]

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. But it can be changed.

The hum of the electricity died. The cable turned grey.

And from the vents of the transformer, a silent, colorless gas began to hiss.

Ren backed away. He counted down.

The raw, exposed flesh under the scales absorbed the gas instantly.

The beast’s eyes snapped open. They were blue, electric.

No sound came out. Its nervous system shut down in a cascading failure. The lightning on its back flickered and died.

The health bar above the beast plummeted. It wasn't a fight. It was an execution.

But Ren didn't watch the beast. He watched the shadows near the fence.

"Come out, Fox," Ren said to the empty air.

A shimmer in the darkness.

A figure materialized. Dressed in lightweight rogue armor, holding two curved daggers. The face was covered by a mask.

"You poisoned it," the rogue said. The voice was distorted. "That destroys the meat."

"I don't eat lizard," Ren said.

The boss let out a final, silent shudder and collapsed.

[World Boss Defeated.]

[Congratulations! You have obtained...]

Ren ignored the notifications. He looked at the rogue.

"You sold me the timer," Ren said. "But you didn't sell me the exclusivity rights."

"There are no rights in the apocalypse," the rogue said, tensing for a sprint. "Only opportunities. I was going to take the core."

"You can try," Ren said.

He lifted his hand. The Shadow Monarch Egg was in his palm. It was vibrating violently now, gorged on the fear of the Iron Skulls and the terror of Unit 1.

The rogue paused. He looked at the egg. He felt the dread radiating from it.

"Inequivalent Exchange," Ren said. "I trade fear for obedience."

The rogue took a step back.

"Keep the kill," the rogue said. "You're crazy."

Ren reached into his inventory. He pulled out the jar of Revitalization Gel—the price he had agreed to pay.

The rogue caught it, surprised.

"A deal is a deal," Ren said. "I pay my debts."

The rogue looked at the jar, then at the dead dragon, then at Ren.

"You're a strange monster," the rogue said.

"I'm a merchant," Ren corrected. "And I'm opening a franchise."

The rogue vanished into the fog.

Ren turned to the carcass. A golden light was rising from the body.

Ren walked over. He placed his hand on the dragon's head.

The currency of secrets had paid off.

Now, he had the capital to build an empire.
